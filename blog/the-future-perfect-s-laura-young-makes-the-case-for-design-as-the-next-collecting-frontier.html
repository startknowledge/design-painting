The Future Perfect’s Laura Young Makes the Case for Design as the Next Collecting Frontier

As the art world continues to evolve, a new frontier is emerging in the realm of collecting: design. According to recent statistics, the global design market has experienced significant growth in recent years, with sales reaching $1.4 billion in 2020 (Source: Artnet, 2020) and $1.7 billion in 2022 (Source: Art Market Monitor, 2022). This trend is expected to continue, with many experts predicting that design will become a major player in the collecting world. At the forefront of this movement is Laura Young, founder of The Future Perfect, a Los Angeles-based gallery that has been championing design as a collectible medium since 2003.

The Rise of Design as a Collectible Medium

Young's vision for The Future Perfect was to create a platform that would showcase design as a unique and valuable form of artistic expression. Over the years, the gallery has featured a wide range of designers and artists, from established figures like _Rick Owens_ and **Gaetano Pesce** to emerging talents like _Chris Wolston_ and **Misha Kahn**. By highlighting the craftsmanship, creativity, and innovation that goes into each piece, Young has helped to elevate design from a purely functional discipline to a collectible art form.

Strategies for Collecting Design

So, how can you get started with collecting design? Here are 10 actionable strategies to consider:

  • Research, research, research: Before making a purchase, take the time to learn about the designer, the materials, and the production process. This will help you understand the value and significance of the piece.
  • Start with what you love: Collecting design is a personal journey, so it's essential to focus on pieces that resonate with you. Whether it's a specific style, material, or era, follow your passion and build your collection around it.
  • Consider the edition: Limited-edition design pieces can be highly valuable, so be sure to factor this into your purchasing decision. A smaller edition size can increase the piece's rarity and desirability.
  • Look for provenance: The history of ownership and exhibition can greatly impact the value of a design piece. Look for pieces with a strong provenance, such as those that have been exhibited in museums or owned by notable collectors.
  • Condition is key: The condition of a design piece can greatly affect its value. Look for pieces that are in excellent condition, or consider working with a conservator or restorer to repair or maintain the piece.
  • Build relationships with galleries and designers: Building relationships with galleries, designers, and other collectors can provide valuable insights and access to unique pieces. Attend design fairs, exhibitions, and events to connect with the design community.
  • Stay up-to-date with market trends: The design market is constantly evolving, with new trends and styles emerging all the time. Stay informed about market trends and shifts in demand to make informed purchasing decisions.
  • Consider the cultural significance: Design pieces can hold significant cultural or historical value. Consider the cultural context in which the piece was created, and how it reflects the time and place in which it was made.
  • Work with a reputable dealer: When purchasing a design piece, it's essential to work with a reputable dealer who can provide guidance and support throughout the process. Look for dealers who are members of professional organizations, such as the Art Dealers Association of America.
  • Have patience: Collecting design is a long-term process, and it may take time to find the perfect piece. Don't rush into a purchase – take the time to research, consider, and reflect on your decision.

Real-World Examples

One notable example of design as a collectible medium is the work of _Ettore Sottsass_, an Italian designer who was a key figure in the Memphis design movement. Sottsass's colorful, playful, and often surreal designs have become highly sought after by collectors, with pieces like his iconic "Carlton" room divider selling for hundreds of thousands of dollars at auction.

Another example is the work of _Ron Arad_, a British designer and artist known for his innovative and often provocative designs. Arad's pieces, such as his "Well-Tempered Chair" series, have been exhibited in museums and galleries around the world and are highly prized by collectors.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

When collecting design, there are several common mistakes to avoid. One of the most significant is not doing your research. Without proper research, you may end up purchasing a piece that is not as valuable or significant as you thought. Another mistake is not considering the condition of the piece. Condition can greatly impact the value of a design piece, so it's essential to carefully inspect the piece before making a purchase.

Additionally, not working with a reputable dealer can be a major mistake. A reputable dealer can provide valuable guidance and support throughout the purchasing process, helping you to avoid common pitfalls and ensure that you are making a wise investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between design and art?
While both design and art can be considered forms of creative expression, the key difference lies in their purpose and function. Design is often created with a specific function or purpose in mind, such as a chair or a table, while art is often created solely for its aesthetic or emotional value.
How do I determine the value of a design piece?
The value of a design piece can be determined by a variety of factors, including its rarity, condition, provenance, and cultural significance. It's essential to work with a reputable dealer or appraiser to determine the value of a piece.
What is the best way to care for and maintain a design piece?
The best way to care for and maintain a design piece will depend on the materials and construction of the piece. Generally, it's essential to avoid exposing the piece to direct sunlight, moisture, or extreme temperatures, and to clean and dust the piece regularly.
Can I commission a custom design piece from a designer or artist?
Yes, many designers and artists offer custom commission services, allowing you to work with them to create a unique and personalized piece. This can be a great way to create a piece that meets your specific needs and tastes.
How do I know if a design piece is an authentic work by the designer or artist?
To ensure that a design piece is an authentic work by the designer or artist, it's essential to work with a reputable dealer or gallery, and to carefully examine the piece for any signs of authenticity, such as a signature or documentation.
