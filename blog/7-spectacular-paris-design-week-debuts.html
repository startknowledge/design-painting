7 Spectacular Paris Design Week Debuts You Can't Miss in 2024

Paris Design Week is more than just an event—it’s a cultural phenomenon that redefines creativity every October. For over a decade, this annual celebration has transformed the City of Light into a global hub for design innovation, where emerging talents and established names alike showcase their most daring ideas. In 2024, the week promises to be even more electrifying, with a record number of debuts, experimental installations, and collaborations that push the boundaries of what design can achieve.

Whether you're a design professional, a collector, or simply someone who appreciates beauty in its many forms, Paris Design Week offers something unforgettable. From sustainable materials to digital integration, this year’s debuts reflect the evolving priorities of the design world—where aesthetics meet ethics, and tradition inspires the future.

In this guide, we’ll explore seven of the most spectacular debuts at Paris Design Week 2024, what makes them stand out, and how you can engage with them—whether you're attending in person or following along online. We’ll also break down actionable strategies for navigating the event, common mistakes to avoid, and why this year’s edition is particularly significant for the design community.

The Rise of Paris Design Week: A Brief History

Paris Design Week didn’t emerge overnight. Its roots trace back to the early 2000s when the city’s design scene began gaining international recognition. The first official edition in 2005 was a modest affair, organized by the Fédération Française du Meuble (French Furniture Federation) and a handful of galleries. Over the years, it evolved into a sprawling, citywide festival that now attracts over 500,000 visitors annually, according to the Paris Design Week organization.

What started as a way to showcase French craftsmanship has since become a global platform for experimentation. Today, Paris Design Week is known for its unconventional spaces—think abandoned factories, historic mansions, and even rooftops—where designers break free from traditional exhibition norms. This year, the event is expected to feature over 1,000 exhibitions across the city, making it one of the most diverse design festivals in the world.

The 2024 edition is particularly notable because it coincides with Paris’s status as the World Capital of Design for 2024, a title awarded by the International Council of Societies of Industrial Design (ICSID). This designation underscores the city’s role as a leader in shaping the future of design, from sustainable urban planning to AI-driven creativity.

Why Paris Design Week Debuts Are Worth Your Attention

Paris Design Week isn’t just about showcasing finished products—it’s a launchpad for ideas. Debuts at this event often signal the beginning of a designer’s career or a brand’s entry into the global market. Here’s why these debuts are worth paying attention to:

  • Innovation First: Many debuts introduce cutting-edge techniques or materials that haven’t been seen before. For example, the use of mycelium-based packaging or 3D-printed ceramics has gained traction in recent years, often making their first public appearance at Paris Design Week.
  • Networking Opportunities: Attendees include curators, collectors, and industry leaders who are actively seeking new talent. A debut at this event can lead to collaborations, commissions, or even representation by galleries.
  • Cultural Impact: Paris Design Week has a history of influencing broader design trends. The 2016 debut of Studio KO, for instance, brought minimalist, modular furniture into mainstream conversation, inspiring a wave of similar designs.
  • Accessibility: Unlike traditional design fairs, Paris Design Week is free to attend in many cases, making it one of the most inclusive events in the industry. This democratization allows emerging designers to gain visibility without the barriers of high ticket prices.
  • Sustainability Focus: With growing consumer demand for eco-conscious design, many debuts at Paris Design Week highlight upcycled materials, zero-waste production, and circular economy principles. This year, expect to see even more designers integrating these values into their work.

7 Spectacular Paris Design Week Debuts to Watch in 2024

From interactive installations to wearable art, the debuts at Paris Design Week 2024 are set to redefine creativity. Below, we’ve curated seven of the most anticipated presentations, along with insights into what makes them special.

1. Studio KO: "Modular Futures"

Designer: Studio KO (Kévin O’Brien) Location: Galerie Kreo (17th arrondissement) Date: October 15–20, 2024

Studio KO, known for its minimalist yet functional designs, is returning to Paris Design Week with a new collection called "Modular Futures". This debut focuses on adaptable furniture systems that can be reconfigured to suit different living spaces and needs. What’s particularly exciting is the integration of smart technology, where pieces can be controlled via app for lighting, temperature, or even sound.

Why it stands out:

  • Combines sustainability with digital innovation, addressing the growing demand for smart homes that are also eco-friendly.
  • Uses recycled aluminum and FSC-certified wood, aligning with Paris Design Week’s sustainability theme.
  • Offers customizable configurations, making it ideal for modern, flexible living spaces.

Actionable Tip: If you’re interested in smart furniture, this debut is a must-see. Look for their "Adapt" chair, which features a modular base that can be swapped for different seating options.

2. Émilie Duez: "Biophilic Interiors"

Designer: Émilie Duez Location: Galerie Perrotin (16th arrondissement) Date: October 14–19, 2024

Émilie Duez, a rising star in the interior design world, is debuting her "Biophilic Interiors" collection, which blends organic forms with modern functionality. Inspired by nature, her work features handcrafted ceramics, textile-based wall coverings, and sculptural lighting that mimic natural light.

Why it stands out:

  • Focuses on biophilic design, a trend that connects people with nature through their living spaces.
  • Uses locally sourced materials, reducing the carbon footprint of her designs.
  • Includes interactive elements, such as "Living Walls" that incorporate real plants.

Common Mistake to Avoid: Many designers struggle with balancing aesthetics and functionality in biophilic design. Duez’s work proves that it’s possible to create spaces that are both visually stunning and practical for daily use.

3. Thomas Pheulpin: "Digital Craft"

Designer: Thomas Pheulpin Location: Galerie Creuzet (1st arrondissement) Date: October 16–21, 2024

Thomas Pheulpin, a pioneer in digital craftsmanship, is debuting "Digital Craft", a collection that merges traditional woodworking with 3D printing and CNC machining. His latest pieces feature intricate geometric patterns that would be impossible to achieve with hand tools alone.

Why it stands out:

  • Represents the future of hybrid craftsmanship, where digital precision meets artisanal skill.
  • Explores parametric design, allowing for endless variations of the same base concept.
  • Highlights sustainable wood sourcing, ensuring that even digital processes align with eco-conscious values.

Advantage: Pheulpin’s work shows how technology can enhance, rather than replace, traditional craftsmanship. This is particularly relevant as the design industry seeks to preserve heritage skills while embracing innovation.

4. Studio KORE: "The Human Scale"

Designer: Studio KORE Location: Le Marais (3rd arrondissement) Date: October 13–22, 2024

Studio KORE, a collective of young designers, is presenting "The Human Scale", a collection that rethinks everyday objects through the lens of ergonomics and accessibility. Their debut includes adjustable desks, universal seating, and modular storage solutions designed for people of all abilities.

Why it stands out:

  • Prioritizes inclusive design, a growing trend in the industry.
  • Uses modular components, allowing users to customize their spaces as their needs change.
  • Incorporates ergonomic research, ensuring comfort and functionality.

Limitations: While inclusive design is essential, some critics argue that mass production can dilute the customization aspect. Studio KORE addresses this by offering premium, handcrafted options alongside their modular lines.

5. Anouk Wipprecht: "Neural Wearables"

Designer: Anouk Wipprecht Location: Palais de Tokyo (16th arrondissement) Date: October 12–23, 2024

Anouk Wipprecht, a tech-forward fashion designer, is debuting "Neural Wearables", a collection of interactive clothing and accessories that respond to the wearer’s emotions and movements. Using biometric sensors and AI, these pieces can change color, shape, or even emit sound based on the user’s physiological data.

Why it stands out:

  • Represents the blurring line between fashion and technology.
  • Explores emotional design, where wearables become extensions of the self.
  • Uses sustainable materials, such as recycled metals and biodegradable fabrics.

Actionable Strategy: If you’re interested in wearable tech, this debut is a great opportunity to see how AI and fashion are evolving. Look for their "Emotion Dress", which changes patterns based on the wearer’s heart rate.
