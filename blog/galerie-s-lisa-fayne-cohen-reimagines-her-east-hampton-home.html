Actionable Strategies for Reimagining Your Home

Reimagining your home, like Lisa Fayne Cohen’s East Hampton home, requires careful planning, attention to detail, and a deep understanding of design and painting principles. Here are 8-10 actionable strategies to help you get started:

  • Start with a clear vision: Define your design style, preferences, and goals to create a cohesive and functional space.
  • Assess your space: Evaluate the layout, natural light, and architectural features of your home to determine the best design and painting approach.
  • Choose a color scheme: Select a palette that reflects your personal style and complements the natural surroundings of your home.
  • Consider the 60-30-10 rule: Allocate 60% of your budget to essential elements, 30% to secondary elements, and 10% to decorative elements.
  • Incorporate texture and pattern: Add depth and visual interest to your space by combining different textures and patterns.
  • Don’t forget about lighting: Use a combination of natural and artificial light sources to create a warm and inviting atmosphere.
  • Bring in plants: Add a touch of natural beauty and freshness to your space with plants and greenery.
  • Make it functional: Ensure that your design and painting choices prioritize functionality and usability.
  • Seek professional advice: Consult with a designer or painter to get expert guidance and advice on your project.
  • Be patient and flexible: Reimagining your home is a process that requires time, effort, and flexibility – be prepared to make adjustments and changes along the way.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Reimagining Your Home

While reimagining your home can be an exciting and rewarding experience, there are common mistakes that can quickly turn your project into a nightmare. Some of the most common mistakes to avoid include:

  • Insufficient planning: Failing to create a clear design plan and budget can lead to costly mistakes and delays.
  • Poor communication: Not communicating effectively with your designer, painter, or contractor can result in misunderstandings and misinterpretations.
  • Inadequate lighting: Failing to consider the lighting needs of your space can lead to a dark and uninviting atmosphere.
  • Ignoring functionality: Prioritizing form over function can result in a space that is beautiful but impractical.
  • Not considering the surroundings: Failing to take into account the natural surroundings and architectural features of your home can lead to a design that is out of place and unharmonious.
