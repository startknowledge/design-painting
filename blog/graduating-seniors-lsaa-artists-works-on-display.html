8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Exposure for Your Graduating Artworks

Graduating from LSAA is just the first step—the real challenge is getting your work seen by the right people. Below are eight proven strategies to ensure your art doesn’t just stay in your studio but grabs attention in galleries, online, and beyond.

1. Develop a Strong Portfolio Website (Beyond Just Behance & Instagram)

Many artists make the mistake of relying solely on Instagram or Behance to showcase their work. While these platforms are great for initial exposure, they lack the depth and professionalism needed to attract galleries and collectors.

What to Do Instead:

  • Create a custom portfolio website (using platforms like Squarespace, Wix, or Adobe Portfolio).
  • Include:
    • A high-quality hero image of your best work.
    • A curated selection (not just everything you’ve ever made).
    • An artist statement (explaining your artistic philosophy).
    • A contact form for galleries, curators, and potential buyers.
    • A blog section (if you write about your creative process).

Real-World Example: A recent LSAA graduate named Eleanor Carter built a minimalist, high-impact website featuring her hyper-realistic ink paintings of urban landscapes. Within six months of graduation, she secured a solo exhibition at a small London gallery after a curator discovered her site.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Overloading your site with too many works—this can make it look cluttered. ✅ Do: Keep it clean, professional, and easy to navigate.

2. Leverage Social Media Strategically (Beyond Just Posting Art)

Social media is a double-edged sword—it can either boost your career or fade into obscurity if not used correctly.

How to Optimize Your Social Presence:

  • Instagram & TikTok:
    • Post behind-the-scenes content (time-lapses of painting, studio tours).
    • Use trending hashtags like #EmergingArtist, #GraduateArt, #ContemporaryPainting.
    • Engage with art influencers and galleries by commenting thoughtfully.
  • LinkedIn:
    • Treat it like a professional portfolio—share your artistic journey, collaborations, and industry insights.
  • Twitter/X:
    • Join art-related Twitter chats (e.g., #ArtTwitter, #GalleryChat).
    • Retweet and engage with curators, collectors, and fellow artists.

Real-World Example: Daniel Reeves, an LSAA graduate specializing in digital abstract paintings, grew his Instagram following from 500 to 50,000 in six months by:

  • Posting short, engaging Reels showing his brushstroke techniques.
  • Collaborating with art supply brands for sponsored content.
  • Using SEO-friendly captions (e.g., "How I blend colors in digital painting—try this at home!").

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Posting only finished works—people love process content. ✅ Do: Share sketches, failures, and progress to build a relatable, human brand.

3. Apply to Graduate & Post-Graduate Exhibitions

One of the best ways to get noticed is by participating in curated exhibitions where your work is seen by gallery owners, collectors, and critics.

Where to Apply:

  • LSAA’s Annual Graduate Show (if you’re still a student, this is a must).
  • Royal Academy Summer Exhibition (open to all artists, not just graduates).
  • Art London (one of the UK’s largest contemporary art fairs).
  • Emerging Artist Programs (e.g., Saatchi Art’s New Contemporaries).
  • Online Platforms (e.g., ArtStation, DeviantArt, or local digital galleries).

Pro Tip:

  • Follow submission deadlines—many exhibitions close months before the event.
  • Tailor your submission to the gallery’s theme (if applicable).

Real-World Example: Sophia Martinez, an LSAA painting graduate, had her work selected for the Royal Academy’s Summer Exhibition after submitting a series of emotional figurative paintings. Her inclusion led to multiple gallery offers within weeks.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Submitting the same work everywhere—curators notice repetition. ✅ Do: Rotate your submissions to show growth and versatility.

4. Collaborate with Other Artists & Designers

Working with other creatives can expand your reach and introduce your work to new audiences.

How to Find Collaborations:

  • Join art collectives (e.g., LSAA’s own student groups).
  • Partner with musicians, poets, or fashion designers for cross-disciplinary projects.
  • Attend local art meetups (check Meetup.com or Eventbrite).

Real-World Example: James Wong, an LSAA graduate specializing in textile art, collaborated with a local fashion brand to create a limited-edition clothing line featuring his paintings. The project doubled his Instagram following and led to a commission from a major fashion house.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Choosing collaborators randomly—make sure they align with your aesthetic and values. ✅ Do: Research potential partners and propose a clear vision for the project.

5. Create Limited-Edition Prints & Merchandise

Selling physical copies of your work is a low-risk, high-reward way to generate income and build a fanbase.

How to Do It:

  • Use print-on-demand services (e.g., Redbubble, Society6, or Printful) for minimal upfront cost.
  • Offer exclusive prints (e.g., "First 50 copies signed by the artist").
  • Sell at local markets or pop-up shops (great for networking).

Real-World Example: Lena Chen, an LSAA graduate known for her surreal ink drawings, launched a Patreon campaign offering exclusive prints and digital wallpapers. Within a year, she earned enough to fund a solo exhibition.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Overpricing your prints—research what similar artists charge. ✅ Do: Start with a mid-range price and adjust based on demand.

6. Network with Galleries & Curators (Even Before Graduation)

Many artists wait until after graduation to reach out to galleries, but proactive networking can fast-track your career.

How to Approach Galleries:

  • Research galleries that represent artists in your style.
  • Send a professional email (not just a DM) with:
    • A polite introduction.
    • A link to your portfolio.
    • A clear ask (e.g., "I’d love to know if you’d be open to viewing my work for potential representation.").
  • Attend gallery openings (even if you don’t have work there yet).

Real-World Example: Olivia Patel, an LSAA graduate, emailed 20 London galleries before graduating. One curator responded, inviting her to submit work for their next group show. She was accepted within two months.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Being too pushy—curators receive hundreds of emails; keep it concise and professional. ✅ Do: Follow up politely if you don’t hear back in 2-3 weeks.

7. Document & Share Your Exhibition Process

Even if you’re not exhibiting yet, documenting your journey can attract opportunities.

How to Do It:

  • Film a vlog of your studio setup, painting process, or exhibition prep.
  • Write a blog post (on your website or Medium) about what you’ve learned.
  • Share testimonials from professors or collaborators.

Real-World Example: Marcus Lee, an LSAA graduate, started a YouTube channel documenting his transition from student to professional artist. His honest, behind-the-scenes content went viral, leading to multiple gallery offers.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Only posting "perfect" content—people connect with real struggles and growth. ✅ Do: Show the messy, creative process—it makes you relatable and authentic.

8. Consider International Opportunities (Even as a Graduate)

The art world is global, and many opportunities arise outside the UK.

How to Expand Your Reach:

  • Apply to international residencies (e.g., MacDowell Colony, Yaddo, or local programs).
  • Submit to global art fairs (e.g., Art Basel Miami, Frieze London).
  • Use platforms like ArtStation to connect with international buyers.

Real-World Example: Aisha Okoro, an LSAA graduate, applied to a residency in Berlin and was accepted. Her exhibition there led to a solo show in Lagos, Nigeria, expanding her international career.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Assuming your work is only for local audiences—many collectors seek out international talent. ✅ Do: Research global art markets and adapt your marketing accordingly.

Common Mistakes Graduating Artists Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Transitioning from student to professional is challenging, and many artists make costly mistakes early in their careers. Here are the most common pitfalls and how to avoid them.

1. Not Having a Clear Artistic Vision

Mistake: Changing styles too frequently without a cohesive body of work. Solution:

  • Develop a signature style (even if it evolves over time).
  • Stick to a theme or concept for at least one major project.

Example: *A graduate who switched between abstract, figurative, and digital work every few months struggled to build recognition. Once they focused on a single theme (e.g., "Nature in Urban Spaces"), their work gained more traction.

2. Undervaluing Their Work

Mistake: Selling prints or commissions for too low a price. Solution:

  • Research market rates (check Saatchi Art, Artsy, or local galleries).
  • Start with mid-range pricing and adjust based on demand.

Example: *A graduate sold a signed print for £20 when similar works sold for £100+. After adjusting pricing, they increased sales by 300%.

3. Ignoring Social Media Growth

Mistake: Posting only finished works without engagement. Solution:

  • Post consistently (3-5 times per week).
  • Engage with followers (reply to comments, ask questions).

Example: *A graduate who only posted finished paintings saw no growth. After sharing sketches, failures, and studio updates, their following grew from 500 to 10,000 in six months.
