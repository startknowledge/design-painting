8 Signature Techniques from LSAA Graduating Seniors

The works on display at LSAA’s exhibition reveal a **rich tapestry of techniques**, each reflecting the artist’s unique perspective. Below, we break down the **top 8 techniques** that define this year’s graduating class—and how you can incorporate them into your own work.

1. Hybrid Digital and Traditional Media

Many LSAA graduates are **blurring the lines between analog and digital**, creating works that feel both tactile and futuristic. For example, **Aisha Patel’s** *"Digital Ghosts"* series combines **oil paint with augmented reality (AR) overlays**, allowing viewers to interact with the artwork via a smartphone app. This technique not only **expands the viewer’s experience** but also **future-proofs the art** in an increasingly digital world.

How to apply this:

  • Start with a **traditional base** (e.g., canvas, paper) and layer digital elements using tools like Procreate or Photoshop.
  • Experiment with **AR apps** like Adobe Aero to create interactive pieces.
  • Document your process on social media to attract tech-savvy audiences.

2. Textural Layering in Mixed-Media Works

Textural depth is a hallmark of LSAA’s graduating class. **Liam Reynolds**, for instance, uses **charcoal, gouache, and found objects** to create relief-like surfaces in his paintings. His piece *"The Weight of Silence"* features **crumpled paper embedded in resin**, adding a tactile dimension that draws the viewer in.

How to apply this:

  • Collect **unconventional materials** (e.g., fabric scraps, old books, metal shavings) to incorporate into your work.
  • Use **mod podge or gel medium** to seal layers and enhance durability.
  • Play with **light and shadow** to highlight textures—consider using a **spotlight or backlighting** in your studio.

3. Minimalist Abstraction with Bold Color Palettes

While abstraction is nothing new, LSAA graduates are taking it to the next level with **high-contrast color schemes** and **geometric precision**. **Mira Patel’s** *"Neon Veils"* series features **monochromatic backgrounds with single, vibrant accents**, creating a sense of **visual tension**. Her use of **acrylic pours** adds an element of unpredictability, making each piece unique.

How to apply this:

  • Limit your palette to **3-5 colors** to maintain focus and impact.
  • Experiment with **color theory**—try complementary colors (e.g., blue and orange) for maximum contrast.
  • Use **stencils or masking tape** to achieve clean, geometric lines.

4. Narrative Storytelling Through Symbolism

Many LSAA graduates use **symbolism and metaphor** to convey deeper meanings. **Daniel Kim’s** *"The Last Broadcast"* is a **digital painting** depicting a lone figure holding a broken radio, surrounded by **static and fragmented images**. The piece critiques **modern disconnection**, using visual noise to represent the chaos of information overload.

How to apply this:

  • Develop a **personal symbolism dictionary**—assign meanings to recurring motifs in your work.
  • Research **art history** to draw inspiration from masters like Magritte or Dali, who excelled in symbolic storytelling.
  • Write a **short artist’s statement** to accompany your work, explaining its conceptual foundation.

5. Unconventional Materials and Found Objects

Sustainability and creativity go hand in hand for many LSAA graduates. **Olivia Thompson’s** *"Waste Landscapes"* series repurposes **plastic waste, glass shards, and discarded metal** into **sculptural installations**. Her piece *"Tides of Trash"* transforms a pile of ocean debris into a **glowing, otherworldly formation** using **UV-reactive paint**.

How to apply this:

  • Visit **thrift stores, construction sites, or beaches** to source materials.
  • Experiment with **resin casting** to encase found objects in protective, artistic forms.
  • Document your **sustainable process**—many galleries and buyers appreciate eco-conscious artists.

6. Dynamic Lighting and Projection Mapping

Light is a **powerful storytelling tool** for LSAA graduates. **Ethan Cole’s** *"Phantom Hours"* uses **projection mapping** to animate a **black-and-white portrait**, overlaying it with **subtle, shifting colors** that evoke the passage of time. This technique turns a static image into an **immersive experience**.

How to apply this:

  • Start with a **simple projection setup** using a laptop and projector.
  • Use **free software** like VLC or Resolume to create dynamic light effects.
  • Collaborate with **lighting designers** or **digital artists** to expand your capabilities.

7. Playful Subversion of Traditional Techniques

Some LSAA graduates **challenge conventions** by reinterpreting classic techniques. **Priya Mehta’s** *"Ink & Irony"* series takes **traditional ink wash painting** and **distorts it with digital glitch effects**, creating a **juxtaposition of serenity and chaos**. This approach **invites viewers to question** the boundaries of art.

How to apply this:

  • Choose a **traditional technique** (e.g., watercolor, etching) and **subvert it** with modern elements.
  • Experiment with **digital distortion tools** like Topaz Glow or Photoshop’s liquify filter**.
