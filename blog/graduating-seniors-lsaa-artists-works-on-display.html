8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Exposure for Your Art

Graduating from LSAA is just the first step—now, you need a strategic plan to ensure your work reaches the right audience. Here are eight proven strategies to boost visibility, secure sales, and build a lasting career.

1. Build a Professional Online Portfolio (Beyond Instagram)

While Instagram is great for initial exposure, a dedicated portfolio website is non-negotiable for serious artists.

How to Do It Right: ✔ Use a clean, minimalist design (think white space, high-quality images, and easy navigation). ✔ Include a "Bio" section with your artistic philosophy, influences, and career highlights. ✔ Feature a "Contact" page with an email, social links, and a commission form. ✔ Optimize for SEO—use keywords like "LSAA graduate artist," "contemporary digital art," and "emerging artist portfolio" in your meta descriptions.

Example: One LSAA graduate, Eleanor V., launched a portfolio site within a month of graduation. She ranked on the first page of Google for "emerging UK digital artists" by blogging about her process and sharing behind-the-scenes content.

2. Leverage Social Media Strategically (Beyond Just Posting Art)

Posting art is not enough—you need a content strategy that engages, educates, and converts followers.

Best Practices: ✔ Post consistently (3-5 times per week) with a mix of:

  • Process videos (timelapses of your work)
  • Behind-the-scenes content (studio tours, sketches)
  • Art-related polls & Q&As (e.g., "What should I paint next?") ✔ Use hashtags smartly—#LSAAArt #EmergingArtist #ContemporaryDigitalArt #UKArtScene ✔ Engage with micro-influencers in the art and design niche—collaborate on takeovers or shoutouts.

Real-World Example: Daniel K., an LSAA graduate specializing in cyberpunk-inspired digital paintings, grew his Instagram from 500 to 20,000 in six months by:

  • Posting "mystery art" teasers (hinting at upcoming works)
  • Running a "guess the medium" challenge (engaging followers with interactive content)
  • Partnering with a small art blog for a feature on his process

3. Apply to Exhibitions & Competitions (Even If You’re Not Selected)

Exhibitions validate your work and expose you to curators, collectors, and critics.

Where to Apply:

  • London Design Biennale (for hybrid art/design works)
  • Digital Art London (DAL) (for digital and interactive art)
  • Saatchi Art New Contemporaries (for emerging UK artists)
  • Local galleries & pop-up spaces (many are open to new talent)

Pro Tip: Even if you’re not selected, network with organizers—they often curate off-site exhibitions or recommend artists to collectors.

Example: Sophia L., an LSAA sculpture graduate, didn’t get into the main DAL exhibition, but she attended the event as a guest and connected with a curator who later invited her to a private collector’s showcase. This led to her first solo sale.

4. Create a "Signature Style" That Stands Out

In a crowded art market, having a distinctive style makes you memorable.

How to Develop One: ✔ Experiment with techniques (e.g., glitch art, mixed media, augmented reality). ✔ Study your influences—what unique twist can you add? ✔ Limit your palette or subject matter (e.g., all cyberpunk themes or monochromatic landscapes).

Example: Marcus T., an LSAA digital artist, specializes in "neon dystopias"—futuristic cityscapes with glowing, distorted architecture. His consistent style has made him recognizable in the digital art scene, leading to multiple commissions from game studios.

5. Collaborate with Other Artists & Brands

Collaborations expand your reach and introduce you to new audiences.

Ways to Collaborate:

  • Artist collectives (e.g., join a group show with other LSAA grads)
  • Brand partnerships (e.g., design a limited-edition print for a fashion brand)
  • Live painting events (stream on Twitch or YouTube with a guest artist)

Example: Aisha R., an LSAA painter, collaborated with a local coffee shop to create a month-long "artist residency" where she painted custom murals in exchange for exclusive coffee blends. This doubled her Instagram followers and attracted local collectors.

6. Offer Limited-Edition Prints & Digital Products

Not everyone can afford original art, but prints and digital downloads make your work accessible.

How to Sell Them: ✔ Use Print-on-Demand services (like Redbubble or Society6) for low-risk sales. ✔ Create a "Patron" or "Kickstarter" campaign for exclusive editions. ✔ Bundle prints with digital wallpapers (great for digital artists).

Example: Rafael M., an LSAA digital artist, sold 500 copies of his "Cyberpunk Wallpaper Pack" in three days after launching it on Gumroad. He later expanded into physical prints, which sold out within a week.

7. Network with Collectors & Galleries (Even Virtually)

Many collectors discover new talent through social media, but direct outreach can accelerate your career.

How to Network Effectively: ✔ Attend virtual gallery openings (check Instagram Live or Zoom links). ✔ DM collectors with a short, personalized message (e.g., "Hi [Name], I loved your recent purchase of [Artist]. My work explores similar themes—would love to send you a digital preview."). ✔ Join art forums (like Reddit’s r/ArtCollecting or Discord art communities).

Example: Liam O., an LSAA mixed-media artist, reached out to a small London gallery after seeing them post about emerging artists. He sent a PDF of his portfolio and scheduled a virtual meeting. Within two weeks, he was offered a solo exhibition.

8. Document & Share Your Journey (Content is King)

People connect with stories, not just art. Documenting your process makes you relatable and marketable.

How to Do It: ✔ Start a blog (on your website) about your artistic journey. ✔ Post "day in the life" videos (even if it’s just sketching in your studio). ✔ Write about your influences (e.g., "How [Artist] Inspired My Latest Series").

Example: Nadia P., an LSAA painter, started a blog called "The Making of Me" where she shared her struggles, wins, and artistic evolution. This built a loyal following, and collectors began reaching out because they felt connected to her story.

Why It’s Notable:
