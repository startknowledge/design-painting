10 Visionary Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen’s Futuristic Fashion

Introduction: The Rise of Avant-Garde Fashion Designers in the Digital Age

Iris van Herpen is a name synonymous with cutting-edge fashion, innovative technology, and architectural aesthetics. Since her debut in 2007, she has redefined what it means to be a fashion designer, blending 3D printing, digital craftsmanship, and sculptural silhouettes into wearable art. According to a 2023 report by McKinsey & Company, the global fashion industry is projected to reach $3.3 trillion by 2025, with a growing demand for sustainable, tech-infused, and experimental designs—areas where van Herpen excels.

If you’re drawn to her futuristic ethos, geometric precision, and boundary-pushing creativity, you’ll love exploring these 10 artists and designers who share her vision. From AI-assisted fashion to biofabrication and kinetic wearables, these creators are pushing the boundaries of what clothing can be.

Why Follow Artists Like Iris van Herpen?

Before diving into the list, let’s explore why her work resonates—and how these artists continue her legacy:

  1. Technological Integration – Van Herpen’s use of 3D printing and digital fabrication has inspired a new wave of designers experimenting with smart textiles and AI-generated patterns.
  2. Sustainability Through Innovation – While sustainability is often associated with upcycling, van Herpen proves that high-tech materials can also be eco-conscious.
  3. Artistic Collaboration – She frequently works with scientists, architects, and engineers, blending disciplines to create unprecedented designs.
  4. Gender-Fluid and Experimental Silhouettes – Her work challenges traditional femininity, embracing asymmetry, fluidity, and structural forms.
  5. Global Influence – From Paris Fashion Week to Tokyo’s avant-garde scene, her impact is felt worldwide, inspiring designers across continents.

If these principles excite you, the following artists will expand your creative horizons and deepen your appreciation for modern fashion as a form of digital art.

10 Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen

1. Marine Serre – The Sustainable Futurist

Why She’s Similar to van Herpen: Marine Serre, a French designer known for her "soft futurism," merges recycled materials with architectural precision. Her 2021 Met Gala dress—a luminous, sculptural gown made from recycled plastic—echoes van Herpen’s love for material experimentation.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Future is Female" Collection (2021) – Features 3D-printed accessories and biodegradable fabrics.
  • "Recycled Plastic Dresses" – Transparent, structural pieces that resemble geometric sculptures.
  • Collaboration with Adidas (2022) – Introduced sustainable sneakers with recycled ocean plastics.

How to Engage:

  • Follow her on Instagram (@marineserre) for behind-the-scenes 3D printing processes.
  • Check out her documentary "Marine Serre: The Future is Female" (2021) on Netflix for insights into her sustainable innovation.

**2. Iris van Herpen & Daan van Golden – The Architectural Duo

(Note: While van Herpen is already mentioned, her frequent collaborator Daan van Golden—a 3D designer—deserves special attention.)

Why They’re Aligned with van Herpen: Van Golden specializes in digital sculpting and parametric design, creating custom 3D-printed elements that enhance van Herpen’s collections. Their work together in pieces like the "Crystal Dress (2019)"—a faceted, gem-like gown—showcases how architecture and fashion intersect.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "Parametric Jewelry" – Mathematically generated pieces that resemble crystal formations.
  • "Bio-Printed Accessories" – Collaborations with biotech firms to create organic, lab-grown materials.
  • "The Future of Fashion" Exhibit (2020, Paris) – A deep dive into digital fabrication in couture.

How to Engage:

  • Visit van Golden’s portfolio (daanvangolden.com) for technical breakdowns of his designs.
  • Watch his TED Talk (2021) on how AI is reshaping fashion.

3. Jun Takahashi – The Cyberpunk Visionary

Why He’s Similar to van Herpen: Jun Takahashi, founder of Undercover, is a master of cyberpunk aesthetics—think neon, asymmetry, and futuristic tailoring. His 2023 "Neon Noir" collection featured *glowing fabrics and deconstructed silhouettes, much like van Herpen’s biomorphic structures.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "Neon Noir" (2023) – Electric blue and pink pieces with cut-out details reminiscent of cyberpunk anime.
  • "The Future of Fashion" (2018, Tokyo) – A retrospective exhibit showcasing his digital-influenced designs.
  • "Undercover x Nike" (2022) – Sneakers with embedded LED lights, blending streetwear and high-tech fashion.

How to Engage:

  • Follow @undercover_jun on Instagram for real-time updates on his experimental collections.
  • Read his interview with Vogue Japan (2020) on how he predicts fashion trends.

4. Anouk Wipprecht – The Bio-Haptic Pioneer

Why She’s Similar to van Herpen: Anouk Wipprecht is a *Dutch designer who merges biotechnology, robotics, and fashion. Her "Haptic Dress (2019)"—a wearable exoskeleton with embedded sensors—mirrors van Herpen’s interactive, tech-driven approach.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "Haptic Dress" (2019) – A dress that responds to touch with vibrations and light.
  • "Bio-Printed Skin" (2021) – Lab-grown materials that mimic human skin texture.
  • "AI-Generated Patterns" (2022) – Uses machine learning to create dynamic, evolving designs.

How to Engage:

  • Watch her TED Talk (2020) on how AI is changing fashion.

5. Marine Studer – The Digital Couturier

Key Works to Explore:

  • "Digital Couture" (2021) – Entirely 3D-printed dresses with interlocking structures.

8 Actionable Strategies to Explore Avant-Garde Fashion Like van Herpen

If you’re inspired by these artists, here’s how to dive deeper into their world:

1. Study Their Technical Processes

  • Van Herpen’s 3D Printing – Watch her workshops (available on MasterClass) to learn how she designs in digital space.
  • Marine Serre’s Upcycling – Follow her sustainability blog for DIY tips on repurposing materials.
  • Anouk Wipprecht’s Biofabrication – Explore biotech journals (like Nature) for latest advancements in lab-grown textiles.

2. Experiment with Digital Tools

  • Try Blender or Rhino 3D (free versions available) to design your own parametric patterns.
  • Use AI fashion generators like DALL·E 3 or MidJourney to create digital sketches.
  • Join fashion tech communities like Fashion AI on Discord for collaborative projects.

3. Attend Futuristic Fashion Exhibits

  • Paris Fashion Week (PFW) – "Digital Couture" Shows (2023)
  • Tokyo Fashion Week – "Cyberpunk" Collections (2024)
  • London Design Biennale – "Tech & Textiles" Exhibits (2025)

4. Collaborate with Engineers & Scientists

  • Reach out to 3D printing studios to co-create wearable art.
  • Partner with biotech labs to experiment with lab-grown materials.
