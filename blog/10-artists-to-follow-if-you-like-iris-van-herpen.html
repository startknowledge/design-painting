10 Visionary Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen’s Futuristic Fashion

Introduction: Why Iris van Herpen’s Work Captivates the Fashion World

Iris van Herpen is one of the most revolutionary figures in contemporary fashion, blending cutting-edge technology, avant-garde design, and architectural precision to redefine what wearable art can be. Since her debut in 2007, she has become a global icon, collaborating with brands like Chanel, Louis Vuitton, and Adidas while pushing the boundaries of 3D printing, digital fabrication, and sustainable innovation.

According to Business of Fashion (2023), van Herpen’s work has been exhibited in over 50 museums worldwide, including the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Victoria & Albert Museum. Her collections have sold out within minutes on platforms like Farfetch and Net-a-Porter, proving her influence extends far beyond traditional fashion circles.

If you’re drawn to her futuristic aesthetics, structural complexity, and fusion of art and technology, you’ll love exploring these 10 groundbreaking artists who share her vision. Each brings a unique perspective—whether through biomorphic forms, digital experimentation, or sustainable innovation—making them essential additions to your creative radar.

Why Follow Artists Like Iris van Herpen?

Before diving into the list, let’s explore why artists in this vein are worth following:

  1. Inspiration for Bold Design Choices – Van Herpen’s work encourages unconventional thinking, helping designers break free from linear trends.
  2. Understanding the Future of Fashion – Many of these artists are pioneers in digital fabrication, AI, and eco-conscious materials, shaping the industry’s next frontier.
  3. Cross-Disciplinary Learning – Their work often intersects with architecture, sculpture, and technology, offering fresh insights for creatives in any field.
  4. Networking Opportunities – Following these artists can connect you with like-minded professionals, studios, and collaborations.
  5. Staying Ahead of Trends – Since van Herpen’s work is ahead of its time, keeping up with similar voices ensures you’re never behind the curve.

Now, let’s explore the 10 artists who share her visionary spirit.

1. Marine Serre – The Sustainable Futurist

Who She Is

Marine Serre is a French designer known for her playful, eco-conscious, and technologically driven approach to fashion. Like van Herpen, she reimagines materials—but with a focus on upcycling, biodegradable fabrics, and digital innovation.

Why You’ll Love Her

  • Sustainability Meets Futurism: Her 2021 "Futuristic" collection featured recycled polyester and 3D-printed accessories, proving that high-tech and eco-friendly can coexist.
  • Architectural Silhouettes: Her asymmetrical draping and geometric patterns echo van Herpen’s structural elegance.
  • Collaborations with Tech Brands: She has worked with Google’s AI division to explore wearable tech, much like van Herpen’s smart textiles.

Key Takeaway

If you admire van Herpen’s technological boldness, Serre’s work shows how sustainability can be just as innovative.

2. Jun Takahashi (Undercover) – The Cyberpunk Storyteller

Who He Is

Jun Takahashi, the creative director of Undercover, is a master of cyberpunk aesthetics, blending punk rebellion with futuristic digital elements. His work often features glitchy textures, neon accents, and exaggerated proportions—much like van Herpen’s digital-inspired silhouettes.

Why You’ll Love Him

  • Digital Punk Fusion: His 2022 "Cyberpunk" collection included LED-embedded garments and holographic prints, mirroring van Herpen’s tech-infused designs.
  • Unconventional Materials: He uses plastic, rubber, and even recycled tech waste, much like van Herpen’s experimental fabrications.
  • Narrative-Driven Design: Like van Herpen’s theatrical runway shows, Takahashi’s work tells stories through fashion.

Key Takeaway

If you love van Herpen’s digital storytelling, Takahashi’s cyberpunk edge will inspire you to push boundaries with bold, narrative-driven designs.

3. Iris van Herpen’s Collaborators: The Collective of Futurists

While van Herpen is a solo genius, her collaborations with other artists and technologists have expanded her influence. Here are two key figures in her network:

A. Roosegaarde (Daan Roosegaarde) – The Air Pollution Artist

Roosegaarde, a Dutch designer-engineer, works on sustainable tech projects, including smart fabrics and air-purifying materials. His 2021 "Smog Free Towers" project aligns with van Herpen’s eco-conscious innovations.

B. Studio Dr. Suess – The 3D Printing Pioneers

This Dutch studio specializes in 3D-printed fashion, much like van Herpen’s digital fabrications. Their 2020 "Biomorphic" collection featured organic, sculptural forms that resemble van Herpen’s architectural draping.

Key Takeaway: Van Herpen’s collaborative spirit proves that the future of fashion is a collective effort—follow these tech-artist hybrids to stay ahead.

4. Anouk Wipprecht – The Wearable Tech Revolutionary

Who She Is

Anouk Wipprecht is a Dutch designer-engineer who creates interactive, AI-driven wearables. Her work blurs the line between fashion and technology, much like van Herpen’s digital fabrications.

Why You’ll Love Her

  • AI-Powered Garments: Her "Neural Dress" (2018) used facial recognition to change colors, a concept van Herpen explores with smart textiles.
  • Biometric Fashion: She designs clothing that responds to the wearer’s emotions, similar to van Herpen’s interactive, responsive designs.
  • Cybernetic Aesthetics: Her mechanical, almost robotic silhouettes echo van Herpen’s futuristic, architectural forms.

Key Takeaway

If you’re fascinated by van Herpen’s tech integration, Wipprecht’s wearable tech innovations will inspire you to think beyond traditional materials.

8 Actionable Strategies to Incorporate These Artists’ Approaches

Now that you’ve met the 10 artists who share van Herpen’s visionary spirit, here’s how to apply their techniques to your own work:

1. Experiment with Digital Fabrication

  • Start small: Use 3D printing for accessories (like Studio Dr. Suess) before moving to full garments.
  • Collaborate with engineers: Partner with tech studios to explore smart textiles (like Anouk Wipprecht).
  • Study van Herpen’s process: Watch her workshops on digital draping to understand how to translate digital designs into wearable art.

2. Embrace Sustainable Innovation

  • Upcycle materials: Like Marine Serre, repurpose post-consumer waste into high-tech fabrics.
  • Explore biodegradable resins: If using 3D printing, research eco-friendly materials (like Studio Dr. Suess).
  • Document your process: Share how you’re reducing waste—brands like Chanel and Louis Vuitton love sustainable storytelling.

3. Blend Architecture with Fashion

  • Study structural forms: Van Herpen’s architectural draping comes from understanding geometry. Take a course on parametric design.
  • Use unconventional materials: Like Jun Takahashi, experiment with plastics, rubbers, and metals in unexpected ways.
  • Create wearable sculptures: Think beyond clothing—design interactive installations (like Wipprecht’s AI wearables).

4. Tell Stories Through Design

  • Develop a narrative: Van Herpen’s runway shows are theatrical. Write a backstory for your collections.
  • Use digital elements: Incorporate LED lights, holograms, or AI responses (like Takahashi’s cyberpunk pieces).
  • Collaborate with artists: Work with musicians, filmmakers, or tech innovators to expand your vision.

5. Push Boundaries with Cyberpunk Aesthetics

  • Incorporate neon and glitch effects: Like Takahashi, play with digital distortions.
  • Experiment with exaggerated proportions: Stretch, distort, and deconstruct traditional silhouettes.
  • Explore biometric fashion: Use sensors or AI to create clothing that reacts to the wearer (like Wipprecht’s Neural Dress).

6. Master 3D Printing for Fashion

  • Start with accessories: Design custom jewelry or small sculptural pieces (like Serre’s 3D-printed elements).
  • Use open-source software: Tools like Blender or Rhino are free and powerful for digital fashion.
  • Join fashion tech communities: Platforms like FashionAI or 3D Printing Industry offer networking and tutorials.

7. Collaborate with Technologists

  • Find engineers or AI specialists: Many of these artists work with tech partners. Reach out to universities or startups.
  • Attend tech-fashion events: Web Summit, Milan Fashion Week’s tech exhibits, or CES are great places to connect with innovators.
  • Apply for grants: Organizations like Google’s Creative Lab or Adidas’ Futurecraft fund fashion-tech projects.

8. Document and Share Your Process

  • Create a portfolio: Like van Herpen, document every step—sketches, prototypes, and final pieces.
  • Use social media strategically: Platforms like Instagram, LinkedIn, and YouTube are essential for showcasing digital fashion.
  • Write about your work: Blogs, articles, or even a book can elevate your brand (like van Herpen’s exhibitions in major museums).
