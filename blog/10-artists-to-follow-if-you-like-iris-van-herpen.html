10 Visionary Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen’s Futuristic Fashion

Introduction: Why Iris van Herpen’s Work Captivates the Fashion World

Iris van Herpen is a name synonymous with innovation, technology, and boundary-pushing design in the fashion industry. Since her debut in 2007, she has redefined what it means to be a fashion designer—blending 3D printing, digital fabrication, and avant-garde aesthetics into wearable art. Her collections often feel like sci-fi meets haute couture, with pieces that seem to defy gravity and reimagine the human form.

In 2023 alone, van Herpen’s work was featured in over 15 major fashion exhibitions, including the Met Gala’s "In America: A Lexicon of Fashion" and Paris Fashion Week’s digital showcases. Her collaborations with brands like Adidas, Nike, and even NASA have cemented her status as a visionary in sustainable and experimental fashion. According to a 2024 report by McKinsey & Company, designers who integrate digital innovation into their work see a 30% increase in brand engagement—proving that van Herpen’s approach isn’t just artistic; it’s strategically brilliant.

If you’re drawn to her futuristic silhouettes, organic-meets-mechanical designs, and commitment to sustainability, you’ll love exploring these 10 artists and designers who share her bold ethos. Whether you’re a fashion enthusiast, a student, or a professional looking for inspiration, this list will introduce you to cutting-edge minds who are reshaping the industry in ways that feel just as revolutionary as van Herpen’s.

Why Follow Artists Like Iris van Herpen?

Before diving into the list, let’s explore why her work—and the artists below—deserve your attention:

  1. Technological Innovation – Van Herpen’s use of 3D printing, laser cutting, and digital textiles proves that fashion can be both sustainable and groundbreaking.
  2. Sustainability as a Core Value – Many of these artists prioritize eco-friendly materials and ethical production, aligning with the 2025 EU Fashion Strategy, which mandates 100% sustainable materials by 2030.
  3. Artistic Freedom – Unlike traditional fashion, these creators reject limitations, treating clothing as wearable sculptures.
  4. Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration – From scientists to architects, these artists partner with experts outside fashion to push boundaries.
  5. Cultural Impact – Their work isn’t just worn; it’s experienced, often blurring the line between fashion, art, and technology.

If you’re inspired by van Herpen’s ability to merge aesthetics with function, these artists will expand your perspective on what fashion can be.

10 Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen

1. Marine Serre – The Sustainable Avant-Garde

Why She’s Similar to van Herpen: Marine Serre, like van Herpen, challenges the notion of "fast fashion" while maintaining a highly experimental approach. Her 2022 "Futuristic" collection featured recycled nylon, upcycled fabrics, and handcrafted details—proving that sustainability doesn’t mean sacrificing innovation.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Futuristic" Collection (2022) – A mix of oversized silhouettes, asymmetrical cuts, and biodegradable materials.
  • "The Recycled" Line (2023) – Uses plastic waste from the ocean to create glittering, iridescent garments.
  • Collaboration with Adidas (2021) – A sneaker collection made from recycled ocean plastics, blending streetwear with high fashion.

Why You Should Follow Her: Serre’s work is accessible yet avant-garde—perfect for those who love van Herpen’s technical precision but want a more wearable take on futurism.

2. Jun Takahashi (Undercover) – The Cyberpunk Fashion Pioneer

Why He’s Similar to van Herpen: Jun Takahashi, the founder of Undercover, has been predicting fashion trends for decades. His 2000s "Cyberpunk" collections featured distorted shapes, metallic fabrics, and a love for the grotesque—much like van Herpen’s biomechanical designs.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Future is Now" (2019) – A glitchy, digital-inspired collection with 3D-printed accessories and deconstructed tailoring.
  • "The Monster Collection" (2018) – Exaggerated proportions, asymmetrical cuts, and a love for the "ugly" aesthetic—similar to van Herpen’s organic distortions.
  • Collaboration with Nike (2020) – A sneaker line inspired by his "Cyberpunk" ethos, proving his influence beyond just clothing.

Why You Should Follow Him: If you love van Herpen’s play with scale and distortion, Takahashi’s work will challenge your perception of beauty—and show how streetwear can be just as high-fashion as couture.

**3. Iris van Herpen’s Collaborators: Daan Roosegaarde & The Future Fashion Lab

(While not a single artist, her collaborations with innovators like Daan Roosegaarde are essential to understand.)

Why They’re Similar to van Herpen: Daan Roosegaarde, a Dutch designer and artist, has worked with van Herpen on projects like "The Dress That Glows in the Dark" (2015), which used electroluminescent wires to create bioluminescent garments. His work, like van Herpen’s, blends art, technology, and sustainability.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Smog Free Tower" (2017) – While not fashion, this air-purifying sculpture shows Roosegaarde’s commitment to environmental innovation—a value van Herpen shares.
  • "The Light Dress" (Collab with van Herpen, 2015) – A wearable LED installation that responds to movement, much like van Herpen’s interactive couture.
  • "The Solar Raincoat" (2016) – A self-charging raincoat that harnesses solar energy, proving that wearable tech can be functional.

Why You Should Follow His Work: If you’re fascinated by how technology can enhance fashion, Roosegaarde’s projects (often in collaboration with van Herpen) are essential viewing.

4. Marine Boyce – The Digital Couturier

Why She’s Similar to van Herpen: Marine Boyce, like van Herpen, uses digital tools to create physical garments. Her 2021 "Digital Couture" collection featured 3D-printed accessories and laser-cut fabrics, showing how digital design can translate into wearable art.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Digital Couture" (2021) – A collection where each piece was digitally designed before being handcrafted.
  • "The Glitch Collection" (2020) – Distorted, pixelated fabrics that mimic digital errors—a nod to van Herpen’s organic-meets-tech aesthetic.
  • Work with Google’s "Project Jacquard" – She created smart textiles that respond to touch, much like van Herpen’s interactive designs.

Why You Should Follow Her: If you love van Herpen’s precision in digital fabrication, Boyce’s work will show you how to bridge the gap between virtual and physical fashion.

5. Rick Owens – The Architect of Fashion

Why He’s Similar to van Herpen: Rick Owens, though more minimalist than van Herpen, shares her love for structural design and unconventional materials. His 2023 "The Architect" collection featured *geometric cuts, asymmetrical shapes, and a focus on sustainable production—much like van Herpen’s eco-conscious approach.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Architect" (2023) – Clothing designed like buildings, with sharp angles and monochromatic palettes.
  • "The Black Collection" (2022) – All-black, sculptural pieces that feel like wearable art installations.
  • Collaboration with Adidas (2021) – A sneaker line that mirrors his architectural aesthetic.

Why You Should Follow Him: If you love van Herpen’s play with scale and structure, Owens’ work will *expand your understanding of how fashion can be both functional and artistic.

6. Marthe Wijer – The Sustainable Futurist

Why She’s Similar to van Herpen: Marthe Wijer, like van Herpen, prioritizes sustainability without sacrificing innovation. Her 2022 "The Future is Now" collection featured *recycled materials, upcycled fabrics, and a love for biomechanical designs—proving that eco-fashion can be just as bold as high couture.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Recycled" Line (2023) – Garments made entirely from post-consumer waste.
  • "The Biomechanical" Collection (2022) – Pieces that mimic natural forms (like van Herpen’s organic distortions).
  • Work with the "Fashion for Good" Initiative – She’s a key figure in sustainable fashion innovation.

Why You Should Follow Her: If you love van Herpen’s commitment to sustainability, Wijer’s work will show you how to merge eco-consciousness with futuristic design.

  • "Body Meets Dress" (1990s) – Clothing that seems to grow out of the body, much like van Herpen’s organic distortions.

Why You Should Follow Her: If you love van Herpen’s play with form and material, Kawakubo’s work will show you the roots of her avant-garde approach.

  • "The Living Dress" (2021) – A dress that changes color based on the wearer’s heart rate.
  • "The Cybernetic Dress" (2019) – Wearable tech that reacts to movement.
  • Work with Google’s "Project Jacquard" – She’s a pioneer in smart textiles.

Why You Should Follow Her: If you love van Herpen’s integration of tech and fashion, Wipprecht’s work will show you the next frontier of wearable innovation.
