10 Visionary Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen’s Futuristic Fashion & Sculptural Design

Introduction: Why Iris van Herpen’s Work Captivates the World of Fashion & Design

Iris van Herpen is one of the most revolutionary figures in contemporary fashion, blending cutting-edge technology, biomimicry, and avant-garde sculpture into wearable art. Since her debut in 2007, she has redefined what it means to be a fashion designer, collaborating with 3D printers, robotic arms, and even NASA to create collections that feel like they belong in a sci-fi film rather than a runway.

Her work has been exhibited in The Metropolitan Museum of Art (2013), MoMA (2016), and Paris Fashion Week, where she consistently pushes boundaries. According to Business of Fashion (2022), van Herpen’s influence on sustainable innovation in fashion has grown by 42% over the past five years, with brands like Balenciaga, Chanel, and Prada citing her as a major inspiration.

If you’re drawn to her hyper-technical, organic-meets-mechanical aesthetic, you’ll love exploring these 10 artists and designers who share her vision—whether through sculptural fashion, digital art, or experimental materials. Each of these creators brings a unique perspective, proving that fashion is not just clothing, but a form of living sculpture.

Why Follow Artists Like Iris van Herpen?

Before diving into the list, let’s explore why her work—and the artists below—deserve your attention:

  1. Innovation Over Tradition – Van Herpen’s designs challenge the notion that fashion must follow rigid rules. Many of the artists here do the same, using unconventional materials, AI, and 3D printing to create groundbreaking pieces.

  2. Sustainability Meets Futurism – With fast fashion’s environmental impact being a growing concern (the fashion industry accounts for 10% of global carbon emissions, per Chatham House, 2021), these artists prove that high-tech design can be eco-conscious.

  3. Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration – Van Herpen works with scientists, engineers, and digital artists. The artists on this list often do the same, blending fashion, art, architecture, and technology into cohesive visions.

  4. Emotional & Conceptual Depth – Her work isn’t just visually stunning; it tells stories about identity, technology, and the human body. The artists here share this philosophical and emotional resonance.

  5. Global Influence – From Japanese cyberpunk designers to African futurist sculptors, these creators represent a diverse, global movement in experimental fashion.

10 Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen

1. Marine Serre – The Sustainable Futurist

Why You’ll Love Her: If van Herpen’s work inspires you with its technological precision, Marine Serre’s designs will captivate you with their organic fluidity and sustainable ethos. Known for her knitted, sculptural dresses that resemble living organisms, Serre’s 2021 collection for Chanel (where she was the first independent designer to collaborate with the house) was a runway masterpiece—each piece felt like a growing, breathing entity.

Key Traits:

  • Upcycled materials (using deadstock fabrics and recycled plastics).
  • Hand-knitted, almost "alive" silhouettes that move with the body.
  • Minimalist yet maximalist—each piece tells a story without excessive embellishment.

Real-World Example: Her 2022 "Futuristic Knitwear" collection featured a dress that looked like a melted ice sculpture, with asymmetrical draping that mimicked glacier formations. The way the fabric shifted with movement was reminiscent of van Herpen’s biomorphic forms, but with a softer, more tactile approach.

How to Engage:

  • Follow her on Instagram (@marineserre) for behind-the-scenes knitting sessions.
  • Check out her 2023 "Eco-Futurism" show at Paris Fashion Week, where she explored digital knitting techniques.

2. Anouk Wipprecht – The Cyberpunk Fashion Tech Pioneer

Why You’ll Love Her: If van Herpen’s 3D-printed, robotic elements fascinate you, Anouk Wipprecht is the next logical step—her work is pure cyberpunk, where fashion meets artificial intelligence and biotech.

Key Traits:

  • Wearable tech (she has designed AI-powered dresses that change color based on heart rate).
  • Collaborations with tech giants (Google, Samsung, and even NASA’s Jet Propulsion Lab).
  • A mix of organic and mechanical—her designs often feature exoskeleton-like structures that enhance (rather than constrain) the body.

Real-World Example: Her "Heartbeat Dress" (2018) was a real-time biometric garment that pulsed in sync with the wearer’s heartbeat, using electrochromic ink and sensors. When the heart rate increased, the dress glowed brighter, creating a visceral, almost alien experience. This is exactly the kind of interactive, tech-infused fashion that van Herpen’s 2019 "Neon DNA" collection aspired toward.

How to Engage:

  • Watch her TED Talk (2019) where she discusses fashion as a bridge between humans and machines.
  • Follow her Instagram (@anouk_wipprecht) for live demos of her AI-driven fashion experiments.

Real-World Example: Her "Evolution Dress" (2021) was a single, flowing piece that **morphed from a **tight-fitting bodysuit into a loose, draping cape, resembling **a growing organism.
