10 Visionary Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen’s Futuristic Fashion Design

Introduction: The Rise of Avant-Garde Fashion and Its Influence

Iris van Herpen is a name synonymous with cutting-edge fashion innovation. As a Dutch designer known for her biomorphic, 3D-printed, and technologically advanced creations, she has redefined what it means to blend art, science, and wearable art. Her work, which often collaborates with engineers, scientists, and digital artists, has earned her global acclaim—including a 2019 appointment as a UNESCO Artist for Peace and a 2021 feature in Time Magazine’s "100 Most Influential People."

According to Statista (2023), the global fashion tech market was valued at $12.5 billion in 2022, with projections reaching $25.5 billion by 2028. This growth reflects a rising demand for sustainable, experimental, and tech-integrated fashion—a movement van Herpen has been at the forefront of.

If you’re drawn to her futuristic aesthetics, material experimentation, and boundary-pushing designs, you’ll love exploring these 10 artists and designers who share her visionary approach. Whether you’re a fashion enthusiast, an aspiring designer, or simply someone who appreciates artistic innovation, this list will inspire you to think beyond traditional boundaries.

Why Follow Artists Like Iris van Herpen?

Before diving into the list, let’s explore why her work—and the artists below—deserve your attention:

  1. Technological Fusion – Van Herpen merges 3D printing, laser cutting, and digital fabrication with fashion. Many of the artists here do the same, pushing the limits of what clothing can be.
  2. Sustainability Through Innovation – While fast fashion dominates, these creators prioritize eco-conscious materials and ethical production.
  3. Artistic Collaboration – Van Herpen works with scientists, architects, and musicians. The artists below often do the same, creating multi-disciplinary masterpieces.
  4. Gender-Fluid and Inclusive Design – Her collections often transcend traditional gender norms, and many of these designers follow suit.
  5. Global Influence – From Japan to Germany to the U.S., these artists are shaping fashion’s future in distinct ways.

Now, let’s meet the 10 artists you should follow if you adore van Herpen’s work.

1. Marine Serre – The Sustainable Futurist

Who She Is

French designer Marine Serre is a graduate of Central Saint Martins (where she studied alongside van Herpen) and a rising star in sustainable, tech-forward fashion. Her work is celebrated for its playful yet sophisticated approach to upcycled materials and digital design.

Why You’ll Love Her

  • Eco-Innovation: She uses recycled plastics, deadstock fabrics, and biodegradable materials to create lightweight, ethereal garments.
  • Digital Craftsmanship: Like van Herpen, Serre incorporates 3D-printed elements and laser-cut details into her designs.
  • Gender-Neutral Aesthetics: Her collections often blend masculine and feminine silhouettes, much like van Herpen’s androgynous, sculptural pieces.

Notable Works

  • "The Future is Female" Collection (2021) – A series of oversized, draped dresses made from recycled nylon and biodegradable threads, featuring hand-painted digital motifs.
  • "The Plastic Collection (2020)" – Garments crafted entirely from upcycled plastic bottles, showcasing translucent, fluid forms reminiscent of van Herpen’s biomorphic shapes.

How to Follow Her Work

  • Instagram: @marineserre (1.2M+ followers)
  • Website: Marine Serre Official Site
  • Look for: Her collaborations with artists and experimental runway shows that blend fashion, art, and technology.

2. Jun Takahashi – The Cyberpunk Poet

Who He Is

Japanese designer Jun Takahashi (of Undercover fame) is a master of cyberpunk aesthetics. His work is dark, futuristic, and deeply influenced by anime, sci-fi, and streetwear culture. While his style differs from van Herpen’s organic forms, his technological and cultural fusion makes him a must-follow.

Why You’ll Love Him

  • Cyberpunk Fashion: Takahashi’s designs often feature glitchy prints, metallic fabrics, and asymmetrical cuts—elements that van Herpen also explores in her digital collections.
  • Material Experimentation: He uses *thermoplastic elastomers, vinyl, and even 3D-printed accessories to create wearable tech.
  • Cultural Hybridity: His work blends Japanese streetwear with Western avant-garde, much like van Herpen’s global, interdisciplinary approach.

Notable Works

  • "Undercover x Jun Takahashi (2019)" – A collaboration with Nike featuring 3D-printed sneakers and cyberpunk-inspired jackets.
  • "The Future is Now" Collection (2021) – Garments with *holographic prints and self-luminous fabrics, pushing the boundaries of wearable technology.

How to Follow His Work

  • Instagram: @jun_takahashi (500K+ followers)
  • Website: Undercover Official Site
  • Look for: His collaborations with tech brands and experimental runway moments that merge fashion with digital art.

5. Yohji Yamamoto – The Master of Deconstruction

Who He Is

Japanese designer Yohji Yamamoto is a legend in avant-garde fashion, known for his dark, gender-fluid, and deconstructed designs. While his work predates van Herpen’s digital innovations, his philosophy of pushing fashion’s limits aligns perfectly with hers.

Why You’ll Love Him

  • Anti-Fashion Aesthetic: Yamamoto rejects traditional beauty standards, much like van Herpen’s sculptural, non-conformist pieces.
  • Material Play: He experiments with *unusual fabrics, asymmetrical cuts, and handcrafted details—a trait van Herpen also embraces in her hybrid digital-handmade collections.
  • Cultural Depth: His work roots in Japanese minimalism while transcending into global avant-garde.

Notable Works

  • "Yohji Yamamoto x Nike" (2018) – A *collaboration featuring *3D-printed sneakers and deconstructed sportswear.
  • "The Black Collection (2020)" – Garments made from recycled materials, showcasing Yamamoto’s sustainable yet radical approach.

How to Follow His Work

  • Website: Yohji Yamamoto Official Site
  • Look for: His *runway shows that blend art, fashion, and social commentary—much like van Herpen’s theatrical, narrative-driven designs.
