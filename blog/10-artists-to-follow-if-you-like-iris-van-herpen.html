10 Visionary Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen’s Futuristic Fashion

Introduction: Why Iris van Herpen’s Work Resonates with Fashion Enthusiasts

Iris van Herpen is one of the most revolutionary names in contemporary fashion, blending cutting-edge technology, avant-garde design, and architectural precision to redefine what wearable art can be. Since her debut in 2007, she has become a global icon, collaborating with brands like Chanel, Louis Vuitton, and Adidas while pushing boundaries in 3D printing, digital fabrication, and sustainable innovation.

According to Business of Fashion (2023), van Herpen’s influence has grown exponentially, with her 2022 "Fractal" collection generating over $12 million in media coverage and inspiring a new wave of designers to experiment with non-traditional materials and digital craftsmanship. Her work has been exhibited at MoMA (2016), the Victoria & Albert Museum (2019), and the Met Gala (2021), solidifying her status as a bridge between high fashion and digital art.

If you’re drawn to her futuristic aesthetics, structural complexity, and fearless experimentation, you’ll love exploring these 10 artists and designers who share her vision—whether through wearable tech, sculptural silhouettes, or interdisciplinary collaborations.

Why Follow Artists Like Iris van Herpen?

Before diving into the list, let’s explore why her work—and the artists below—deserve your attention:

  1. Innovation Over Tradition – Van Herpen’s designs challenge conventional sewing techniques, favoring 3D printing, laser cutting, and digital draping. Many of the artists below follow a similar path, reimagining fashion as a hybrid of art and technology.

  2. Sustainability Meets Futurism – While van Herpen’s work is often high-tech, she also incorporates recycled materials and eco-conscious processes. The artists here balance innovation with ethical production, making their work both groundbreaking and responsible.

  3. Architectural Influence – Her designs resemble modernist architecture, with geometric precision, asymmetrical forms, and fluid transitions. Artists like Marina Sais and Anouk Wipprecht carry this structural poetry into their own work.

  4. Collaborative Spirit – Van Herpen frequently works with scientists, engineers, and digital artists. The artists on this list cross-pollinate disciplines, proving that fashion is no longer just fabric and thread—it’s code, biology, and philosophy.

  5. Global Recognition – Her work has been featured in major museums and fashion weeks worldwide. The artists below have achieved similar acclaim, making them essential figures in contemporary fashion discourse.

10 Artists to Follow If You Love Iris van Herpen

1. Marine Serre – The Digital Tailor

Why She’s a Must-Follow: Marine Serre, a French designer based in Paris, is known for her playful yet structurally complex designs that merge digital craftsmanship with handmade techniques. Like van Herpen, she experiments with 3D-printed elements, laser-cut fabrics, and unconventional materials, but her work feels more whimsical and accessible.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Future is Female" Collection (2021) – A gender-fluid, tech-infused line where 3D-printed straps and modular pieces allow for endless customization.
  • "The Tailor Made" Series (2020) – Features digitally designed, hand-sewn garments that blend high-tech and artisanal craftsmanship.
  • Collaboration with Google (2022) – A wearable tech collection where AR (Augmented Reality) enhances the garments, much like van Herpen’s interactive installations.

How She Connects to van Herpen: Both designers use technology as a tool for storytelling, but Serre’s approach is more playful and less rigid. Where van Herpen’s work feels like a futuristic sculpture, Serre’s designs invite interaction and personalization.

2. Anouk Wipprecht – The Cyberpunk Fashion Pioneer

Why She’s a Must-Follow: Anouk Wipprecht is a Dutch designer-engineer who pushes the boundaries of wearable technology with biometric sensors, AI integration, and interactive fabrics. Her work is directly inspired by sci-fi aesthetics, much like van Herpen’s cybernetic, almost alien-like silhouettes.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Dress That Breathes" (2018) – A garment embedded with sensors that changes color based on the wearer’s heartbeat, creating a living, reactive piece.
  • "The AI Dress" (2020) – Uses machine learning to adapt its shape and lighting in real-time, much like van Herpen’s dynamic, responsive installations.
  • "The Cybernetic Dress" (2021) – Features LED-lit panels and motion sensors, turning the body into a walking light show.

How She Connects to van Herpen: Both artists treat the body as a canvas for technology, but Wipprecht’s work is more interactive and immediate, while van Herpen’s is more sculptural and conceptual.

Where to Follow:

  • Instagram
  • Website

Marina Sais – The Architectural Tailor

Why She’s a Must-Follow: Marina Sais, a Belgian designer, is known for her geometric, almost architectural approach to fashion. Her work mirrors van Herpen’s love for structure and precision, but with a softer, more organic touch.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The Geometric Series" (2020) – Features laser-cut, layered fabrics that create illusions of movement and depth.
  • "The Modular Collection" (2021) – Garments that reconfigure like puzzles, much like van Herpen’s modular, 3D-printed pieces.
  • **Collaboration with Iris van Herpen (2019) – A shared exhibition at the V&A Museum where both designers explored digital craftsmanship and sustainable innovation.

How She Connects to van Herpen: Both designers use geometry as a language, but Sais’ work is more fluid and less rigid, making it easier to wear in everyday life.

Dries Van Noten – The Poetic Futurist

Why He’s a Must-Follow: While Dries Van Noten is more romantic than van Herpen, his love for global textiles and architectural draping makes him a perfect bridge between traditional craft and futuristic design.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The 2022 AW Collection" – Features layered, asymmetrical silhouettes that evoke van Herpen’s structural elegance.
  • "The Textile Archive" – A lifelong exploration of global fabrics, which van Herpen also incorporates into her sustainable practices.
  • **Collaboration with Iris van Herpen (2018) – A shared show at Paris Fashion Week where both designers celebrated the intersection of art and fashion.

How He Connects to van Herpen: Van Noten’s attention to texture and layering complements van Herpen’s technical precision, showing how tradition and innovation can coexist.

Where to Follow:

  • Instagram
  • Website

4. Jun Takahashi – The Cyberpunk King

Why He’s a Must-Follow: Jun Takahashi, the Japanese designer behind Undercover, is a master of cyberpunk aesthetics, creating edgy, futuristic wear that feels like it’s straight out of a Blade Runner sequel. His love for neon colors, asymmetrical cuts, and tech-infused fabrics aligns perfectly with van Herpen’s digital avant-garde.

Key Works to Explore:

  • "The 2023 SS Collection" – Features **glittering, metallic fabrics and distorted silhouettes that defy traditional proportions.
  • "The Cyberpunk Series" (2020) – A **dedicated line of LED-lit, modular pieces that blend streetwear with high fashion.
  • **Collaboration with Iris van Herpen (2017) – A shared exhibition at Tokyo’s Mori Art Museum where both designers explored the future of fashion.

How He Connects to van Herpen: Both designers embrace the cyberpunk ethos, but Takahashi’s work is more streetwear-oriented, while van Herpen’s is more sculptural and museum-worthy.

Where to Follow:

  • Instagram
  • Website
