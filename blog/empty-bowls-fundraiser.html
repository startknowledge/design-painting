Empty Bowls Fundraiser: A Creative & Impactful Way to Raise Awareness & Funds for Your Community

Introduction: The Power of Empty Bowls in Community Building & Philanthropy

In an era where digital engagement often overshadows hands-on community involvement, the Empty Bowls Fundraiser stands out as a unique, heartfelt, and highly effective way to bring people together while supporting a meaningful cause. Originating in 1990 in Iowa, this tradition has since spread across the United States and beyond, raising millions of dollars for local food banks, homeless shelters, and hunger relief programs.

According to Feeding America, over 38 million Americans experienced food insecurity in 2023, with 1 in 8 households struggling to afford enough nutritious food. The Empty Bowls Fundraiser has become a powerful response to this crisis, combining art, pottery, and community service into a single, memorable event.

If you're an artist, designer, educator, or community organizer looking to host your own Empty Bowls Fundraiser, this guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from planning and execution to marketing and maximizing impact. By the end, you’ll have a step-by-step blueprint to create an event that not only raises funds but also inspires, educates, and unites your community.

What Is an Empty Bowls Fundraiser?

Before diving into strategies, let’s clarify what makes an Empty Bowls Fundraiser special.

The Core Concept

An Empty Bowls Fundraiser is a pottery-based fundraiser where participants:

  1. Create ceramic bowls (either by hand or through pre-made designs).
  2. Sell the bowls at an auction or silent auction, with proceeds going to a local charity.
  3. Serve soup or stew in the bowls during the event, symbolizing community, sharing, and the fight against hunger.

The "empty bowl" represents the lack of food that many struggle with, while the act of filling it with soup symbolizes hope, generosity, and collective action.

Why It Works So Well

  • Artistic Appeal – The bowls become collectible pieces, making them desirable for donors.
  • Emotional Connection – Attendees feel a personal stake in the cause.
  • Scalability – Events can range from small studio gatherings to large community festivals.
  • Memorable Experience – Unlike traditional fundraisers, this one is interactive, visual, and immersive.

10 Actionable Strategies to Host a Successful Empty Bowls Fundraiser

Hosting an Empty Bowls Fundraiser requires careful planning, creativity, and community engagement. Below are 10 proven strategies to ensure your event is well-attended, well-funded, and impactful.

1. Define Your Mission & Choose a Charity Partner

Before anything else, decide who will benefit from your fundraiser.

How to Choose the Right Charity:

  • Local Focus: Partner with a food bank, homeless shelter, or community kitchen in your area.
  • Alignment with Values: Ensure the charity’s mission resonates with your community (e.g., hunger relief, youth programs, disaster relief).
  • Transparency: Clearly communicate how much of the proceeds will go directly to the charity (aim for 80-90% to build trust).

Example: The Portland Art Museum’s Empty Bowls Fundraiser (2022) partnered with Oregon Food Bank, raising over $150,000 for families in need. They ensured that 95% of donations went directly to meal programs.

Pro Tip:

  • Reach out early—charities may have specific funding needs (e.g., school meal programs, winter food drives).
  • Offer a "matching donation" from a local business to double the impact.

2. Plan the Bowls: Handmade vs. Pre-Made

The bowls are the heart of your fundraiser, so decide whether to:

  • Handmade Bowls (more labor-intensive but deeply personal)
  • Pre-Made Bowls (faster, allows more participants, still artistic)

Option A: Handmade Bowls (Best for Artists & Workshops)

  • Who Should Do This? Potters, ceramics classes, or art schools.
  • How to Organize:
    • Offer beginner-friendly workshops (e.g., "Paint Your Own Bowl" sessions).
    • Provide pre-fired clay and basic tools to keep costs low.
    • Charge a material fee (e.g., $20-$40) that covers clay, glazes, and firing.
  • Example: The Ceramic Arts Center of Denver hosted an Empty Bowls event where participants paid $30 for a custom-designed bowl, with all proceeds going to Denver Rescue Mission. The center provided glazes and firing, and attendees took home a one-of-a-kind piece while supporting a local cause.

Option B: Pre-Made Bowls (Best for Large Groups & Time Constraints)

  • Where to Source:
    • Local potters (ask for bulk discounts).
    • Etsy or craft suppliers (search for "blank ceramic bowls for fundraisers").
    • Thrift stores (for a rustic, upcycled look).
  • Customization Ideas:
    • Let participants paint or decorate pre-made bowls at the event.
    • Offer themed designs (e.g., "Community Strong," "Hope in Clay").

Pro Tip:

  • If going pre-made, ensure bowls are uniform in size for a professional look during auctions.
  • Include a small business card inside each bowl with the charity’s info.

3. Set a Clear Fundraising Goal & Pricing Strategy

Without a financial target, your fundraiser may fall short. Use these steps to maximize donations:

Step 1: Research & Benchmark

  • Look at past Empty Bowls events in your area (check local Facebook groups or charity websites).
  • Example: A $50 bowl at a well-marketed event might sell for $100+ at auction.

Step 2: Determine Pricing

Bowl Type Suggested Starting Price Auction Reserve Price
Handmade (basic) $30-$50 $75-$120
Handmade (custom) $50-$80 $120-$200
Pre-made (painted) $25-$40 $60-$100
Signature Artist Bowl $100+ $200+

Step 3: Offer Tiers for Donors

  • "Sponsor a Bowl" – Businesses can sponsor a bowl (e.g., "This bowl is brought to you by XYZ Coffee Shop").
  • "Name a Bowl" – Let donors inscribe their name on a bowl for a small fee.
  • "Silent Auction Add-Ons" – Include extras like:
    • A local artist’s sketch.
    • A gift basket from a sponsor.
    • A personalized note from the charity’s founder.

Example: The San Francisco Empty Bowls event (2023) set a $250,000 goal and exceeded it by $30,000. They priced bowls at $75-$150 and offered corporate sponsorships where companies could bid on bowls as part of their charity matching program.

4. Create a Memorable Event Experience

An Empty Bowls Fundraiser isn’t just about selling bowls—it’s about creating an experience that people want to share.

Key Elements to Include: ✅ Soup & Bread Bar – Serve local, seasonal soups (e.g., lentil, tomato basil, or a vegan option). ✅ Live Music or Storytelling – A local musician, poet, or charity speaker adds depth. ✅ Photo Booth with Bowls – Encourage attendees to pose with their bowls for social media. ✅ Child-Friendly Zone – Kids can decorate mini bowls (sold at a lower price). ✅ Charity Talks – Have a representative from the nonprofit share real stories of how funds will be used.

Example: The Empty Bowls at the Denver Art Museum (2021) featured:

  • A live jazz band.
  • A storytelling segment where a homeless advocate shared personal experiences.
  • Free soup and sourdough bread served in the bowls.
  • A "Bowl of Hope" auction, where the highest bidder got to name a bowl in honor of a loved one.

Pro Tip:

  • Record a short video of the event and share it on social media afterward to extend engagement.

5. Leverage Local Artists & Sponsors

Partnering with artists and businesses can dramatically increase your fundraiser’s reach and revenue.

How to Recruit Artists:

  • Local Potters & Ceramists – Offer them exposure in exchange for donated or discounted bowls.
  • Art Students – University ceramics programs often love community engagement.
  • Calligraphy & Painting Add-Ons – Some artists can inscribe bowls with poetry or charity slogans.

How to Attract Sponsors:

  • Offer Sponsorship Tiers (e.g., $500 for logo placement, $1,000 for a booth).
  • Highlight Their Impact – Example: "By sponsoring this event, XYZ Bakery will help feed 500 families this winter."
  • Provide Marketing Support – Include their logo in event flyers, social media, and auction catalogs.

Example: The Austin Empty Bowls event (2022) secured $40,000 in sponsorships from:

  • Local breweries (who donated free beer for silent auction prizes).
  • Art supply stores (who offered discounts on glazes and tools).
  • A major hotel chain (who provided venue space for free in exchange for branding).

6. Master the Auction Strategy (Silent vs. Live)

The auction format can make or break your fundraiser. Here’s how to maximize bids:

Option A: Silent Auction (Best for Large Groups)

  • How It Works: Bowls are displayed with starting bids, and attendees write down their offers on a sheet.
  • Pros:
    • Less pressure for bidders.
    • Easier to organize with multiple items.
  • Cons:
    • Harder to create urgency.

Option B: Live Auction (Best for High-Value Items)

  • How It Works: A host (emcee) auctions bowls one by one, driving up bids.
  • Pros:
    • More excitement and higher final prices.
    • Great for storytelling (e.g., "This bowl was made by a local teen in our youth program—bid now!").
  • Cons:
    • Requires a skilled auctioneer.

Hybrid Approach (Best of Both Worlds):

  • Start with a silent auction for most bowls.
  • Feature 2-3 "signature bowls" (e.g., a master potter’s piece) in a live auction.

Example: The Seattle Empty Bowls event (2023) used a hybrid model and raised $220,000. They:

  • Sold 50 pre-made bowls at $50-$80 via silent auction.
  • Featured 5 live-auction bowls (each starting at $150, selling for $400-$800).

Pro Tip:

  • Use a bidding app (like AuctionZip or LiveAuctioneer) to track bids digitally and eliminate errors.
  • Train a volunteer to read bids aloud during the live portion.

7. Market Your Event Like a Pro

Even the best fundraiser fails if no one knows about it. Use these marketing strategies to fill your space.

A. Social Media Hype (3-6 Months Before)

  • Teaser Posts: "Something special is coming… stay tuned!" (with a countdown).
  • Artist Spotlights: Feature participating potters with behind-the-scenes content.
  • Countdown Stories: Use Instagram/Facebook Stories to build anticipation.
  • Hashtag Strategy: Create a unique hashtag (e.g., #YourCityEmptyBowls).

B. Email & Local Press

  • Partner with Local Blogs & News Outlets – Pitch a story like:

    "How [Your City]’s Empty Bowls Fundraiser is Fighting Hunger One Bowl at a Time"

  • Send Press Releases to food banks, art magazines, and community newspapers.
  • Email List Building – Collect emails at local events and send monthly updates.

C. Community Engagement

  • Pop-Up Workshops – Host free "Paint Your Own Bowl" sessions at libraries, coffee shops, and farmers' markets.
  • School Involvement – Partner with art classes to decorate bowls as a class project.
  • Corporate Team-Building – Offer exclusive corporate packages (e.g., "Bring your team to decorate bowls and support hunger relief").

Example: The Chicago Empty Bowls event (2022) used a multi-channel approach and sold out in two weeks thanks to:

  • A viral TikTok video of a local potter’s process.
  • A feature in the Chicago Tribune about the event’s impact.
  • Partnerships with 10+ local businesses who shared flyers in their newsletters.

8. Handle Logistics Like a Boss

A smooth event depends on detailed planning. Here’s a checklist to avoid chaos:

Venue & Permits

  • Book early (popular venues fill up fast).
  • Check for permits (some cities require food service licenses).
  • Ensure accessibility (ramps, wheelchair-friendly tables).

Day-of Event Flow

Time Task
3 Hours Before Set up auction tables, soup station, and bowl displays.
2 Hours Before Test sound equipment (if live auction).
1 Hour Before Greeters welcome attendees, soup is prepped, bidding sheets are ready.
During Event Assign roles (auctioneer, soup servers, volunteers).
After Event Count donations, thank sponsors, send follow-up emails.

Donation & Payment Systems

  • Square or PayPal Reader – For in-person donations.
  • GoFundMe or Classy – For online donations (if pre-selling bowls).
  • Receipts & Tax Deductions – Provide donation receipts for tax purposes.
