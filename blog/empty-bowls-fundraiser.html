How to Host a Successful Empty Bowls Fundraiser: A Step-by-Step Guide for Artists & Designers

Introduction: The Power of Empty Bowls Fundraisers in 2024

In recent years, community-driven fundraisers have surged in popularity, with Empty Bowls ceremonies becoming one of the most impactful ways to raise awareness and funds for local causes. According to a 2023 report by Nonprofit Times, community art fundraisers like Empty Bowls generate an average of $5,000–$20,000 per event, with some high-profile gatherings exceeding $100,000. These events not only support charitable missions but also foster creativity, collaboration, and a sense of shared purpose among participants.

For artists, designers, and creative professionals, hosting an Empty Bowls fundraiser is a fantastic opportunity to showcase your skills, engage your community, and make a meaningful impact. Whether you're a painter, ceramicist, or digital designer, this guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from planning and promotion to execution and beyond.

What Is an Empty Bowls Fundraiser?

An Empty Bowls fundraiser is a community art event where local artists create handcrafted bowls (ceramic, painted, or even digital designs) that are sold at auction or for a set price. The proceeds go toward a charity of choice, often related to hunger relief, education, or community support.

The event itself is a simple yet powerful ceremony where participants gather around a table, each with an empty bowl. A storyteller or organizer shares the mission of the charity, and then, one by one, each person fills their bowl with a donated item (food, art supplies, books, etc.). The act symbolizes giving back to the community and reinforces the event’s purpose.

Why Host an Empty Bowls Fundraiser?

✅ Supports a Cause – Directly funds local nonprofits. ✅ Showcases Your Art – Perfect for artists to gain exposure. ✅ Builds Community – Brings people together for a shared goal. ✅ Encourages Creativity – Challenges artists to innovate within a theme. ✅ Tax-Deductible Donations – Many charities provide receipts for contributors.

Step-by-Step Guide to Hosting a Successful Empty Bowls Fundraiser

1. Choose a Charity & Define Your Mission

Before anything else, select a charity that aligns with your values and community needs. Some popular options include:

  • Local food banks (e.g., Feeding America affiliates)
  • Children’s hospitals or shelters
  • Educational programs (e.g., after-school arts initiatives)
  • Environmental conservation groups

Pro Tip: Reach out to the charity early to discuss their needs, preferred donation structure, and how proceeds will be used. Some organizations may even provide marketing materials or testimonials to help promote your event.

Example: In 2022, a group of ceramic artists in Portland, Oregon, partnered with Oregon Food Bank and raised $12,000 by selling handmade bowls and hosting a silent auction.

2. Set a Budget & Secure Sponsors

Fundraisers don’t have to be expensive, but planning ahead ensures smooth execution. Consider these cost factors:

  • Materials (clay, paints, glazes, packaging)
  • Venue rental (community centers, galleries, or even outdoor spaces)
  • Marketing (flyers, social media ads, email campaigns)
  • Staffing (volunteers, emcees, auctioneers)

Actionable Strategy:

  • Apply for grants – Many arts councils offer funding for community events.
  • Partner with local businesses – Bakeries, cafes, or art supply stores may donate food, drinks, or materials in exchange for promotion.
  • Host a pre-event fundraiser – Sell tickets or take donations to cover initial costs.

Example: A group of graphic designers in Austin, Texas, secured $3,000 in sponsorships from a local print shop and a coffee roaster in exchange for featuring their logos at the event.

3. Plan the Artwork: The Heart of Your Fundraiser

The bowls themselves are the centerpiece of your event. Here’s how to approach them:

A. Decide on the Medium

  • Ceramic Bowls – Traditional and timeless (best for experienced potters).
  • Painted Wooden Bowls – A great option for painters and mixed-media artists.
  • Digital Art Bowls – If you’re a digital designer, create printable designs that attendees can frame or use as place settings.
  • Recycled Materials – Upcycle glass jars, tin cans, or found objects for an eco-friendly twist.

B. Set a Theme (Optional but Engaging)

A theme can unify your artwork and attract more participants. Some ideas:

  • "Community Roots" – Bowls featuring local landmarks or family histories.
  • "Hope & Healing" – Abstract designs with uplifting messages.
  • "Cultural Exchange" – Artists from different backgrounds create bowls inspired by their heritage.

Example: In 2021, an Empty Bowls event in Chicago had a "Chicago Skyline" theme, where each artist incorporated the city’s architecture into their bowl designs. The event sold out within two weeks.

4. Organize a Team & Assign Roles

No single person can run an Empty Bowls fundraiser alone. Delegate tasks to keep everything running smoothly:

Role Responsibilities
Event Coordinator Oversees planning, budget, and timeline.
Artistic Director Ensures artwork meets quality standards.
Marketing Lead Handles promotions, social media, and press.
Auctioneer Runs the silent or live auction.
Volunteers Set up, greet guests, and assist during the event.

Pro Tip: Use a shared calendar (Google Calendar or Trello) to track deadlines and keep everyone on the same page.

5. Promote Your Fundraiser Effectively

Marketing is key to filling seats and bowls. Here’s how to maximize reach:

A. Leverage Social Media

  • Instagram & Facebook – Post behind-the-scenes content (artists at work, bowl progress).
  • TikTok & Reels – Share quick videos of the creative process or charity impact.
  • Hashtags – Use #EmptyBowls, #ArtForACause, #CommunityFundraiser.

B. Partner with Local Influencers

  • Invite local artists, bloggers, or food critics to attend and share their experience.
  • Offer free bowls or VIP tickets in exchange for promotion.

C. Email & Direct Outreach

  • Local art schools, galleries, and studios – Ask if they’ll promote the event.
  • Community boards – Post flyers at libraries, coffee shops, and community centers.

Example: A fundraiser in Seattle used Instagram Stories to showcase each artist’s process, leading to a 30% increase in ticket sales compared to previous years.

6. Sell Tickets & Pre-Book Bowls

Decide on ticket pricing and bowl sales structure:

  • Pre-sale tickets (for the ceremony) – $20–$50 per person.
  • Bowl auctions – Start bids at $20–$50 (adjust based on artist skill level).
  • Donation-based entry – Some events allow attendees to pay what they can.

Actionable Strategy:

  • Offer early-bird discounts to incentivize early sign-ups.
  • Sell "Artist Packages" – Include a bowl + a handwritten note from the creator.

Example: In 2023, a fundraiser in Denver used a waitlist system for tickets, which created exclusivity and higher demand.

7. Plan the Event Day Logistics

On the day of the event, smooth operations ensure a memorable experience.

A. Venue Setup

  • Table arrangement – Place bowls in auction baskets with numbered tags.
  • Seating – Ensure there’s enough space for guests to move around.
  • Signage – Label donation jars, auction rules, and charity info.

B. The Ceremony Flow

  1. Welcome & Introduction (10 min) – Thank sponsors, introduce the charity.
  2. Artist Spotlight (15 min) – Let each artist briefly share their inspiration.
  3. Bowl Auction (30–45 min) – Silent or live auction (see tips below).
  4. Empty Bowls Ceremony (20 min) – Guests fill their bowls with donations.
  5. Closing Remarks & Thank You (10 min) – Announce total funds raised.

Pro Tip: Have a backup plan for weather if outdoors—set up a tent or indoor space.

8. Run a Successful Auction

Auctions can maximize profits, but they require strategy.

A. Silent vs. Live Auction

  • Silent Auction – Bidders write bids on cards (easier for large groups).
  • Live Auction – An auctioneer hypes up items (great for high-value bowls).

B. Pricing Strategy

  • Start low, end high – Example: A bowl priced at $30–$80 (bid increments of $5).
  • Bundle items – Sell a "Family Pack" (3 bowls + a charity donation).

Example: A live auction in Boston saw $15,000 in bids when the auctioneer told a personal story about how the charity helped a local family.

9. Post-Event Follow-Up & Impact Reporting

After the event, keep the momentum going:

  • Send thank-you emails to attendees, artists, and sponsors.
  • Share results – Post a video recap or blog with the total funds raised.
  • Engage with donors – Some charities allow quarterly updates on how funds were used.

Pro Tip: Create a donor recognition page on your website to highlight contributions.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

1. Underestimating Costs

Mistake: Not accounting for unexpected expenses (last-minute material shortages, venue fees). Solution: Budget 20% extra for contingencies.

2. Poor Promotion

Mistake: Relying only on social media without local outreach. Solution: Combine digital and offline marketing (flyers, word-of-mouth).

3. Overcomplicating the Auction

Mistake: Making the auction too confusing with unclear rules. Solution: Keep it simple—use numbered tags and clear bid increments.

4. Ignoring Artist Feedback

Mistake: Not communicating expectations to artists (e.g., deadlines, themes). Solution: Hold a kickoff meeting to align on goals.

5. Forgetting Accessibility

Mistake: Hosting in a hard-to-reach location (no parking, stairs, etc.). Solution: Choose a central, accessible venue.

FAQs About Empty Bowls Fundraisers

1. How much does it cost to host an Empty Bowls fundraiser?

The cost varies based on size, but small local events typically range from $500–$2,000. Larger events (50+ attendees) may cost $5,000+. Budget for materials, venue, marketing, and staffing.

2. Can I host an Empty Bowls fundraiser digitally?

Yes! You can:

  • Sell digital bowl designs as printable art.
  • Host a virtual auction via platforms like LiveAuctioneers.
  • Use Zoom or Instagram Live for the ceremony.

3. How do I choose the right charity?

Ask yourself:

  • Does the charity align with my community’s needs?
  • Is it transparent about fund usage?
  • Can they provide testimonials or marketing support?

4. What if no one buys my bowls?

  • Offer pre-sales to secure some sales before the event.
  • Partner with a gallery to display bowls for a week beforehand.
  • Run a "Name Your Price" sale for the last few bowls.

5. How can I make my bowls stand out?

  • Tell a story – Each bowl can have a short artist note.
  • Use unique materials – Incorporate recycled glass, wood, or mixed media.
  • Offer limited editions – Example: "First 20 bowls signed by the artist."

Conclusion: Turn Your Vision into Impact

Hosting an Empty Bowls fundraiser is more than just an art event—it’s a powerful way to give back while celebrating creativity. Whether you're a painter, ceramicist, or digital designer, this guide has given you actionable steps to plan, promote, and execute a successful fundraiser that benefits your community.

Your Next Steps:

✅ Pick a charity and secure partnerships. ✅ Gather your team and assign roles. ✅ Start creating bowls (or digital designs) today! ✅ Promote early and often—social media, local press, and word-of-mouth. ✅ Host with passion—make it an experience people remember.

Ready to make a difference? Start planning now—your community (and your art) will thank you.

  • Portland’s Empty Bowls for Hunger Relief (2023)
  • Seattle’s Art for a Cause (2022)

Join the movement—your bowls could change lives. 🍲✨
