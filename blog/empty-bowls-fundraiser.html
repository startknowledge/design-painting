How to Organize a Successful Empty Bowls Fundraiser: A Step-by-Step Guide for Artists & Nonprofits

Introduction: The Power of Empty Bowls in Community & Fundraising

Empty Bowls is more than just a fundraiser—it’s a movement. Since its inception in 1990 by artist David Coursey at the University of Wisconsin–Stout, this unique event has raised millions for local charities while fostering creativity, community engagement, and artistic expression. In recent years, Empty Bowls fundraisers have grown exponentially, with over 1,000 events held annually across the U.S. and beyond (Empty Bowls Foundation, 2023). In 2022 alone, these events collectively raised $3.2 million for hunger relief programs (Feeding America, 2023).

For artists, designers, and nonprofit organizers, Empty Bowls offers a perfect blend of creativity and philanthropy. Unlike traditional galas or bake sales, this event encourages local artisans to contribute handmade ceramic bowls, which are then sold to participants who enjoy a meal (often donated by local restaurants) and take home a bowl to support the cause.

If you're part of a design studio, pottery collective, or nonprofit, hosting an Empty Bowls fundraiser can be a highly rewarding way to: ✔ Showcase local talent (artists, potters, designers) ✔ Raise funds for a meaningful cause (hunger relief, homelessness, education) ✔ Build community connections (restaurants, schools, businesses) ✔ Create a memorable, interactive experience for attendees

This guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from planning and promotion to execution and beyond—so your Empty Bowls event is a smashing success.

Why Empty Bowls Works: The Science Behind Its Success

Before diving into logistics, let’s explore why Empty Bowls resonates with both donors and participants:

1. Emotional Connection Through Art

Studies show that art triggers emotional responses, making people more likely to donate (Journal of Consumer Psychology, 2018). When attendees purchase a bowl, they’re not just buying a meal—they’re supporting a cause while owning a piece of local art.

2. Low Overhead, High Impact

Unlike large-scale fundraisers, Empty Bowls has minimal costs (just materials, venue, and food). The majority of proceeds go directly to the charity, making it an efficient fundraising model.

3. Community-Driven & Inclusive

Empty Bowls celebrates local artists, ensuring that every region has a unique, handcrafted bowl. This regional pride increases participation and word-of-mouth marketing.

4. Multi-Sensory Experience

Unlike passive fundraisers (e.g., online donations), Empty Bowls engages sight, touch, and taste, making it a memorable event that people want to share.

5. Scalable & Adaptable

Whether you're organizing a small studio event or a city-wide gala, Empty Bowls can be customized to fit any budget or space.

Step-by-Step Guide: How to Organize a Flawless Empty Bowls Fundraiser

Phase 1: Planning & Logistics (3-6 Months Before the Event)

1. Define Your Mission & Choose a Charity

Before anything else, decide who you’re fundraising for. Some common causes include:

  • Hunger relief (food banks, soup kitchens)
  • Homelessness & shelter support
  • Youth education & after-school programs
  • Local arts & cultural initiatives

Example: The Empty Bowls event at the Portland Art Museum (2022) raised $150,000 for Oregon Food Bank, serving over 10,000 meals to families in need. The museum partnered with 12 local potters, each creating a unique bowl design inspired by Oregon’s landscape.

Pro Tip:

  • Reach out to 2-3 charities and ask which one aligns best with your community’s needs.
  • Secure a partnership—many nonprofits provide donated meals, volunteers, or promotional support.

2. Assemble Your Team

A successful Empty Bowls event requires collaboration. Your team should include:

  • Event Coordinator (oversees logistics)
  • Artistic Director (works with potters, ensures bowl quality)
  • Marketing Lead (handles promotions, social media)
  • Volunteer Manager (recruits and trains helpers)
  • Food & Venue Liaison (secures meals and location)

Example: *The Chicago Empty Bowls Festival (2023) had a 15-person core team, including:

  • A ceramic artist who curated bowl submissions
  • A restaurant owner who donated meals for 500 attendees
  • A nonprofit director from Chicago Cares (the charity partner)
  • A graphic designer from a local studio who created all promotional materials*

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Assuming one person can do everything → Leads to burnout. ✅ Delegate tasks early and hold weekly check-ins.

Phase 2: Securing Art & Logistics (4-5 Months Before)

3. Recruit & Curate Artists

The heart of Empty Bowls is the bowls themselves. You need high-quality, unique pieces that reflect your community.

How to Find Artists:

  • Local pottery studios (offer them a commission fee or percentage of sales)
  • Art schools & universities (students often need portfolio pieces)
  • Call for submissions via social media, local art groups, or community boards

Example: The Denver Empty Bowls event (2021) received 87 submissions from potters across Colorado. The organizers selected 20 artists, ensuring a mix of beginner and master potters to keep costs low while maintaining quality.

Tips for Artist Selection: ✔ Set clear guidelines (e.g., "Bowls must hold at least 1.5 cups of soup"). ✔ Offer a small stipend (e.g., $20–$50 per bowl) to attract serious artists. ✔ Ask for a sample photo before finalizing selections.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Accepting low-quality bowls → Hurts the event’s reputation. ✅ **Provide a quality checklist (e.g., "No cracks, even glaze, functional shape").

4. Secure a Venue & Permits

You’ll need a space that can accommodate:

  • Art displays (tables, shelves, or a gallery-style setup)
  • Food service (if meals are included)
  • Seating for 50–200+ attendees (depending on scale)

Venue Ideas:

  • Community centers
  • Art galleries & museums
  • Restaurants with event spaces
  • Public parks (with permits)

Example: *The San Francisco Empty Bowls event (2022) took place at The City Arts & Recreation Center, which provided:

  • Free venue rental (in exchange for charity support)
  • Electricity & water access for artists
  • A stage for live music

Permits to Check:

  • Food service license (if meals are served)
  • Public gathering permit (for outdoor events)
  • Alcohol permit (if serving drinks)

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Assuming any space will work → Last-minute venue issues cause stress. ✅ Visit the space early and confirm power, restrooms, and parking.

Phase 3: Promotion & Marketing (2-3 Months Before)

5. Build a Strong Brand & Messaging

Your Empty Bowls event needs a clear, compelling story. Example messaging:

"This year, Empty Bowls: Feeding Hearts & Hands brings together local artists and hungry neighbors to create a meal—and a movement. Every bowl you purchase feeds a family in need. Will you help us fill the bowls… and the bellies?"

Branding Elements:

  • Event name (e.g., "Empty Bowls: A Ceramic Celebration for [Charity]")
  • Tagline (e.g., "One Bowl, One Meal, One Community")
  • Color scheme & fonts (match your charity’s branding)

Example: The Boston Empty Bowls event (2023) used a deep blue and gold theme, mirroring the Harvard University colors (where it was hosted). Their slogan was:

*"Breakfast for Thought. Lunch for Life."*

6. Leverage Social Media & Local Partnerships

Platforms to Use:

  • Instagram & Facebook (post artist spotlights, behind-the-scenes content)
  • Nextdoor & local Facebook groups (hyper-local reach)
  • Pinterest (for artists to share their bowl designs)
  • Email newsletters (partner with local businesses)

Post Ideas: ✅ "Meet the Artists" series (interviews with potters) ✅ "Bowl of the Week" (highlight a unique design) ✅ "Countdown to Empty Bowls" (teasers, behind-the-scenes) ✅ "Why We Fundraise" (share stories from the charity)

Example: *The Austin Empty Bowls event (2022) partnered with local food bloggers to promote the event. They hosted a "Soup & Stories" live stream where attendees could vote on the best bowl design before the event.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Overpromising without details → Leads to confusion. ✅ Always include:

  • Date, time, location
  • Charity name & mission
  • How to buy tickets/RSVP

Phase 4: Final Preparations (1 Month Before)

7. Finalize Logistics & Volunteer Training

Key Tasks:

  • Confirm artist deliveries (ensure bowls arrive on time)
  • Set up payment processing (Square, PayPal, or Venmo for ticket sales)
  • Train volunteers (roles: check-in, food service, cleanup)

Example: *The Seattle Empty Bowls event (2021) had a volunteer training session where helpers practiced:

  • Efficiently tagging bowls with artist names
  • Directing guests to food stations
  • Handling cash & donations

Volunteer Perks:

  • Free bowl & meal (motivates participation)
  • Event T-shirt or certificate (for recognition)

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Underestimating volunteer needs → Leads to chaos. ✅ **Plan for a 1:10 volunteer-to-attendee ratio (e.g., 10 helpers for 100 people).

8. Plan the Event Flow

A well-structured timeline keeps the event smooth and enjoyable. Example schedule:

Time Activity
6:00 PM Doors open, guests arrive
6:30 PM Welcome speech & charity overview
7:00 PM Soup & bread service (donated meals)
7:30 PM Bowl auction (optional)
8:00 PM Live music/performance
8:30 PM Closing remarks & donation drive
9:00 PM Cleanup & artist thank-you

Example: *The Portland Empty Bowls event (2023) included:

  • A live band playing folk music
  • A "Bowl of the Year" contest (voted by attendees)
  • A donation station where guests could round up their purchase for extra impact

Phase 5: The Day Of & Post-Event

9. Execute with Grace

On-Site Checklist: ✔ Artist check-in (verify bowls are ready) ✔ Food stations stocked (label allergens) ✔ Payment processing (have backups like cash) ✔ Photographer/volunteer to capture moments

Example: *The New York Empty Bowls event (2022) had a dedicated "photo booth" where guests could take pictures with their bowls, which were then shared on social media with a hashtag (#NYEmptyBowls2022).*

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Underestimating cleanup time → Leads to rushed exits. ✅ Hire extra help or provide trash bags & recycling bins upfront.

10. Post-Event Follow-Up

What to Do After:

  • Thank you emails to attendees, artists, and donors
  • Share impact stats (e.g., "We raised $25,000 for [Charity]!")
  • Highlight artist success (feature their bowls in a gallery or online)
  • Plan next year’s event (gather feedback for improvements)

Example: *The Chicago Empty Bowls team (2023) sent a personalized thank-you note to each bowl buyer, including:

  • A photo of their bowl
  • A receipt for their donation
  • A link to the charity’s impact report

10 Actionable Strategies to Make Your Empty Bowls Event Unforgettable

1. Turn It Into a "Bowl-Off" Competition

Instead of just selling bowls, make it a contest! Examples:

  • "Best Functional Bowl" (judged by a panel)
  • "Most Creative Glaze" (voted by attendees)
  • "Bowl That Raises the Most Money" (track sales per artist)

Example: The Denver event (2021) had a "Bowl Olympics" where artists demonstrated throwing techniques while live music played. The winner got a feature in a local art magazine.

2. Partner with Local Restaurants for Donated Meals

Food is a major cost, but many restaurants love supporting local causes. Approach:

  • Soup kitchens (they often have leftover ingredients)
  • Cafés & bakeries (offer bread, pastries, or sandwiches)
  • Ethnic restaurants (provide authentic dishes like ramen, curry, or tacos)

Example: *The Seattle Empty Bowls event (2022) partnered with Piroshky Piroshky (a local bakery) and Tacoma’s La Taqueria, providing free meals for all attendees.

3. Offer a "Silent Auction" for Premium Bowls

Some artists create limited-edition, high-end bowls that can be auctioned off. Examples:

  • "Artist’s Choice" bowl (signed by the creator)
  • "Charity’s Favorite" bowl (voted by the nonprofit)
  • "Mystery Bowl" (a surprise design from a famous potter)

Example: The Austin Empty Bowls event (2023) had a $500 bowl by a Texas-based ceramicist, which sold for $1,200 at auction.
