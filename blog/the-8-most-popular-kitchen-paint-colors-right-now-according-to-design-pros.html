The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros (2024 Trends & Expert Tips)

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Making a Bold Comeback in 2024

Your kitchen is more than just a place to cook—it’s the heart of your home, a space where memories are made, meals are shared, and design trends evolve. Over the past few years, kitchen paint colors have shifted dramatically, moving away from the overly neutral palettes of the early 2010s and embracing bold, mood-enhancing hues that reflect modern lifestyles.

According to a 2023 survey by the National Kitchen & Bath Association (NKBA), 78% of homeowners prioritize color and paint choices when renovating their kitchens, with 62% stating that color significantly impacts their home’s resale value. Meanwhile, Color Trends 2024 by Sherwin-Williams highlights that earthy tones, soft neutrals, and deep jewel colors are dominating kitchen design, driven by a desire for warmth, tranquility, and personal expression.

But which colors are truly leading the charge in 2024? We’ve gathered insights from top interior designers, color consultants, and industry experts to bring you the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors right now—along with real-world examples, expert tips, and common mistakes to avoid so you can transform your kitchen into a space that feels timeless, stylish, and uniquely yours.

Why Kitchen Paint Colors Matter More Than Ever

Before diving into the trends, let’s explore why color selection is one of the most impactful decisions you’ll make in a kitchen renovation:

  1. First Impressions & Mood Setting

    • A kitchen’s color palette sets the tone for the entire home. Warm tones (like terracotta or olive green) create coziness, while cool blues and grays evoke calmness. According to a 2022 study by the Journal of Environmental Psychology, color influences cognitive performance and emotional well-being, meaning your kitchen’s paint can actually affect your stress levels.

  2. Enhancing Natural Light & Space Perception

    • The right shade can make a small kitchen feel larger or warm up a dark, windowless space. For example, light greige (gray-beige blends) reflects light better than pure white, reducing the need for overhead lighting.

  3. Increasing Home Value

    • A 2023 Remodeling Magazine report found that kitchen remodels with updated paint recoup ~80% of their cost at resale. Neutral yet stylish colors (like sage green or warm white) appeal to the broadest buyer base.

  4. Personal Expression & Trend Adaptability

    • Unlike flooring or cabinetry, paint is easy to update without a full renovation. This makes it perfect for staging homes for sale or refreshing a space without a major investment.

  5. Psychological & Emotional Impact

    • Blue kitchens are linked to productivity and focus, making them ideal for home offices. Yellow kitchens (like buttery mustard) boost appetite and happiness, while deep reds (used sparingly) add energy and warmth.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Backed by Design Pros)

Now, let’s break down the top 8 kitchen paint colors that designers are recommending this year—why they work, how to use them, and real-world examples of spaces that nail the look.

1. Warm White (Sherwin-Williams "Pure White" or Benjamin Moore "Chantilly Lace")

Why It’s Trending: Warm white isn’t just a classic—it’s a versatile, timeless choice that pairs with any cabinet style, from shaker to modern flat-panel. Unlike cool whites (which can feel sterile), warm whites have subtle undertones of cream or beige, adding depth without overwhelming the space.

Designer Insight: "Warm white is the new ‘safe bet,’" says Sarah Johnson, a NYC-based kitchen designer. "It’s light enough to brighten small kitchens but warm enough to feel inviting. It also makes wood tones look richer and stainless steel appliances pop."

How to Use It:

  • Pair with: Light wood cabinets, matte black appliances, or brass hardware.
  • Best for: Open-concept homes, farmhouse-style kitchens, and modern minimalist spaces.
  • Pro Tip: If your kitchen has yellow-toned lighting, opt for a slightly cooler warm white (like Sherwin-Williams "Alabaster") to avoid a yellow cast.

Real-World Example: Imagine a bright, airy kitchen in a modern farmhouse home with white shaker cabinets, butcher block countertops, and a large island. The walls are painted in Benjamin Moore’s "Chantilly Lace", a warm white with just enough creaminess to soften the space. The matte black refrigerator stands out against the walls, while warm wood flooring ties everything together. The result? A clean, spa-like kitchen that feels warm and welcoming—perfect for hosting.

2. Sage Green (Sherwin-Williams "Clary Sage" or Farrow & Ball "Green Smoke")

Why It’s Trending: After years of greige dominance, sage green is making a strong comeback—but this time, it’s softer, more sophisticated, and less "earthy" than the 2010s trend. Designers love it because it adds a touch of nature without being overwhelming.

Designer Insight: "Sage green is the perfect middle ground—it’s not too bold like emerald, but it’s not boring like white," explains Michael Chen, a Los Angeles-based color consultant. "It works in both traditional and modern kitchens and pairs beautifully with wood, metal, and even bold accents."

How to Use It:

  • Pair with: Light wood cabinets, brass fixtures, or white subway tile backsplashes.
  • Best for: Rustic, cottage, or Scandinavian-style kitchens.
  • Pro Tip: If your kitchen has limited natural light, choose a slightly deeper sage (like Farrow & Ball’s "Green Smoke") to avoid a washed-out look.

Real-World Example: Picture a cozy, sunlit kitchen in a coastal cottage home. The walls are painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Clary Sage", a muted, mossy green that feels calming and organic. The light oak cabinets contrast beautifully with the paint, while matte black countertops add a modern touch. A woven rattan pendant light hangs above the island, tying the natural elements together. The result? A serene, nature-inspired kitchen that feels timeless and relaxing.

3. Deep Navy Blue (Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy" or Sherwin-Williams "Naval")

Why It’s Trending: After years of soft blues, deep navy is back—and it’s dramatic. Designers are using it to create a luxurious, moody kitchen that feels sophisticated and intimate. It’s especially popular in open-concept homes where the kitchen flows into a living area.

Designer Insight: "Navy is the new black," jokes Lisa Patel, a Chicago-based interior designer. "It’s bold enough to make a statement but still elegant enough for everyday living. The key is balancing it with light wood, gold, or white to keep it from feeling too heavy."

How to Use It:

  • Pair with: Light wood cabinets, gold hardware, or white quartz countertops.
  • Best for: Modern, transitional, or industrial-style kitchens.
  • Pro Tip: If your kitchen is small or dark, use navy on one accent wall (like behind the sink or island) rather than all four walls.

Real-World Example: Consider a contemporary kitchen in a loft-style apartment with dark wood cabinets and a large island. The walls are painted in Benjamin Moore’s "Hale Navy", a rich, velvety blue that feels luxurious and moody. The white quartz countertops reflect light, keeping the space from feeling closed-in, while brass pendant lights add a touch of warmth. A large statement mirror above the sink helps bounce light around, making the navy walls feel spacious rather than oppressive.

4. Terracotta (Sherwin-Williams "Canyon Clay" or Farrow & Ball "Red Earth")

Why It’s Trending: Terracotta is not just for pottery anymore—it’s a warm, earthy tone that’s trendy, affordable, and full of character. It’s inspired by Mediterranean and Southwestern aesthetics, bringing organic warmth to modern kitchens.

Designer Insight: "Terracotta is the perfect way to add warmth and texture without being overwhelming," says David Kim, a San Francisco-based designer. "It pairs beautifully with wood, rattan, and natural stone, making it ideal for boho, farmhouse, or rustic-modern spaces."

How to Use It:

  • Pair with: Light wood cabinets, rattan pendant lights, or white subway tile.
  • Best for: Farmhouse, boho, or Mediterranean-style kitchens.
  • Pro Tip: If terracotta feels too warm for your space, balance it with cool accents like white or gray to keep the palette from feeling overly rustic.

Real-World Example: Imagine a vibrant, sun-drenched kitchen in a rural cottage home. The walls are painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Canyon Clay", a soft, peachy terracotta that feels inviting and organic. The light oak cabinets contrast beautifully with the paint, while woven jute rugs and rattan chairs add to the boho charm. A large terracotta planter filled with herbs sits on the windowsill, tying the color palette together. The result? A warm, earthy kitchen that feels lived-in and full of personality.

5. Greige (Sherwin-Williams "Agreeable Gray" or Benjamin Moore "Revere Pewter")

Why It’s Trending: Greige (gray-beige blends) has been a staple for years, but 2024’s versions are softer and more refined. Designers love it because it bridges the gap between cool gray and warm beige, making it versatile for any style.

Designer Insight: "Greige is the ultimate ‘safe but stylish’ choice," says Emily Rodriguez, a Houston-based designer. "It’s light enough to brighten a space but has enough depth to feel modern and sophisticated."

How to Use It:

  • Pair with: Dark wood cabinets, matte black appliances, or white quartz countertops.
  • Best for: Modern, transitional, or minimalist kitchens.
  • Pro Tip: If your kitchen has yellow-toned lighting, choose a slightly cooler greige (like Benjamin Moore’s "Revere Pewter") to avoid a yellow cast.

Real-World Example: Think of a sleek, contemporary kitchen in a modern condo. The walls are painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Agreeable Gray", a soft, warm greige that feels neutral yet inviting. The dark walnut cabinets provide contrast and depth, while matte black appliances add a modern edge. A large statement light fixture (like a brass industrial pendant) draws the eye upward, balancing the neutral palette. The result? A clean, timeless kitchen that feels elegant and understated.

6. Butter Yellow (Sherwin-Williams "Chewy" or Farrow & Ball "India Yellow")

Why It’s Trending: After years of cool neutrals, **buttery yellow is making a joyful comeback—and it’s not just for 1950s diners anymore. Designers are using it to add warmth, energy, and personality to kitchens.

Designer Insight: "Yellow is the new ‘happy color,’" says James Lee, a New York-based color expert. "It’s bright enough to energize a space but soft enough to feel warm and cozy—perfect for kitchens where we spend a lot of time."

How to Use It:

  • Pair with: White cabinets, black appliances, or natural wood accents.
  • Best for: Modern, farmhouse, or eclectic-style kitchens.
  • Pro Tip: If your kitchen is small or dark, use butter yellow on one accent wall or in small doses (like a yellow backsplash).

Real-World Example: Picture a vibrant, sunlit kitchen in a modern farmhouse home. The walls are painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Chewy", a warm, buttery yellow that feels inviting and cheerful. The white shaker cabinets provide contrast, while black countertops add a modern touch. A large round farmhouse table sits in the center, surrounded by wooden chairs with yellow cushions—tying the color palette together. The result? A bright, happy kitchen that feels full of life and warmth.

7. Charcoal Gray (Sherwin-Williams "Iron Ore" or Benjamin Moore "Gray Owl")

Why It’s Trending: While soft grays have been popular for years, **charcoal gray is now making a bold statement—especially in modern and industrial kitchens. It’s darker, richer, and more sophisticated than traditional gray, making it ideal for statement walls or small kitchens.

Designer Insight: "Charcoal gray is the new ‘black’ for kitchens," says Sophia Martinez, a Los Angeles-based designer. "It’s dramatic enough to make a statement but still versatile enough to pair with almost anything."

How to Use It:

  • Pair with: White or light wood cabinets, brass fixtures, or white quartz countertops.
  • Best for: Modern, industrial, or transitional kitchens.
  • Pro Tip: If your kitchen is small or dark, use charcoal gray on one accent wall (like behind the sink or island) to add depth without overwhelming the space.

Real-World Example: Imagine a stylish, industrial kitchen in a loft-style apartment. The walls are painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Iron Ore", a deep, velvety charcoal gray that feels luxurious and moody. The white shaker cabinets provide contrast, while brass pendant lights add a touch of warmth. A large statement mirror above the sink helps bounce light around, making the space feel larger and more open. The result? A sophisticated, modern kitchen that feels bold yet balanced.
