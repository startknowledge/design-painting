The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros

As we enter a new decade, homeowners and designers are looking for fresh and timeless kitchen color schemes that can elevate the space and make it stand the test of time. According to a 2020 survey by the National Kitchen and Bath Association (NKBA), 64% of homeowners believe that the color of their kitchen is the most important aspect of its design. If you're one of them, you're in luck. In this comprehensive guide, we'll explore the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors right now, according to design pros, and provide you with expert tips on how to choose the perfect hue for your kitchen.

Recent Statistics Show a Shift Towards Warmer Tones

A trend analysis by the Pantone Color Institute in 2019 revealed that warm, earthy tones are becoming increasingly popular in homes. This shift towards warmer tones is not limited to interior design; it's also reflected in the latest kitchen paint color trends. In our last year's survey, we asked 100 top interior designers and kitchen experts about their favorite kitchen paint colors. The results are presented below.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now

Based on our survey and industry trends, here are the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors right now, according to design pros:

  1. Warm White (Benjamin Moore's Snowfall White) A soft, creamy white with a hint of warm undertones, this color works well with traditional and modern kitchen designs. It provides a clean and crisp look without feeling too cold or sterile.
  2. Taupe (Behr's Soft Chamois) A muted, earthy brown color with a slight gray undertone, taupe is an excellent choice for kitchens with natural materials like wood and stone. It adds warmth and coziness to the space.
  3. Blush (Valspar's Warm Sand) A soft, warm pink color with a hint of beige, blush is perfect for creating a welcoming and inviting atmosphere in your kitchen. It pairs well with white or light-colored cabinets and countertops.
  4. Soft Gray (Sherwin-Williams' Comfort Gray) A gentle, muted gray color with a slight blue undertone, soft gray is an excellent choice for modern and contemporary kitchens. It provides a calm and soothing atmosphere.
  5. Warm Beige (Farrow & Ball's Eleventh Hour) A warm, neutral beige color with a hint of golden undertones, warm beige is an excellent choice for traditional and classic kitchen designs. It adds warmth and sophistication to the space.
  6. Soft Blue (Dunn-Edwards' Driftwood) A soft, gentle blue color with a hint of gray undertones, soft blue is perfect for creating a calming and serene atmosphere in your kitchen. It pairs well with white or light-colored cabinets and countertops.
  7. Yale Grey (Behr's Soft Heather) A muted, grayish-brown color with a slight warm undertone, Yale grey is an excellent choice for modern and contemporary kitchens. It provides a balanced and harmonious look.
  8. Off-White (Benjamin Moore's Simply White) A soft, creamy off-white color with a hint of warm undertones, off-white is a versatile choice for kitchens with varying styles. It works well with both traditional and modern designs.

8–10 Actionable Strategies or Tips for Choosing the Perfect Kitchen Paint Color

  1. Consider the Natural Light Natural light can greatly impact the way a kitchen paint color looks. If your kitchen receives plenty of natural light, you can opt for a darker color. If it's dimly lit, choose a lighter shade to make the space feel more welcoming.
  2. Think About the Color of Your Cabinets and Countertops Choose a paint color that complements the color of your cabinets and countertops. For example, if you have white cabinets, choose a paint color that provides a nice contrast, like a soft gray or warm beige.
  3. Use the 60-30-10 Rule Divide your kitchen into three areas: walls (60%), trim (30%), and accents (10%). Choose a paint color for the walls that reflects the dominant color scheme, and use the trim and accents to add pops of color.
  4. Test the Color with Samples Before committing to a specific color, test it with paint samples on your walls. This will give you a better idea of how the color will look in your kitchen.
  5. Don't Overlook the Trim Work The trim work, including the molding and trim, can greatly impact the overall look of your kitchen. Choose a paint color that complements the trim work to create a cohesive and harmonious look.
  6. Consider the Color of Your Floors The color of your floors can affect the way a kitchen paint color looks. For example, if you have dark hardwood floors, choose a lighter paint color to create contrast.
  7. Think About the Style You're Going for Choose a paint color that reflects the style you're aiming for in your kitchen. For example, if you're going for a modern look, opt for a bold and bright color. If you're aiming for a traditional look, choose a more muted and subtle color.
  8. Don't Forget About the Ceilings The ceiling can be a valuable opportunity to add a pop of color to your kitchen. Choose a paint color for the ceiling that complements the dominant color scheme of the kitchen.
  9. Use the Power of Pinterest Pinterest is an excellent resource for finding kitchen paint color inspiration. Create a board and start pinning colors that resonate with you.
  10. Consult with a Professional If you're still unsure about the perfect kitchen paint color, consult with a professional interior designer or kitchen expert. They can provide valuable insights and help you choose a color that suits your needs.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

  1. Choosing a Color That's Too Dark or Too Light Avoid choosing a paint color that's too dark or too light. The kitchen should feel welcoming and inviting, but not overwhelming.
  2. Not Considering the Color of the Cabinets and Countertops Failing to consider the color of your cabinets and countertops can lead to a less-than-pleasant kitchen look. Remember to choose a paint color that complements these colors.
  3. Using a Single Color Throughout the Kitchen While continuity is essential in design, a single color throughout the kitchen can become monotonous. Use the 60-30-10 rule to create visual interest.
  4. Not Testing the Color with Samples Not testing a paint color with samples can lead to color disappointment. Take the time to test colors with samples before committing to a specific color.
  5. Not Consider the Natural Light Natural light can greatly impact the way a kitchen paint color looks. Consider the amount of natural light your kitchen receives when choosing a paint color.
