The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros (2024 Trends & Expert Tips)

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Making a Bold Comeback in 2024

Kitchens are no longer just functional spaces—they’re the heart of the home, where style meets functionality. With the rise of open-concept living and the demand for aesthetic yet practical design, kitchen paint colors have taken center stage. According to a 2023 report by the National Kitchen & Bath Association (NKBA), over 60% of homeowners prioritize color and materials when remodeling their kitchens, with paint being the most cost-effective way to transform the space.

But what colors are trendy right now? And more importantly—how should you choose them? We’ve gathered insights from top interior designers, color consultants, and industry experts to bring you the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors of 2024, along with real-world examples, expert tips, and common mistakes to avoid.

Whether you’re renovating, repainting, or simply dreaming of a fresh look, this guide will help you make an informed decision—without breaking the bank.

Why Kitchen Paint Colors Matter More Than Ever

Before diving into the trends, let’s explore why color selection is so crucial in kitchen design:

  1. First Impressions & Resale Value – A well-painted kitchen can increase a home’s perceived value by up to 10% (per a 2023 Remodeling Magazine study). Neutral yet stylish shades like greige (gray-beige) and warm white remain evergreen, while bold hues like deep navy or terracotta add personality.

  2. Psychological Impact – Color influences mood and behavior. For example:

    • Cool tones (blues, greens) promote calmness and reduce stress.
    • Warm tones (reds, oranges, yellows) stimulate appetite and conversation.
    • Earthy neutrals (beige, taupe) create a cozy, timeless feel.

  3. Lighting & Space Perception – The right paint can make a small kitchen feel larger (light colors reflect light) or add depth to a spacious one (darker accents).

  4. Trend vs. Timelessness – While 2023 saw a surge in muted pastels and jewel tones, 2024 is blending boldness with subtlety, favoring high-contrast pairings (e.g., black cabinets with cream walls) and monochromatic schemes (different shades of the same color).

  5. Durability & Maintenance – Kitchens endure spills, grease, and humidity, so semi-gloss or eggshell finishes are ideal for walls, while satin or matte work well for ceilings.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Backed by Design Pros)

Designers agree that 2024’s kitchen color palette is all about balance—mixing trendy hues with classic neutrals for a modern yet inviting look. Below are the top 8 colors dominating the scene, along with how to use them effectively.

1. Warm White (Benjamin Moore "White Dove" or Sherwin-Williams "Alabaster")

Why It’s Popular: Warm white is the ultimate neutral—bright enough to reflect light but warm enough to feel cozy. Unlike stark white, it has subtle undertones (often beige or soft gray), making it less clinical and more inviting.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Pairing: Works with dark wood cabinets, brass hardware, and metallic accents for a luxury farmhouse look.
  • Lighting: Enhances warm LED lighting (3000K-3500K) to create a sunlit, airy feel.
  • Textures: Complements textured walls (like chalk paint or linen finishes) for depth.

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Portland, Oregon, features Benjamin Moore’s "White Dove" on the walls with dark walnut cabinets, a butcher block island, and open shelving displaying vintage dishes. The result? A bright, spacious feel that feels warm and lived-in—not sterile.

Pro Tip: If your kitchen has cool-toned lighting, add a yellow or peach throw pillow to balance the warmth.

2. Greige (Gray-Beige) – Sherwin-Williams "Agreeable Gray" or Farrow & Ball "Skimming Stone")

Why It’s Popular: Greige is the perfect middle ground—neutral enough for versatility but slightly warm to avoid a cold gray look. It’s been a top choice since 2022 and remains 2024’s most adaptable color.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Flexibility: Pairs with any cabinet color—from white shaker cabinets to dark walnut or even black lacquer.
  • Accents: Lets bold backsplashes (matte black, terracotta, or deep green) stand out.
  • Small Spaces: Makes compact kitchens feel larger while adding subtle sophistication.

Real-World Example: A minimalist kitchen in Brooklyn uses Sherwin-Williams "Agreeable Gray" on the walls with white cabinets, a quartz countertop in "White Tempest," and black stainless steel appliances. The greige softens the contrast, making the space feel calm and intentional rather than harsh.

Pro Tip: To avoid a too-neutral look, add one pop of color—like emerald green dishware or terracotta tiles in the backsplash.

3. Deep Navy Blue (Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy" or Farrow & Ball "Drawing Room Blue")

Why It’s Popular: After years of soft blues, 2024 is embracing rich, moody navy—a bold yet timeless choice that adds dramatic depth to kitchens.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Contrast: Pairs beautifully with white or light wood cabinets for a high-contrast, modern look.
  • Metallic Accents: Gold or brass fixtures elevate the luxury factor.
  • Textured Walls: Works well with textured paint finishes (like Farrow & Ball’s "Slipper Smooth" for a velvety feel).

Real-World Example: A luxury kitchen in Los Angeles features Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy" on the walls with white cabinetry, a black marble island, and gold faucets. The navy creates a moody yet elegant backdrop, while the white cabinets keep it bright and airy. Homeowners love how it feels sophisticated without being overwhelming.

Pro Tip: If you’re nervous about too much darkness, limit the navy to one wall (as an accent) or use it on upper cabinets while keeping lower cabinets light.

4. Terracotta (Sherwin-Williams "Cavalier" or Farrow & Ball "Red Earth")

Why It’s Popular: Inspired by Mediterranean and Moroccan aesthetics, terracotta is making a big comeback—adding warmth, texture, and a touch of boho charm to kitchens.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Accent Walls: Perfect for one feature wall behind a wooden island or range hood.
  • Tile Pairings: Complements handmade clay tiles or subway tiles in terracotta.
  • Wood Tones: Pairs well with light oak or walnut cabinets for a natural, earthy look.

Real-World Example: A rural-style kitchen in Texas uses Sherwin-Williams "Cavalier" on the lower cabinets and backsplash, paired with light wood upper cabinets and a butcher block countertop. The terracotta adds warmth without being too intense, making the space feel cozy and inviting.

Pro Tip: To soften the intensity, pair terracotta with cream or white (instead of dark wood) and add metallic accents (like brass or copper) for balance.

5. Sage Green (Benjamin Moore "October Mist" or Farrow & Ball "Green Smoke")

Why It’s Popular: Sage green is back in a big way—offering a calming, nature-inspired alternative to traditional white. It’s softer than emerald but more vibrant than mint.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Neutral Base: Works as a primary wall color or an accent in small doses.
  • Wood Pairings: Pairs beautifully with light oak, walnut, or even black cabinets.
  • Textured Finishes: Looks stunning with chalky or matte finishes for a soft, organic feel.

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Colorado uses Benjamin Moore "October Mist" on the walls with white cabinets, a quartz countertop in "White Tempest," and open shelving with green glassware. The sage creates a serene, nature-inspired vibe that feels fresh and airy.

Pro Tip: If your kitchen has cool lighting, add a yellow or peach throw blanket to warm up the space.

6. Warm Gray (Sherwin-Williams "Repose Gray" or Benjamin Moore "Gray Owl")

Why It’s Popular: A softer, warmer take on traditional gray, this shade is less harsh and more inviting—perfect for those who love gray but want less coolness.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Versatile Pairings: Works with any cabinet color, from white to dark wood.
  • Subtle Depth: Adds dimension without overwhelming the space.
  • Small Kitchens: Makes compact spaces feel larger while adding modern elegance.

Real-World Example: A contemporary kitchen in Chicago features Sherwin-Williams "Repose Gray" on the walls with white shaker cabinets, a black marble island, and brass hardware. The warm gray softens the contrast, making the space feel cohesive and stylish.

Pro Tip: To avoid a flat look, add textured elements like woven baskets or a linen backsplash.

7. Mustard Yellow (Farrow & Ball "India Yellow" or Benjamin Moore "Mustard Seed")

Why It’s Popular: Mustard yellow is making a bold return—adding warmth, energy, and a touch of vintage charm to kitchens. It’s richer than lemon yellow but lighter than gold.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Accent Walls: Perfect for one feature wall behind a wooden island or range hood.
  • Metallic Pairings: Pairs beautifully with gold, brass, or copper for a luxury feel.
  • Textured Surfaces: Works well with textured tiles or chalky paint finishes.

Real-World Example: A vintage-inspired kitchen in New York uses Farrow & Ball "India Yellow" on the upper cabinets and backsplash, paired with white lower cabinets and a black marble countertop. The mustard adds warmth and personality, making the space feel cozy yet chic.

Pro Tip: If you’re worried about too much brightness, balance it with dark wood or black accents.

8. Warm Taupe (Benjamin Moore "Chantilly Lace" or Sherwin-Williams "Accessible Beige")

Why It’s Popular: Taupe is the ultimate neutral—warmer than gray, softer than beige, and perfect for creating a cozy, timeless kitchen.

How Design Pros Use It:

  • Flexible Pairings: Works with any cabinet color, from white to dark wood.
  • Subtle Depth: Adds warmth without being overwhelming.
  • Small Spaces: Makes compact kitchens feel larger while adding elegance.

Real-World Example: A modern kitchen in San Francisco features Benjamin Moore "Chantilly Lace" on the walls with white cabinets, a quartz countertop in "White Tempest," and black stainless steel appliances. The taupe creates a soft, inviting atmosphere that feels luxurious yet understated.

Pro Tip: To add interest, incorporate textured elements like a woven rug or a linen curtain.

10 Actionable Tips from Design Pros to Choose the Perfect Kitchen Paint Color

Now that you know the top 8 colors, how do you choose the right one for your space? Here are 10 expert-backed tips to help you make the best decision.

1. Consider Your Kitchen’s Lighting

  • North-facing kitchens (cool, dim light) benefit from warm whites or soft grays.
  • South-facing kitchens (bright, direct light) can handle bolder colors like navy or terracotta.
  • East-facing kitchens (morning sun) look best with cool neutrals (greige, sage green).
  • West-facing kitchens (evening sun) can pull off richer tones (mustard, deep red).

Pro Move: Test paint swatches at different times of day to see how they change under natural light.

2. Think About Your Cabinets & Countertops

  • White cabinets? Pair with greige, warm white, or navy for contrast.
  • Dark wood cabinets? Use light grays, warm taupe, or sage green to balance.
  • Black cabinets? Go for creamy whites, mustard yellow, or deep red for drama.
  • Quartz countertops? Neutral walls (greige, taupe) keep the focus on the countertop.

3. Don’t Fear Bold Accents

If you love a bold color but aren’t sure about walls, try:

  • An accent wall (navy, terracotta, or mustard).
  • Upper cabinets (a different shade than lower cabinets).
  • Ceiling paint (a slightly darker or lighter tone for depth).

4. Test Before You Commit

  • Paint small sections (like a door or trim) to see how the color interacts with your space.
  • Use sample pots (not just the can) to get an accurate representation.
  • Observe for 24-48 hours—colors can look different in varying light.

5. Balance Warm & Cool Tones

If your kitchen has cool-toned elements (white, gray, black), add warmth with:

  • Wood tones (oak, walnut, bamboo).
  • Metallic accents (brass, gold, copper).
  • Textured fabrics (linen, wool, rattan).

6. Consider Your Lifestyle

  • Busy families? Opt for durable, easy-to-clean finishes (semi-gloss or eggshell).
  • Minimalists? Stick to neutral palettes (greige, warm white, taupe).
  • Entertainment lovers? Bold colors (navy, terracotta) create a vibrant, social space.

**7. Layer with Textures
