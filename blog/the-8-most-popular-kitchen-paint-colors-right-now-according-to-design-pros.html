The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros (2024 Trends & Expert Tips)

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Making a Bold Comeback in 2024

Kitchens are no longer just functional spaces—they’re the heart of the home, where style meets functionality. With the rise of open-concept living and hybrid workspaces, homeowners are investing more than ever in kitchen design. According to a 2023 Houzz Kitchen & Bathroom Trends Report, 78% of homeowners prioritize aesthetics in their kitchen remodels, with paint color being the top choice for a quick yet impactful upgrade.

But what colors are design professionals actually recommending in 2024? After analyzing the latest trends from interior designers, color consultants, and home staging experts, we’ve identified the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors that are dominating shelves—and why they work so well.

From soothing neutrals to bold statement hues, these shades are being used in everything from modern farmhouse kitchens to luxury minimalist spaces. Whether you're planning a full remodel or just a fresh coat of paint, this guide will help you choose the perfect shade for your kitchen.

Why Kitchen Paint Colors Matter More Than Ever

Before diving into the trends, let’s explore why paint color is such a powerful design element in kitchens:

  1. First Impressions & Resale Value – A well-painted kitchen can increase a home’s perceived value by up to 10% (per the 2023 National Association of Realtors® Report). Neutral tones like soft whites and warm grays are particularly effective for staging.
  2. Mood & Functionality – Color psychology plays a role: cool blues can make a space feel more open, while warm terracottas add coziness. The right shade can even influence appetite (yes, really!).
  3. Trend vs. Timelessness – While bold colors like emerald green and deep navy are trending, classic whites and greige (gray-beige blends) remain evergreen choices.
  4. Lighting & Space Perception – A kitchen’s natural and artificial lighting affects how paint colors appear. North-facing kitchens benefit from warmer tones, while south-facing spaces can handle cooler, brighter shades.
  5. DIY vs. Professional Application – A perfectly painted kitchen can be achieved with high-quality paint (like Benjamin Moore Regal Select or Sherwin-Williams Emerald) and the right techniques.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Backed by Design Pros)

Designers are moving away from overly trendy, short-lived colors and instead favoring versatile, long-lasting shades that work across multiple styles. Here are the top 8 kitchen paint colors that are leading the charge in 2024, along with real-world examples and expert tips for implementation.

1. Warm White (Benjamin Moore "Chantilly Lace") – The Ultimate Neutral

Why It’s Popular: Warm white is not the same as stark white—it has subtle undertones of beige or cream, making it feel inviting rather than sterile. According to Sherwin-Williams’ 2024 Color Forecast, warm whites are the #1 choice for kitchens because they brighten spaces without overwhelming them.

Best For:

  • Open-concept homes (blends seamlessly with living areas)
  • Small kitchens (creates an airy, spacious feel)
  • Traditional, farmhouse, and transitional styles

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Nashville, Tennessee, used Benjamin Moore Chantilly Lace (OC-65) on the cabinets and walls. The warm white softened the natural wood tones of the butcher block countertop while keeping the space bright and welcoming. Homeowners reported that guests often commented on how "cozy" the kitchen felt, even though it was one of the largest rooms in the house.

Pro Tip:

  • Avoid cool white (like Sherwin-Williams "Pure White") if you want a warm, cozy feel.
  • Pair with wood accents (oak, walnut) to enhance warmth.
  • Use a satin or eggshell finish for easy cleaning and durability.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using a flat finish – Flat paint shows scuffs and fingerprints easily. ✅ Solution: Opt for semi-gloss or satin on trim and cabinets for a clean, polished look.

2. Greige (Sherwin-Williams "Agreeable Gray") – The Perfect Neutral Blend

Why It’s Popular: Greige (gray + beige) is the most balanced neutral—it’s warmer than gray but lighter than beige, making it endlessly versatile. According to Color Marketing Group’s 2023 report, greige is the #2 most requested color for kitchens, especially in urban and suburban homes.

Best For:

  • Modern, contemporary, and transitional kitchens
  • Homes with dark wood or black appliances (creates contrast)
  • Spaces needing a fresh update without going bold

Real-World Example: A luxury condo in Chicago featured Sherwin-Williams Agreeable Gray (SW 7029) on the walls, paired with dark walnut cabinets and matte black appliances. The greige softened the contrast, making the kitchen feel sophisticated yet approachable. The homeowner noted that renters loved the "calm, modern" vibe, which helped the property stay leased quickly.

Pro Tip:

  • Test in natural light—greige can look too cool in some conditions.
  • Add warm wood or brass hardware to balance cool undertones.
  • Use on upper cabinets for a fresh, updated look without painting everything.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Choosing a gray that’s too cool (like "Repose Gray" by Sherwin-Williams) – can make a kitchen feel sterile. ✅ Solution: Stick to warm greige (like "Agreeable Gray" or "Accessible Beige" by Benjamin Moore).

3. Soft Sage Green (Farrow & Ball "Green Smoke") – The Relaxing Trend

Why It’s Popular: After years of mustard yellow and olive green, soft sage is making a subtle but sophisticated comeback. According to Farrow & Ball’s 2024 Color Report, green is the #3 most loved color for kitchens because it evokes calm and connection to nature.

Best For:

  • Earthy, organic, and Scandinavian-inspired kitchens
  • Kitchens with white or light wood cabinetry (creates a fresh contrast)
  • Spaces needing a moody yet cozy feel

Real-World Example: A rustic-chic kitchen in Portland, Oregon, used Farrow & Ball Green Smoke (No. 47) on the walls, paired with white Shaker-style cabinets and warm oak flooring. The sage green softened the space, making it feel like a cozy garden retreat. The homeowner mentioned that evenings felt more relaxed in the kitchen, likely due to the calming effect of green.

Pro Tip:

  • Pair with natural materials (stone, wood, rattan) for an organic feel.
  • Use on an accent wall if you want subtle color without commitment.
  • Avoid in small kitchens—it can make spaces feel too enclosed.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using a dark sage in a small kitchen – can make the space feel cramped. ✅ Solution: Stick to light to medium sage (like "Green Smoke" or "Pale Powder" by Sherwin-Williams).

4. Warm Terracotta (Behr "Terracotta Clay") – The Bold, Earthy Statement

Why It’s Popular: Terracotta is back in a big way, but this time, it’s softer and more refined. According to Behr’s 2024 Color Trends, warm terracotta is the #4 most requested color for bold yet timeless kitchens. It’s richer than clay red but less overwhelming than deep red.

Best For:

  • Mediterranean, Moroccan, and bohemian-style kitchens
  • Kitchens with white or light-colored countertops (creates striking contrast)
  • Homeowners who love warm, inviting spaces

Real-World Example: A Spanish-inspired kitchen in Santa Fe, New Mexico, used Behr Terracotta Clay (M15-10) on the walls, paired with white subway tile backsplash and wrought-iron lighting. The terracotta added warmth without overwhelming the space, making it feel like a sun-drenched courtyard. The homeowner loved that it stood out in the desert climate while still feeling cohesive with the outdoor patio.

Pro Tip:

  • Balance with white or light wood to prevent overwhelm.
  • Use on an accent wall or upper cabinets for subtle drama.
  • Avoid in kitchens with cool lighting—it can look muddy.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using a flat finish – terracotta shows scratches and wear easily. ✅ Solution: Use semi-gloss or eggshell for durability and depth.

5. Deep Navy Blue (Sherwin-Williams "Naval") – The Luxe, Moody Choice

Why It’s Popular: Navy blue is making a major comeback, but this time, it’s not just for formal dining rooms. According to Sherwin-Williams’ 2024 Color Forecast, deep blues are the #5 most requested color for modern, high-end kitchens because they add sophistication and depth.

Best For:

  • Contemporary, industrial, and coastal-style kitchens
  • Kitchens with white or light-colored countertops (creates striking contrast)
  • Homeowners who want a bold yet timeless look

Real-World Example: A luxury loft kitchen in Brooklyn, New York, featured Sherwin-Williams Naval (SW 6244) on the walls, paired with white quartz countertops and black steel appliances. The navy created a moody yet elegant atmosphere, making the space feel like a high-end restaurant. The homeowner noted that it was the first thing guests noticed, and it increased the property’s perceived value.

Pro Tip:

  • Pair with gold or brass hardware for luxury contrast.
  • Use on upper cabinets to keep the lower cabinets light.
  • Ensure good lighting—navy can look too dark in dim spaces.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using navy in a small kitchen without enough contrast – can make the space feel claustrophobic. ✅ Solution: Balance with white or light wood and maximize natural light.

6. Warm Gray-Brown (Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy") – The Sophisticated Neutral

Why It’s Popular: This isn’t just gray—it’s a warm, slightly brown-tinted gray that adds depth without being overwhelming. According to Benjamin Moore’s 2024 Trends Report, warm grays are the #6 most popular choice for modern and transitional kitchens.

Best For:

  • Industrial, transitional, and modern farmhouse kitchens
  • Kitchens with white or light wood cabinetry (creates rich contrast)
  • Homeowners who want a subtle, grown-up look

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Austin, Texas, used Benjamin Moore Hale Navy (2139-10) on the walls, paired with white Shaker cabinets and wide-plank oak flooring. The warm gray-brown added sophistication while keeping the farmhouse charm. The homeowner loved that it felt timeless and didn’t date the space.

Pro Tip:

  • Pair with brass or matte black hardware for modern contrast.
  • Use on upper cabinets for a fresh, updated look.
  • Avoid in small kitchens—it can make spaces feel too heavy.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Choosing a gray that’s too cool (like "Repose Gray") – can look sterile. ✅ Solution: Stick to warm gray-brown (like "Hale Navy" or "Chestnut" by Sherwin-Williams).

7. Soft Butter Yellow (Farrow & Ball "Setting Plaster") – The Cheerful Classic

Why It’s Popular: After years of mustard and olive, soft butter yellow is back—but this time, it’s lighter, cleaner, and more modern. According to Farrow & Ball’s 2024 report, warm yellows are the #7 most requested color for bright, cheerful kitchens.

Best For:

  • Traditional, farmhouse, and cottage-style kitchens
  • Kitchens with dark wood or black appliances (creates warm contrast)
  • Homeowners who want a happy, uplifting space

Real-World Example: A Victorian-style kitchen in Boston, Massachusetts, used Farrow & Ball Setting Plaster (No. 228) on the walls, paired with dark walnut cabinets and white subway tile. The soft yellow added warmth and brightness, making the space feel like a sunny afternoon. The homeowner reported that it was the first thing guests noticed, and they always commented on how "cheerful" the kitchen felt.

Pro Tip:

  • Pair with white or light wood to enhance brightness.
  • Use on an accent wall for subtle color.
  • Avoid in small kitchens—it can make spaces feel too warm.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using a flat finish – yellow shows scratches and wear easily. ✅ Solution: Use semi-gloss or eggshell for durability and vibrancy.

8. Deep Charcoal (Sherwin-Williams "Iron Ore") – The Edgy, Modern Choice

Why It’s Popular: Charcoal isn’t just for walls—it’s making a bold statement in kitchens as a dark, sophisticated alternative to black. According to Sherwin-Williams’ 2024 Trends, deep charcoals are the #8 most requested color for contemporary and industrial kitchens.
