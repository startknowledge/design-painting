The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros (2024 Trends & Expert Tips)

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Making a Bold Comeback in 2024

Kitchens are no longer just functional spaces—they’re the heart of the home, where style meets functionality. With the rise of open-concept living (a trend that surged 30% between 2020 and 2023, according to the National Association of Home Builders) and the growing demand for high-impact design, kitchen paint colors have taken center stage.

But what colors are design professionals actually choosing in 2024? According to a recent survey by the Color Marketing Group and Architectural Digest, 78% of interior designers report that kitchen paint trends are shifting toward bold, warm, and nature-inspired hues—moving away from the overly neutral palettes of the past decade.

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll break down the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors favored by top designers, backed by real-world examples, expert tips, and common mistakes to avoid. Whether you're renovating, remodeling, or simply refreshing your kitchen, this guide will help you make an informed (and stylish) choice.

Why Kitchen Paint Colors Matter More Than Ever

Before diving into the trends, let’s explore why paint color selection is so critical in kitchens:

  1. First Impressions & Curb Appeal – A well-painted kitchen can increase a home’s perceived value by up to 10%, according to the Appraisal Institute.
  2. Mood & Functionality – The right shade can enhance productivity (cool tones for focus) or create warmth (warm tones for coziness).
  3. Trend vs. Timelessness – While trends come and go, neutral bases with bold accents tend to last longer than purely trendy choices.
  4. Lighting & Space Perception – A dark kitchen can feel claustrophobic, while a light one may lack depth—color science plays a huge role.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Backed by Design Pros)

Designers agree that 2024’s kitchen color palette blends earthy tones, soft neutrals, and unexpected pops of color. Below, we’ve curated the top 8 colors based on industry reports, designer polls, and real-world applications.

1. Warm White (Benjamin Moore "White Dove" or Sherwin-Williams "Alabaster")

Why It’s Trending: Warm whites are making a comeback after the dominance of cool, crisp whites in the early 2010s. These shades have undertones of beige or soft gray, creating a cozy yet clean look.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Pair with natural wood cabinets (oak or walnut) for warmth.
  • Avoid pairing with black hardware—opt for brushed gold or matte brass instead.

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Portland, Oregon, used Benjamin Moore’s "White Dove" on the walls with white shiplap trim and butcher block countertops. The result? A bright, airy space that still felt inviting—perfect for hosting.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Overusing white in small kitchens—it can make the space feel sterile. Instead, add texture with a textured paint finish (like satin or eggshell) or wood accents.

2. Deep Navy Blue (Sherwin-Williams "Naval" or Farrow & Ball "Hague Blue")

Why It’s Trending: After years of soft blues, designers are embracing rich, moody blues—especially in traditional and transitional kitchens. Navy adds depth and sophistication, making it ideal for larger spaces.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Balance navy with gold or brass accents to prevent the space from feeling too heavy.
  • Use navy on upper cabinets while keeping lower cabinets white or wood-toned for contrast.

Real-World Example: A Grand Rapids, Michigan, kitchen featured Sherwin-Williams "Naval" on the walls with dark wood cabinets and matte black appliances. The moody yet elegant look was complemented by white subway tile backsplash and warm pendant lighting, creating a luxurious yet cozy vibe.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Using navy in small or poorly lit kitchens—it can make the space feel cramped. If you love navy but have a small kitchen, limit it to one wall (an accent wall) or upper cabinets.

3. Terracotta (Behr "Terracotta Clay" or Benjamin Moore "Rustic Clay")

Why It’s Trending: Inspired by Mediterranean and Southwestern aesthetics, terracotta brings earthy warmth to kitchens. It’s bold but not overwhelming, making it perfect for modern, rustic, or boho-style homes.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Pair with warm wood tones (like terracotta tiles or reclaimed wood) for cohesion.
  • Balance with metallic hardware (copper or bronze) to keep the look modern.

Real-World Example: A Santa Fe, New Mexico, kitchen used Benjamin Moore "Rustic Clay" on the walls with handmade clay tiles and open shelving. The organic, textured elements made the space feel lived-in and stylish, perfect for a family-friendly home.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Using terracotta in a kitchen with cool-toned appliances (like stainless steel). Instead, opt for black or matte black appliances to complement the warmth.

4. Sage Green (Sherwin-Williams "Clary Sage" or Farrow & Ball "Green Smoke")

Why It’s Trending: After the overuse of gray, sage green is back as a calming, nature-inspired alternative. It’s softer than emerald but still bold enough to stand out.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Use sage on lower cabinets while keeping walls white or light gray for balance.
  • Add natural elements (like wood, plants, or rattan) to enhance the organic feel.

Real-World Example: A Seattle, Washington, kitchen featured Sherwin-Williams "Clary Sage" on the lower cabinets with white upper cabinets and a butcher block island. The soft green created a relaxing, spa-like atmosphere, ideal for a home office or casual dining area.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Using sage in a kitchen with yellow or orange lighting—it can make the green look muddy. Instead, opt for warm white LED bulbs (2700K-3000K).

5. Warm Gray (Benjamin Moore "Revere Pewter" or Sherwin-Williams "Agreeable Gray")

Why It’s Trending: While gray was once the dominant neutral, designers are now favoring warmer, more inviting shades—think beige undertones rather than cool blues.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Pair with wood or matte black appliances for contrast.
  • Use gray on upper cabinets while keeping lower cabinets white or wood-toned.

Real-World Example: A Chicago, Illinois, kitchen used Benjamin Moore "Revere Pewter" on the walls with dark wood cabinets and a marble countertop. The soft, sophisticated look was enhanced by black pendant lights, making it a modern yet timeless space.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Using gray in a kitchen with a lot of black—it can make the space feel too heavy. Instead, balance with light wood or white accents.

6. Mustard Yellow (Farrow & Ball "India Yellow" or Behr "Mustard")

Why It’s Trending: Inspired by vintage and mid-century modern styles, mustard yellow is bold, cheerful, and unexpected. It’s perfect for accent walls or lower cabinets in traditional or eclectic kitchens.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Use mustard on one wall (like a dining nook or breakfast bar) to avoid overwhelming the space.
  • Pair with brass or gold hardware for a luxurious touch.

Real-World Example: A Denver, Colorado, kitchen featured Farrow & Ball "India Yellow" on the dining nook wall with white cabinets and a wooden table. The vibrant pop of color made the space feel energetic and inviting, perfect for family gatherings.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Using mustard in a small kitchen—it can feel too overwhelming. If you love the color, limit it to one feature wall.

7. Charcoal Gray (Sherwin-Williams "Iron Ore" or Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy")

Why It’s Trending: A darker, more sophisticated take on gray, charcoal is trendy yet timeless. It works well in modern, industrial, and transitional kitchens.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Use charcoal on upper cabinets while keeping lower cabinets white or wood-toned.
  • Add metallic accents (like brass or black matte finishes) to keep the look modern.

Real-World Example: A Brooklyn, New York, kitchen used Sherwin-Williams "Iron Ore" on the walls with black matte appliances and a concrete countertop. The moody yet sleek design was balanced by white subway tile and warm lighting, creating a luxury industrial vibe.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Using charcoal in a kitchen with a lot of black—it can make the space feel too dark. Instead, balance with light wood or white elements.

8. Soft Pink (Sherwin-Williams "Rose Quartz" or Benjamin Moore "First Light")

Why It’s Trending: After the overuse of gray, soft pink is making a gentle comeback—especially in modern farmhouse and Scandinavian-style kitchens. It’s calming, feminine, and unexpected.

Design Pro Tip:

  • Use pink on lower cabinets while keeping walls white or light gray.
  • Pair with wood or matte black appliances for contrast.

Real-World Example: A Austin, Texas, kitchen featured Benjamin Moore "First Light" on the lower cabinets with white upper cabinets and a butcher block island. The soft pink added a whimsical yet elegant touch, making it a perfect space for brunch or family meals.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Using pink in a kitchen with a lot of red or orange—it can make the space feel too chaotic. Instead, stick to neutral or cool-toned accents.

10 Actionable Strategies for Choosing the Perfect Kitchen Paint Color

Now that you know the top 8 colors, how do you choose the right one for your space? Here are 10 expert-backed strategies to help you decide:

1. Consider Your Kitchen’s Lighting

  • North-facing kitchens (cool, dim light) benefit from warm, light colors (white, beige, soft gray).
  • South-facing kitchens (bright, direct light) can handle darker or bolder hues (navy, terracotta, mustard).
  • East-facing kitchens (morning sun) work well with medium tones (sage, warm gray).

Pro Tip: Test paint samples at different times of day to see how the color changes with lighting.

2. Match Your Lifestyle

  • Busy families? Opt for durable, easy-to-clean finishes (semi-gloss or satin).
  • Minimalists? Choose soft neutrals (warm white, beige, light gray).
  • Bold lovers? Go for deep blues, terracotta, or mustard—just balance with neutrals.

3. Use the 60-30-10 Rule for Balance

  • 60% dominant color (walls)
  • 30% secondary color (cabinets, countertops)
  • 10% accent color (backplash, hardware, decor)

Example:

  • Walls (60%): Warm white
  • Cabinets (30%): Dark wood or charcoal
  • Accent (10%): Brass hardware + terracotta tiles

4. Test Paint in Small Sections

  • Buy sample pots and paint large swatches (not just small strips) on your walls.
  • Observe how the color changes with different lighting (daylight, incandescent, LED).
  • Check for glare—some colors (like bright white) can reflect too much light.

5. Think About Future Resale Value

  • Neutrals (warm white, beige, soft gray) sell faster.
  • Bold colors (navy, terracotta, mustard) can be too personal for buyers.
  • If selling soon, stick to timeless shades—but if you love color, use it in a way that’s easy to update (like an accent wall).

7. Use Paint Finishes for Functionality

  • Semi-gloss or satin (best for high-traffic areas like near the sink).
  • Eggshell or flat (great for accent walls but harder to clean).
  • Matte (trendy but not ideal for kitchens—fingerprints and smudges show easily).

8. Incorporate Color Through Accents

If you love a bold color but don’t want to commit to walls:

  • Paint an accent wall (behind the sink, dining area, or island).
  • Use color on upper cabinets while keeping lower ones neutral.
  • Add a colorful backsplash (tile or paint).

9. Consider the Size of Your Kitchen

  • Small kitchens: Stick to light, neutral colors (warm white, soft gray).
  • Medium kitchens: Medium tones work well (sage, warm gray, terracotta).
  • Large kitchens: Bold colors shine (navy, mustard, charcoal).
