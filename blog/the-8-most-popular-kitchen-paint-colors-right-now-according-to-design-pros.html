The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros (2024 Trends & Expert Tips)

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Making a Major Comeback in 2024

Kitchens have always been the heart of the home—a space where functionality meets style. But in recent years, kitchen paint colors have taken center stage, evolving from neutral backdrops to bold statements that define modern interiors. According to a 2023 report by the National Kitchen & Bath Association (NKBA), over 60% of homeowners prioritize color when remodeling their kitchens, with 72% preferring paint over other finishes for walls.

The trend isn’t just about aesthetics—it’s about psychological impact. Studies from the Journal of Environmental Psychology (2022) show that warm tones like terracotta and soft greens can reduce stress, while cool blues and grays promote focus and productivity, making them ideal for spaces where families gather and meals are prepared.

In 2024, design professionals are leaning toward timeless yet trendy hues that balance sophistication with approachability. Whether you’re renovating or simply refreshing your kitchen, choosing the right paint color can increase your home’s resale value by up to 10% (per a 2023 Zillow Home Value Index).

This guide breaks down the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors right now, backed by top interior designers, along with real-world examples, expert tips, and common mistakes to avoid. By the end, you’ll have the confidence to pick a shade that feels personal, stylish, and future-proof.

Why Kitchen Paint Colors Matter More Than Ever

Before diving into the trends, let’s explore why color selection is so critical in kitchen design:

1. First Impressions & Curb Appeal

Your kitchen is often the first space visitors see when entering your home. A well-chosen paint color can make it feel warm, inviting, and luxurious—even before they step inside.

2. Psychological & Emotional Impact

Colors influence mood and behavior:

  • Warm tones (reds, oranges, yellows) stimulate appetite and conversation.
  • Cool tones (blues, grays, greens) create a calming, hygienic feel—ideal for food prep.
  • Neutrals (beige, taupe, white) provide a versatile backdrop for bold decor.

3. Resale Value Boost

Homes with neutral, high-impact paint colors sell faster and for higher prices (per 2023 Realtor.com data). However, trendy colors (like deep emerald or burnt orange) may limit appeal to future buyers unless balanced with timeless elements.

4. Lighting & Space Perception

The right color can enhance natural light or create cozy ambiance in smaller kitchens. For example:

  • Light grays make spaces feel airy and open.
  • Deep navy adds dramatic depth in large kitchens.

5. Durability & Maintenance

Not all paint colors are equal. High-gloss or semi-gloss finishes resist stains and moisture, making them ideal for kitchen backsplashes and cabinets. Meanwhile, matte finishes hide imperfections but may show wear faster.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Designer-Approved)

Design professionals agree: 2024’s kitchen color palette blends warmth, nature-inspired tones, and modern neutrals—with a few unexpected standouts. Below, we explore the top 8 colors, their design benefits, and how to use them effectively.

1. Warm White (Benjamin Moore "White Dove" or Sherwin-Williams "Alabaster")

Why It’s Trending: Warm white is the safest, most versatile choice—it brightens spaces while adding subtle depth. Unlike stark white, warm whites have soft undertones (beige, cream, or ivory), making them feel cozy rather than sterile.

Design Pros’ Take:

  • "Warm white is the new neutral," says Sarah Richardson, ASID, a leading kitchen designer. "It pairs with everything—from dark wood cabinets to bold backsplashes—and keeps kitchens feeling fresh for years."
  • Best for: Open-concept homes, small kitchens, and spaces needing a light, airy feel.

Real-World Example: Imagine a modern farmhouse kitchen with white shaker cabinets, a quartz countertop in warm gray, and open shelving displaying vintage dishes. The walls in Benjamin Moore’s "White Dove" (a warm white with a soft beige undertone) create a clean, inviting backdrop that doesn’t overwhelm the space. The color reflects natural light, making the kitchen feel larger and more welcoming.

How to Style It: ✅ Pair with: Dark wood floors, brass hardware, and textured linens for warmth. ✅ Avoid: Cool-toned decor (e.g., icy blues) that can clash with the warmth. ✅ Pro Tip: Use two shades of white (e.g., a lighter wall color + a darker trim) for depth without heaviness.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Choosing a true white (like "Pure White") that feels sterile. ✅ Fix: Opt for warm white with undertones (test samples under morning and evening light).

2. Soft Sage Green (Sherwin-Williams "Sea Salt" or Farrow & Ball "Green Smoke")

Why It’s Trending: After years of cool grays dominating, soft sage green is making a soothing comeback. It’s nature-inspired, calming, and surprisingly modern—perfect for kitchens that blend organic and contemporary elements.

Design Pros’ Take:

  • "Green is the new gray," says Michael S. Smith, NKBA-certified designer. "It’s less sterile, adds life to spaces, and pairs beautifully with wood and metal."
  • Best for: Traditional, transitional, and farmhouse-style kitchens.

Real-World Example: Picture a coastal-inspired kitchen with **white cabinets, butcher block countertops, and a subway tile backsplash in soft gray. The walls are painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Sea Salt"—a muted sage green that softens the space without feeling overwhelming. The color complements the natural wood of the island and balances the cool tones of the tile, creating a harmonious, beachy vibe.

How to Style It: ✅ Pair with: Warm wood (oak, walnut), brass or matte black hardware, and linen or rattan accents. ✅ Avoid: Overusing dark green decor (stick to one or two accents). ✅ Pro Tip: Lighting matters—use warm LED bulbs (2700K-3000K) to enhance the green’s warmth.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Using too much green (e.g., green cabinets + green walls) which can feel claustrophobic. ✅ Fix: Keep green to walls or one accent wall, then balance with neutrals and wood.

3. Terracotta & Warm Clay (Benjamin Moore "Clay Stock" or Behr "Terracotta Clay")

Why It’s Trending: Inspired by Mediterranean and Southwestern aesthetics, terracotta and warm clay tones are back in a big way. These earthy, rustic hues add warmth and texture, making them ideal for cozy, lived-in kitchens.

Design Pros’ Take:

  • "Terracotta is bold but not overwhelming—it’s perfect for statement walls or accent kitchens," says Lisa McDonald, ASID. "It pairs beautifully with warm wood, rattan, and metallic finishes."
  • Best for: Rustic, farmhouse, and global-inspired kitchens.

Real-World Example: Consider a Mexican-inspired kitchen with **dark wood cabinets, a clay tile backsplash, and wrought-iron lighting. The walls are painted in Benjamin Moore’s "Clay Stock"—a rich, warm terracotta that ties the space together. The color contrasts beautifully with the dark wood, while copper pendants and woven baskets add textural interest. This kitchen feels inviting, cultural, and full of personality.

How to Style It: ✅ Pair with: Warm wood, rattan, copper, and natural stone. ✅ Avoid: Cool-toned metals (chrome)—opt for brass or matte black instead. ✅ Pro Tip: Use textured paint finishes (like sponge or rag rolling) to enhance the rustic, handmade feel.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Using too much terracotta (e.g., terracotta cabinets + terracotta walls) which can feel overwhelming. ✅ Fix: Limit terracotta to one wall or backsplash, then balance with neutrals and wood.

4. Deep Navy Blue (Sherwin-Williams "Naval" or Farrow & Ball "Hague Blue")

Why It’s Trending: After years of soft blues, deep navy is making a dramatic return. It’s luxurious, timeless, and surprisingly versatile—especially when paired with gold, brass, or white.

Design Pros’ Take:

  • "Navy is the new black for kitchens," says David Hinton, NKBA. "It’s bold but not dated, and it works in both modern and traditional spaces."
  • Best for: Grand kitchens, coastal homes, and statement walls.

Real-World Example: Imagine a luxury kitchen with **white cabinets, a quartz countertop in warm gray, and a subway tile backsplash in black and white. The walls are painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Naval"—a deep, rich navy that adds depth and sophistication. The contrast with the white cabinets and black accents creates a modern, high-end look, while gold fixtures and a large statement chandelier elevate the space further.

How to Style It: ✅ Pair with: White, gold, brass, and black. ✅ Avoid: Pastel or overly bright colors—navy needs strong contrasts. ✅ Pro Tip: Use navy on one wall (behind the sink or island) for impact without overwhelming.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Using navy in small kitchens without enough light (can feel claustrophobic). ✅ Fix: Maximize natural light and balance with white or light wood.

5. Warm Gray (Benjamin Moore "Revere Pewter" or Sherwin-Williams "Agreeable Gray")

Why It’s Trending: While cool gray still dominates, warm gray is gaining traction for its cozy, inviting feel. It’s less sterile than cool gray but still modern and versatile.

Design Pros’ Take:

  • "Warm gray is the sweet spot—it’s not too cool, not too warm," says Emily Henderson, ASID. "It’s perfect for transitional kitchens."
  • Best for: Modern, contemporary, and transitional styles.

Real-World Example: Picture a minimalist kitchen with **white shaker cabinets, a quartz countertop in warm gray, and matte black appliances. The walls are painted in Benjamin Moore’s "Revere Pewter"—a soft, warm gray that softens the space without feeling dull. The color complements the quartz countertop, while matte black appliances and brass fixtures add modern contrast.

How to Style It: ✅ Pair with: White, wood, brass, and matte black. ✅ Avoid: Overly cool metals (chrome)—opt for brass or matte finishes. ✅ Pro Tip: Use warm gray on cabinets or trim for depth without heaviness.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Choosing a cool gray when you want warmth. ✅ Fix: Look for beige or taupe undertones in the gray.

6. Mustard Yellow (Sherwin-Williams "Copper Canyon" or Farrow & Ball "India Yellow")

Why It’s Trending: Mustard yellow is back in a major way, inspired by vintage kitchens and global aesthetics. It’s warm, inviting, and surprisingly modern—especially when balanced with neutral tones.

Design Pros’ Take:

  • "Mustard is bold but not dated—it’s perfect for accent walls or small kitchens," says David Hinton. "It pairs beautifully with wood, brass, and black."
  • Best for: Vintage, farmhouse, and global-inspired kitchens.

Real-World Example: Imagine a retro-inspired kitchen with **white cabinets, a butcher block countertop, and black-and-white subway tile. The accent wall behind the sink is painted in Sherwin-Williams’ "Copper Canyon"—a rich, warm mustard. The color adds personality without overwhelming, while brass fixtures and a vintage-style range hood enhance the retro charm.

How to Style It: ✅ Pair with: Wood, brass, black, and white. ✅ Avoid: Overusing mustard (stick to one wall or backsplash). ✅ Pro Tip: Use **mustard on a smaller kitchen’s backsplash for impact without overwhelming.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Using mustard in a small, dark kitchen (can feel claustrophobic). ✅ Fix: Balance with white or light wood and maximize lighting.
