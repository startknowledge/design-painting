The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros (2024 Trends & Expert Tips)

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Making a Bold Comeback in 2024

Kitchens are no longer just functional spaces—they’re the heart of the home, where style meets functionality. With the rise of open-concept living and the demand for multi-purpose spaces, kitchen design has evolved into an art form. According to the 2024 Color Trends Report by the National Kitchen & Bath Association (NKBA), 68% of homeowners prioritize color and paint choices when remodeling their kitchens, up from 55% in 2022.

But why are paint colors trending so strongly? Psychology, sustainability, and personal expression play major roles. A well-chosen kitchen paint can:

  • Enhance natural light (making small spaces feel larger).
  • Set the mood (calming blues for relaxation, warm neutrals for coziness).
  • Increase home value (studies show that kitchens with bold, modern paint sell 12% faster than those with outdated schemes).
  • Reflect current design movements, such as biophilic design (nature-inspired hues) and maximalist minimalism (rich, layered tones).

In this ultimate guide, we’ll dive into the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors of 2024, as recommended by top interior designers, architects, and color consultants. We’ll break down: ✅ Why each color is trending (with expert insights) ✅ How to pair them with cabinetry, flooring, and decor ✅ Real-world examples of stunning kitchen transformations ✅ Common mistakes to avoid (and how to fix them) ✅ Actionable tips for choosing the perfect shade

Let’s get started!

1. Warm Terracotta: The Earthy Trend That’s Here to Stay

Why It’s Popular

Terracotta—a deep, rustic orange-red—has surged in popularity due to its warmth, grounding energy, and connection to nature. According to Benjamin Moore’s 2024 Color Forecast, terracotta was named one of the "Top 10 Colors for Home Interiors" because it:

  • Evokes Mediterranean and Moroccan influences, perfect for those who love global aesthetics.
  • Pairs beautifully with natural materials like wood, stone, and rattan.
  • Creates a cozy, inviting atmosphere—ideal for family kitchens.

How to Use It in Your Kitchen

Terracotta works best in small doses to avoid overwhelming the space. Here’s how to incorporate it:

Option 1: Accent Wall

  • Paint one wall behind the sink or range for a focal point.
  • Pair with light oak cabinets and white subway tile for contrast.
  • Add woven baskets, clay pots, and terracotta planters for cohesion.

Option 2: Cabinets & Trim

  • Use terracotta on upper cabinets while keeping lower cabinets white or gray.
  • Combine with matte black hardware for a modern twist.

Option 3: Full Wall Treatment

  • If you love bold statements, try a terracotta backsplash (using paintable tiles or a painted ceramic backsplash).
  • Balance with neutral flooring (like wide-plank oak) to prevent visual heaviness.

Real-World Example: The Rustic Farmhouse Kitchen

Imagine a 1920s farmhouse in New Mexico, where the original kitchen had peeling white paint and dark wood. The homeowners hired a designer who:

  • Painted the walls a warm terracotta (Benjamin Moore’s "Terracotta Pot").
  • Kept the original hardwood floors but refinished them in a natural oak stain.
  • Installed a butcher block island with live-edge wood details.
  • Added a clay tile backsplash in earthy tones. The result? A cozy, sun-drenched kitchen that feels like a retreat—perfect for hosting dinner parties under string lights.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

❌ Overusing terracotta → Sticks to one wall or upper cabinets only. ❌ Pairing with cool tones → Avoid icy blues or grays—stick to warm neutrals, wood, and brass. ❌ Ignoring lighting → Terracotta looks dull in low light; test samples in your space.

2. Deep Navy Blue: The Sophisticated Luxe Choice

Why It’s Popular

Navy blue is making a major comeback after years of being overshadowed by soft grays. According to Sherwin-Williams’ 2024 Color Study, navy was named "The Most Luxurious Kitchen Color" because it:

  • Adds depth and drama without being overwhelming.
  • Complements modern, industrial, and traditional styles.
  • Pairs effortlessly with gold, brass, and black accents.

How to Use It in Your Kitchen

Navy works in multiple ways, depending on your design style.

Option 1: Full-Wall Navy

  • Paint all four walls navy for a bold, gallery-worthy look.
  • Pair with white or light gray cabinets and matte black countertops.
  • Add mirrors, metallic fixtures, and white subway tile to keep it fresh.

Option 2: Navy Cabinets with Light Walls

  • Upper cabinets in navy with lower cabinets in white or cream.
  • Flooring in wide-plank oak or light gray tile.
  • Black or brass hardware for a sleek finish.

Option 3: Navy Accent Wall

  • Use navy behind the range or sink for a dramatic backdrop.
  • Balance with neutral countertops (like quartz in "White Dove").

Real-World Example: The Modern Industrial Kitchen

A loft apartment in Brooklyn had a dated kitchen with all-white cabinets and a linoleum floor. The designer:

  • Painted the walls a deep navy (Farrow & Ball "Hague Blue").
  • Kept the original black steel appliances (refurbished for a vintage look).
  • Installed a concrete countertop with a black marble edge.
  • Added exposed brick on one wall and industrial pendant lights. The result? A high-end, urban kitchen that feels spacious and stylish, perfect for entertaining.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

❌ Using navy in a small kitchen → Stick to one wall or upper cabinets. ❌ Pairing with too many dark elements → Balance with light wood, white, or metallic accents. ❌ Ignoring lighting → Navy can look too dark in low-light spaces; opt for satin or eggshell finish for better reflection.

3. Soft Sage Green: The Calming Biophilic Favorite

Why It’s Popular

Sage green is back in a big way, thanks to biophilic design (bringing nature indoors). According to Pantone’s 2024 Color Report, "Very Peri" (a soft teal) and "Sage Green" were among the top 10 colors for wellness-focused spaces. Why?

  • Reduces stress (studies show green walls lower blood pressure).
  • Pairs with wood, linen, and natural textures.
  • Works in both modern and farmhouse styles.

How to Use It in Your Kitchen

Sage green is versatile but best used subtly to avoid a "cottagecore overload."

Option 1: Painted Cabinets

  • Upper cabinets in sage with lower cabinets in white or cream.
  • Flooring in light oak or whitewashed wood.
  • Black or brass hardware for contrast.

Option 2: Accent Wall

  • Paint one wall behind the sink in sage.
  • Pair with white countertops and wooden butcher block island.

Option 3: Full Wall with Texture

  • Use textured paint (like a faux stucco finish) for a rustic-chic look.
  • Add woven baskets, ceramic planters, and linen curtains.

Real-World Example: The Coastal Farmhouse Kitchen

A beachfront home in Maine had a kitchen with peeling white paint and dark wood. The designer:

  • Painted the walls a soft sage (Sherwin-Williams "Sea Salt").
  • Kept the original hardwood floors but added a whitewashed rug.
  • Installed a butcher block island with a marble countertop.
  • Added open shelving with ceramic dishes and linen napkins. The result? A serene, airy kitchen that feels like a summer escape.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

❌ Using too much sage → Stick to one wall or cabinets. ❌ Pairing with dark wood → Opt for light wood, white, or metallic accents. ❌ Ignoring undertones → Some sage greens have yellow or gray undertones; test in natural light.

4. Warm Gray: The Neutral That’s Still Trending

Why It’s Popular

While gray was once the dominant kitchen color, it’s now evolving into warmer, more sophisticated shades. According to Behr’s 2024 Color Trends, "Warm Gray" (like "Agreeable Gray") is still a top choice because:

  • Neutral but not boring—adds depth without overwhelming.
  • Pairs with any color scheme (from black to terracotta).
  • Makes small kitchens feel larger.

How to Use It in Your Kitchen

Warm gray is the ultimate neutral—here’s how to style it.

Option 1: Full-Wall Warm Gray

  • Paint all walls a soft warm gray (like Benjamin Moore "Revere Pewter").
  • Cabinets in white or light wood.
  • Black or brass fixtures for contrast.

Option 2: Gray Cabinets with Light Walls

  • Upper cabinets in warm gray with lower cabinets in white.
  • Flooring in light oak or white tile.
  • Matte black countertops for a modern look.

Option 3: Gray Accent Wall

  • Use gray behind the range or island for a subtle focal point.

Real-World Example: The Modern Minimalist Kitchen

A mid-century modern home in California had a bright white kitchen that felt too sterile. The designer:

  • Painted the walls a warm gray (Sherwin-Williams "Agreeable Gray").
  • Kept the original black steel appliances but added brass hardware.
  • Installed a concrete countertop with a white marble edge.
  • Added open shelving with black ceramic dishes. The result? A clean, sophisticated kitchen that’s warm and inviting.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

❌ Using cold gray → Always choose a warm undertone (test with a light source). ❌ Pairing with too many dark elements → Balance with light wood or white. ❌ Ignoring texture → Add wood, linen, or metal to prevent a flat look.

5. Cream & Off-White: The Timeless Classic

Why It’s Popular

Cream and off-white never go out of style, but 2024’s versions are richer and more textured. According to Farrow & Ball’s 2024 Trends, "Soft White" and "Warm Cream" are top choices because:

  • Makes kitchens feel larger and brighter.
  • Pairs with any color (from navy to terracotta).
  • Gives a luxurious, high-end feel.

How to Use It in Your Kitchen

Cream and off-white are versatile but best used in modern or transitional styles.

Option 1: Full-Wall Cream

  • Paint all walls a soft cream (like Farrow & Ball "Strong White").
  • Cabinets in white or light wood.
  • Black or brass fixtures for contrast.

Option 2: Cream Cabinets with Light Walls

  • Upper cabinets in cream with lower cabinets in white.
  • Flooring in light oak or white tile.
  • Matte black countertops for a modern look.

Option 3: Textured Cream Walls

  • Use faux plaster or chalk paint for a luxurious, high-end feel.

Real-World Example: The Luxury Farmhouse Kitchen

A historic mansion in New England had peeling white paint and dark wood. The designer:

  • Painted the walls a warm cream (Benjamin Moore "White Dove").
  • Kept the original hardwood floors but added a persian rug.
  • Installed a butcher block island with a marble countertop.
  • Added open shelving with antique china and linen napkins. The result? A timeless, elegant kitchen that feels warm and inviting.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

❌ Using pure white → Opt for cream or warm off-white for depth. ❌ Pairing with dark wood → Balance with light wood or metallic accents. ❌ Ignoring lighting → Cream can look too bright in small spaces; use semi-gloss or eggshell for better light reflection.

6. Black: The Bold Statement That’s Still Going Strong

Why It’s Popular

Black kitchens are not just for the daring—they’re trending in a big way due to modern, industrial, and maximalist design. According to Paint & Paper Library’s 2024 Report, black was named "The Most Dramatic Kitchen Color" because:

  • Makes small spaces feel luxurious.
  • Pairs with gold, brass, and white for contrast.
  • Adds a modern, high-end feel.

How to Use It in Your Kitchen

Black works best in small doses to avoid feeling too heavy.

Option 1: Black Cabinets with Light Walls

  • Upper and lower cabinets in black with white or light gray walls.
  • Flooring in light oak or white tile.
  • Gold or brass fixtures for contrast.

Option 2: Black Accent Wall

  • Paint one wall behind the sink or range black.
  • Pair with white countertops and wooden elements.

Option 3: Black & White Contrast

  • Black cabinets with white walls and black countertops.
  • Brass or gold hardware for a luxurious touch.

Real-World Example: The Modern Industrial Kitchen

A loft in Chicago had a dated white kitchen. The designer:

  • Painted the walls a deep black (Farrow & Ball "Off-Black").
  • Kept the original black steel appliances but added brass fixtures.
  • Installed a concrete countertop with a black marble edge.
  • Added exposed brick and industrial pendant lights. The result? A high-end, urban kitchen that’s bold and stylish.
