The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Trending in 2024

Kitchens are no longer just functional spaces—they’re the heart of the home, where style meets functionality. With the rise of open-concept living (a trend that surged post-2020, according to the National Association of Home Builders, which reported a 30% increase in open-plan kitchen designs between 2020 and 2023), the choice of paint color has become a make-or-break decision for homeowners and designers alike.

In 2024, interior designers and color consultants are steering clear of overly bold or dated hues in favor of versatile, mood-enhancing shades that balance warmth, durability, and timeless appeal. According to a **2023 survey by the Color Marketing Group, 78% of homeowners prioritize paint color when renovating their kitchens, with neutral tones and soft pastels leading the charge.

This guide dives deep into the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors of 2024, as recommended by top designers, along with real-world examples, expert tips, and common mistakes to help you choose the perfect shade for your space.

Why Kitchen Paint Colors Matter More Than Ever

Before we explore the trending colors, let’s examine why paint selection is a critical decision in kitchen design:

  1. First Impressions & Resale Value – A well-painted kitchen can increase a home’s perceived value by up to 10% (per a 2023 Remodeling Magazine study). Neutral tones like greige (gray-beige) and warm whites are particularly effective for attracting buyers.

  2. Mood & Ambiance – Color psychology plays a subtle but powerful role in how we feel in a space. For example:

    • Soft blues promote calmness (ideal for a family kitchen).
    • Warm terracottas add energy and coziness.
    • Deep greens evoke freshness and balance.

  3. Durability & Maintenance – Kitchens endure spills, grease, and humidity, so semi-gloss or satin finishes are essential. Darker colors hide stains better, while lighter shades require frequent cleaning.

  4. Lighting & Space Perception – The right paint can make a small kitchen feel larger (light colors reflect light) or add warmth to a cold, modern space (deep jewel tones).

  5. Trend vs. Timelessness – While bold colors like emerald green or mustard yellow are making a comeback, designers warn against overdoing trends—opt for shades that can evolve with decor changes.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Backed by Design Pros)

Designers from firms like Studio McGee, Benjamin Obdyke, and Farrow & Ball have identified these top 8 kitchen paint colors for 2024, balancing trendiness, versatility, and longevity.

1. Warm White (Benjamin Moore "White Dove" or Sherwin-Williams "Pure White")

Why It’s Trending: Warm whites (with a slight beige or ivory undertone) are dominating 2024 because they soften modern spaces while keeping them bright and airy. Unlike cool whites, which can feel sterile, warm whites add subtle warmth without overwhelming a room.

Design Pro Insight: "Warm whites are the safest bet for kitchens—they work with any cabinetry, from white shaker to dark walnut, and they never go out of style," says Sarah Susanka, architect and designer.

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Nashville, Tennessee, features Benjamin Moore’s "White Dove" on the walls with distressed white cabinets and matte black appliances. The result? A clean, timeless look that feels both cozy and sophisticated.

Best For:

  • Open-concept kitchens
  • Minimalist or Scandinavian-style homes
  • Spaces needing a fresh, bright feel

How to Style It:

  • Pair with natural wood accents (butcher block counters, oak flooring).
  • Add textured elements (linen curtains, woven baskets).
  • Use metallic hardware (brass or matte black) for contrast.

2. Greige (Sherwin-Williams "Repose Gray" or Farrow & Ball "Skimming Stone")

Why It’s Trending: Greige (gray + beige) is the ultimate neutral—it’s warmer than cool gray but more sophisticated than plain beige. According to Farrow & Ball’s 2023 color report, greige was the #1 most requested shade for kitchens, offering flexibility for decades.

Design Pro Insight: *"Greige is the perfect middle ground—it’s not too warm, not too cool, and it pairs with everything from black cabinets to terracotta tiles," explains Benjamin Obdyke’s design team.

Real-World Example: A luxury kitchen in Los Angeles uses Sherwin-Williams "Repose Gray" on the walls with dark walnut cabinets and quartz countertops. The soft gray-beige tone makes the space feel spacious and elegant, while the wood cabinets add warmth.

Best For:

  • Contemporary and transitional kitchens
  • Homes with cool-toned decor
  • Spaces needing a modern yet cozy feel

How to Style It:

  • Use deep green or navy accents for contrast.
  • Incorporate textured backsplashes (subway tile, matte glass).
  • Add warm lighting (LED bulbs with a 2700K-3000K color temperature).

3. Soft Sage Green (Sherwin-Williams "Clary Sage" or Behr "Breezeway")

Why It’s Trending: After years of bold greens, designers are now favoring soft, muted sage—a calming, nature-inspired shade that elevates kitchens without overwhelming them. According to Pantone’s 2023 Color Report, earthy greens are rising in popularity due to their biophilic (nature-connected) appeal.

Design Pro Insight: *"Sage green is the perfect balance—it’s soothing like a forest but sophisticated enough for a high-end kitchen," says Studio McGee’s founder, McGee & Co.

Real-World Example: A rustic-chic kitchen in Portland, Oregon, features Sherwin-Williams "Clary Sage" on the walls with whitewashed wood cabinets and stone countertops. The soft green tone creates a serene, spa-like atmosphere, while the natural materials enhance the organic feel.

Best For:

  • Eco-friendly or farmhouse-style kitchens
  • Spaces needing a calming, stress-reducing vibe
  • Homes with wood or stone elements

How to Style It:

  • Pair with warm wood tones (oak, walnut).
  • Use metallic finishes (brass, bronze) for accents.
  • Add plants (herbs, small ferns) to enhance the green theme.

4. Terracotta (Farrow & Ball "Red Earth" or Benjamin Moore "Burnt Almond")

Why It’s Trending: Terracotta is making a bold comeback—this warm, earthy orange-red adds coziness and Mediterranean flair to kitchens. According to Architectural Digest’s 2024 Design Forecast, terracotta is the #3 most popular accent color for kitchens, especially in rural and coastal homes.

Design Pro Insight: *"Terracotta is unexpected but timeless—it’s warm, inviting, and full of character," says Farrow & Ball’s color consultant, Joaquin Chiu.

Real-World Example: A Spanish-inspired kitchen in San Diego uses Farrow & Ball "Red Earth" on the walls with white tile backsplashes and wrought-iron accents. The deep terracotta creates a vibrant, sun-drenched feel, perfect for entertaining.

Best For:

  • Mediterranean, Moroccan, or Southwestern-style kitchens
  • Spaces needing warmth and energy
  • Homes with clay or stone decor

How to Style It:

  • Pair with white or cream cabinets for contrast.
  • Use copper or brass hardware.
  • Add textured tiles (handmade clay, mosaic).

5. Deep Navy Blue (Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy" or Farrow & Ball "Hague Blue")

Why It’s Trending: While navy has been a classic for years, designers are now using it in richer, deeper tones—creating luxurious, dramatic kitchens. According to Sherwin-Williams’ 2023 Color Study, deep blues are 25% more popular in high-end renovations due to their bold yet elegant appeal.

Design Pro Insight: *"Navy is the ultimate power color—it’s sophisticated, mysterious, and timeless," says Benjamin Obdyke’s lead designer, Emily Henderson.

Real-World Example: A modern industrial kitchen in Brooklyn features Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy" on the walls with black steel appliances and matte white quartz counters. The deep blue makes the space feel moody and luxurious, while the metallic accents keep it contemporary.

Best For:

  • Modern, moody, or industrial kitchens
  • Spaces needing a dramatic yet elegant look
  • Homes with black or dark wood elements

How to Style It:

  • Pair with gold or brass finishes.
  • Use white or light gray cabinets for contrast.
  • Add warm lighting (dimmable LED strips).

6. Warm Beige (Sherwin-Williams "Accessible Beige" or Behr "Creamy Beige")

Why It’s Trending: Beige isn’t just for grandmas’ kitchens anymore—modern beige is soft, textured, and slightly warm, making it a versatile neutral. According to Paint & Paper Library’s 2024 Trends Report, warm beiges are the #2 most requested neutral after greige.

Design Pro Insight: *"Warm beige is the perfect blank canvas—it’s warm enough to feel cozy but neutral enough to change decor easily," says Studio McGee’s Sarah Richardson.

Real-World Example: A transitional kitchen in Chicago uses Sherwin-Williams "Accessible Beige" on the walls with white shaker cabinets and matte black countertops. The soft beige creates a warm, inviting space that never feels dated.

Best For:

  • Transitional or farmhouse-style kitchens
  • Spaces needing a soft, neutral backdrop
  • Homes with wood or stone decor

How to Style It:

  • Pair with dark wood or black accents.
  • Use linen or woven textiles.
  • Add metallic touches (brass, copper).

7. Soft Butter Yellow (Farrow & Ball "Setting Plaster" or Benjamin Moore "Buttermilk")

Why It’s Trending: After years of cool yellows, designers are embracing warm, creamy butter yellows—a sunny, cheerful shade that brightens kitchens without being overwhelming. According to Pantone’s 2023 report, warm yellows are back as a trendy yet timeless choice.

Design Pro Insight: *"Butter yellow is the perfect happy medium—it’s warm and inviting but not too bold," says Benjamin Obdyke’s design team.

Real-World Example: A bright, airy kitchen in Austin, Texas, features Farrow & Ball "Setting Plaster" on the walls with white cabinets and matte black appliances. The soft yellow makes the space feel cheerful and spacious, perfect for family gatherings.

Best For:

  • Bright, cheerful kitchens
  • Spaces needing a lift in energy
  • Homes with white or light wood decor

How to Style It:

  • Pair with black or dark wood accents.
  • Use textured linens (burlap, linen).
  • Add gold or brass hardware.

8. Charcoal Gray (Sherwin-Williams "Gray Cash" or Benjamin Moore "Hale Charcoal")

Why It’s Trending: While cool grays have been popular for years, charcoal gray is now the go-to for modern, high-contrast kitchens. According to Architectural Digest’s 2024 Trends, deep charcoal is 20% more popular than lighter grays due to its dramatic yet timeless appeal.

Design Pro Insight: *"Charcoal gray is the ultimate modern neutral—it’s sleek, sophisticated, and pairs with everything," says Studio McGee’s Sarah Susanka.

Real-World Example: A contemporary kitchen in New York City uses Sherwin-Williams "Gray Cash" on the walls with white cabinets and black countertops. The deep charcoal creates a luxurious, high-end look that feels both bold and refined.

Best For:

  • Modern, minimalist, or industrial kitchens
  • Spaces needing a dramatic yet clean look
  • Homes with black or white decor

How to Style It:

  • Pair with white or light gray cabinets.
  • Use brass or gold accents.
  • Add matte black appliances for contrast.

10 Actionable Tips for Choosing the Perfect Kitchen Paint Color

Now that you know the top 8 colors, here’s how to choose the right one for your space:

1. Test Paint Samples in Your Space

  • Never judge color by the store—lighting and surroundings change perception.
  • Paint large swatches (at least 12x12 inches) on different walls and observe them at different times of day.
  • Use a "paint cheat sheet" (a small card with the color name and number) to compare shades.

2. Consider Your Kitchen’s Lighting

  • North-facing kitchens (cool, natural light) benefit from warmer tones (greige, terracotta).
  • South-facing kitchens (bright, direct light) can handle darker shades (navy, charcoal).
  • Artificial lighting (LED, bulbs) should be 2700K-3000K for warmth.

3. Match Your Cabinets & Countertops

  • White cabinets? Try warm white, greige, or soft sage.
  • Dark wood cabinets? Opt for navy, charcoal, or terracotta.
  • Quartz/granite counters? **Neutral tones (beige, gray) work best
