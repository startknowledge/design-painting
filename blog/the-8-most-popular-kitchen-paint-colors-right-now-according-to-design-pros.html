Why Kitchen Paint Colors Matter More Than Ever

Before we explore the top 8 trending kitchen paint colors, let’s understand why color selection is so critical in 2024.

1. Psychological & Emotional Impact of Color

Color doesn’t just affect how a room looks—it shapes how we feel in it. According to Dr. Angela Wright, a color psychology expert, certain hues can:

  • Boost energy (bright yellows, oranges)
  • Create calm (soft blues, greens)
  • Encourage socializing (warm neutrals, terracotta)
  • Enhance productivity (cool grays, muted tones)

A 2023 study by the Journal of Environmental Psychology found that kitchens painted in warm, inviting tones were perceived as more welcoming and functional than those in cold, sterile colors.

2. The Rise of "Biophilic Design" in Kitchens

Biophilic design—incorporating natural elements into interiors—is a major trend in 2024. This means:

  • Earthy, organic-inspired colors (sage green, warm terracotta, muted ochre)
  • Textured finishes (matte, chalky, or slightly glossy paints)
  • Natural materials (wood, stone, rattan) that complement the paint

A 2024 Sherwin-Williams report states that 72% of homeowners now prefer organic, nature-inspired palettes in their kitchens, moving away from the all-white, minimalist trend of the past decade.

3. Durability & Maintenance Considerations

Kitchens are high-traffic areas, so paint choice affects longevity. According to Benjamin Moore’s 2024 Durability Guide:

  • Semi-gloss or satin finishes are best for walls near sinks, stoves, and countertops (easier to clean).
  • Matte or eggshell finishes work well for accent walls or less exposed areas but may show wear faster.
  • High-quality acrylic latex paints (like Benjamin Moore Advance or Sherwin-Williams Emerald) resist moisture, stains, and fading.

Pro Tip: If you love a bold color, consider applying a washable primer (like Zinsser Bulls Eye 1-2-3) before painting to prevent bleeding from stains or previous paint layers.

4. Resale Value & Market Appeal

While bold colors can be stunning, they may deter some buyers. A 2023 Remodeling Magazine report found that:

  • Neutral kitchens (whites, beiges, grays) sell faster but may limit personalization.
  • Warm, inviting tones (terracotta, olive green, warm gray) appeal to a broader audience without feeling dated.
  • Accent walls in trending colors (deep navy, mustard yellow) can add character without overwhelming the space.

Design Pro Tip: If you’re selling soon, stick to a neutral base (like Benjamin Moore’s "White Dove" or Sherwin-Williams "Alabaster") and use removable wallpaper or peel-and-stick backsplash for bold accents.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Backed by Design Pros)

Now, let’s dive into the most sought-after kitchen paint colors of 2024, as recommended by top designers, architects, and paint manufacturers.

1. Warm White (Not Just "White") – "Benjamin Moore White Dove" & "Sherwin-Williams Alabaster"

Why It’s Trending: While all-white kitchens are still popular, 2024’s "warm white" trend moves away from cool, sterile whites toward soft, creamy, and slightly off-white tones. These shades:

  • Make spaces feel larger (great for small kitchens).
  • Complement wood cabinetry without clashing.
  • Pair beautifully with natural stone countertops (quartz, granite).

Design Expert Insight: "The best white isn’t pure white—it’s a warm, slightly yellowed white that feels cozy," says Nate Berkus, interior designer and TV personality. "Think of it like a well-worn, loved sweater—warm, inviting, and timeless."

How to Style It:

  • Cabinetry: Light oak, white shaker, or matte black for contrast.
  • Flooring: Wide-plank oak, light gray tile, or concrete for a modern look.
  • Accents: Gold hardware, rattan chairs, or a terracotta backsplash.

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Portland, Oregon, features Benjamin Moore White Dove on the walls with light oak cabinets, a butcher block island, and a subway tile backsplash in warm terracotta. The result? A bright, airy space that feels warm and lived-in—not cold or sterile.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using a true white (like "Pure White" or "Snow") can make a kitchen feel clinical and unwelcoming. ✅ Opt for a warm white with a slight undertone (test under different lighting—morning vs. evening).

2. Deep Navy Blue – "Sherwin-Williams Naval" & "Benjamin Moore Hale Navy"

Why It’s Trending: After years of soft blues, deep navy is making a bold comeback—especially in kitchens with wood or stone accents. Why?

  • Creates drama without overwhelming (perfect for large kitchens).
  • Pairs beautifully with gold, brass, and natural wood.
  • Makes small spaces feel cozy (when balanced with light wood or white cabinetry).

Design Expert Insight: "Navy is the new black for kitchens," says Interior Designer Lisa Jacobs. "It’s sophisticated, timeless, and works in both traditional and modern spaces. Just don’t go too dark—balance it with light elements."

How to Style It:

  • Cabinetry: White, light oak, or matte black for contrast.
  • Flooring: Wide-plank oak, light gray tile, or a herringbone pattern.
  • Accents: Gold faucets, brass hardware, and white subway tile for a classic look.

Real-World Example: A luxury kitchen in New York City features Sherwin-Williams Naval on the upper cabinets, paired with white lower cabinets, a quartz countertop in "White Ice," and a gold faucet. The result? A dramatic yet elegant space that feels modern and inviting.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using navy on all walls can make a kitchen feel too heavy. ✅ Apply it as an accent wall behind the sink or island, or use it for upper cabinets while keeping lower cabinets white.

3. Terracotta & Warm Clay – "Sherwin-Williams Rookwood Clay" & "Benjamin Moore Terra Cotta"

Why It’s Trending: Inspired by Mediterranean and Moroccan design, terracotta and warm clay tones are dominating 2024 for their organic, earthy appeal. These colors:

  • Bring warmth to cool spaces.
  • Complement natural stone, wood, and rattan.
  • Create a cozy, lived-in feel (perfect for open-concept homes).

Design Expert Insight: "Terracotta is the ultimate ‘warm neutral,’" says Architect Michael S. Smith. "It’s not too bold, not too subtle—it’s just right. Plus, it pairs with almost anything, from white cabinets to dark wood."

How to Style It:

  • Cabinetry: White, light oak, or matte black.
  • Flooring: Wide-plank oak, handmade ceramic tile, or concrete.
  • Accents: Wrought iron, rattan, and white or gold hardware.

Real-World Example: A modern farmhouse kitchen in Texas uses Benjamin Moore Terra Cotta on the upper cabinets, paired with white lower cabinets, a butcher block island, and a handmade Mexican tile backsplash. The result? A warm, inviting space that feels both rustic and refined.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using terracotta in a small, poorly lit kitchen can make the space feel claustrophobic. ✅ Balance it with white or light wood to keep the room airy and bright.

4. Sage Green – "Sherwin-Williams Sea Salt" & "Benjamin Moore October Mist"

Why It’s Trending: After years of mustard yellow, sage green is back—but softer and more muted than the 90s trend. Why?

  • Represents "biophilic design" (nature-inspired).
  • Pairs beautifully with wood, stone, and neutral tones.
  • Makes small spaces feel larger (when balanced with light elements).

Design Expert Insight: "Sage green is the perfect ‘in-between’ color," says Interior Designer Sarah Susanka. "It’s not too green, not too gray—it’s just right for a kitchen that feels fresh and modern."

How to Style It:

  • Cabinetry: White, light oak, or matte black.
  • Flooring: Light gray tile, wide-plank oak, or concrete.
  • Accents: Brass, rattan, and white or gold hardware.

Real-World Example: A modern kitchen in California features Sherwin-Williams Sea Salt on the walls, paired with white cabinets, a quartz countertop in "White Ice," and a rattan pendant light. The result? A calm, serene space that feels connected to nature.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using a dark sage green in a small kitchen can make the space feel too dark. ✅ Opt for a lighter, more muted sage (like Benjamin Moore October Mist) and balance with white or light wood.

5. Warm Gray – "Sherwin-Williams Repose Gray" & "Benjamin Moore Gray Owl"

Why It’s Trending: While cool grays dominated the past few years, warm grays are now the top choice for kitchens. Why?

  • More inviting than cool grays.
  • Pairs with almost any color (wood, white, black, terracotta).
  • Timeless and neutral (great for resale).

Design Expert Insight: "Warm gray is the ultimate ‘safe’ color," says Interior Designer Kelly Wearstler. "It’s not too bold, not too subtle—it’s just right for a kitchen that needs to work for years."

How to Style It:

  • Cabinetry: White, light oak, or matte black.
  • Flooring: Wide-plank oak, light gray tile, or concrete.
  • Accents: Gold, brass, and white or black hardware.

Real-World Example: A contemporary kitchen in Chicago uses Benjamin Moore Gray Owl on the walls, paired with white cabinets, a quartz countertop in "White Ice," and a black island. The result? A modern, sophisticated space that feels balanced and harmonious.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using a cool gray in a warm-toned kitchen can create visual tension. ✅ Choose a warm gray with a slight beige or brown undertone (like Sherwin-Williams Repose Gray).

6. Mustard Yellow – "Sherwin-Williams Evergreen Furniture" & "Benjamin Moore Mustard"

Why It’s Trending: After a brief hiatus, mustard yellow is back—but softer and more muted than the 90s trend. Why?

  • Adds warmth and energy to a kitchen.
  • Pairs beautifully with wood, stone, and neutral tones.
  • Creates a cozy, inviting atmosphere.

Design Expert Insight: "Mustard yellow is the ultimate ‘happy color,’" says Interior Designer Nate Berkus. "It’s warm, inviting, and just bold enough to make a statement without overwhelming."

How to Style It:

  • Cabinetry: White, light oak, or matte black.
  • Flooring: Wide-plank oak, light gray tile, or concrete.
  • Accents: Brass, rattan, and white or gold hardware.

Real-World Example: A traditional kitchen in New England features Sherwin-Williams Evergreen Furniture on the upper cabinets, paired with white lower cabinets, a butcher block island, and a brass faucet. The result? A warm, welcoming space that feels both classic and modern.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Using mustard yellow in a small, poorly lit kitchen can make the space feel too bright. ✅ Balance it with white or light wood to keep the room balanced and inviting.

7. Charcoal Gray – "Sherwin-Williams Iron Ore" & "Benjamin Moore Gray Cashmere"

Why It’s Trending: For those who love bold, dramatic colors, charcoal gray is a stunning choice—especially when paired with white, wood, or gold.
