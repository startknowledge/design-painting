The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors Right Now, According to Design Pros (2024 Trends & Expert Tips)

Introduction: Why Kitchen Paint Colors Are Making a Bold Comeback in 2024

Kitchens are no longer just functional spaces—they’re the heart of the home, where style meets functionality. With the rise of open-concept living (a trend that surged 30% between 2020 and 2023, according to the National Association of Home Builders) and the growing demand for multi-purpose rooms, kitchen design has never been more important.

But choosing the right paint color isn’t just about aesthetics—it’s about psychology, lighting, and long-term durability. In 2024, design professionals are steering clear of overly trendy hues in favor of timeless, versatile, and mood-enhancing shades. Whether you’re renovating or simply refreshing your kitchen, selecting the perfect paint color can transform the space from dull to dazzling.

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll dive into the 8 most popular kitchen paint colors backed by top interior designers, along with real-world examples, expert tips, and common mistakes to avoid. By the end, you’ll have the confidence to pick a color that not only looks stunning but also elevates your home’s value.

Why Trust Design Pros for Kitchen Paint Colors?

Before we explore the trending shades, it’s worth understanding why professionals rely on specific color palettes for kitchens:

  1. Psychological Impact – Colors influence mood, appetite, and even productivity. A cool-toned blue can create a calming atmosphere, while a warm terracotta adds energy.
  2. Lighting Adaptability – A great kitchen paint color should look good under natural light, overhead lighting, and task lighting (like under-cabinet LEDs).
  3. Durability & Maintenance – Kitchens are high-traffic areas, so paint must resist grease splatters, heat, and humidity without fading.
  4. Resale Value – Neutral and slightly bold colors (like greige or deep navy) tend to appeal to more buyers than overly saturated or pastel shades.
  5. Trend Forecasting – Designers follow Color of the Year reports (e.g., Sherwin-Williams’ "Peace at Home" in 2023) but also consider regional preferences and cultural influences.

According to a 2023 survey by Houzz, 68% of homeowners regret their kitchen paint choice, often due to poor lighting assessment or ignoring the "paint test" phase. That’s why we’ll cover how to test colors properly and what to avoid in the next sections.

The 8 Most Popular Kitchen Paint Colors in 2024 (Backed by Design Pros)

Designers are embracing a mix of soft neutrals, deep jewel tones, and earthy accents—but not all shades work for every kitchen. Below, we break down the top 8 colors, their psychological effects, best pairings, and real-world applications.

1. Warm Greige (Sherwin-Williams "Revere Pewter") – The Ultimate Neutral

Why It’s Trending: Greige (gray + beige) has dominated for years, but warmer, more inviting tones are now preferred. Revere Pewter (#7033) is a soft, slightly warm gray-beige that feels cozy yet modern.

Psychological & Functional Benefits:

  • Creates a warm, welcoming atmosphere without overwhelming the space.
  • Works with any cabinet style—from shaker to modern—making it a versatile choice.
  • Hides dirt and imperfections better than pure white, ideal for high-traffic kitchens.

Real-World Example:

Imagine a coastal-style kitchen in a sunny, airy home near the Pacific Northwest. The walls are painted Revere Pewter, while the cabinets are light oak with matte finish. The contrast between the warm wood and cool greige creates a balanced, timeless look. Under soft pendant lighting, the space feels luxurious yet relaxed—perfect for entertaining.

Best Pairings:

  • Cabinets: Light oak, white, or dark walnut
  • Accents: Brass hardware, terracotta tiles, or black metal fixtures
  • Flooring: Wide-plank white oak or gray concrete

How to Test It:

  • Paint a small section of wall (about 2x3 feet) and observe it at different times of day.
  • If your kitchen has cool-toned lighting, opt for a slightly warmer greige (like Sherwin-Williams "Accessible Beige").

2. Deep Navy Blue (Benjamin Moore "Hale Navy") – Bold & Sophisticated

Why It’s Trending: After years of soft blues, designers are embracing rich, deep navy for a luxurious, dramatic effect. Hale Navy (#2160-10) is a moody yet elegant shade that pairs well with gold, brass, and white.

Psychological & Functional Benefits:

  • Psychologically calming—studies show blue reduces stress (per Journal of Environmental Psychology, 2021).
  • Makes small kitchens feel larger by creating a striking contrast with light countertops and cabinets.
  • Timeless appeal—navy has been a classic since the 1920s but is now getting a modern, high-end makeover.

Real-World Example:

A city loft kitchen in Manhattan features Hale Navy walls with white quartz countertops and matte black cabinets. The gold-toned faucet and pendant lights add a luxury touch, making the space feel industrial-chic. Despite the bold color, the open shelving with white dishes keeps it from feeling too heavy.

Best Pairings:

  • Cabinets: White, black, or light wood
  • Accents: Gold, brass, or black metal
  • Flooring: White marble, wide-plank wood, or gray tile

How to Test It:

  • Avoid navy if your kitchen has limited natural light—it can make the space feel claustrophobic.
  • For small kitchens, consider accent walls instead of full coverage.

3. Soft Sage Green (Sherwin-Williams "Clary Sage") – Earthy & Relaxing

Why It’s Trending: After the aesthetic era of pastel pinks and blues, muted, natural greens are back—thanks to biophilic design (a trend that grew 40% in 2023, per Interior Design Magazine). Clary Sage (#6181) is a soft, slightly warm green that feels organic and calming.

Psychological & Functional Benefits:

  • Reduces stress—green is associated with nature and renewal (per Color Psychology Today).
  • Pairs beautifully with wood and stone, making it ideal for rural or farmhouse-style kitchens.
  • Hides imperfections better than white, great for older homes with textured walls.

Real-World Example:

A modern farmhouse kitchen in rural Vermont has Clary Sage walls with distressed white shaker cabinets and butcher block countertops. The open shelving displays vintage dishes, while wrought-iron chandeliers add rustic charm. The result? A cozy, nature-inspired space that feels both lived-in and luxurious.

Best Pairings:

  • Cabinets: White, light oak, or reclaimed wood
  • Accents: Brass, copper, or natural stone
  • Flooring: Wide-plank oak, herringbone tile, or concrete

How to Test It:

  • If your kitchen has cool lighting, opt for a warmer sage (like Benjamin Moore "October Mist").
  • Avoid sage in small, windowless kitchens—it can make the space feel too enclosed.

4. Warm Terracotta (Farrow & Ball "Red Earth") – Earthy & Inviting

Why It’s Trending: Inspired by Mediterranean and Spanish influences, terracotta is making a bold comeback—but in muted, earthy tones rather than the bright reds of the past. Red Earth (#64) is a soft, warm terracotta that adds warmth and texture.

Psychological & Functional Benefits:

  • Stimulates appetite—warm tones like terracotta are linked to digestion and energy (per Color Psychology Today).
  • Adds depth to kitchens with white or light-colored cabinets.
  • Pairs well with natural materials like stone, clay, and wood.

Real-World Example:

A Spanish-inspired kitchen in a Southern California home features Red Earth walls with white ceramic tile backsplash and dark walnut cabinets. The wrought-iron range hood and terracotta pots on open shelves enhance the Mediterranean vibe. The result? A warm, sun-drenched space that feels both rustic and refined.

Best Pairings:

  • Cabinets: White, light oak, or dark wood
  • Accents: Copper, brass, or natural stone
  • Flooring: Clay tile, wide-plank wood, or concrete

How to Test It:

  • Terracotta can clash with cool-toned metals (like stainless steel), so stick to brass or copper for fixtures.
  • If your kitchen has limited natural light, consider accent walls instead of full coverage.

5. Soft White (Benjamin Moore "Chantilly Lace") – Clean & Timeless

Why It’s Trending: While all-white kitchens are still popular, warmer, slightly off-white shades are now preferred. Chantilly Lace (#OC-65) is a soft, warm white with a subtle beige undertone, making it less sterile than pure white.

Psychological & Functional Benefits:

  • Makes spaces feel larger—light colors reflect more natural light.
  • Hides grease and stains better than pure white, great for high-traffic kitchens.
  • Pairs with any color scheme, from black to pastel.

Real-World Example:

A modern Scandinavian kitchen in a Minneapolis home has Chantilly Lace walls with matte black cabinets and quartz countertops. The open shelving displays white ceramics, while black pendant lights add contrast. The result? A clean, minimalist space that feels both bright and cozy.

Best Pairings:

  • Cabinets: Black, white, or light wood
  • Accents: Brass, gold, or black metal
  • Flooring: White marble, wide-plank oak, or gray tile

How to Test It:

  • Avoid pure white if your kitchen has yellow or orange undertones—it can make the space feel dirty.
  • For small kitchens, consider textured paint (like Benjamin Moore "White Dove") to add depth.

6. Charcoal Gray (Sherwin-Williams "Iron Ore") – Modern & Moody

Why It’s Trending: After years of light gray, designers are embracing deeper, more dramatic charcoal tones. Iron Ore (#7069) is a rich, slightly warm gray that adds sophistication without being overwhelming.

Psychological & Functional Benefits:

  • Creates a luxurious, high-end look.
  • Pairs well with gold and brass for a modern, upscale feel.
  • Hides imperfections better than white, great for older homes.

Real-World Example:

A contemporary kitchen in a Chicago penthouse has Iron Ore walls with white quartz countertops and matte black cabinets. The gold-toned faucet and pendant lights add a luxury touch, while the open shelving displays blackware for contrast. The result? A dramatic yet balanced space that feels both industrial and elegant.

Best Pairings:

  • Cabinets: White, black, or light wood
  • Accents: Gold, brass, or black metal
  • Flooring: White marble, wide-plank wood, or gray tile

How to Test It:

  • Avoid charcoal in small, windowless kitchens—it can make the space feel too dark.
  • For small kitchens, consider accent walls or upper cabinets in charcoal.

7. Warm Beige (Sherwin-Williams "Accessible Beige") – Cozy & Welcoming

Why It’s Trending: After the aesthetic era of pastels, warm, neutral beiges are back—offering a soft, inviting alternative to cool greige. Accessible Beige (#7036) is a slightly warm, creamy beige that feels both modern and timeless.

Psychological & Functional Benefits:

  • Creates a warm, welcoming atmosphere.

Best Pairings:

  • Cabinets: Light oak, white, or dark wood
  • Accents: Brass, copper, or natural stone
  • Flooring: Wide-plank oak, herringbone tile, or concrete

How to Test It:

  • If your kitchen has cool lighting, opt for a warmer beige (like Benjamin Moore "White Dove").
  • Avoid beige in small, windowless kitchens—it can make the space feel too neutral.

8. Soft Pink (Benjamin Moore "First Light") – Playful & Feminine

Why It’s Trending: After the aesthetic era of pastel pinks, muted, soft pinks are making a subtle comeback—especially in modern and Scandinavian-inspired kitchens. First Light (#2102-70) is a delicate, warm pink that adds gentle warmth without being overwhelming.
