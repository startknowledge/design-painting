The Story Behind the East Lansing Mural Project: A Blueprint for Success

Why This Collaboration Worked: The Vision & Goals

The mural, titled "Growing Together: Roots of East Lansing," was not just a decorative piece—it was a symbol of unity. The East Lansing Girl Scout Troop, led by Troop Leader Sarah Thompson, sought a project that would:

  • Educate young girls about art, teamwork, and community service
  • Foster cross-generational collaboration with MSU students
  • Celebrate local culture, including Michigan’s agricultural heritage and diversity

Meanwhile, MSU’s Art Fraternity (Sigma Phi Delta), a student-led organization focused on fine arts and community engagement, saw this as an opportunity to:

  • Give back to the city of East Lansing
  • Develop leadership skills in their members
  • Create a lasting piece that would inspire future artists

Key Takeaway: The best mural projects align with the values of all participants—whether it’s education, artistry, or social impact.

Step 1: Planning – From Idea to Permits

Before a single brushstroke was made, months of planning went into ensuring the project’s success.

1. Defining the Purpose & Theme

  • The Girl Scouts wanted a positive, empowering message for young girls.
  • The Art Fraternity proposed a nature-inspired theme, tying into Michigan’s farmland and urban growth.
  • Compromise: "Growing Together"—a mural featuring roots, trees, and diverse hands holding seeds, symbolizing community growth.

2. Securing Location & Permits

  • Location: A blank wall on the side of the East Lansing Public Library, a high-traffic area.
  • Permits: The city required:
    • A temporary use permit (since the wall was public property)
    • Insurance coverage (both groups had liability insurance)
    • Environmental approval (to ensure no harm to the wall’s structure)
  • Funding: The project was partially funded by:
    • A Girl Scout troop grant ($1,500)
    • MSU’s Student Activities Fee ($2,000)
    • Local business sponsorships (a coffee shop donated materials)

Pro Tip: Always check local regulations—some cities require public input sessions before mural approval.

3. Assembling the Team

  • Girl Scouts (Ages 11-17): 12 members, divided into roles:
    • Designers (sketching initial concepts)
    • Colorists (selecting a cohesive palette)
    • Applicators (painting with guidance)
  • MSU Art Fraternity Members (20 students): Handled:
    • Structural prep (cleaning, priming, stenciling)
    • Advanced techniques (spray painting, texture work)
    • Logistics (transporting supplies, setting up scaffolding)

Lesson: Assign roles based on skill level—even young artists can contribute meaningfully with guidance.

8 Actionable Strategies for a Successful Mural Collaboration

Whether you’re working with students, community groups, or professional artists, these proven strategies will help your mural project thrive.

1. Start with a Clear Vision & Shared Goals

Problem: Without alignment, teams waste time debating direction. Solution:

  • Hold a kickoff meeting where all participants brainstorm themes, colors, and messages.
  • Use a visual mood board (digital or physical) to guide decisions.
  • Example: The East Lansing mural used Michigan’s state colors (green and white) with earthy tones to reflect nature.

Action Step: ✔ Create a shared document (Google Docs or Trello) where everyone can contribute ideas. ✔ Vote on final concepts to ensure buy-in.

2. Invest in Proper Surface Preparation

Problem: Skipping prep leads to peeling paint, uneven surfaces, and wasted effort. Solution:

  • Clean the wall thoroughly (remove dirt, mold, loose paint).
  • Apply a primer (especially important for outdoor murals).
  • Use painter’s tape for clean edges and stencils.
  • Test paint samples on a small area first.

Real-World Example: In 2021, a mural in Detroit had to be repainted after rain caused chalking because the surface wasn’t properly sealed. The lesson? Never skip the prep work.

Action Step: ✔ Hire a professional painter for prep if the wall is large or in poor condition. ✔ Use high-quality acrylic paint (better adhesion than tempera).

3. Break the Project into Manageable Phases

Problem: Trying to paint an entire mural in one day leads to burnout and mistakes. Solution: Divide the mural into sections (e.g., background, main elements, details) and assign teams to each part.

Example Breakdown for the East Lansing Mural:

Phase Team Responsible Time Allotted
Background (sky & foliage) MSU Fraternity 2 days
Main Figures (hands & roots) Girl Scouts 3 days
Textures (grass, bark) Both groups 1 day
Final Touches (shading, details) MSU Fraternity 1 day

Action Step: ✔ Schedule a timeline with buffer days for unexpected delays. ✔ Assign a "project manager" (even if it’s a volunteer) to keep things on track.

4. Use the Right Tools & Materials

Problem: Cheap or wrong tools ruin paint quality and slow progress. Solution:

  • Brushes & Rollers:
    • Angled brushes for details
    • Large rollers for backgrounds
    • Sponge brushes for texture
  • Paint:
    • Acrylic paint (waterproof, durable)
    • Spray paint (for quick coverage on large areas)
    • Sealer (for outdoor murals)
  • Safety Gear:
    • Masks (for spray painting)
    • Gloves & aprons (to protect clothing)

Common Mistake: Using house paint instead of mural-specific paint—it cracks and fades faster.

Action Step: ✔ Buy from art supply stores (not dollar stores) for better quality. ✔ Test paint on scrap material before applying to the wall.

5. Incorporate Educational Elements

Problem: Murals can feel just decorative without deeper meaning. Solution:

  • Teach techniques (e.g., blending, stenciling, spray painting).
  • Include local history or culture (e.g., Michigan’s agricultural roots).
  • Use the mural as a learning tool (e.g., discuss ecology, diversity).

Example from East Lansing:

  • The Girl Scouts researched Michigan’s farmland and incorporated symbols like corn, apples, and bees.
  • MSU students taught the Scouts about perspective in mural painting.

Action Step: ✔ Turn the mural into a lesson plan—share it with schools or community centers. ✔ Document the process (photos, videos) to create a case study for future projects.

6. Foster Teamwork & Communication

Problem: Miscommunication leads to conflicting designs or wasted effort. Solution:

  • Daily check-ins (even 15 minutes) to align on progress.
  • Assign a "design lead" (someone who ensures consistency).
  • Use visual aids (sketches, color swatches) to avoid confusion.

Real-World Example: In 2020, a mural in Portland, Oregon, had to be repainted because different artists interpreted the theme differently. The fix? A central design guide was created before starting.

Action Step: ✔ Hold a "design review" before painting begins. ✔ Use a shared whiteboard (digital or physical) for real-time updates.

7. Plan for Weather & Environmental Factors

Problem: Outdoor murals are vulnerable to rain, wind, and temperature changes. Solution:

  • Choose a covered or sheltered location if possible.
  • Use weather-resistant paint (acrylic with a UV protector).
  • Have a backup plan (e.g., move indoors if rain is forecasted).

Example from East Lansing:

  • The mural was painted on a partially covered porch of the library.
  • On rainy days, the team shifted to indoor prep work (priming, sketching).

Action Step: ✔ Check the weather forecast daily and adjust the schedule. ✔ Invest in a tarp to protect the wall if needed.

8. Celebrate & Document the Process

Problem: Many murals are forgotten after completion. Solution:

  • Host a completion ceremony (invite the community, take photos).
  • Create a social media campaign (#EastLansingMural).
  • Turn the mural into an educational resource (e.g., a school field trip).

Example Impact: The East Lansing mural became a local landmark, featured in:

  • MSU’s art department newsletter
  • East Lansing’s tourism brochures
  • A Girl Scout troop fundraiser

Action Step: ✔ Press release to local media (newspapers, blogs). ✔ Make a short documentary (even with a smartphone).

Common Mistakes in Mural Projects (And How to Avoid Them)

Even well-intentioned mural projects can go wrong. Here’s what not to do—and how to fix it.

Mistake Why It Happens How to Avoid It
Poor Planning Underestimating time, cost, or effort Create a detailed timeline and stick to it.
Wrong Paint Choice Using cheap or non-mural paint Test samples and invest in acrylic or spray paint.
No Clear Design Guide Artists interpret the theme differently Assign a design lead and approve sketches first.
Skipping Surface Prep Assuming the wall is "good enough" Clean, prime, and seal before painting.
Ignoring Weather Painting outdoors without protection Check forecasts and use weather-resistant materials.
No Community Involvement Treating the mural as just an art project Engage locals—ask for input on themes.
Overcomplicating Details Trying to do too much in one session Break into phases and focus on one element at a time.
No Backup Plan Assuming everything will go smoothly Have a contingency (extra paint, tools, weather plan).

Key Takeaway: The best mural projects are well-researched, flexible, and community-driven.
