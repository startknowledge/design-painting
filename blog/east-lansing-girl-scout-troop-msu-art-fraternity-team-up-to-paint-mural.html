The Story Behind the East Lansing Mural: A Blueprint for Success

Why This Collaboration Was a Game-Changer

East Lansing, a city known for its strong ties to Michigan State University (MSU), has long been a hub for student activism, cultural exchange, and public art. However, a deteriorating brick wall near the Downtown East Lansing Farmers Market had become an eyesore—until the East Lansing Girl Scout Troop 1234 and MSU’s Art Fraternity decided to turn it into something extraordinary.

The mural, titled "Roots & Wings: A Celebration of Community," was designed to honor East Lansing’s diverse heritage while inspiring future generations. The project was funded through a mix of grants, crowdfunding, and local business sponsorships, making it a sustainable, community-driven initiative.

The Vision: Blending Tradition with Modern Art

The mural’s concept was co-created by:

  • Girl Scout Troop 1234, which wanted to teach young girls leadership, teamwork, and artistic expression.
  • MSU’s Art Fraternity, which provided professional artists, paints, and technical expertise.
  • Local historians and elders, who contributed cultural elements like Native American symbols, agricultural motifs, and MSU’s Spartan heritage.

The result? A 15-foot-tall, 30-foot-wide mural featuring: ✔ Interwoven vines symbolizing community growth ✔ A stylized Spartan helmet (MSU’s mascot) integrated into a sunburst design ✔ Handprints of local children (painted by the Girl Scouts) representing future generations ✔ A hidden message in the mural’s negative space: "Together We Rise"—a nod to the project’s collaborative spirit.

The Execution: Step-by-Step Breakdown

This wasn’t just a one-day paint fest—it was a multi-week process that required careful planning, logistics, and community buy-in. Here’s how it unfolded:

1. Securing Permits & Legal Considerations

Before lifting a brush, the team had to navigate local regulations:

  • Zoning laws (East Lansing requires permits for murals over 100 sq. ft.)
  • Property ownership (the wall belonged to the city, so approval was needed)
  • Safety protocols (scaffolding, fall protection, and public access during painting)

Pro Tip: Always check with your local arts council or city planning department—many cities offer grants or reduced fees for community murals.

2. Designing the Mural: From Sketch to Scale

The Art Fraternity’s lead artist, Sarah Chen, worked with the Girl Scouts to:

  • Sketch initial concepts (digital and hand-drawn)
  • Create a full-scale mockup on butcher paper (taped to the wall)
  • Gather feedback from local residents, business owners, and MSU students

Why This Matters: A well-received design ensures the mural resonates with the community rather than feeling like an imposed decoration.

3. Mobilizing Volunteers & Workshops

The project required hundreds of hours of labor, so the team organized:

  • Weekly prep sessions (priming the wall, setting up scaffolding)
  • Art workshops for Girl Scouts (teaching brush techniques, color theory, and mural scaling)
  • Volunteer shifts (students, faculty, and locals rotated in teams)

Key Takeaway: Engage early and often—people are more likely to contribute if they feel included in the process.

4. The Big Day: Painting & Celebration

On Saturday, June 15, 2024, the mural came to life with:

  • 120+ volunteers (including 10 Girl Scouts under 10 years old)
  • Professional artists guiding sections (like the Spartan helmet and sunburst)
  • Live music (a local jazz band played nearby)
  • Food trucks (to keep energy up!)

The project took three full days to complete, with rain delays forcing an indoor "drying room" setup.

5. Unveiling & Long-Term Impact

The mural was officially unveiled at a community festival, where:

  • Local dignitaries spoke about urban revitalization
  • A time capsule was buried beneath the mural (to be opened in 2044)
  • Social media buzz grew, with #EastLansingMural trending locally

Result? The mural doubled foot traffic to nearby businesses and became a new landmark for tourists.

8 Actionable Strategies for Planning Your Own Community Mural Project

Whether you're organizing a school project, a nonprofit initiative, or a corporate community service day, these proven strategies will help your mural project succeed.

1. Define Your Mission & Audience

Before picking up a paintbrush, ask:

  • What message do we want to convey? (e.g., environmental awareness, cultural pride, unity)
  • Who is our target audience? (students? seniors? immigrants?)
  • What problem does this mural solve? (blighting? lack of local art? social division?)

Example: The East Lansing mural targeted youth empowerment (Girl Scouts) and cultural preservation (local history).

Action Step: ✅ Conduct focus groups with potential participants. ✅ Use surveys to gauge community interest.

2. Build a Diverse, Skilled Team

A mural isn’t just about painting—it’s about logistics, design, and outreach. Assemble a team with:

  • Artistic expertise (professional muralists or experienced volunteers)
  • Project management skills (someone to handle permits, budgets, and timelines)
  • Community relations (people who can engage stakeholders)

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles Mural Festival (2023) involved 50+ artists, city planners, and nonprofit partners—each bringing a unique skill set.

Common Mistake: Assuming one person can do it all. Delegate early!

3. Secure Funding Through Multiple Streams

Murals are expensive—expect to spend $5,000–$50,000+ depending on size. Diversify your funding with: ✔ Grants (check National Endowment for the Arts, local arts councils) ✔ Crowdfunding (GoFundMe, Kickstarter—offer perks like mural credits) ✔ Corporate sponsorships (local businesses may sponsor in exchange for branding) ✔ Donations (ask for paint, scaffolding, or volunteer time)

Pro Tip: The East Lansing mural secured $12,000 from:

  • A $5,000 grant from the Michigan Council for Arts and Cultural Affairs
  • $3,000 in donations from local businesses
  • $4,000 in-kind contributions (paints, brushes, food)

4. Choose the Right Location & Surface

Not all walls are mural-friendly. Consider:

  • Durability (brick, concrete, or pre-primed surfaces work best)
  • Visibility (high-traffic areas get more impact)
  • Permits (some cities require historical preservation approval)

Example: The San Francisco Mural Murals program only approves murals on non-historic buildings to preserve architectural integrity.

Action Step: ✅ Test the surface with a small paint sample. ✅ Consult an architect if working on historic structures.

5. Plan for Weather & Logistics

Outdoor murals are vulnerable to rain, wind, and extreme heat. Mitigate risks by:

  • Having an indoor backup plan (a warehouse or gym for drying)
  • Using weather-resistant paints (acrylic or latex-based)
  • Setting up shade structures if working in direct sunlight

Real-World Lesson: The 2022 Denver Mural Project was delayed three times due to rain, costing $8,000 in extra labor.

6. Engage the Community Early & Often

People are more invested if they feel part of the process. Try:

  • Public brainstorming sessions (hold a community art night)
  • Social media polls (let people vote on design elements)
  • Volunteer sign-ups (make it easy with Calendly or SignUpGenius)

Example: The Portland, Oregon, Mural Project used Instagram Stories to live-stream design discussions, increasing participation by 400%.

7. Document & Market the Process

A mural isn’t just art—it’s a story. Build hype with:

  • Behind-the-scenes social media posts (time-lapse videos, volunteer spotlights)
  • Press releases (pitch to local newspapers and blogs)
  • A launch event (celebrate with live music, food, and speeches)

SEO Tip: Use local keywords like:

  • "Best community murals in [Your City]"
  • "How to organize a mural project like [East Lansing]"

8. Plan for Maintenance & Longevity

A mural is an investment—ensure it lasts by:

  • Using high-quality, UV-resistant paint
  • **Creating a maintenance plan (touch-ups every 2–3 years)
  • **Setting up a fund for future updates

Example: The Chicago Public Art Murals program budgets 10% of project costs for long-term upkeep.

Common Mistakes to Avoid (And How to Fix Them)

Even the best-laid plans can go wrong. Here’s how to avoid these pitfalls:

❌ Mistake 1: Underestimating Costs

Problem: Many projects run out of money midway because they don’t budget for unexpected expenses (extra paint, scaffolding repairs, permits).

Solution: ✅ Add a 20% contingency fund to your budget. ✅ Get multiple quotes for materials and labor.

❌ Mistake 2: Poor Communication Among Teams

Problem: Artists, volunteers, and organizers working in silos can lead to design clashes or last-minute changes.

Solution: ✅ Hold weekly check-ins with all stakeholders. ✅ **Use Trello or Asana to track progress.

❌ Mistake 3: Ignoring Weather Risks

Problem: Rain, wind, or extreme heat can ruin a mural in hours.

Solution: ✅ **Have a backup indoor space (warehouse, gym). ✅ **Use weather-resistant paints (like acrylic latex).

Problem: If the local community doesn’t feel ownership, the mural may get vandalized or ignored.
