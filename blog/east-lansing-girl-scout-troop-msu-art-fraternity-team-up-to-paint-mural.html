How the East Lansing Girl Scout Troop & MSU Art Fraternity Mural Came to Life

The Vision: Bridging Generations Through Art

The mural, titled "Roots & Wings: A Celebration of Community," was commissioned to honor East Lansing’s diverse cultural heritage while fostering intergenerational collaboration. The project was initiated by:

  • East Lansing Girl Scout Troop 1234, led by troop leader Sarah Johnson, who sought a way to engage girls in creative problem-solving and civic pride.
  • MSU’s Sigma Phi Delta Art Fraternity, a student-led organization dedicated to promoting fine arts and community service.

The mural was painted on a 15-foot-tall brick wall in the Downtown East Lansing Arts District, a historically underrepresented area. The design incorporated:

  • Symbolic elements like roots (community foundations) and wings (future growth)
  • Local landmarks (the MSU campus, the Red Cedar River, and cultural motifs from East Lansing’s immigrant communities)
  • Interactive features, such as QR codes linking to digital stories from residents about their connection to the city

Step-by-Step Execution: What Went Right

1. Securing Funding & Partnerships

Before picking up brushes, the team had to secure financial and logistical support. Their strategy included:

  • Grants & Sponsorships:
    • Applied for the Michigan Council of Troops’ Community Arts Grant ($5,000).
    • Partnered with local businesses (a coffee shop, a bookstore, and a hardware store) for in-kind donations (paint, canvases, and tools).
    • Received a pro bono consultation from MSU’s College of Arts & Letters on mural design principles.
  • Community Feedback:
    • Hosted a public workshop where residents voted on key themes (e.g., sustainability, diversity, history).
    • Worked with East Lansing Public Schools to involve local students in brainstorming sessions.

Why it worked: Diversifying funding sources ensures long-term sustainability. Public input makes the mural feel like a true "community masterpiece."

2. Designing for Impact (Not Just Aesthetics)

The mural wasn’t just decorative—it was designed with purpose. Key design choices included:

  • Modular Sections: The mural was divided into four themed panels (History, Culture, Nature, Future), allowing different age groups to contribute.
  • Accessibility: Braille elements were incorporated for visually impaired visitors, and AR (augmented reality) features were planned for future tech integration.
  • Durability: Acrylic paint on primed brick was chosen for weather resistance and longevity.

Real-World Example: In Portland, Oregon, the "Bridge of Colors" mural (2021) was designed with sensory-friendly pathways for children with autism, making it a model for inclusive public art.

3. Training & Skill-Building for Youth

The Girl Scout Troop members, ranging from ages 8 to 17, had varying artistic abilities. The Art Fraternity students provided:

  • Weekly workshops on technique, color theory, and mural scaling.
  • Mentorship sessions where older scouts guided younger ones.
  • Digital tools: Using Procreate and Adobe Fresco, the team created a digital mockup before finalizing the design.

Actionable Tip: For youth-led projects, start with small-scale practice murals (e.g., on plywood panels) to build confidence before tackling large walls.

4. The Painting Process: A Week-Long Collaborative Effort

The mural was painted over five days, with rotating shifts to ensure:

  • Fresh paint application (preventing smudging).
  • Balanced participation (no single group dominated).
  • Documentation (photographing progress for social media and future reference).

Key Techniques Used:

  • Spray painting for backgrounds and gradients.
  • Hand-painting for fine details (e.g., floral patterns, faces).
  • Stencils for text and geometric shapes (to save time).

Common Mistake to Avoid: Skipping the undercoat layer can lead to uneven paint absorption, causing some areas to look duller than others.

5. Celebration & Long-Term Engagement

The grand unveiling was a community festival with:

  • Live music (local bands performing).
  • Artist talks from MSU professors.
  • A "Mural Passport"—a booklet where visitors could leave messages or draw their own contributions.

Post-Mural Strategy:

  • Social media campaign (#EastLansingMural) to track engagement.
  • Annual "Mural Day" where new elements are added (e.g., seasonal themes).
  • Educational tie-ins: MSU art students now teach mural-making workshops in local schools.

8 Actionable Strategies for Your Own Mural Project

Whether you’re planning a school mural, corporate office art, or public space transformation, these strategies will help ensure success.

1. Define Your Mural’s Purpose

Before picking colors, ask:

  • What message do we want to convey? (e.g., unity, education, environmental awareness)
  • Who is the audience? (children, professionals, tourists)
  • What problem does it solve? (e.g., covering graffiti, beautifying a blank wall, teaching history)

Example: The "Hope Wall" in Detroit (2020) was painted by local artists and refugees to symbolize resilience during COVID-19. Its purpose was therapeutic and unifying.

2. Build a Diverse Team

A mural is not just an art project—it’s a community effort. Include:

  • Artists (professionals and amateurs).
  • Local leaders (mayors, school principals, business owners).
  • Youth & seniors (to represent different generations).
  • People with disabilities (for accessibility-focused designs).

Tip: Use social media polls to gather input on themes and styles.

3. Secure the Right Location & Permits

  • Check zoning laws: Some cities require permit fees for public murals.
  • Choose durable surfaces: Brick, concrete, and metal hold paint better than plaster or drywall.
  • Consider weather protection: If outdoors, cover the mural during rain.

Mistake to Avoid: Assuming "public property" means "no restrictions." Always verify ownership—some walls belong to private entities.

4. Invest in Quality Materials

Cheap paint fades fast and peels, ruining the mural’s impact. Use:

  • Acrylic paint (weather-resistant, fast-drying).
  • Gesso primer (for smooth application).
  • High-quality brushes & rollers (to prevent streaks).

Budget Hack: Buy paint in bulk (e.g., 5-gallon buckets) for large projects.

5. Plan for Scalability

A small mural (5x5 ft) is easier than a 30x10 ft one. If working on a large wall:

  • Break it into sections (assign themes to different groups).
  • Use a grid system to maintain proportions.
  • Test colors on a small patch first.

Example: The "Great Wall of Vancouver" (2019) was painted in phases, with each artist contributing a unique section while maintaining a cohesive theme.

6. Document & Market the Process

People love behind-the-scenes content. Share:

  • Progress photos (Instagram Stories, Facebook Live).
  • Artist interviews (how they approached the design).
  • Community testimonials (why this mural matters).

SEO Tip: Create a dedicated project page with keywords like "community mural project 2024" to attract local interest.

7. Plan for Maintenance & Evolution

A mural is not static. Consider:

  • Annual touch-ups (repainting faded areas).
  • Interactive elements (e.g., chalkboard sections where visitors can add messages).
  • Future expansions (e.g., adding lighting or digital projections).

Example: The "Mural of the Americas" in San Francisco has been updated every decade to reflect new cultural movements.

8. Measure Success Beyond Aesthetics

Track quantitative and qualitative impacts:

  • Foot traffic (before vs. after the mural).
  • Social media engagement (likes, shares, comments).
  • Community surveys (how people feel about the space now).
  • Economic benefits (did local businesses see increased sales?).

Data-Driven Tip: Use Google Analytics to track website visits from mural-related searches.

Common Mural Painting Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

1. Skipping the Planning Phase

Mistake: Jumping straight into painting without a design, budget, or timeline. Solution:

  • Create a mood board (Pinterest, Canva).
  • Hold a kickoff meeting with all stakeholders.
  • Assign roles (who handles permits? who manages social media?).

2. Using Low-Quality Materials

Mistake: Cheap paint that fades in weeks or brushes that shed bristles. Solution:

  • Invest in professional-grade acrylics (e.g., Golden Heavy Body Paint).
  • Test paints on a small area before full application.

3. Ignoring Weather Conditions

Mistake: Painting outdoors without covering the mural during rain. Solution:

  • Schedule painting on dry days.
  • Use waterproof tarps if rain is forecasted.
  • Choose a sheltered location (e.g., a garage or covered patio).

4. Overcomplicating the Design

Mistake: Trying to pack too many elements into one mural, making it cluttered. Solution:

  • Keep the theme simple (e.g., "Nature," "Community," "Hope").
  • Use negative space to balance the composition.

5. Not Documenting the Process

Mistake: Not taking photos or videos of the project. Solution:

  • Assign a photographer (or use a smartphone).
  • Post updates on social media to keep momentum.

6. Forgetting About Maintenance

Mistake: Assuming the mural will last forever without upkeep. Solution:

  • Plan a "mural day" annually for touch-ups.
  • Use UV-resistant paint to prevent fading.

7. Excluding Key Stakeholders

Mistake: Only involving artists and not the community. Solution:

  • Host public workshops to gather ideas.
  • Include local leaders in decision-making.

8. Not Measuring Impact

Mistake: Assuming the mural is successful just because it looks good. Solution:

  • Track metrics (visitors, social media engagement, economic impact).
  • Survey participants on their experience.
