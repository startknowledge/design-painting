Why This Mural Project Stood Out: A Case Study in Collaboration

The East Lansing Girl Scout Troop & MSU Art Fraternity mural wasn’t just another painted wall—it was a well-structured, inclusive, and visually impactful project that engaged multiple generations. Here’s how it came together:

1. The Vision: A Mural Celebrating East Lansing’s Heritage

The mural was designed to honor the city’s agricultural roots, MSU’s legacy, and the spirit of Girl Scouts. The artists chose a sunburst motif—symbolizing growth, community, and the future—with interwoven elements like:

  • A stylized cornfield (nodding to Michigan’s farming history)
  • A book and paintbrush (representing education and art)
  • A Girl Scout badge and MSU Spartan mascot (unifying the groups)

The mural was 60 feet long and 12 feet tall, making it one of the largest community-collaborative murals in the region.

2. The Planning Phase: Logistics, Permits, and Budgeting

Before the first brushstroke, the organizers had to navigate legal, financial, and logistical challenges. Here’s how they did it:

A. Securing Location & Permits

  • Location: The mural was painted on the outer wall of the East Lansing Public Library, a high-traffic area.

8 Actionable Strategies for Organizing Your Own Community Mural Project

Whether you're leading a school mural, a business facade refresh, or a public art installation, these proven strategies will help you avoid common pitfalls and create a successful, long-lasting mural.

1. Define Your Mural’s Purpose & Audience

Before picking a design, ask:

  • What message do we want to convey? (e.g., environmental awareness, historical tribute, community pride)
  • Who is our target audience? (kids, students, families, professionals)
  • Where will it be placed? (high foot traffic? Indoor/outdoor?)

Example: The East Lansing mural was designed to educate younger Girl Scouts about MSU’s history while engaging college students in community service.

Pro Tip: Conduct a quick survey of potential viewers to gauge interest in themes.

2. Assemble a Strong Team (And Delegate Roles)

A mural project requires multiple skill sets. Assign roles like:

  • Project Manager (handles permits, budget, timeline)
  • Lead Artist (designs the mural, oversees execution)
  • Volunteer Coordinator (recruits and organizes helpers)
  • Marketing Lead (promotes the event)
  • Safety Officer (ensures proper scaffolding, fall protection)

Real-World Example: The Girl Scout Troop & MSU Fraternity team had:

  • A senior art student as lead painter
  • A Girl Scout troop leader managing logistics
  • MSU ROTC cadets handling security and setup

3. Choose the Right Location & Prepare the Surface

Not all walls are mural-friendly. Test your surface with:

  • A small paint sample (if the wall is old, it may need priming).
  • Weather considerations (outdoor murals should be painted on dry, mild days).
  • Structural integrity (ensure the wall can support scaffolding or ladders).

Common Mistake: Skipping surface prep leads to peeling paint or uneven coverage.

Solution: Use gesso primer for smooth surfaces and sandblasting for rough textures.

4. Develop a Detailed Design (With Input from All Parties)

The best murals reflect community values. Involve stakeholders in the design process:

  • Hold a workshop where participants sketch ideas.
  • Use digital tools (like Procreate or Adobe Illustrator) to create a scalable template.
  • Get feedback from neighbors, business owners, or city officials.

Example: The East Lansing mural was designed in three phases:

  1. Initial sketches by MSU art students.
  2. Community feedback session at the library.
  3. Final digital mockup shared via social media for approval.

5. Secure Funding & Sponsorships

Mural projects are expensive—don’t let budget constraints derail your vision. Try:

  • Crowdfunding (GoFundMe, Kickstarter)
  • Local business sponsorships (offer branding in exchange for donations)
  • Grant applications (check NEA, local arts councils, or corporate foundations)
  • In-kind donations (paint, scaffolding, food)

Pro Tip: Offer naming rights (e.g., "The [Business Name] Community Mural") for major sponsors.

6. Recruit & Train Volunteers

A successful mural requires skilled and enthusiastic helpers. To attract volunteers:

  • Partner with schools, colleges, and youth groups (like Girl Scouts).
  • Offer training sessions (basic brush techniques, color mixing).
  • Provide safety briefings (how to use ladders, handle scaffolding).

Real-World Example: The MSU Art Fraternity trained 20 volunteers in:

  • Color theory (how to blend shades for smooth gradients).
  • Stencil techniques (for intricate details).
  • Teamwork drills (assigning sections to different groups).

7. Plan for Weather & Contingencies

Outdoor murals are vulnerable to rain, wind, and extreme temperatures. Mitigate risks by:

  • Choosing a covered location (if possible).
  • Having a backup indoor space for bad weather.
  • Using weather-resistant paint (acrylic or latex).
  • Setting up tarps to protect the mural during breaks.

Common Mistake: Painting in direct sunlight causes paint to dry too fast, leading to cracking.

Solution: Work in shaded areas or early morning/late afternoon.

8. Promote the Mural & Celebrate the Process

A mural isn’t just an end product—it’s an event. Build hype with:

  • Social media teasers (countdown posts, behind-the-scenes clips).
  • Local press coverage (pitch to newspapers, radio stations).
  • A grand opening ceremony (invite officials, artists, and sponsors).
  • Documentation (take photos/videos for future marketing).

Example: The East Lansing mural had:

  • A Facebook Live stream of the painting process.
  • A local news segment on the project’s impact.
  • An official ribbon-cutting with city officials.

Common Mistakes in Mural Projects (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake #1: Skipping the Design Phase

Problem: Jumping straight to painting without a clear vision leads to disorganized, messy murals.

Solution:

  • Create a detailed storyboard (even if it’s hand-drawn).
  • Use grid methods for scaling (divide the mural into sections).

Mistake #2: Underestimating Costs

Problem: Budgeting only for paint and ignoring permits, scaffolding, or insurance can derail the project.

Solution:

  • Request quotes from multiple vendors (scaffolding, paint suppliers).
  • Set a 20% contingency fund for unexpected expenses.

Mistake #3: Poor Volunteer Management

Problem: Untrained volunteers can damage the mural or slow progress.

Solution:

  • Hold a pre-event workshop on techniques.
  • Assign clear roles (e.g., "You’re in charge of the sky section").

Mistake #4: Ignoring Weather Conditions

Problem: Outdoor murals crack, peel, or fade if not painted under ideal conditions.

Solution:

  • Check the forecast daily and reschedule if needed.
  • Use weather-resistant materials (acrylic paint holds up better than oil).

Mistake #5: Not Promoting the Mural

Problem: A hidden mural loses its impact—it should inspire and engage.

Solution:

  • Host an opening event with live music or speeches.
  • Create social media content (timelapse videos, artist interviews).

FAQs About Community Mural Projects

Here are five frequently asked questions about organizing a mural, with detailed answers to help you plan.

1. How much does it cost to paint a mural?

Answer: The cost varies widely based on size, materials, and labor. Here’s a rough breakdown:

  • Small mural (10x10 ft): $500–$2,000 (DIY or with volunteers).
  • Medium mural (20x20 ft): $2,000–$8,000 (professional artists + materials).
  • Large mural (50x20 ft+): $10,000–$50,000+ (permits, scaffolding, paid labor).

Pro Tip: Sponsorships and grants can cover 60–80% of costs if you apply early.

2. How do I find volunteers for a mural project?

Answer: Recruiting the right people is key to success. Try these methods:

  • Partner with schools, colleges, and youth groups (Girl Scouts, Boy Scouts, art clubs).
  • Post on community boards (Facebook groups, Nextdoor, local bulletin lists).
  • Offer training (many volunteers want to learn mural techniques).
  • Leverage social media (Instagram, TikTok—artists love sharing their work).

Example: The East Lansing mural had 30+ volunteers after the MSU Art Fraternity posted on their campus group.

3. What’s the best paint for outdoor murals?

Answer: For long-lasting durability, use:

  • Acrylic paint (water-based, quick-drying, weather-resistant).
  • Latex paint (budget-friendly, good for large areas).
  • Specialty outdoor murals paints (like Sherwin-Williams ProClassic).

Avoid:

  • Oil paint (slow-drying, not weatherproof).
  • Cheap craft paints (fade quickly in sunlight).

Pro Tip: Test paint samples on a small section first to check colorfastness.

4. How do I get city permits for a mural?

Answer: Permit requirements vary by city, but here’s a general process:

  1. Check local ordinances (some cities require public art reviews).
  2. Contact the arts commission or planning department.
  3. Submit an application (may include design plans, budget, and location details).
  4. Pay fees (typically $100–$500 depending on size).
  5. Wait for approval (can take weeks to months).
