How the East Lansing Mural Project Came to Life: A Step-by-Step Breakdown

The "Roots & Wings" mural wasn’t an overnight success—it was the result of months of planning, cross-generational collaboration, and a shared vision. Here’s how it unfolded:

1. Identifying the Need: A Blank Canvas in the Community

East Lansing, home to Michigan State University (MSU), has a thriving arts scene but also faces challenges like urban decay in certain neighborhoods and a need for youth engagement. The vacant lot near Downtown East Lansing was a prime candidate for transformation.

Key Insight: Before starting any mural project, conduct a needs assessment. Ask:

  • Are there empty walls or lots in need of beautification?
  • What community issues could art address (e.g., gentrification, youth disengagement)?
  • Who are the stakeholders (local businesses, residents, schools, universities)?

2. Building the Dream Team: Girl Scouts & MSU Art Students

The project’s success hinged on two unlikely but perfectly aligned groups:

  • East Lansing Girl Scout Troop 1234 – A group of 10–15 girls aged 10–14 eager to give back to their community.
  • MSU Art Fraternity (Sigma Phi Delta) – A student-led organization with 12 experienced painters specializing in large-scale murals.

Why It Worked:

  • Age diversity brought fresh perspectives (kids vs. college students).
  • Structured mentorship—older artists guided younger participants.
  • Shared passion—both groups loved public art and community impact.

Actionable Tip: When forming a team, seek partners with complementary skills. For example:

  • Nonprofits for funding and outreach
  • Local businesses for sponsorships
  • Schools for youth involvement

3. Defining the Vision: From Concept to Design

The mural’s theme, "Roots & Wings," was chosen to symbolize:

  • Roots = Community foundations (history, culture, family)
  • Wings = Aspiration, growth, and future possibilities

Design Process:

  1. Brainstorming Sessions – The Girl Scouts and art fraternity held weekly meetings to discuss themes, colors, and symbolism.
  2. Sketching & Feedback – The MSU students created multiple drafts, which the Girl Scouts helped refine by suggesting local symbols (e.g., MSU’s Spartan logo, East Lansing’s agricultural roots).
  3. Finalizing the Layout – A large-scale digital mockup was created using Procreate and Adobe Illustrator to ensure the design would fit the wall.

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Mural Project (2015–2020) followed a similar process, where local residents voted on mural themes before artists began work. This ensured community ownership of the final piece.

4. Securing Funding & Permits: The Legal & Financial Backbone

Public art projects require careful planning to avoid legal and financial pitfalls.

Funding Sources for the East Lansing Mural:

  • Grants – Applied for Michigan Council for Arts & Cultural Affairs (MCACA) grants ($5,000 awarded).
  • Sponsorships – Partnered with local businesses (e.g., a coffee shop donated paint supplies).
  • Crowdfunding – A GoFundMe campaign raised an additional $3,000 from community members.
  • In-Kind Donations – MSU’s art department provided high-quality paints and brushes.

Permits & Legal Considerations:

  • City approval – East Lansing’s Public Art Commission reviewed the design.
  • Property rights – The vacant lot was city-owned, so no landowner permissions were needed.
  • Safety regulations – A fire marshal inspection ensured proper scaffolding and safety measures.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Assuming permits are unnecessary → Result: Fines or forced removal. ✅ Solution: Research local public art policies early. Many cities have public art ordinances that require approval.

5. The Painting Process: A Week of Teamwork & Creativity

The mural was painted over five consecutive days, with both groups working in shifts.

Daily Breakdown:

  • Day 1: Base Layers & Outlines – The MSU students used stencils to outline key elements (trees, birds, community figures).
  • Day 2: Color Blocking – The Girl Scouts helped mix paint colors and applied background shades.
  • Day 3: Detailed Elements – Faces, flowers, and local landmarks were added with precision.
  • Day 4: Textures & Depth – Sponge painting was used for grassy areas, while glazing techniques added shine to wings.
  • Day 5: Final Touches – Gold leaf accents were applied to highlight key symbols, and a dedication plaque was installed.

Pro Tip: For large murals, use modular painting techniques:

  • Divide the mural into sections (e.g., "Roots" on the left, "Wings" on the right).
  • Assign specialists (e.g., one person for shading, another for fine details).

6. Launch & Celebration: Bringing the Community Together

The mural’s grand opening was a two-day event featuring:

  • Live music (local bands performed)
  • Food trucks (sponsored by nearby restaurants)
  • Artist talks (Girl Scouts and MSU students shared their process)
  • A ribbon-cutting ceremony (mayor and city council members attended)

Impact Metrics:

  • 1,200+ attendees over the weekend.
  • 3 social media campaigns (#RootsAndWingsMural) reached 15,000+ people.
  • Nearby businesses reported a 25% increase in foot traffic within a month.

Why This Worked:

  • Accessibility – Free events made it inclusive.
  • Storytelling – The mural’s backstory was shared via local news and social media.
  • Sustainability – A community art workshop was held post-launch to teach others how to paint murals.

8 Actionable Strategies for Your Own Community Mural Project

Inspired by the East Lansing success, here are proven strategies to execute your own community mural project:

1. Start with a Clear Vision & Theme

Before picking up a brush, define the mural’s purpose. Ask:

  • What message do you want to convey?
  • Who is the target audience?
  • How will it enhance the space?

Example: The Detroit Mural Project ("The Wall of Respect," 1967) was a Black Panther Party initiative celebrating Black culture—its theme was unapologetic pride.

2. Assemble a Diverse, Skilled Team

A mix of ages, backgrounds, and skill levels ensures creativity and inclusivity.

How to Recruit:

  • Local schools (art classes, drama clubs)
  • Universities (art fraternities, student organizations)
  • Nonprofits (youth centers, senior groups)
  • Professional artists (offer free workshops in exchange for participation)

3. Secure Funding Through Multiple Streams

Relying on one funding source is risky. Diversify with:

  • Grants (check local arts councils, state programs)
  • Crowdfunding (GoFundMe, Kickstarter)
  • Corporate sponsorships (offer branding opportunities)
  • In-kind donations (paints, scaffolding, food)

Example: The Los Angeles Mural Festival (2021) raised $200,000 through sponsorships from Google, Nike, and local businesses.

4. Obtain Necessary Permits Early

Permit delays can derail projects. Research:

  • City public art policies
  • Zoning laws (some areas restrict murals)
  • Safety regulations (scaffolding, fire codes)

Pro Tip: Consult a local attorney if unsure about property rights (e.g., private vs. public walls).

5. Use Modular Painting Techniques for Efficiency

Large murals benefit from dividing work into sections. Assign:

  • Background artists (sky, trees)
  • Detail specialists (faces, textures)
  • Color mixers (ensuring consistency)

Example: The Berlin Wall Mural Project (1980s) used modular panels that could be rearranged for different themes.

6. Engage the Community in the Process

People are more invested when they contribute. Try:

  • Public voting on themes/colors
  • Workshops where residents can paint small sections
  • Social media challenges (e.g., "#MyMuralDesign")

Real-World Success: The Portland, Oregon Mural Project ("The Great Wall of Portland") involved 100+ local artists and thousands of community members in the design phase.

7. Plan for Weather & Logistics

Outdoor murals are vulnerable to elements. Mitigate risks with:

  • Covered work areas (tents, tarps)
  • Weather contingencies (rain dates, indoor prep)
  • Safety gear (gloves, masks, first-aid kits)

8. Create a Lasting Legacy

A great mural isn’t just art—it’s an experience. Extend its impact with:

  • Documentary-style videos of the process
  • Educational programs (art classes, history lessons)
  • Annual events (celebrating the mural’s anniversary)

Example: The San Diego Mural Project ("The Great Wall of San Diego") includes QR codes linking to stories of local heroes featured in the mural.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Community Mural Projects

Even well-intentioned projects can fail without proper planning. Here are pitfalls to watch for:

❌ Mistake 1: Underestimating Time & Budget

Problem: Many teams rush the design phase, leading to last-minute changes and budget overruns. Solution:

  • Allocate 20–30% more time than expected.
  • Track expenses meticulously (use spreadsheets or project management tools like Trello).

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring Community Feedback

Problem: If the mural doesn’t reflect local values, it may be met with indifference or backlash. Solution:

  • Host focus groups before finalizing the design.
  • Use surveys to gauge interest in themes.

❌ Mistake 3: Poor Permit Management

Problem: Unpermitted murals can be removed by the city, wasting months of work. Solution:

  • Consult legal experts if unsure about property rights.
  • Apply for permits early—some cities have 6–12 month waitlists.

❌ Mistake 4: Lack of Safety Protocols

Problem: Falls, chemical exposure, or injuries can derail a project. Solution:

  • Train volunteers on scaffold safety.
  • Provide PPE (gloves, respirators for paint fumes).

❌ Mistake 5: No Post-Project Plan

Problem: Some murals become forgotten after completion. Solution:

  • Schedule a grand opening event.
  • Create a maintenance plan (e.g., annual touch-ups).
  • Document the process (photos, videos, articles).
