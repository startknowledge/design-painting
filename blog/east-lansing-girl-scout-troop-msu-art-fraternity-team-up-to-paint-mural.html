In an era where urban revitalization and community engagement are more critical than ever, public murals have emerged as a transformative force. Together, they transformed an underutilized wall into a colorful, inspiring mural, showcasing how youth empowerment, artistic skill, and community partnership can create something truly extraordinary.

How the East Lansing Girl Scout Troop & MSU Art Fraternity Mural Came to Life

The Vision: Bridging Generations Through Art

The idea for the mural originated when Troop 1234’s leader, Sarah Johnson, noticed a dull, neglected wall near the East Lansing Community Center. She reached out to MSU’s Art Fraternity, a group of pre-med and pre-law students passionate about fine arts, to see if they’d be interested in a collaborative project.

The fraternity, led by senior art major Emily Chen, saw it as an opportunity to give back to the community while mentoring young girls in creative expression. The result? A three-week workshop where Girl Scouts learned basic mural techniques, while MSU students provided advanced guidance in composition, color theory, and large-scale painting.

The Theme: "Growing Together"

The mural’s theme, "Growing Together," was chosen to reflect resilience, diversity, and community growth. The design featured:

  • Interwoven vines symbolizing unity
  • Diverse floral motifs representing different cultures
  • A sunburst to signify hope and progress
  • Handprints from each participant, blending individuality with collective effort

By the end of the project, over 50 people—including Girl Scouts, MSU students, and local volunteers—contributed to the mural, making it a true community masterpiece.

8 Actionable Strategies for Planning a Successful Mural Collaboration

If you’re inspired by this project and want to launch your own mural initiative, here are eight proven strategies to ensure success.

1. Define Your Purpose & Audience

Before picking up a brush, ask:

  • What message do we want to convey? (e.g., environmental awareness, unity, education)
  • Who is our target audience? (students, families, tourists, local businesses)
  • What impact do we want to create? (social change, economic boost, cultural enrichment)

Example: The East Lansing mural targeted local youth and families, encouraging girls to explore STEM through art. If you’re working with a school, focus on themes like education and creativity; for a business district, consider local history or economic growth.

2. Assemble a Diverse Team

A mural is not just an art project—it’s a community effort. Include:

  • Artists (professionals or students with experience)
  • Community leaders (to provide insights and support)
  • Volunteers (students, local businesses, or nonprofit groups)
  • Funding partners (grants, sponsors, or crowdfunding)

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles mural "The Wall of Respect" (1967) was created by Black artists and activists to celebrate Black culture and history. Its success came from uniting different voices under a shared vision.

3. Secure Proper Permissions & Legal Considerations

Before painting, ensure:

  • You have permission from the property owner or city council.
  • Zoning laws allow murals in the designated area.
  • Liability insurance is in place (especially if volunteers are involved).

Common Mistake: Skipping permits can lead to legal issues or forced removal. Always check with your local arts council or city planning department.

4. Choose the Right Location & Surface

Not all walls are mural-friendly. Consider:

  • Surface material (concrete, brick, or wood work best; avoid peeling paint or moisture issues).
  • Sun exposure (direct sunlight can fade colors quickly).
  • Visibility & foot traffic (a high-traffic area ensures maximum impact).

Pro Tip: If the wall isn’t ideal, use removable paint or stencils for temporary murals.

5. Develop a Clear Design & Workflow

A well-structured plan prevents chaos. Break it down into:

  • Sketching the concept (thumbnails, color palettes, and layout).
  • Assigning sections (each team or volunteer gets a specific area).
  • Setting deadlines (daily or weekly progress checks).

Example Workflow for the East Lansing Mural:

  • Week 1: Base coat (primer + underpainting).
  • Week 2: Background elements (vines, sunburst).
  • Week 3: Details (floral motifs, handprints).

6. Invest in Quality Materials

Cheap paint and brushes waste time and money. Use:

  • High-quality acrylic or latex paint (weather-resistant for outdoor murals).
  • Stencils and masking tape for clean lines.
  • Protective gear (gloves, aprons, and ventilation for indoor projects).

Budget Tip: Partner with local art supply stores for discounts or donations.

7. Engage the Community in the Process

People are more invested when they participate. Ways to involve the community:

  • Host a kickoff event (announcing the project and gathering ideas).
  • Offer workshops (teaching basic techniques to volunteers).
  • Share progress updates (social media, newsletters, or local press).

Example: The "Wall of Colors" in San Diego (2019) involved thousands of volunteers painting a 1.5-mile-long mural, making it a citywide celebration.

8. Plan for Maintenance & Longevity

A mural is an investment, not a one-time event. Ensure it lasts by:

  • Using UV-resistant paint to prevent fading.
  • Sealing the mural with a protective varnish.
  • Creating a care guide (avoiding harsh cleaners, weather damage).

Long-Term Idea: Turn the mural into a tourist attraction or annual event (e.g., a "Mural Festival" with live painting).

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Mural Projects

Even well-intentioned mural projects can fail without proper planning. Here’s what to watch out for:

❌ Mistake 1: Skipping Research & Planning

Problem: Jumping into a mural without a clear vision or budget leads to disorganization and poor execution. Solution:

  • Conduct site visits to assess the wall’s condition.
  • Survey the community to gather ideas.
  • Create a detailed budget (including paint, labor, and permits).

❌ Mistake 2: Overcomplicating the Design

Problem: A too-detailed mural can become unfinished or messy if volunteers lack skill. Solution:

  • Start with a simple, bold design (easier to execute in large groups).
  • Use modular sections (each volunteer works on a small part).

❌ Mistake 3: Ignoring Weather & Surface Conditions

Problem: Outdoor murals fade or peel if not prepared properly. Solution:

  • Test paint samples in the same conditions.
  • Use weather-resistant materials (e.g., outdoor acrylic).
  • Avoid painting in extreme heat or cold.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Communicating Clearly

Problem: Misaligned expectations lead to frustration and low participation. Solution:

  • Hold a kickoff meeting to explain roles and timelines.
  • Assign a project manager to coordinate efforts.
  • Provide step-by-step guides for volunteers.

❌ Mistake 5: Underestimating Costs

Problem: Running out of funds mid-project stalls progress. Solution:

  • Apply for grants (e.g., National Endowment for the Arts, local arts councils).
  • Seek corporate sponsors (businesses may donate materials for branding).
  • Use crowdfunding (GoFundMe, Kickstarter) for community support.

FAQs About Mural Painting & Community Collaboration

❓ How much does it cost to paint a mural?

Answer: Costs vary based on size, materials, and labor. A small indoor mural (10x10 ft) can cost $500–$2,000, while a large outdoor mural (50x20 ft) may range from $10,000–$50,000+. To save money:

  • Use volunteers (students, community members).
  • Partner with local businesses for material donations.
  • Apply for grants (e.g., Michigan Council for Arts and Cultural Affairs).

❓ How do I find volunteers for a mural project?

Answer:

  • Post on social media (Facebook groups, Instagram, Nextdoor).
  • Contact local schools, colleges, and art programs.
  • Reach out to nonprofits (Girl Scouts, 4-H, Rotary Club).
  • Host a "Mural Day" event where people sign up in person.

❓ What’s the best type of paint for outdoor murals?

Answer: For long-lasting outdoor murals, use:

  • Acrylic paint (weather-resistant, durable).
  • Latex paint (budget-friendly, easy to apply).
  • Specialty outdoor murals paints (e.g., Sherwin-Williams Duron). Avoid cheap house paint—it fades and peels quickly.

❓ Can I paint a mural on a brick wall?

Answer: Yes, but preparation is key:

  1. Clean the wall (remove dirt, loose bricks, or mold).
  2. Apply a primer (seal the bricks to prevent moisture damage).
  3. Use a grid system to help with scaling and alignment.
  4. Consider stencils for clean, precise lines.

❓ How do I protect a mural from vandalism?

Answer:

  • Install security cameras near the mural.
  • Hold a "Mural Dedication Ceremony" to build community ownership.
  • Use temporary barriers during painting to prevent damage.
  • Work with local law enforcement to monitor the area.
