8 Actionable Strategies for Commissioning Your Own Mural (Like The Levitt Does)

If you’re a developer, business owner, or property manager looking to bring a mural to your space, follow these proven strategies to ensure a successful, high-impact installation.

Strategy 1: Define Your Goals Before Commissioning

Before reaching out to artists, ask yourself:

  • What message do I want the mural to convey? (Brand identity? Social cause? Local history?)
  • Who is my target audience? (Tourists? Employees? Residents?)
  • Will this be an indoor or outdoor mural? (Outdoor requires weather-resistant materials)

Example: A co-working space in Brooklyn commissioned a mural depicting diverse remote workers collaborating, reinforcing their inclusivity and flexibility brand.

Strategy 2: Research & Shortlist Artists Carefully

  • Look beyond Instagram fame—some of the best muralists are lesser-known but highly skilled.
  • Check portfolios for consistency—do their large-scale works hold up well?
  • Read reviews & testimonials from past clients.

Where to Find Artists:

  • Mural Festivals & Competitions (e.g., Mural Arts Philadelphia, Street Art Detroit)
  • Online Platforms (ArtStation, Behance, Mural Arts’ official directory)
  • Local Art Schools & Community Centers

Strategy 3: Set a Clear Budget & Timeline

Murals can range from $5,000 to $100,000+, depending on:

  • Size of the mural (per square foot pricing is common)
  • Artist’s reputation (established names cost more)
  • Materials used (spray paint is cheaper than hand-painted acrylics)

Pro Tip: Always include a 10-15% contingency budget for unexpected costs (e.g., scaffolding, permits).

Strategy 4: Ensure Proper Permits & Legal Compliance

  • Check local zoning laws—some cities require special permits for outdoor murals.
  • Consult an architect if the mural involves structural modifications (e.g., adding scaffolding).
  • Get written approval from property owners if the mural is on leased or shared walls.

Common Pitfall: Skipping permits can lead to fines or forced removal—always verify requirements first.

Strategy 5: Collaborate with the Artist on Concept Development

A great mural starts with a strong concept. Work with the artist to:

  • Develop a theme (e.g., "The Future of Work," "Urban Nature")
  • Sketch multiple designs before finalizing
  • Consider interactive elements (e.g., QR codes linking to the artist’s story)

Example: A Los Angeles Levitt property featured a mural with hidden QR codes—when scanned, they revealed local historical facts, making the piece both decorative and educational.

Strategy 6: Plan for Installation Logistics

  • Will the mural be indoors or outdoors? (Outdoor requires UV-resistant paints)
  • Do you need scaffolding or safety equipment? (Hire professionals if needed)
  • How will the mural be protected during construction? (Cover with tarps to avoid damage)

Pro Tip: Schedule the mural during off-peak hours to minimize disruption.

Strategy 7: Market the Mural Before, During, and After Installation

  • Announce the artist & theme in advance (build anticipation).
  • Host a launch event (invite local media, influencers, and community leaders).
  • Encourage user-generated content (e.g., "Tag us in your mural photos!").

Example: A Seattle Levitt project turned its mural unveiling into a community festival, complete with live music and food trucks.

Strategy 8: Maintain & Preserve the Mural Long-Term

  • Schedule regular cleanings (avoid harsh chemicals that can damage paint).
  • Monitor for weather damage (outdoor murals may need touch-ups every 2-3 years).
  • Consider insurance in case of vandalism or accidents.

Common Mistakes When Commissioning Murals (And How to Avoid Them)

Even well-intentioned mural projects can go wrong. Here are five frequent mistakes and how to prevent them:

Mistake 1: Skipping the Artist Selection Process

Problem: Choosing an artist based only on Instagram likes or price without evaluating their technical skill and vision alignment. Solution:

  • Request a detailed portfolio (not just social media posts).
  • Ask for references from past clients.
  • Hold a shortlist interview to discuss your vision in depth.

Mistake 2: Underestimating Budget & Timeline

Problem: Assuming a mural will be cheap or quick, leading to compromised quality or rushed work. Solution:

  • Get multiple quotes and factor in hidden costs (scaffolding, permits, touch-ups).
  • Build a 20% buffer into your budget.
  • Start negotiations early—top artists book 6-12 months in advance.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Maintenance Requirements

Problem: Outdoor murals fade, crack, or get vandalized if not properly maintained. Solution:

  • Use weather-resistant paints (e.g., acrylics with UV protection).
  • Schedule annual inspections (especially in high-traffic areas).
  • Consider insurance for accidental damage or theft.

Mistake 4: Not Engaging the Community

Problem: A mural that feels like an afterthought rather than a community asset. Solution:

  • Host a public voting process for mural themes.
  • Partner with local schools or nonprofits to involve residents in the process.
  • Offer free workshops around the mural’s creation.
