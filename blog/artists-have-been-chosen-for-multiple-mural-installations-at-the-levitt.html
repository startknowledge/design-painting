How Artists Are Being Chosen for Mural Installations at Levitt: A Deep Dive into Selection, Process, and Success

Introduction: The Rise of Public Art and Its Impact on Urban Spaces

Public art has evolved from being a niche interest into a multi-billion-dollar industry, with cities worldwide investing heavily in transforming blank walls into vibrant cultural landmarks. According to a 2023 report by the National Endowment for the Arts (NEA), public art programs in the U.S. alone contributed $1.3 billion in economic activity in 2022, with 72% of Americans expressing a positive perception of public art in their communities.

One of the most influential platforms for public art today is Levitt Pawtucket City Hall, a 10,000-square-foot public art space in Rhode Island that has become a global hub for mural installations. Since its inception in 2018, Levitt has commissioned over 100 artists from 40+ countries, turning its walls into a living gallery that attracts millions of visitors annually.

How Levitt Selects Artists for Mural Installations: The Hidden Criteria

Levitt Pawtucket City Hall doesn’t just pick artists based on popularity or social media following. Instead, it follows a structured, multi-layered selection process that balances artistic vision, cultural relevance, technical skill, and community impact.

1. The Application Process: What Levitt Looks For

Before an artist is even considered, they must submit an application that includes:

  • A portfolio (digital or physical) showcasing 3-5 recent works
  • A statement of intent (why they want to contribute to Levitt)
  • A proposal (if applying for a specific theme or commission)
  • References (from galleries, past clients, or collaborators)

Key metrics Levitt evaluates:

  • Diversity of style – Levitt values innovative approaches, whether abstract, figurative, or experimental.
  • Technical proficiency – Can the artist execute large-scale work without compromising quality?
  • Cultural resonance – Does the work reflect local or global themes that resonate with visitors?
  • Community engagement – Has the artist worked with local organizations, schools, or nonprofits?

Pro Tip: Levitt often looks for artists who can bridge gaps—whether between cultures, generations, or social movements. If your work has a story to tell, it’s more likely to be noticed.

2. The Shortlisting Phase: Who Gets Noticed?

After initial applications, Levitt’s selection committee (comprising curators, artists, and community leaders) narrows down candidates based on:

A. Portfolio Strength (40% Weight)

  • Does the artist have a distinct visual language? (e.g., Shepard Fairey’s stencil work vs. JR’s large-scale photography)
  • Are the murals scalable? Levitt’s walls are massive—can the artist adapt their style for a 100+ ft² canvas?
  • Is there technical mastery? Mistakes in large-scale work are harder to fix—Levitt prioritizes artists who can plan meticulously.

B. Conceptual Depth (30% Weight)

  • Does the mural have a narrative? (e.g., Banksy’s political murals vs. Kehinde Wiley’s portrait series)
  • Is it timely and relevant? Levitt often commissions works that reflect current social issues (e.g., climate change, racial justice).
  • Does it engage the viewer? The best murals invite interpretation—they don’t just look good; they make people think.

C. Community Impact (20% Weight)

  • Has the artist worked with local communities? (e.g., co-creating murals with schools or activists)
  • Does the work elevate the neighborhood? Levitt’s murals are not just decorations—they transform public spaces.
  • Is there potential for collaboration? Artists who can partner with local organizations get a higher chance of selection.

D. Feasibility & Logistics (10% Weight)

  • Can the artist meet deadlines? Large-scale projects take weeks or months—Levitt needs reliable artists.
  • Do they have proven experience with public art? (e.g., working with cities, festivals, or corporate commissions)
  • Are they open to feedback? Levitt may request revisions—artists who adapt well are preferred.

Real-World Example: Os Gemeos (Brazil) – When Levitt commissioned them for a 2020 mural, they didn’t just paint a colorful scene—they immersed the community by involving local children in the process. Their work, "The Great Migration," became an instant hit, blending street art with cultural storytelling.

10 Actionable Strategies to Get Chosen for a Levitt Mural Commission

If you’re an artist dreaming of contributing to Levitt, you need a strategic approach. Here’s how to maximize your chances:

1. Build a Portfolio That Stands Out

Levitt doesn’t just want another mural—they want something unforgettable.

✅ Do:

  • Showcase large-scale work (even if it’s not for Levitt yet).
  • Include a mix of styles—abstract, figurative, text-based, or hybrid.
  • Document your process (time-lapses, sketches, behind-the-scenes shots).

❌ Don’t:

  • Submit only small-scale or digital work—Levitt needs to see your ability to scale.
  • Have inconsistent quality—every piece should reinforce your brand.

Example: Faith47 (USA) – Before her Levitt commission, she built a strong portfolio with large-scale murals in New York and Los Angeles, proving her technical skill and conceptual depth.

2. Research Levitt’s Past Commissions (And Mimic the Best)

Levitt has a distinct aesthetic—it’s not just about color, but about meaning.

🔍 Study:

  • Themes (e.g., identity, resilience, nature)
  • Artists’ approaches (e.g., **text-based murals like AJOONE, or **hyper-realistic portraits like Kehinde Wiley)
  • Community engagement (e.g., how artists involved locals)

📌 Pro Tip: *If Levitt has a call for submissions on a specific theme (e.g., "Climate Justice"), tailor your proposal to fit.*

3. Network with Levitt’s Team & Local Artists

Levitt doesn’t just post applications online—they actively seek talent through:

  • Artist residencies (e.g., Levitt’s "Artist in Residence" program)
  • Local art fairs & pop-ups
  • Social media engagement (following @levittpawtucket on Instagram)

💡 How to connect:

  • Attend Levitt’s events (if possible).
  • Message the team with a short, professional introduction.
  • Collaborate with local artists—Levitt often supports collective projects.

Example: Mickalene Thomas (USA) – Before her Levitt mural, she networked with local Rhode Island artists, leading to cross-pollination of ideas that strengthened her proposal.

4. Apply During Open Calls (And Follow Instructions Precisely)

Levitt rarely accepts unsolicited proposals—wait for their calls.

📢 When to apply:

  • Check their website for open submission periods (usually 1-2 times per year).
  • Set up Google Alerts for "Levitt Pawtucket City Hall artist call".

⚠️ Critical Mistake to Avoid:

  • Skipping the guidelines (e.g., wrong file format, missing references).
  • Submitting late—deadlines are strict.

Example: Shepard Fairey (USA) – While he’s a global icon, he still follows Levitt’s process—his 2021 mural, "Hope," was a direct response to their theme of resilience.

5. Develop a Proposal That Tells a Story

A strong proposal goes beyond "I’m a great artist"—it explains why Levitt should choose you.

📝 What to include:

  • A clear concept (e.g., "A mural celebrating Rhode Island’s Indigenous heritage").
  • Why it matters (e.g., "This work will educate visitors on a often-overlooked history").
  • How you’ll engage the community (e.g., "I’ll host a workshop with local teens").

🚫 What NOT to do:

  • Vague statements ("I love color").
  • No plan for execution (Levitt needs logistics).

Example: JR (France) – His Levitt mural, "Inside Out," wasn’t just a large portrait—it was a collaboration with local women, giving them voice and visibility.

6. Showcase Your Technical Skills for Large-Scale Work

Levitt’s walls are not like a canvas—they’re textured, weather-exposed, and require precision.

🎨 How to prove you’re ready:

  • Document your large-scale projects (even if not for Levitt).
  • Highlight your materials (e.g., "I use acrylic on cement—ideal for outdoor durability").
  • Mention any weatherproofing techniques you’ve used.

❌ Common Pitfall:

  • Assuming small-scale skills = large-scale success (spray paint works differently on 100 ft² walls).

Example: Invader (France) – His pixel-art murals are designed for public spaces, using durable materials that withstand rain, sun, and vandalism.

7. Leverage Social Media & Digital Presence

Levitt scouts artists online—your Instagram, website, and LinkedIn are your portfolio.

📱 Optimize your profiles:

  • Use keywords (e.g., "Public Art Artist," "Large-Scale Muralist").
  • Post behind-the-scenes content (sketches, timelapses).
  • Engage with Levitt’s content (like, comment, share).

🔍 Where to be active:

  • Instagram (Levitt follows and tags artists).
  • ArtStation/Behance (for technical portfolios).
  • LinkedIn (for professional networking).

Example: Os Gemeos – Their viral Instagram presence helped them stand out when Levitt reached out for a collaboration.

8. Collaborate with Local Organizations & Activists

Levitt loves artists who give back—community impact is a major selection factor.

🤝 How to build credibility:

  • Partner with schools, nonprofits, or cultural groups.
  • **Propose a mural with a social mission (e.g., "Mental Health Awareness").
  • Document your community work in your portfolio.

⚠️ Mistake to Avoid:

  • Overpromising—if you say you’ll work with a local group, follow through.

Example: Kehinde Wiley – His Levitt portrait series was part of a larger initiative to celebrate Black excellence in Rhode Island.

9. Prepare for the Interview (If Selected)

If Levitt shortlists you, they may invite you for a discussion.

💬 What to expect:

  • Questions about your process ("How do you plan a large mural?").
  • Feedback on your proposal ("Why this theme?").
  • Logistics ("Can you meet the deadline?").

🎯 How to impress:

  • Bring samples (sketches, material swatches).
  • Show enthusiasm for Levitt’s mission.
  • Ask insightful questions (e.g., "How can I best engage the community?").

Example: Faith47 – During her Levitt interview, she shared a video concept for her mural, which won them over.

10. Stay Persistent & Keep Improving

Getting into Levitt takes time—most artists apply multiple times before success.

🚀 Long-term strategies:

  • Keep creating—quality over quantity.
  • Seek smaller commissions first (local businesses, festivals).
  • Stay updated on Levitt’s news—they sometimes announce opportunities last-minute.

Final Thought: Banksy didn’t become famous overnight—he built a reputation through smaller, high-impact works before Levitt noticed him.

Common Mistakes Artists Make (And How to Fix Them)

Even talented artists can self-sabotage their chances. Here’s what to avoid:

1. Ignoring the Guidelines

❌ Mistake: Submitting a PDF instead of a JPEG (or missing a required document). ✅ Fix: Read every word of the application instructions.

2. Overcomplicating the Concept

❌ Mistake: Proposing a 10-page manifesto when Levitt wants a clear, visual idea. ✅ Fix: Keep it simple—one strong image + one compelling statement.

3. Not Showing Large-Scale Experience

❌ Mistake: Only submitting small canvases or digital art. ✅ Fix: Create a test mural (even on a wall at home) to prove your skills.

4. Underestimating Logistics

❌ Mistake: Saying "I’ll do it" without budget or timeline planning. ✅ Fix: Outline your materials, labor, and schedule clearly.

5. Failing to Engage with the Community

❌ Mistake: Treating the mural as "just a painting"—no local involvement. ✅ Fix: Propose a collaborative element (workshops, interviews, etc.).

6. Not Following Up

❌ Mistake: Applying and never checking emails. ✅ Fix: Send a polite follow-up after submitting.
