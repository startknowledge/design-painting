8 Actionable Strategies for Commissioning & Executing Successful Mural Installations

If you’re a developer, city planner, or artist looking to bring a mural project to life—whether for a residential complex, commercial space, or public square—here are eight proven strategies to ensure success.

1. Define the Purpose & Audience Before Selecting Artists

Not all murals are created equal. Before reaching out to artists, ask:

  • What message do we want to convey? (Cultural pride? Social commentary? Branding?)
  • Who is our target audience? (Residents? Tourists? Corporate clients?)
  • What’s the scale and location? (A small alleyway vs. a 50-foot wall)

Example: A mural in Brooklyn’s DUMBO district was commissioned to celebrate the neighborhood’s maritime history, so the artist was chosen for their seafaring-inspired, nautical-themed work. The result? A visually stunning piece that doubles as a local landmark.

Action Step: ✅ Conduct stakeholder interviews (residents, business owners, city officials). ✅ Create a mood board to guide artist selection. ✅ Define key performance indicators (KPIs)—e.g., "Increase foot traffic by 30% within 6 months."

2. Balance Local & International Talent for Maximum Impact

While local artists bring authenticity and community buy-in, international artists can elevate prestige and attract global attention.

How to strike the balance:

  • 50% local, 50% international (a common ratio in successful projects).
  • Hybrid collaborations—pair a local artist with an international one for a fresh perspective.
  • Artist residencies—invite international artists to live in the area for a few weeks to immerse themselves in the culture.

Example: The High Line in New York features murals by both local graffiti artists and international street art legends, creating a dynamic, ever-evolving visual experience.

Action Step: ✅ Research artist directories (e.g., Street Art Cities, Artnet, or local art collectives). ✅ Host an open call for submissions to discover hidden gems. ✅ Consider artist grants or sponsorships to bring in top-tier talent.

3. Prioritize Durability & Weather Resistance

A mural that fades in a year is a wasted opportunity. Durability should be a non-negotiable factor in material and technique selection.

Key considerations:

  • Paint type: Acrylic or latex-based paints (water-resistant, UV-stable).
  • Surface prep: Prime the wall to prevent peeling.
  • Sealing: Apply a clear protective coating (e.g., polyurethane or epoxy).
  • Environmental factors: If near coastal areas, use mildew-resistant paints.

Example: The Los Angeles River Revitalization Project features murals that have withstood 15+ years of sun, rain, and pollution thanks to industrial-grade, self-cleaning paints.

Action Step: ✅ Consult with art conservation experts before finalizing materials. ✅ Test small-scale samples in the actual environment before full installation. ✅ Include a maintenance clause in the contract (e.g., "Artist agrees to touch-ups every 5 years").

4. Involve the Community in the Design Process

A mural that feels imposed will fail to engage. Community input ensures ownership and longevity.

Ways to engage:

  • Public workshops where residents can vote on themes or elements.
  • Social media polls (e.g., "Should the mural feature local wildlife or historical figures?").
  • Artist-led discussions where the community shapes the narrative.

Example: The "People’s Mural" in Melbourne, Australia, was created through a crowdsourced design process, with 10,000+ residents contributing ideas before the final piece was painted.

Action Step: ✅ Use surveys (Google Forms, Typeform) to gather input. ✅ Host pop-up exhibitions where residents can see drafts. ✅ Assign a community liaison to facilitate feedback.

5. Plan for Legal & Permitting Challenges Early

Permits, zoning laws, and property rights can derail even the best mural projects. Proactive planning is key.

Common legal hurdles:

  • Easement restrictions (can the mural cover a shared wall?)
  • Historic preservation laws (some buildings require architectural approval).
  • Copyright issues (if using existing artwork or logos).
  • Liability concerns (who’s responsible if the mural damages the wall?)

Example: A mural in San Francisco’s Mission District was halted for months due to disputes over easement rights between the property owner and a neighboring business.

Action Step: ✅ Work with a real estate attorney specializing in public art law. ✅ Submit permit applications early (some cities have 6-12 month wait times). ✅ Include a legal clause in the contract stating who owns the mural (artist, property owner, or public entity).

6. Budget for More Than Just the Artwork

Many developers underestimate the true cost of a mural project. Beyond the artist’s fee, factor in:

  • Surface preparation (sanding, priming, sealing).
  • Scaffolding & safety equipment.
  • Insurance (liability coverage for the artist and crew).
  • Marketing & unveiling events.
  • Long-term maintenance.

Example: A $50,000 mural budget in Chicago ballooned to $120,000 after unexpected structural repairs were needed before painting.

Action Step: ✅ Break down costs into a detailed spreadsheet (use Mural Cost Calculator tools). ✅ Allocate 10-15% contingency fund for surprises. ✅ Get multiple quotes from contractors.

7. Create a Timeline with Buffer Time

Rushing a mural compromises quality. A realistic timeline ensures:

  • Artist availability (top talents book 6-12 months in advance).
  • Weather delays (rain, extreme heat, or cold can halt work).
  • Permit processing (some cities take months).

Example: The "Walls of Respect" mural in Chicago (1967) took three months to complete due to community input and logistical challenges.

Action Step: ✅ Use a Gantt chart to map out phases (design, permits, painting, unveiling). ✅ Schedule weekly check-ins with the artist and contractor. ✅ Plan for a 20% buffer in the timeline.

8. Market the Mural Strategically for Maximum Impact

A great mural is worthless if no one sees it. Smart marketing ensures visibility, engagement, and ROI.

Marketing strategies:

  • Social media teases (behind-the-scenes content, artist interviews).
  • Local press releases (pitch to architecture, art, and real estate blogs).
  • AR integration (some murals now have scanable QR codes leading to digital stories).
  • Partnerships with influencers (local artists, travel bloggers, architects).
  • Themed events (e.g., "Mural Night" with live music and food trucks).

Example: The "Little Italy Mural Project" in NYC saw a 300% increase in foot traffic after a TikTok challenge encouraged visitors to recreate poses in front of the art.

Action Step: ✅ Create a content calendar (3 months pre-launch, 1 month post-unveiling). ✅ Identify key influencers in the area (Instagram, YouTube, blogs). ✅ Track engagement metrics (social shares, website traffic, foot traffic).
