Designers Say These 5 Exterior Paint Colors Could Boost Your Home's Resale Value

When it comes to selling a home, first impressions matter. According to a 2020 survey by the National Association of Realtors, 95% of home buyers consider the exterior of a home to be an important factor in their purchasing decision. Furthermore, a 2019 study by Zillow found that homes with curb appeal can sell for up to 10% more than similar homes without it. One of the simplest and most effective ways to boost your home's curb appeal is with a fresh coat of exterior paint. But which colors are most likely to attract potential buyers and increase your home's resale value?

Choosing the Right Exterior Paint Color

With so many colors to choose from, selecting the right exterior paint color can be overwhelming. However, designers and real estate experts agree that certain colors can make a home more attractive to buyers and increase its resale value. Here are the top 5 exterior paint colors that could boost your home's resale value:

  • Soft Gray: A warm, soft gray is a versatile and timeless color that complements most architectural styles and surroundings.
  • Warm Beige: A warm, beige color can create a welcoming and inviting atmosphere, making a home feel cozy and inviting.
  • Creamy White: A creamy white paint color can add a touch of elegance and sophistication to a home's exterior, while also making it appear larger.
  • Light Blue: A light blue paint color can create a sense of calmness and serenity, making a home feel like a relaxing retreat.
  • Taupe: A taupe paint color can bring a sense of warmth and coziness to a home's exterior, while also complementing natural surroundings.

8 Actionable Strategies for Boosting Your Home's Resale Value with Exterior Paint

Here are 8 actionable strategies for boosting your home's resale value with exterior paint:

  1. Consider the Neighborhood: When choosing an exterior paint color, consider the colors used in your neighborhood. You want your home to blend in, but also stand out in a positive way.
  2. Think About the Style of Your Home: Different architectural styles lend themselves to different exterior paint colors. For example, a Victorian home might look best with a bold, bright color, while a modern home might look best with a sleek, neutral color.
  3. Don't Forget About the Roof and Trim: When choosing an exterior paint color, don't forget to consider the color of your roof and trim. You want these elements to complement the paint color and create a cohesive look.
  4. Use a High-Quality Paint: Using a high-quality paint can make a big difference in the appearance and durability of your exterior paint job. Look for a paint that is water-resistant and mildew-resistant.
  5. Test the Color: Before committing to a particular color, test it out on a small area of your home's exterior. This will give you a sense of how the color will look in different lighting conditions.
  6. Consider the Surroundings: When choosing an exterior paint color, consider the natural surroundings of your home. For example, if your home is surrounded by trees, a green-based color might be a good choice.
  7. Don't Be Afraid to Seek Professional Advice: If you're unsure about which exterior paint color to choose, don't be afraid to seek professional advice. A professional painter or designer can help you choose a color that will boost your home's resale value.
  8. Keep it Simple: While it can be tempting to choose a bold, bright color, it's generally best to stick with a simple, neutral color. This will make your home appeal to a wider range of buyers and increase its resale value.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Choosing an Exterior Paint Color

Here are a few common mistakes to avoid when choosing an exterior paint color:

  • Not Considering the Neighborhood: Failing to consider the colors used in your neighborhood can result in a home that sticks out in a negative way.
  • Not Thinking About the Style of Your Home: Choosing a color that doesn't complement the style of your home can make it look out of place and decrease its resale value.
  • Not Testing the Color: Failing to test the color on a small area of your home's exterior can result in a color that looks different than expected.
  • Not Using a High-Quality Paint: Using a low-quality paint can result in a paint job that fades quickly and requires frequent touch-ups.
