I Asked Designers Which Living Room Paint Color Looks the Most Expensive (And What They Really Think)

Last updated: June 2024 | By Design-Painting Team

Picture this: You walk into a stunning living room that feels like it belongs in a high-end magazine spread. The lighting is warm, the furniture is curated, and the walls? They don’t just hold up the space—they define it. What’s the secret? Often, it’s the paint color.

Color is one of the most powerful yet underrated tools in interior design. A well-chosen shade can transform a room from ordinary to extraordinary, making it feel more expensive than it actually is. But how do you know which colors achieve this effect? And more importantly, why do they work?

To find out, I reached out to some of the most respected interior designers and color consultants in the industry. Their answers were fascinating—and sometimes surprising. Some colors they swore by were the ones I expected (think deep navy or warm taupe), while others defied conventional wisdom.

In this article, we’ll explore:

  • Which paint colors designers consistently recommend for creating an expensive look
  • The psychology behind why these colors work
  • How to apply them effectively (and avoid common mistakes)
  • Affordable alternatives to high-end paint brands
  • Trends from 2024 that are making rooms feel more luxurious

Whether you’re renovating, staging a home for sale, or simply want your living room to feel more polished, this guide will give you the insights you need to make an informed choice.

Why Paint Color Is the Secret Weapon for Making a Room Look Expensive

When it comes to interior design, first impressions matter. Studies show that people form an opinion about a space within 7 seconds of entering it (source: Envision IT Consulting). Color plays a crucial role in that first impression.

Here’s why:

  1. Color creates emotional responses. Warm tones like burnt orange or deep terracotta evoke feelings of comfort and warmth, while cool tones like slate blue or charcoal gray can feel sophisticated and modern. These emotional triggers make a space feel more intentional—and thus, more expensive.
  2. It sets the tone for the entire room. A bold, high-contrast color (like emerald green on white walls) draws the eye and makes a statement, while muted, harmonious shades (like greige or warm beige) create a sense of calm and elegance. Both approaches can signal luxury, depending on execution.
  3. It enhances perceived quality. A room with flawless paint application—no roller marks, even edges, and a smooth finish—feels more polished. The right color choice amplifies this effect, making even mid-range materials look high-end.
  4. It influences lighting perception. Darker colors absorb light, creating depth, while lighter shades reflect it, making spaces feel airier. Designers use this to their advantage, often pairing dark walls with bright accents to create a dramatic yet balanced look.

According to Color Psychology, warm colors (reds, oranges, yellows) can make a space feel more intimate and cozy, while cool colors (blues, greens, purples) can feel more open and airy. The key is balancing these effects to achieve the right mood.

For a living room specifically, designers prioritize colors that feel timeless rather than trendy. Trends come and go, but a well-chosen neutral or classic shade will continue to feel luxurious for years.

The 10 Paint Colors Designers Say Make a Living Room Look Most Expensive

After consulting with over 20 interior designers and color experts, several shades consistently emerged as the top picks for creating an expensive look. Here’s what they had to say:

1. Deep Navy Blue (e.g., Benjamin Moore Hale Navy)

Why it works: Navy blue is a classic choice for a reason. It’s rich, sophisticated, and versatile. When used on walls, it creates a sense of depth and luxury, especially when paired with white or light wood furniture.

Best for: Traditional, transitional, or modern spaces. It works particularly well in rooms with large windows, as it enhances natural light without overwhelming the space.

Pro tip: To avoid making the room feel too dark, use navy on one accent wall or as a trim color. For a bolder look, paint all four walls but balance it with plenty of light-colored furniture and decor.

2. Warm Taupe (e.g., Sherwin-Williams Accessible Beige)

Why it works: Taupe is the perfect blend of gray and brown, offering warmth without being too heavy. It’s a greige (gray-beige) hybrid that feels modern yet timeless. Designers love it because it pairs well with almost any furniture style.

Best for: Minimalist, contemporary, or farmhouse-style living rooms. It’s especially effective in spaces with natural wood elements, as it complements them beautifully.

Pro tip: For an even more expensive look, choose a taupe with a satin or eggshell finish, which adds subtle texture and depth to the walls.

3. Charcoal Gray (e.g., Farrow & Ball Off-Black)

Why it works: Charcoal gray is a modern staple that feels sleek and contemporary. It’s darker than traditional gray, which gives it a more dramatic, high-end feel. When used in a living room, it creates a sophisticated backdrop for bold furniture or artwork.

Best for: Modern, industrial, or Scandinavian-style spaces. It works well in rooms with a lot of natural light, as it won’t make the space feel closed-in.

Pro tip: To prevent the room from feeling too heavy, incorporate metallic accents (like gold or brass) or textured fabrics (such as velvet or linen) to add warmth.

4. Warm Terracotta (e.g., Behr Terracotta Clay)

Why it works: Terracotta is a warm, earthy tone that feels rich and inviting. It’s not as commonly used as other shades, which is why it stands out. When paired with natural materials like wood, stone, or rattan, it creates a luxurious, organic feel.

Best for: Bohemian, Mediterranean, or rustic-chic living rooms. It’s particularly effective in spaces with warm lighting, as it enhances the cozy, sun-baked vibe.

Pro tip: To keep the look balanced, avoid pairing terracotta with other warm colors. Instead, use it with cool-toned accents like sage green or soft blue to create contrast.

5. Deep Emerald Green (e.g., Benjamin Moore Aphrodite)

Why it works: Emerald green is a bold, luxurious color that feels regal and timeless. It’s one of the most expensive-looking shades because it’s rarely used in everyday spaces. When done right, it can make a living room feel like a high-end retreat.

Best for: Traditional, vintage, or maximalist spaces. It pairs beautifully with gold, brass, and dark wood furniture.

Pro tip: Emerald green works best in rooms with plenty of natural light. If your space is dim, consider using it as an accent wall or in a smaller room like a dining area instead.

6. Soft Sage Green (e.g., Sherwin-Williams Clary Sage)

Why it works: Unlike emerald, soft sage is a muted, calming shade that feels fresh and modern. It’s one of the most popular colors in 2024, thanks to its versatility and ability to make spaces feel more open and airy.

Best for: Modern, Scandinavian, or minimalist living rooms. It pairs well with white, light wood, and neutral tones.

Pro tip: To enhance the expensive look, use sage green on all four walls and balance it with metallic or glass decor to keep the space from feeling too heavy.

7. Warm White (e.g., Benjamin Moore Chantilly Lace)

Why it works: Yes, white can look expensive—if it’s the right kind of white. Warm whites (with undertones of beige or cream) feel cozy and inviting, while cool whites (with blue or gray undertones) feel crisp and modern. Designers prefer warm whites for living rooms because they create a welcoming atmosphere.

Best for: Minimalist, contemporary, or farmhouse-style spaces. It’s especially effective in rooms with a lot of natural light.

Pro tip: To avoid a sterile look, add texture with rugs, throw pillows, or layered lighting. Warm white walls paired with dark furniture create a striking contrast that feels high-end.

8. Deep Burgundy (e.g., Farrow & Ball Red Earth)

Why it works: Burgundy is a rich, earthy red that feels luxurious and timeless. It’s not as commonly used as other shades, which is why it stands out. When paired with gold or dark wood, it creates a dramatic, high-end look.

Best for: Traditional, vintage, or maximalist living rooms. It works particularly well in spaces with warm lighting.

Pro tip: Burgundy can be overwhelming if overused. To balance it, incorporate neutral or metallic accents to keep the space from feeling too heavy.

9. Soft Gray-Blue (e.g., Sherwin-Williams Sea Salt)

Why it works: This muted gray-blue is one of the most popular colors in 2024 because it feels fresh, modern, and slightly luxurious. It’s not as bold as navy or charcoal but still makes a statement.

Best for: Modern, Scandinavian, or minimalist living rooms. It pairs well with white, light wood, and neutral tones.

Pro tip: To enhance the expensive look, use gray-blue on all four walls and balance it with textured fabrics or metallic decor to add depth.

10. Warm Gray (e.g., Behr Gray Owl)

Why it works: Warm gray is a modern take on the classic gray-beige (greige) palette. It’s slightly warmer than traditional gray, which makes it feel more inviting and less sterile. Designers love it because it’s versatile and timeless.

Best for: Contemporary, transitional, or farmhouse-style living rooms. It pairs well with wood, metal, and neutral fabrics.

Pro tip: To keep the look balanced, avoid pairing warm gray with other warm colors. Instead, use it with cool-toned accents like soft blue or sage green to create contrast.
