Common Mistakes to Avoid at the Visual Art and Design Showcase

Even the most enthusiastic attendees can fall into pitfalls that detract from their experience. Here are five common mistakes—and how to avoid them.

1. Overwhelm from Too Many Choices

With hundreds of artworks, speakers, and workshops, it’s easy to feel paralyzed by indecision. Solution:

  • Set a daily agenda—limit yourself to 3-5 must-see sessions per day.
  • Use a map or app to navigate efficiently and avoid wasted time.
  • Accept that you can’t see everything—focus on quality over quantity.

2. Ignoring the Smaller Exhibits

Many attendees focus only on the "big names" and miss hidden talents. Solution:

  • Explore lesser-known galleries—they often feature innovative, unconventional work.
  • Talk to artists directly—they may offer exclusive insights not found in formal talks.
  • Look for "artist talks" in less crowded areas—these can be more intimate and revealing.

3. Not Engaging with Other Attendees

Some people stay in their own bubble, missing out on valuable connections. Solution:

  • Introduce yourself to strangers—you never know who you’ll meet.
  • Join group discussions—even if it’s just sharing opinions on an artwork.
  • Attend networking events—these are designed for connection.

4. Taking Too Many Photos (and Not Enough Notes)

While documenting the event is important, over-relying on photos means you miss key insights. Solution:

  • Use your phone for quick references but take detailed notes during talks.
  • Sketch ideas—sometimes drawing by hand helps solidify learning.
  • Limit photo-taking during workshops—participate fully instead.

5. Not Following Up After the Event

The networking and inspiration from the showcase won’t last forever. Solution:

  • Send thank-you emails to speakers, artists, and new connections.
  • Join the event’s online community to continue discussions.
  • Apply what you learned—start a new project or skill inspired by the event.
