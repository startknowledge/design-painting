Common Mistakes in Temporary Cultural Installations (And How to Avoid Them)

Hosting a large-scale event like ADN FÓRUM’s pink wave is exciting but risky. Here are five common pitfalls and how to prevent them:

1. Overlooking Logistics

Mistake: Underestimating crowd control, transportation, or security. Solution:

  • Conduct a dry run with staff and volunteers.
  • Hire professional event managers for large gatherings.
  • Have an emergency plan (e.g., medical stations, evacuation routes).

Example: The 2019 Coachella disaster (crowd crush) led to stricter capacity limits and better crowd management.

2. Ignoring Accessibility

Mistake: Designing spaces that exclude people with disabilities. Solution:

  • Ensure ramps, elevators, and accessible restrooms.
  • Provide sign language interpreters for performances.
  • Use clear, high-contrast signage for visually impaired attendees.

Example: The 2022 London Design Biennale was fully accessible, earning praise for its inclusive design.

3. Failing to Engage the Local Community

Mistake: Treating the event as a one-way spectacle rather than a two-way exchange. Solution:

  • Host pre-event workshops to gather community input.
  • Feature local artists and businesses prominently.
  • Offer free or discounted tickets to residents.

Example: The "People’s Republic of Stokes Croft" (Bristol) is a year-round festival where locals co-create events, ensuring long-term engagement.

4. Neglecting Sustainability

Mistake: Using disposable materials or high-energy setups. Solution:

  • Choose reusable or biodegradable decor.
  • Use solar or kinetic energy for lighting.
  • Partner with eco-conscious vendors.

Example: The 2020 Venice Biennale used 100% recycled materials for its sustainable pavilion.

5. Poor Post-Event Follow-Up

Mistake: Disbanding the team and losing momentum after the event ends. Solution:

  • Host a "thank-you" event for attendees and partners.
  • Share data and insights (e.g., "Here’s what worked—and what didn’t").
  • Plan a sequel or related project to keep the conversation going.

Example: The "Tate Exchange" (London) runs multiple pop-up events per year, keeping its community engaged year-round**.
