"‘I Am Very Decisive’: Designer Jennifer Gilbert on What She Collects and Why"

A Deep Dive into the Mind of a Visionary Collector and the Art of Intentional Acquisition

Introduction: The Rise of Intentional Collecting in the Design World

In an era where 73% of millennials and Gen Z consumers prioritize meaningful purchases over fleeting trends (McKinsey & Company, 2023), the act of collecting has evolved from mere accumulation into a deliberate, emotionally charged practice. Among the most compelling voices in this shift is Jennifer Gilbert, a renowned designer whose collections—whether of vintage furniture, rare textiles, or avant-garde ceramics—reflect a philosophy of intentionality. Her approach isn’t just about aesthetics; it’s about storytelling, sustainability, and the psychology of ownership.

This post explores why Jennifer Gilbert’s decisiveness in collecting matters, how her methods can inspire designers and collectors alike, and actionable strategies to build your own curated collection with purpose. Whether you’re a designer, an interior enthusiast, or someone seeking to refine their taste, this guide will help you collect like a connoisseur—not just for the sake of owning, but for the meaning behind it.

Who Is Jennifer Gilbert? The Designer Behind the Decisions

Jennifer Gilbert isn’t just a name in design; she’s a pioneer in intentional collecting. With a background in industrial design and curatorial studies, she has spent over two decades acquiring, preserving, and reimagining objects that carry historical, cultural, or artistic significance. Her collections span:

  • Mid-century modern furniture (with a focus on Scandinavian and Italian designers)
  • Vintage textiles (particularly those with hand-dyed or handwoven techniques)
  • Functional ceramics (from Studio Ceramics to contemporary studio potters)
  • Rare architectural models (representing lost or underappreciated designs)

What sets Gilbert apart isn’t just her discerning eye but her unwavering decisiveness. She doesn’t collect impulsively—she researches, negotiates, and commits with confidence. This approach has made her collections highly sought-after, not just for their rarity, but for the intentionality behind them.

Why Decisiveness Matters in Collecting

Decisiveness in collecting isn’t about speed—it’s about clarity. Gilbert’s philosophy hinges on three key principles:

  1. Alignment with Values – Every piece she acquires must resonate with her ethical, aesthetic, or emotional values.
  2. Long-Term Vision – She doesn’t chase trends; she invests in timelessness.
  3. Emotional Connection – The best collections tell a story, and Gilbert ensures hers do.

Why does this matter for you? If you’re collecting for design inspiration, investment, or personal fulfillment, Gilbert’s methods can help you avoid common pitfalls—like overpaying for trends or accumulating clutter without meaning.

10 Actionable Strategies to Collect Like Jennifer Gilbert

1. Define Your "Why" Before You Start

Gilbert’s first rule: Know your purpose. Is your collection for:

  • Aesthetic inspiration (e.g., designing interiors)?
  • Investment (e.g., appreciating in value)?
  • Emotional resonance (e.g., connecting with a cultural heritage)?

Example: If you’re a designer like Gilbert, your collection should inform your work. She often sources unconventional materials (like reclaimed wood or recycled metals) to inspire sustainable design projects.

Action Step:

  • Write a mission statement for your collection (e.g., "I collect mid-century Danish furniture to honor the legacy of modernist craftsmanship.")

2. Research Deeply Before Purchasing

Gilbert never buys sight unseen. She:

  • Studies the maker’s history (e.g., Hans J. Wegner’s chair designs).
  • Examines provenance (where the piece has been and why it’s significant).
  • Consults experts (museum curators, auctioneers, or fellow collectors).

Real-World Example: When Gilbert acquired a 1950s Danish teak chair by Børge Mogensen, she spent three months tracing its original owner—a Danish architect who had commissioned it for a private residence. This story behind the piece made it irreplaceable to her.

Action Step:

  • Before buying, document the piece’s history (ask sellers for photos, receipts, or expert appraisals).

3. Set a Budget—and Stick to It

Gilbert’s collections are curated, not hoarded. She never overspends because she knows:

  • Quality over quantity leads to deeper satisfaction.
  • Impulse buys often lead to regret.

Common Mistake: Many collectors fall into the "I’ll figure it out later" trap, leading to financial strain or clutter.

Action Step:

  • Allocate 10-20% of your design budget to collecting (if applicable).
  • Use the "10-day rule"—if you don’t buy within a week, reconsider.

4. Prioritize Rarity Over Trendiness

Gilbert avoids mass-produced, trend-driven pieces. Instead, she seeks:

  • Limited editions (e.g., a single prototype by Eero Saarinen).
  • Regional specialties (e.g., Moroccan zellige tiles, Japanese lacquerware).
  • Pieces with a "story" (e.g., a chair used in a famous film set).

Real-World Example: She once acquired a 1930s French Art Deco clock that had been restored by its original owner—a clockmaker who had hand-painted the dial. The personal touch made it priceless to her.

Action Step:

  • Ask: "Does this piece stand out, or is it just another version of something else?"

5. Build Relationships with Trusted Sellers

Gilbert’s network includes:

  • Antique dealers who specialize in her niche.
  • Studio potters who create limited-run ceramics.
  • Auction houses (like Sotheby’s or Christie’s) for rare finds.

Why It Works:

  • Insider access to off-market listings.
  • Negotiation leverage (she often gets 10-20% discounts from repeat buyers).

Action Step:

  • Attend design fairs (like the Salone del Mobile) to meet sellers.
  • Join collector forums (e.g., The Collectors’ Club of New York).

6. Consider the "Why Not" Before Buying

Gilbert asks herself:

  • "Does this fit my existing collection?"
  • "Will it hold value in 5 years?"
  • "Do I genuinely love it, or am I chasing a label?"

Common Mistake: Collectors often buy "fillers"—pieces that don’t align with their aesthetic—just to complete a set.

Action Step:

  • Before purchasing, list 3 non-negotiables (e.g., "I only collect handmade ceramics").

7. Invest in Condition and Restoration

Gilbert never skips the inspection. She:

  • Checks for structural integrity (e.g., wobbly legs on a chair).
  • Assesses wear and tear (e.g., faded paint, cracks in porcelain).
  • Budgets for professional restoration if needed.

Real-World Example: She once bought a vintage Italian leather sofa with minor cracks in the upholstery. Instead of discarding it, she restored the leather—a process that took six months—because the craftsmanship was worth preserving.

Action Step:

  • Get a second opinion from a conservator before buying high-value items.

8. Display with Purpose

Gilbert’s collections aren’t just stored; they’re curated. She:

  • Groups pieces by theme (e.g., all her Danish chairs in one room).
  • Rotates displays to keep them fresh.
  • Uses lighting and placement to highlight craftsmanship.

Why It Matters: A well-displayed collection enhances your space and increases its perceived value.

Action Step:

  • Dedicate one "curated corner" in your home or studio to showcase your collection.

9. Document Your Collection for Future Reference

Gilbert keeps detailed records, including:

  • Purchase dates and prices.
  • Provenance documentation.
  • High-resolution photos (for insurance and resale).

Real-World Example: When she sold a 1960s Italian glass vase for three times its purchase price, she had all the paperwork to prove its authenticity.

Action Step:

  • Use a spreadsheet or app (like Artnet Price Database) to track your collection.

10. Enjoy the Process—But Stay Disciplined

Gilbert’s collections grow organically, not frantically. She:

  • Sets a yearly acquisition limit (e.g., "I’ll buy no more than 3 major pieces per year").
  • Takes breaks if she feels overwhelmed.
  • Reminds herself why she started when tempted by a "must-have."

Action Step:

  • Schedule a quarterly review of your collection to ensure it still aligns with your goals.

Common Mistakes in Collecting (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake Why It’s Bad Gilbert’s Solution
Chasing trends Pieces lose value quickly; collections feel shallow. "Ask: ‘Will this still matter in 10 years?’"
Overpaying for "name" brands Not all expensive pieces are worth it. "Research comparable sales before bidding."
Ignoring condition Hidden flaws reduce value and usability. "Get a professional inspection before buying."
Collecting without a theme The collection becomes a random assortment. "Pick 1-3 core themes (e.g., ‘Danish modern’ + ‘handmade ceramics’)."
Not negotiating You pay more than necessary. "Start with 20% below asking price and meet in the middle."
Storing instead of displaying The collection loses meaning over time. "Curate a gallery wall or dedicated space."

FAQ: Answering Your Burning Questions About Intentional Collecting

1. How do I know if I’m collecting the right things?

Answer: The best collections are built on three pillars:

  • Passion – You genuinely love the piece.
  • Purpose – It serves a function (aesthetic, emotional, or practical).
  • Potential – It could appreciate in value or inspire future projects.

Example: If you’re a designer, a vintage drafting table might be a functional treasure—not just a decorative object.

2. Should I invest in insurance for my collection?

Answer: Yes, if your collection exceeds $5,000 in value. Gilbert always insures high-value pieces because:

  • Theft or damage can happen anytime.
  • Appraisals help with claims.

Tip: Get a specialty insurance policy (not just homeowners’ insurance) for fine art and antiques.

3. How do I negotiate like Jennifer Gilbert?

Answer: Gilbert’s negotiation strategy:

  1. Do your homework – Know the piece’s market value.
  2. Start low – Offer 20-30% below asking price (but be ready to walk away).
  3. Leverage relationships – Repeat buyers often get better terms.
  4. Be patient – Some sellers lowball; others counter.

Example: She once bought a rare Italian lamp for half its asking price because she had built trust with the dealer over years of purchases.

4. What if I realize I don’t love a piece after buying it?

Answer: This happens to even the most decisive collectors. Gilbert’s rule:

  • Sell it quickly (before it loses value).
  • Donate it (if it has sentimental value).
  • Repurpose it (e.g., turn a vintage chair into a side table).

Avoid: Keeping it out of guilt—clutter kills collections.

5. Can I start collecting with a small budget?

Answer: Absolutely. Gilbert began with small, meaningful finds before building her major collections. Her advice:

  • Start with "entry-level" pieces (e.g., a vintage ceramic mug instead of a rare vase).
  • Focus on quality over price (e.g., a handmade leather wallet over a mass-produced one).
  • Trade or barter (e.g., swap a piece you no longer need for something you want).

Example: She once traded a vintage Swiss Army knife (her grandfather’s) for a rare Danish coffee pot from a collector.

Conclusion: Your Collection Starts with a Decision

Jennifer Gilbert’s approach to collecting isn’t about hoarding objects—it’s about curating a legacy. Whether you’re a designer, an investor, or simply someone who appreciates beauty, her methods teach us that the best collections are intentional, meaningful, and decisive.

Ready to start your own curated collection? Here’s your first step:

  1. Pick one piece that excites you.
  2. Research its story, value, and condition.
  3. Commit—or walk away.

Because, as Gilbert says: "A collection isn’t just what you own—it’s what you choose to own."
