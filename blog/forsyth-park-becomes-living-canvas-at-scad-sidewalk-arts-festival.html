This event is not just a celebration of creativity—it’s a masterclass in urban art integration, blending street art, interactive installations, and community-driven expression.

How the SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival Transforms Forsyth Park

A Brief History: From Humble Beginnings to Global Recognition

The SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival began as a small, student-led initiative in 1996, organized by Savannah College of Art and Design (SCAD) students. What started as a one-day street fair has since grown into a multi-day extravaganza, drawing artists, musicians, and visitors from around the world.

Today, the festival spans three days, featuring:

  • Over 100 artists from diverse disciplines (painting, sculpture, graffiti, digital art)
  • Interactive installations that encourage visitor participation
  • Live performances blending music, theater, and dance
  • Workshops and demonstrations for all ages

Forsyth Park, with its historic oak trees, manicured lawns, and central location, serves as the perfect backdrop for this artistic explosion. The festival doesn’t just decorate the park—it redefines its purpose, turning it into a temporary but immersive art museum.

The Festival’s Structure: A Masterclass in Event Design

The SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival is meticulously planned, ensuring a seamless experience for both artists and attendees. Here’s how it’s structured:

  1. Artist Selection & Diversity

    • The festival curates a mix of established and emerging artists, ensuring a broad spectrum of styles (traditional, contemporary, experimental).
    • Local Savannah artists are given priority, but national and international contributors are also invited to maintain freshness.

  2. Zoned Exhibitions

    • The park is divided into themed zones, each with its own artistic focus:
      • The Graffiti & Street Art Zone – Features large-scale murals and spray-paint demonstrations.
      • The Sculpture & Installation Zone – Displays 3D artworks, kinetic sculptures, and interactive pieces.
      • The Traditional & Fine Art Zone – Showcases paintings, ceramics, and mixed-media works.
      • The Digital & Interactive Zone – Hosts VR experiences, light projections, and touchscreen art.

  3. Community Engagement & Workshops

    • Free workshops are held throughout the festival, teaching visitors painting, pottery, and graffiti techniques.
    • Family-friendly activities include face painting, art-making stations, and live music to keep all ages engaged.

  4. Sustainability & Logistics

    • The festival partners with local businesses for food, beverages, and event supplies.
    • Eco-friendly practices are encouraged, such as recycling stations and biodegradable materials.

  5. Fundraising & Community Impact

    • A portion of proceeds goes toward local arts programs and youth education initiatives.
    • The festival boosts Savannah’s tourism economy, with many visitors extending their stays to explore the city.

8 Actionable Strategies to Replicate SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival Success

If you’re looking to organize a public art festival in your city, town, or even a local park, the SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival offers valuable lessons. Here are eight proven strategies to ensure your event is engaging, sustainable, and impactful.

1. Choose the Right Location: Make the Park the Star

Why it works: Forsyth Park isn’t just a venue—it’s a brand in itself. Its historic charm, central location, and open space make it an ideal canvas.

How to apply it:

  • Select a location with high foot traffic (downtown, near tourist attractions, or in a revitalizing neighborhood).
  • Ensure the space has permits for large gatherings and temporary installations.
  • Consider accessibility—wheelchair-friendly paths, shaded areas, and restrooms are essential.
  • Work with local businesses to cross-promote the event (e.g., cafes, hotels, and shops offering discounts to festival-goers).

Real-world example: In Austin, Texas, the Austin Street Art Festival (now Art on the Green) transformed Zilker Park into a street art mecca, drawing over 100,000 visitors annually. The park’s natural beauty and urban setting made it the perfect backdrop for large-scale murals and interactive installations.

2. Curate a Diverse & Inclusive Artist Lineup

Why it works: The SCAD festival balances local talent with national/international artists, ensuring freshness and variety.

How to apply it:

  • Host open calls for submissions, but prioritize local artists to strengthen community ties.
  • Include underrepresented voices (women, LGBTQ+, artists of color, disabled artists).
  • Offer different pricing tiers for artists (some may work for exposure, others for fees).
  • Provide artist support (easels, power outlets, rest areas) to ensure a smooth experience.

Real-world example: The Baltimore Street Procession (now B-More Art) in 2019 featured over 50 artists, including graffiti legends like Os Gemeos and local Baltimore artists. The festival’s diverse lineup attracted 200,000+ attendees, proving that mixing established and emerging talent drives success.

3. Create Themed Zones for a Cohesive Experience

Why it works: Instead of random artworks scattered across the park, themed zones guide visitors through a curated journey.

How to apply it:

  • Define 3-5 distinct zones based on artistic mediums (e.g., Street Art, Sculpture, Digital Art).
  • Use signage and wayfinding to help visitors navigate the festival.
  • Rotate artists in zones to keep the experience fresh over multiple years.
  • Include interactive elements in each zone (e.g., graffiti workshops in the Street Art zone).

Real-world example: The Coachella Arts & Music Festival (though not a public art festival) uses themed stages to create a seamless experience. Similarly, Forsyth Park’s zoning ensures visitors don’t feel overwhelmed—each area has its own vibe and focus.

4. Offer Free or Low-Cost Workshops & Activities

Why it works: The SCAD festival makes art accessible by offering free workshops, which increase engagement and education.

How to apply it:

  • Partner with local schools and art programs to lead workshops.
  • Feature "artist talks" where creators discuss their process.
  • Include family-friendly activities (e.g., kids’ painting stations, DIY craft tables).
  • Promote workshops on social media to attract different demographics.

Real-world example: The Philadelphia Mural Arts Program runs free community workshops as part of its Public Art Program. These sessions teach graffiti techniques, mural painting, and street art history, empowering locals to create their own art.

5. Integrate Technology & Digital Art for a Modern Twist

Why it works: The SCAD festival blends traditional and digital art, appealing to tech-savvy audiences.

How to apply it:

  • Set up a "Digital Art Zone" with VR experiences, light projections, or augmented reality (AR) installations.
  • Use social media challenges (e.g., "Tag your favorite mural" for a chance to win prizes).
  • Live-stream workshops for remote participants.
  • Create a festival app with artist bios, maps, and interactive filters (e.g., AR filters that overlay digital art onto real-world locations).

Real-world example: The Burning Man Festival (though not a public art festival) incorporates digital art installations like LED murals and holographic performances. Similarly, Savannah’s Digital Art Zone at SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival attracts tech enthusiasts looking for cutting-edge experiences.

6. Ensure Sustainability & Eco-Friendly Practices

Why it works: Modern audiences care about environmental impact, and festivals that prioritize sustainability gain goodwill.

How to apply it:

  • Use biodegradable or recycled materials for installations.
  • Set up recycling stations and composting areas.
  • Partner with local eco-conscious businesses (e.g., solar-powered food trucks).
  • Promote carpooling or public transit for attendees.
  • Donate leftover materials to schools or community centers.

Real-world example: The Green Festival Network (which includes Green Festival LA) has zero-waste policies, ensuring 90%+ of waste is recycled or composted. Similarly, SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival has reduced single-use plastics in recent years, appealing to eco-conscious visitors.

7. Leverage Local Businesses & Cross-Promotions

Why it works: Partnerships with businesses extend the festival’s reach and boost local economies.

How to apply it:

  • Offer discounts to festival-goers at nearby restaurants, shops, and hotels.
  • Feature local vendors selling art supplies, books, or food.
  • Host a "Business Art Walk" where companies sponsor specific art installations.
  • Create a festival hashtag (e.g., #SCADSidewalk2024) and encourage businesses to use it.

Real-world example: The Austin City Limits (ACL) Music Festival partners with local breweries, food trucks, and hotels, creating a multi-day economic boost. Similarly, Savannah’s restaurants and hotels see increased business during the SCAD festival, making it a win-win for both parties.

8. Plan for Long-Term Impact: Beyond the Festival

Why it works: The best festivals leave a lasting legacy, whether through permanent installations, educational programs, or community art projects.

How to apply it:

  • Donate a portion of proceeds to local arts organizations.
  • Create a "Permanent Art Commission" where festival artists propose long-term installations.
  • Launch a "Festival Legacy Fund" to support ongoing public art projects.
  • Document the festival with photos, videos, and artist interviews to share online.

Real-world example: The Baltimore Street Procession (now B-More Art) has permanent murals installed in the city, revitalizing neighborhoods long after the festival ends. Similarly, SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival has contributed to Savannah’s growing mural collection, making the city a street art destination.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Hosting a Public Art Festival

While the SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival is a model of success, many organizers make critical errors that can derail their event. Here are five common pitfalls and how to avoid them:

1. Underestimating Logistics & Permits

Mistake: Failing to secure permits, insurance, or venue agreements in advance. Solution:

  • Start permit applications early (some cities take months to process).
  • Hire a festival manager to handle logistics, security, and vendor contracts.
  • Conduct a dry run of setup and breakdown to avoid last-minute chaos.

Real-world example: A 2022 street art festival in Portland had to cancel its final day due to unsecured permits, costing $50,000 in lost revenue. Proper planning could have prevented this disaster.

2. Ignoring Artist & Visitor Safety

Mistake: Not providing adequate security, first aid, or emergency exits. Solution:

  • Hire trained security staff to monitor crowds.
  • Set up first aid stations and partner with local EMS.
  • Ensure clear emergency routes and visible safety signs.

Real-world example: The 2021 Coachella Arts Festival faced crowd control issues, leading to injuries and delays. Proper safety planning would have minimized risks.

3. Overlooking Accessibility

Mistake: Creating barriers for disabled visitors (e.g., uneven paths, lack of ramps). Solution:

  • Design accessible pathways with smooth surfaces and handrails.
  • Provide sign language interpreters for workshops.
  • Ensure restrooms are wheelchair-accessible.

Real-world example: The 2020 Sydney Festival was criticized for poor accessibility, with many venues lacking ramps or elevators. In contrast, SCAD Sidewalk Arts Festival has consistently improved accessibility over the years.

4. Failing to Market Effectively

Mistake: Relying only on word-of-mouth without a digital marketing strategy. Solution:

  • Launch a website with ticketing, artist bios, and a countdown timer.
  • Run targeted social media ads (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook).
  • Partner with influencers to promote the festival.
  • Offer early-bird discounts to boost initial sales.

Real-world example: The 2023 Austin Street Art Festival saw a 30% increase in attendance after launching a TikTok challenge (#ArtOnTheGreenChallenge), which went viral.
