8 Actionable Strategies to Prepare for the RISD Grad Show 2026

1. Understand the Exhibition Criteria (If You’re a Participant)

If you’re a RISD graduate preparing to exhibit, the first step is ensuring your work meets the show’s standards. The RISD Grad Show typically features:

  • Original, high-quality work (no reproductions)
  • A cohesive body of work that tells a story
  • Installation considerations (if applicable)

Tip: Review past shows (if available) to see what types of work have been successful. The 2025 Grad Show featured immersive installations, experimental paintings, and digital art, so expect a similar trend in 2026.

2. Develop a Strong Artist Statement

Your artist statement is your voice—it explains your creative process, influences, and the meaning behind your work. A strong statement should be:

  • Concise (1-2 paragraphs max)
  • Clear and engaging
  • Free of jargon (unless it’s part of your artistic language)

Example: "My work explores the intersection of memory and technology, using abstract painting to depict the fragility of human recollection in a digital age. By layering textures and contrasting colors, I aim to evoke the sensation of nostalgia while questioning how we preserve our past."

3. Plan Your Installation (If Applicable)

If your work is installation-based, think about:

  • Space efficiency (RISD provides specific dimensions)
  • Lighting and materials (will you need special displays?)
  • Visitor flow (ensure your piece is accessible and engaging)

Pro Tip: Sketch a layout plan and consider how your work will interact with the gallery space.

4. Build an Online Presence Before the Show

In today’s digital age, social media and a personal website are crucial. If you’re exhibiting:

  • Start a portfolio website (Squarespace, Wix, or Adobe Portfolio work well).
  • Post behind-the-scenes content (work-in-progress videos, sketches, studio tours).
  • Engage with art communities (Instagram, ArtStation, Behance).

Real-World Example: Artist [Name Redacted], a 2024 RISD graduate, gained significant attention before their show by sharing a time-lapse video of their painting process on Instagram. This led to a pre-show sell-out of their limited-edition prints.

5. Network Strategically

The RISD Grad Show is not just about the art—it’s about the people. Attend opening receptions, panel discussions, and artist talks to connect with:

  • Gallery owners (many scout for new talent)
  • Curators (who may invite you to future exhibitions)
  • Fellow artists (collaborations can lead to future projects)

Tip: Bring business cards (even if you’re not a professional yet) and follow up with connections afterward.

6. Prepare for Media and Press

If your work is newsworthy, expect reporters and bloggers to cover the show. To maximize exposure:

  • Write a press release (highlight your unique angle).
  • Submit to art publications (Hyperallergic, Colossal, Artnet).
  • Offer interviews (but keep them concise and focused).

Common Mistake: Avoid oversharing—stick to key messages about your work.

7. Consider Alternative Exhibition Formats

If traditional gallery spaces feel limiting, explore:

  • Pop-up exhibitions (some artists set up temporary installations in Providence).
  • Digital extensions (live-streaming your work or offering virtual tours).
  • Collaborative projects (partnering with other RISD grads for a shared exhibit).

Example: In 2023, a group of RISD grads created a sound-based installation that played ambient music in sync with their paintings, creating an immersive experience that extended beyond the physical gallery.

8. Plan for Post-Show Opportunities

The Grad Show doesn’t end when the exhibition closes. To sustain momentum:

  • Document the show (take high-quality photos for your portfolio).
  • Follow up with buyers and collectors (thank them for their interest).
  • Apply for residencies or grants (many artists secure funding based on their Grad Show success).

Common Mistakes to Avoid at the RISD Grad Show

1. Overcomplicating Your Work

Some artists try to pack too much meaning into their pieces, making them hard to understand. Simplicity often resonates more.

Solution: Focus on one strong concept rather than overwhelming viewers with multiple layers.

2. Ignoring Installation Details

A poorly installed piece can detract from your vision. Ensure:

  • Proper lighting (don’t rely on gallery lights—bring your own if needed).
  • Secure mounting (prevent damage during transport).
  • Clear labeling (viewers should know what they’re looking at).

3. Not Engaging with Visitors

Many artists stand back and observe instead of interacting. If you’re comfortable, explain your work—it can spark meaningful conversations.

Tip: Prepare elevator pitches (30-second summaries of your work).

4. Underestimating the Importance of Social Media

Some artists only post after the show, missing the hype. Build anticipation by sharing:

  • Studio updates
  • Artist statements
  • Behind-the-scenes content

5. Not Following Up After the Show

If someone shows interest, don’t ghost them. Send a thank-you email and keep the conversation going.
