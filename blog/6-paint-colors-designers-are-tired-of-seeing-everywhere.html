6 Paint Colors Designers Are Tired Of Seeing Everywhere

When it comes to interior design, paint colors can make or break the aesthetic of a room. In recent years, certain paint colors have become incredibly popular, with **73% of homeowners** opting for neutral shades in 2020, according to a survey by the National Kitchen and Bath Association (NKBA). However, by 2022, designers were already noticing a trend of overused paint colors, with **60% of designers** reporting that they were tired of seeing the same colors everywhere, as stated in a report by the American Society of Interior Designers (ASID). In this post, we'll explore the 6 paint colors designers are tired of seeing everywhere and provide actionable strategies for choosing alternative shades that can elevate your space.

Introduction to Overused Paint Colors

The paint colors that designers are tired of seeing everywhere are not necessarily bad colors, but rather, they have been overused to the point of becoming clichés. These colors include sherwin-williams' comfort gray, benjamin moore's simply white, and behr's soft chamois, among others. While these colors may have been popular in the past, they have lost their appeal due to their widespread use. In fact, a report by the Paint Quality Institute found that **80% of paint sales** in 2020 were for neutral colors, which has led to a lack of creativity in interior design.

Strategies for Choosing Alternative Paint Colors

To avoid using overused paint colors, designers recommend the following strategies:

  • Consider the natural light in the room and choose a color that complements it. For example, a room with plenty of natural light can handle a bolder color, while a room with limited light may require a lighter shade.
  • Think about the mood you want to create in the room. Different colors can evoke different emotions, so choose a color that aligns with the mood you want to create.
  • Look to nature for inspiration. Nature has a wide range of colors that can be used in interior design, from the greens of plants to the blues of the sky.
  • Experiment with different shades of a single color. Instead of using a single, overused color, try experimenting with different shades of that color to create a unique look.
  • Consider the 60-30-10 rule, which states that 60% of the room should be a dominant color, 30% a secondary color, and 10% an accent color. This rule can help create a balanced and harmonious color scheme.
  • Don't be afraid to add a pop of color with a bold, bright shade. This can add visual interest to the room and create a unique focal point.
  • Use color to create a sense of flow in the room. Choose colors that flow smoothly from one area to another to create a sense of continuity.
  • Consider the psychological effects of color and choose colors that promote the desired mood or atmosphere. For example, blue is often associated with calmness and serenity, while red is associated with energy and excitement.
  • Get inspiration from art and culture, such as the bold colors used in abstract art or the rich colors of traditional cultural textiles.
  • Don't forget about the ceiling, which can be painted a different color than the walls to create a unique and interesting look.

Real-World Examples of Alternative Paint Colors

There are many real-world examples of alternative paint colors that can be used to create a unique and interesting look. For example, a room with a bold, blue ceiling and crisp, white walls can create a sense of calmness and serenity. Alternatively, a room with warm, earthy tones and rich, wooden accents can create a cozy and inviting atmosphere. Another example is a room with a statement wall in a bright, bold color and neutral-colored furniture, which can add visual interest and create a unique focal point.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Choosing Paint Colors

When choosing paint colors, there are several common mistakes to avoid. These include:

  • Not considering the natural light in the room, which can affect how the color looks.
  • Not testing the color before committing to it, which can lead to unexpected results.
  • Not thinking about the mood you want to create in the room, which can result in a color that doesn't align with the desired atmosphere.
  • Not considering the color of the furniture and decor, which can clash with the paint color and create a discordant look.
  • Not using a color wheel to choose colors that complement each other, which can result in a color scheme that doesn't work well together.
