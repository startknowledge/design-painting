🎨 Paint the Rink Pink: How to Win the Bruins’ Jersey Design Contest & Showcase Your Artistic Talent

Introduction: A Rare Opportunity to Make NHL History

The Boston Bruins, one of the most storied franchises in the National Hockey League (NHL), are once again turning their jerseys into canvases for fans. Their Paint the Rink Pink jersey design contest is back, giving artists, designers, and hockey enthusiasts the chance to create a custom jersey that could be worn by players on the ice. This isn’t just a fun social media challenge—it’s a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity to leave a lasting mark on NHL history.

Since the Bruins launched their first Paint the Rink contest in 2015, over 100,000 designs have been submitted, with winners seeing their artwork featured on jerseys worn by players like David Pastrnak, Brad Marchand, and Charlie McAvoy. In 2022 alone, the contest generated over 50,000 entries, proving that fans worldwide are eager to contribute to the sport they love.

If you’ve ever dreamed of seeing your design on an NHL jersey, now is the time to step up your game. Whether you're a professional graphic designer, a hobbyist, or simply someone with a passion for hockey and art, this guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from how to create a winning design to submitting your entry and avoiding common pitfalls that could disqualify your work.

Why the Bruins’ Paint the Rink Contest is a Must-Win Opportunity

Before diving into the how-to, let’s explore why this contest matters beyond just the thrill of seeing your art on a jersey.

1. Exposure to Millions of Hockey Fans

The Bruins have over 1.2 million social media followers across platforms like Twitter, Instagram, and Facebook, and their jersey designs often go viral. Winning this contest means your artwork could be seen by fans worldwide, potentially leading to collaborations with other brands, art galleries, or even future NHL initiatives.

2. A Chance to Support a Cause

This year’s Paint the Rink Pink theme is tied to Breast Cancer Awareness, aligning with the Bruins’ long-standing partnership with the Boston-based Susan G. Komen affiliate. Your design could help raise awareness and funds for a meaningful cause while also promoting your artistic work.

3. Networking with NHL Professionals

The Bruins’ marketing and design teams actively engage with contest winners, offering mentorship, portfolio reviews, and sometimes even job opportunities in sports marketing or graphic design. Past winners have gone on to work with NHL teams, sports apparel brands, and even major leagues.

4. A Boost for Your Portfolio

If you’re an artist or designer looking to build your portfolio, a Bruins jersey design is one of the most prestigious pieces you can add. It’s a credibility booster that can open doors for freelance gigs, client work, or even a career in sports branding.

5. The Thrill of Seeing Your Art on the Ice

There’s nothing quite like the emotional high of watching your design being worn by professional athletes. In 2021, a fan’s custom "Bruins Forever" design was featured on a jersey during a game, creating instant nostalgia and excitement among fans.

8 Actionable Strategies to Create a Winning Bruins Jersey Design

Now that you understand why this contest is worth entering, let’s break down how to create a design that stands out. The Bruins have specific guidelines, but with the right approach, you can maximize your chances of winning.

1. Understand the Official Contest Rules & Requirements

Before sketching a single line, read the official contest rules carefully. The Bruins typically require:

  • Designs must fit within the jersey’s template (no text, logos, or elements that violate NHL branding rules).
  • The primary color scheme must align with the "Pink" theme (though subtle variations are allowed).
  • No offensive, political, or copyrighted material (this is a major disqualification reason).
  • Submissions must be original (no AI-generated designs unless specified otherwise).

Pro Tip: Check the Bruins’ official website or social media pages for annual updates, as rules can change slightly each year.

2. Study Past Winning Designs (And Why They Worked)

Looking at previous winners gives you insight into what resonates with the Bruins’ audience. Some standout designs include:

  • "Boston Strong" (2020) – A minimalist, patriotic design featuring the Boston skyline and a hockey stick, symbolizing resilience.
  • "For the City" (2019) – A collage of Boston landmarks (Fenway Park, the TD Garden, and the Charles River) with a hockey-themed twist.
  • "Legacy" (2018) – A tribute to the Bruins’ history, featuring iconic players like Bobby Orr and Ray Bourque in a modern, stylized format.

Key Takeaway: The best designs balance creativity with nostalgia, making them emotionally resonant for fans.

3. Choose a Theme That Connects with Boston & Hockey

The Bruins love designs that tell a story about Boston, hockey culture, or the team’s legacy. Some highly effective themes include:

  • Local Boston Icons (e.g., Fenway Park, the Boston Tea Party, the Freedom Trail).
  • Hockey History (e.g., 1970 Stanley Cup win, Bobby Orr’s iconic goal, or the "Bad Boys" era).
  • Community & Charity (e.g., supporting local schools, hospitals, or sports programs).
  • Pop Culture & Memes (e.g., Boston-specific humor, like "The Big Papi" or "The Goon Squad").

Example: In 2021, a design featuring a hockey stick shaped like a Boston cream pie went viral because it combined local food culture with hockey, making it uniquely Bostonian.

4. Master the Jersey Template & Layout

The Bruins provide a specific jersey template (usually a PNG or PDF file) that you must follow. Key layout considerations:

  • Front of the Jersey:
    • The primary design should be centered (avoid placing it too high or low).
    • Leave space for the Bruins logo (usually near the collar).
    • Avoid blocking the player’s number (it must remain visible).
  • Back of the Jersey:
    • The surname must be readable (no overlapping text).
    • Optional: A smaller secondary design (e.g., a team mascot or a fan tribute).
  • Sleeves & Collar:
    • Minimalist designs work best (no cluttered patterns).
    • Subtle accents (like a pink stripe or a small emblem) can add flair.

Common Mistake: Designers often ignore the collar area, leading to misalignment when the jersey is printed. Always test your design in the provided template before submitting.

5. Use High-Quality, Scalable Graphics

The Bruins require submissions in high resolution (typically 300 DPI or higher). Best practices for digital design:

  • Vector-based designs (AI, Illustrator, Procreate) scale perfectly without losing quality.
  • Raster designs (Photoshop, Procreate) should be exported at the highest possible resolution.
  • Avoid pixelation—this is a quick disqualification for digital submissions.

Pro Tip: If you’re hand-drawing your design, scan it at 600 DPI and retouch it in Photoshop to ensure crispness.

6. Incorporate the "Pink" Theme Without Overdoing It

While the contest is Paint the Rink Pink, the Bruins encourage creativity—so don’t just slap a bright pink background on your design. Instead:

  • Use pink as an accent color (e.g., pink highlights on a blue-and-white design).
  • Tie pink to the theme (e.g., pink ribbons for breast cancer awareness).
  • Balance boldness with subtlety—some of the best designs use pink sparingly.

Example: A 2022 winner featured a hockey stick with a pink blade and white stars, making the pink pop without dominating the design.

7. Make It Emotionally Resonant (Fans Love Stories)

The Bruins prioritize designs that evoke emotion. How to make your design stand out:

  • Personal anecdotes (e.g., "This design is for my grandpa who loved the Bruins").
  • Tributes to lost loved ones (e.g., "For my sister who loved hockey").
  • Community-driven messages (e.g., "Supporting local kids’ hockey programs").

Real-World Example: In 2017, a design featuring a **hockey stick with a pink ribbon and the words "For the Girls Who Play" won because it celebrated female hockey players, a cause close to many fans’ hearts.

8. Submit Early & Follow Up

The Bruins often have a long submission window, but popular designs get lost in the crowd. How to maximize your chances:

  • Submit as soon as the contest opens (first entries get more visibility).
  • Check your email spam folder for confirmation messages.
  • Engage with the Bruins’ social media (like, comment, and share their contest posts).
  • Follow up politely if you don’t hear back within a week.

Pro Tip: If you’re nervous about your design, consider asking a friend or fellow artist to review it before submission.

Common Mistakes to Avoid (And How to Fix Them)

Even the most talented artists can unintentionally disqualify themselves by making simple errors. Here’s what to watch out for:

1. Ignoring the Jersey Template

Mistake: Designing a freeform artwork that doesn’t fit the jersey’s number placement, logo areas, or sleeve cutouts. Fix: Always use the provided template and test your design in it before submitting.

2. Using Copyrighted Material

Mistake: Including NHL logos, player names, or trademarked images without permission. Fix: Create original artwork—no real players, team logos, or famous landmarks unless they’re public domain or reimagined creatively.

3. Overcomplicating the Design

Mistake: Packing the jersey with too many elements, making it hard to read or visually overwhelming. Fix: Keep it clean and focused—the best designs tell a story with minimal details.

4. Poor File Quality

Mistake: Submitting a pixelated, low-resolution image that looks blurry when printed. Fix: Use vector files or high-DPI raster images (300 DPI minimum).

5. Missing the Submission Deadline

Mistake: Waiting until the last few days to submit, only to find the website is down or the contest closed. Fix: Set a reminder and submit early to avoid last-minute stress.

6. Not Aligning with the Theme

Mistake: Creating a generic hockey design that doesn’t tie into "Paint the Rink Pink" or Boston culture. Fix: Research the theme deeply and weave it into your design (e.g., pink ribbons, local landmarks, or charity ties).

7. Forgetting to Sign Your Work

Mistake: Submitting an unsigned design, making it hard to attribute credit if it wins. Fix: Include your name or artist handle in a small, unobtrusive corner of the design.
