Common Mistakes When Relocating an Exhibition (And How to Avoid Them)

1. Ignoring the New Venue’s Unique Characteristics

Mistake: Treating the new space as a carbon copy of the old one. Solution:

  • Visit the space multiple times at different times of day (lighting changes dramatically).
  • Consult with local historians to understand the venue’s unwritten stories.

2. Underestimating Installation Costs

Mistake: Budgeting only for art transport, not structural adjustments. Solution:

  • Get multiple quotes from installers.
  • Account for unexpected expenses (e.g., reinforcing walls for heavy pieces).

3. Overlooking Accessibility Requirements

Mistake: Assuming the new venue is fully compliant with ADA standards. Solution:

  • Audit the space for barriers (e.g., steep stairs, narrow doorways).
  • Work with accessibility consultants to make necessary modifications.

4. Failing to Communicate the Change to Audiences

Mistake: Announcing the move as an afterthought rather than a major event. Solution:

  • Tease the relocation in advance (e.g., "Why La Posada?" blog series).
  • Host a press conference with both venues to highlight the transition.

5. Not Planning for Post-Relocation Maintenance

Mistake: Assuming the exhibition will run smoothly without ongoing adjustments. Solution:

  • Schedule regular check-ins with the venue’s staff.
  • Have a backup plan for climate control, security, or visitor flow issues.
