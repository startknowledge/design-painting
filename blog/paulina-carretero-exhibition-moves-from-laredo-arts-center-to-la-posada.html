Common Mistakes in Exhibition Relocations (And How to Avoid Them)

1. Ignoring the New Venue’s Audience

Mistake: Assuming the same audience will visit. Solution: Research the new location’s demographics and tailor the exhibition accordingly.

Example: A New York-based artist moved an exhibition to Houston without adjusting for local tastes, resulting in lower attendance.

2. Poor Logistics & Damage

Mistake: Not securing proper insurance or movers. Solution: Hire a professional art relocation service and document everything.

Example: A famous painter’s works were scratched during transport due to improper packing, leading to legal disputes.

3. No Digital Strategy

Mistake: Focusing only on in-person attendance. Solution: Combine physical and digital marketing (social media, SEO, VR).

Example: An exhibition in Dallas saw only 10% of inquiries come from online searches because they didn’t optimize their website.

4. Overlooking Accessibility

Mistake: Not ensuring the new venue is wheelchair-accessible or ADA-compliant. Solution: Check local accessibility laws before finalizing the move.

Example: A San Antonio gallery was sued for not having ramps, leading to negative PR.

5. No Post-Event Follow-Up

Mistake: Treating the exhibition as a one-time event. Solution: Plan for a catalog, documentary, or future shows.

Example: The "Border Stories" exhibition (2020) faded after the show ended because there was no digital archive.
