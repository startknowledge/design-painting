The Step-by-Step Transformation of Willoughby Gilson Park’s Concession Stand

The Willoughby Gilson Park mural wasn’t an accident—it was the result of careful planning, community collaboration, and artistic vision. Here’s how it happened:

Step 1: Identifying the Need & Vision

Before any brushstrokes were made, the Columbus Parks & Recreation Department recognized that:

  • The original concession stand was outdated and unappealing.
  • Visitors often complained about the lack of character in the park’s amenities.
  • Local artists were eager for more opportunities to contribute to public spaces.

The team decided to repurpose the concession stand not just as a functional space, but as a cultural landmark.

Step 2: Selecting the Right Artist & Collaborators

Instead of hiring a single artist, the park partnered with three local Columbus-based muralists:

  1. Sarah Chen – Known for dynamic, nature-inspired murals.
  2. Marcus Johnson – Specialized in urban storytelling and community portraits.
  3. Elena Vasquez – Focused on abstract, vibrant patterns that engage children.

This collaborative approach ensured the mural would have multiple artistic voices, making it more inclusive and visually rich.

Step 3: Designing a Mural That Reflects the Community

The artists spent three months researching before starting:

  • They interviewed park visitors about their favorite local memories.
  • They studied the park’s history, including its role in the Civil Rights Movement (Willoughby Gilson was a key gathering spot in the 1960s).
  • They incorporated elements from nearby neighborhoods, such as Ohio State University’s campus murals and Franklin Park Conservatory’s botanical themes.

The final design featured:

  • A large, stylized tree symbolizing growth and community.
  • Silhouettes of historical figures from the park’s past.
  • Interactive elements, like QR codes linking to audio stories about the park’s history.
  • Bright, playful colors to attract families and children.

Step 4: Securing Funding & Permits

Public art projects require careful budgeting and legal considerations. The Willoughby Gilson Park mural was funded through:

  • A $50,000 grant from the Ohio Arts Council (part of the NEA’s Creative Places program).
  • A $20,000 sponsorship from a local beverage company (who saw the mural as brand alignment with community values).
  • A $15,000 crowdfunding campaign led by the Columbus Mural Arts Program.

Permits were secured through:

  • The City of Columbus Public Art Commission (ensuring the design met aesthetic guidelines).
  • The Ohio Historical Society (for historical accuracy in depictions).

Step 5: The Painting Process – From Canvas to Reality

The mural was painted over four weeks using:

  • High-quality, weather-resistant acrylic paint (to withstand Ohio’s harsh winters).
  • A custom-built scaffolding system to ensure safety and precision.
  • A phased approach, where sections were completed and publicly unveiled incrementally to build excitement.

The artists worked in shifts, with local students from nearby schools assisting in smaller decorative elements (like leaf patterns and geometric shapes). This youth involvement ensured the mural had generational appeal.

Step 6: Launch & Community Celebration

The grand opening was a two-day event featuring:

  • Live music by local bands.
  • A mural dedication ceremony with speeches from city officials.
  • Free ice cream and snacks (provided by the concession stand itself).
  • A social media challenge (#WilloughbyMural) encouraging visitors to share their favorite parts.

Within three months, the mural was featured in local newspapers, travel blogs, and even national art publications, bringing new visitors to the park.

Step 7: Maintenance & Long-Term Stewardship

To ensure the mural remained vibrant, the park implemented:

  • Quarterly touch-ups by the original artists.
  • A "Mural Ambassadors" program, where volunteers report any damage (like graffiti) to maintenance staff.
  • An annual "Mural Day" where the community cleans and celebrates the artwork.

8 Actionable Strategies to Transform a Public Space Like Willoughby Gilson Park

If you’re looking to replicate the success of the Willoughby Gilson Park mural, here are eight proven strategies to apply in your own projects:

1. Start with a Clear Vision & Community Input

Problem: Many public art projects fail because they lack alignment with community values. Solution:

  • Host public workshops to gather ideas.
  • Survey visitors about their favorite local symbols.
  • Hire a facilitator to mediate discussions and ensure fair representation.

Example: The High Line in New York City (a famous elevated park) involved local artists and residents in every phase of its design, ensuring it reflected multiple cultural perspectives.

2. Partner with Local Artists (Not Just Big Names)

Problem: Hiring a single famous artist can be expensive and less inclusive. Solution:

  • Create a shortlist of 3-5 local artists with different styles.
  • Offer collaborative opportunities (like the Willoughby Gilson project).
  • Provide stipends to emerging artists to lower costs while boosting local talent.

Example: The Detroit Mural Project has trained over 500 local artists in mural painting, ensuring economic benefits stay within the community.

3. Incorporate Interactive & Educational Elements

Problem: Static murals can lose engagement over time. Solution:

  • Add QR codes linking to audio stories, videos, or AR experiences.
  • Include puzzles or challenges (e.g., "Find the hidden animal in the mural").
  • Work with schools to create educational guides about the artwork.

Example: The Los Angeles River Revitalization Project features interactive murals where visitors can scan codes to learn about the river’s history.

4. Secure a Mix of Funding Sources

Problem: Relying on one funding stream can lead to delays or cancellations. Solution:

  • Apply for grants (e.g., NEA, local arts councils).
  • Partner with corporations that align with social responsibility goals.
  • Launch a crowdfunding campaign with perks like naming rights for donors.

Example: The Chicago Public Art Program funds projects through a mix of city budgets, private donations, and corporate sponsorships, ensuring long-term sustainability.

5. Use Durable, Weather-Resistant Materials

Problem: Cheap paint and materials fade quickly, making the art look neglected. Solution:

  • Use acrylic or epoxy paints designed for outdoor use.
  • Apply a protective sealant to prevent cracking.
  • Test materials in different climates before finalizing.

Example: The San Francisco Mural Program uses UV-resistant paints to ensure murals last 20+ years without significant fading.

6. Plan for Phased Rollouts & Public Engagement

Problem: A fully revealed mural all at once can lose the "wow" factor. Solution:

  • Unveil sections incrementally (e.g., one week per section).
  • Host "mural progress" events where artists answer questions.
  • Use social media teasers to build anticipation.

Example: The Boston Mural Festival reveals new murals every month, keeping the city’s art scene dynamic and fresh.

7. Create a Maintenance & Stewardship Plan

Problem: Without proper care, even the best murals degrade quickly. Solution:

  • Assign a "mural ambassador" to monitor condition.
  • Schedule regular cleanings (gentle pressure washing, not harsh chemicals).
  • Train volunteers to report vandalism or damage.

Example: The Portland Public Art Collection has a dedicated maintenance team that inspects murals quarterly and repairs them within 48 hours of reporting.

8. Measure Success Beyond Aesthetics

Problem: Some projects focus only on looks, not impact. Solution:

  • Track foot traffic before and after the mural.
  • Survey visitors on their perception of the space.
  • Monitor local business sales in the area.
  • Assess community engagement (e.g., social media mentions, events attendance).

Example: The Austin Public Art Program uses data analytics to track how murals influence tourism and economic activity, ensuring every dollar spent delivers measurable results.
