Actionable Strategies for Building an Art Collection

For those looking to start or expand their own art collection, here are 8-10 actionable strategies to consider:

  • Set a budget: Before starting your collection, it's essential to determine how much you are willing to spend. This will help you narrow down your options and focus on pieces that fit within your budget.
  • Research, research, research: Learn as much as you can about the artists, styles, and periods that interest you. This will help you make informed decisions when selecting pieces for your collection.
  • Work with a reputable dealer: A good art dealer can provide valuable guidance and help you find pieces that fit your budget and interests.
  • Consider the condition of the piece: The condition of a piece can greatly impact its value. Make sure to carefully inspect the piece before purchasing and consider any necessary restoration or conservation work.
  • Look for provenance: Provenance refers to the ownership history of a piece. A piece with a strong provenance can increase its value and authenticity.
  • Don't be afraid to take risks: Building an art collection is not just about buying pieces that are likely to appreciate in value. It's also about taking risks and trying new things.
  • Store and display your pieces properly: Proper storage and display are crucial to maintaining the condition and value of your pieces. Consider investing in high-quality frames, mats, and storage materials.
  • Keep your collection diverse: A diverse collection that includes a range of styles, periods, and mediums can be more interesting and dynamic than a collection that focuses on a single area.
  • Stay up-to-date with market trends: The art market is constantly evolving, and staying informed about current trends and sales can help you make informed decisions about your collection.
  • Have fun: Building an art collection is a personal and enjoyable process. Don't be afraid to try new things and have fun with it!

The Middletons' collection is just one example of a successful art collection. For example, Steve Cohen has been known to take risks on emerging artists, purchasing works by artists such as Jeff Koons and Cindy Sherman before they became well-known. This approach has allowed him to build a diverse and dynamic collection that includes a range of styles and periods.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Building an Art Collection

While building an art collection can be a rewarding and enjoyable process, there are also common mistakes to avoid. These include:

  • Not doing your research: Failing to research an artist or piece can lead to purchasing a fake or overvalued work.
  • Not considering the condition of the piece: Failing to inspect a piece carefully can lead to purchasing a piece that is damaged or in poor condition.
  • Not working with a reputable dealer: Working with a disreputable dealer can lead to purchasing a fake or overvalued work.
  • Not keeping your collection diverse: Failing to diversify your collection can lead to a lackluster and uninteresting collection.
  • Not staying up-to-date with market trends: Failing to stay informed about current market trends can lead to making uninformed decisions about your collection.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best way to start an art collection?
Starting an art collection can be as simple as purchasing a single piece that you love. From there, you can begin to build your collection, considering factors such as budget, style, and period.
How do I know if a piece is authentic?
Ensuring the authenticity of a piece can be done by working with a reputable dealer, researching the artist and piece, and considering the provenance of the work.
What is the most important thing to consider when purchasing a piece of art?
The most important thing to consider when purchasing a piece of art is the condition of the piece. A piece that is in poor condition can be difficult and expensive to restore, and may not be worth the investment.
How do I store and display my art collection?
Proper storage and display of your art collection are crucial to maintaining its condition and value. Consider investing in high-quality frames, mats, and storage materials, and keep your pieces away from direct sunlight and moisture.
Can I make a living by investing in art?
While it is possible to make a living by investing in art, it is not a guaranteed investment. The art market can be volatile, and the value of a piece can fluctuate over time. It's essential to approach art collecting as a long-term investment and to do your research before making a purchase.
