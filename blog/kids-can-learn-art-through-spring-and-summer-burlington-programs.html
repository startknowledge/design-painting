Top 10 Actionable Strategies to Help Kids Thrive in Burlington’s Art Programs

1. Choose the Right Program Based on Your Child’s Interests

Not all art programs are created equal. Some focus on technique, others on experimentation, and a few on collaborative projects. Before enrolling, ask yourself:

  • Does my child love drawing, painting, or digital art?
  • Are they more independent or social?
  • Do they prefer structured lessons or free-form creativity?

Example: If your child is passionate about nature, consider ECHO’s family art workshops, where they can create watercolor landscapes inspired by Lake Champlain.

2. Set Clear Goals for the Program

Work with your child to define what success looks like. For instance:

  • "I want my child to learn basic shading techniques."
  • "I want them to feel more confident in sharing their artwork."

Tip: Some programs offer portfolio reviews—ask if your child can showcase their work at the end of the session.

3. Encourage a Growth Mindset

Art is about learning, not perfection. Teach your child that mistakes are part of the process. For example:

  • If they struggle with a watercolor wash, guide them to practice blending colors rather than getting frustrated.
  • Praise effort ("I love how you kept trying!") over results ("This looks great!").

Real-World Example: At Burlington’s ArtWorks Studio, instructors use positive reinforcement to help kids see art as a journey, not a competition.

4. Balance Structured Learning with Free Play

Some programs are highly structured, while others encourage open-ended creativity. The best approach?

  • First half: Follow the instructor’s guidance.
  • Second half: Let them experiment freely—this builds confidence and originality.

Example: At The Vermont Studio Center’s youth programs, kids start with guided projects (like painting a still life) but then transition to personal themes, such as imaginary creatures or futuristic cities.

5. Incorporate Outdoor Art Experiences

Burlington’s natural beauty is a goldmine for inspiration. Many programs take advantage of this by:

  • Sketching in parks (like Waterfront Park)
  • Creating nature-based sculptures (using found materials)
  • Painting en plein air (outdoor painting)

Tip: If a program doesn’t offer outdoor sessions, combine it with local hikes—bring a sketchbook and document your surroundings.

6. Use Technology to Enhance Learning

Many Burlington programs now blend traditional and digital art. For example:

  • Tablet drawing classes (using Procreate or Adobe Fresco)
  • 3D modeling workshops (for future architects and designers)
  • Stop-motion animation (great for storytelling)

Example: Burlington’s Digital Arts Lab offers weekend workshops where kids learn digital painting techniques while still exploring traditional media.

7. Foster Collaboration Over Competition

Some art programs rank or compare students, which can be demoralizing. Instead, look for programs that:

  • Encourage peer feedback (e.g., "What do you like about this drawing?")
  • Host group exhibitions (so kids see their work displayed)
  • Focus on team projects (like community murals)

Real-World Example: Burlington’s Mural Arts Project invites kids to design sections of public murals, teaching them teamwork and urban art techniques.

8. Keep a Creative Journal

Encourage your child to document their progress with:

  • Sketchbook entries (doodles, experiments, finished pieces)
  • Photos of their work (if allowed)
  • Reflections on what they learned

This helps them track growth and build a portfolio for future programs.

9. Connect with Local Artists & Mentors

Many Burlington programs offer guest artist visits or mentorship opportunities. These can:

  • Provide real-world insights into careers in art
  • Offer personalized feedback
  • Inspire kids to see art as a viable profession

Example: The Vermont Arts Institute pairs young artists with professional sculptors for one-on-one sessions.

10. Make Art a Year-Round Habit

Summer programs are fantastic, but consistency matters. After the session ends:

  • Visit local galleries (like The Flynn Center’s art exhibits)
  • Join a local kids’ art club (check Meetup.com or Facebook groups)
  • Sign up for a fall/winter class to keep the momentum going

Tip: Some studios offer discounted memberships for regular attendance, making art a long-term investment.

Common Mistakes Parents Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake #1: Enrolling Without Researching the Instructor

Problem: Some programs are led by passionate amateurs rather than experienced artists or educators. Solution:

  • Ask for instructor bios—do they have teaching experience?
  • Check reviews or testimonials from past parents.
  • Visit the studio before signing up (if possible).

Mistake #2: Overemphasizing Competition

Problem: Some programs rank or judge kids, which can kill creativity. Solution:

  • Look for process-focused programs (e.g., "How did you create this?" vs. "Is this the best?").
  • Avoid contest-style classes unless your child loves challenges.

Mistake #3: Not Considering Logistics

Problem: Long drives, uncomfortable spaces, or inflexible schedules can diminish the experience. Solution:

  • Choose local programs (within 30 minutes of home).
  • Check class times—are they too early/late for your child’s energy levels?
  • Ask about snack breaks (kids need fuel for creativity).

Mistake #4: Ignoring Supplies Needed

Problem: Some programs require expensive materials, leading to frustration. Solution:

  • Ask for a supply list—can you rent or borrow tools?
  • Some studios provide materials (e.g., watercolors, brushes).
  • If buying, start with basics (e.g., sketchbook, pencils, paints) before investing in high-end tools.

Mistake #5: Not Following Up After the Program

Problem: The magic of summer art fades when kids return to school. Solution:

  • Display their work at home (create a gallery wall).
  • Visit a local gallery together to inspire future projects.
  • Sign up for a fall/winter class to keep the momentum.

FAQ Section: Everything You Need to Know About Burlington’s Art Programs

❓ What age groups do Burlington’s art programs cater to?

Answer: Burlington offers art programs for all ages, from toddlers (ages 3–5) in parent-child workshops to teens (ages 13–18) in advanced digital art and sculpture classes. Some programs, like those at ECHO, are family-friendly, while others, such as Vermont Studio Center, focus on older students (10+). Always check the specific age ranges when registering.

❓ How much do Burlington’s summer art programs cost?

Answer: Costs vary widely depending on the program:

  • Drop-in workshops (e.g., at Burlington ArtWorks) can range from $15–$30 per session.
  • Week-long camps (e.g., at ECHO) typically cost $100–$200 per child.
  • Intensive programs (e.g., at Vermont Studio Center) may run $500–$1,200 for a two-week session.
  • Scholarships and discounts are often available—ask when inquiring!

Pro Tip: Some nonprofits and community centers offer sliding-scale fees based on income.

❓ Do I need to provide my own art supplies?

Answer: Not always! Many programs include supplies in the tuition, especially short workshops (like ECHO’s Family Art Days). However, for longer programs (e.g., summer camps), you may need to bring your own sketchbook, pencils, or paints. Always confirm supply requirements when registering.

Budget Tip: Start with basic supplies (e.g., a cheap sketchbook, colored pencils) before investing in expensive materials.

❓ Are there any free or low-cost art programs in Burlington?

Answer: Yes! Burlington has several free or affordable options:
