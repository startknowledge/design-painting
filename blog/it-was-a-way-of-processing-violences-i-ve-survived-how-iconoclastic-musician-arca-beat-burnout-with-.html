So, how did Arca use painting to overcome burnout? Here are five strategies that he employed:

### 1. **Embracing Spontaneity**
Arca's approach to painting was characterized by spontaneity and a lack of self-criticism. He allowed himself to freely express his emotions without worrying about creating a perfect piece of art. ### 2. **Experimenting with Different Mediums**
Arca experimented with various painting mediums, including oil, acrylic, and watercolor. This experimentation allowed him to explore different textures, colors, and techniques, which in turn helped him to express his emotions in new and innovative ways. For example, he used bold, vibrant colors to convey feelings of anger and frustration, while softer, more muted colors represented moments of sadness and introspection.

### 3. **Creating a Safe Space**
Arca created a safe and comfortable space for himself to paint, free from distractions and criticism. This space allowed him to fully immerse himself in the creative process and focus on his emotions. As he noted in an interview with Pitchfork, "I was making music in a way that was very solitary, and painting was a way for me to be solitary in a different way... It was a way for me to be alone with my thoughts."

### 4. **Setting Boundaries**
Arca established boundaries for himself when it came to painting, setting aside specific times and days to focus on his art. This allowed him to prioritize his creative time and avoid burnout. As he stated in an interview with The Fader, "I had to set boundaries for myself, like, 'Okay, I'm going to paint from 10 to 2, and then I'm going to take a break'... It was a way for me to take care of myself."

### 5. **Embracing Imperfection**
Arca's approach to painting was characterized by a willingness to embrace imperfection. He recognized that his paintings didn't have to be perfect, and that the process of creating was more important than the end result. This mindset allowed him to focus on the therapeutic benefits of painting, rather than worrying about creating a masterpiece. As he noted in an interview with i-D, "I'm not trying to make a perfect painting... I'm trying to make a painting that feels true to me."
