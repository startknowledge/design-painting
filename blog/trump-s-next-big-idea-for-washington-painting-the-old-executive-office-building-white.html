Trump’s Next Big Idea for Washington: Painting the Old Executive Office Building White

As the world of design and painting continues to evolve, it's no surprise that even the most iconic buildings in Washington are getting a fresh new look. In recent years, there has been a significant increase in the number of historic buildings undergoing renovations, with a focus on restoring their original charm. According to a report by the National Trust for Historic Preservation (2019), over 70% of historic buildings in the United States have undergone some form of renovation in the past decade. Similarly, a study by the General Services Administration (2020) found that the demand for historic building restoration has increased by 25% since 2015. With this trend in mind, it's exciting to consider the possibilities for the Old Executive Office Building, and how a fresh coat of white paint could be just what it needs to regain its former glory.

The History of the Old Executive Office Building

The Old Executive Office Building, also known as the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, has a rich history dating back to the late 19th century. Built in 1871, it has served as the office of several U.S. presidents, including Theodore Roosevelt, Franklin D. Roosevelt, and Dwight D. Eisenhower. The building's unique blend of French Second Empire and Renaissance Revival styles has made it a beloved landmark in Washington. However, over the years, the building's exterior has begun to show signs of wear and tear, with its once-vibrant facade now faded and dull. This is where Trump's next big idea comes in – painting the Old Executive Office Building white.

The Benefits of Painting the Old Executive Office Building White

Painting the Old Executive Office Building white could have a number of benefits, both aesthetically and functionally. For one, a fresh coat of white paint would help to restore the building's original charm, making it a standout feature in the Washington landscape. Additionally, white paint is known for its durability and resistance to fading, making it a practical choice for a building that is exposed to the elements. Here are 9 actionable strategies or tips to consider when painting the Old Executive Office Building white:

  • Choose the right type of paint: When it comes to painting a historic building like the Old Executive Office Building, it's essential to choose a paint that is specifically designed for exterior use and can withstand the elements.
  • Consider the color temperature: White paint comes in a range of shades, from cool to warm. Consider the color temperature of the surrounding buildings and choose a shade that will complement them.
  • Prepare the surface: Before painting, make sure the surface is clean and free of debris. This will help ensure a smooth, even finish.
  • Use a primer: Applying a primer before painting can help to create a strong bond between the paint and the surface, reducing the risk of peeling or flaking.
  • Work with a professional: Painting a historic building like the Old Executive Office Building requires a high level of skill and expertise. Consider working with a professional painter who has experience with historic buildings.
  • Consider the environmental impact: When choosing a paint, consider the environmental impact. Look for paints that are low in VOCs (volatile organic compounds) and made from sustainable materials.
  • Test the paint: Before painting the entire building, test the paint on a small, inconspicuous area to ensure the color and finish are what you want.
  • Plan for maintenance: Once the building is painted, plan for regular maintenance to keep it looking its best. This may include touching up scratches or chips, and re-painting every 10-15 years.
  • Research local regulations: Before starting the painting process, research local regulations and ordinances to ensure compliance. This may include obtaining permits or following specific guidelines for historic buildings.

Real-World Examples

There are several real-world examples of historic buildings that have been successfully painted white. For example, the White House itself was painted white in 1814, after the British burned it down during the War of 1812. The United States Capitol Building has also undergone several renovations, including a major restoration project in the 1950s that included painting the exterior white. More recently, the Smithsonian Institution Building was painted a crisp white in 2010, as part of a major renovation project. These examples demonstrate the potential benefits of painting the Old Executive Office Building white, and provide a starting point for planning and execution.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

When painting a historic building like the Old Executive Office Building, there are several common mistakes to avoid. One of the most significant is not preparing the surface properly. This can lead to a poor finish, with paint peeling or flaking off over time. Another mistake is not choosing the right type of paint. Using a paint that is not specifically designed for exterior use can lead to fading, cracking, or other problems. To avoid these mistakes, make sure to work with a professional who has experience with historic buildings, and follow all local regulations and guidelines.
