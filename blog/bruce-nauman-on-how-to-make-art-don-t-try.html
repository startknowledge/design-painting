Bruce Nauman on How to Make Art: Don’t Try

As of 2022, the art market has experienced significant growth, with sales reaching over $67 billion, a 29% increase from 2020 (Source: Art Market Report 2022). This surge in interest has led many aspiring artists to seek guidance on how to create successful art pieces. One artist who has been a significant influence on the contemporary art scene is Bruce Nauman, known for his innovative and often provocative works. In this blog post, we will explore Bruce Nauman's approach to art-making, which can be summarized as "**don't try**". We will delve into the reasoning behind this philosophy and provide actionable strategies for artists to create authentic and meaningful art.

Introduction to Bruce Nauman's Philosophy

Bruce Nauman's approach to art is rooted in the idea that **trying too hard** can be detrimental to the creative process. He believes that when artists try to force their ideas or conform to certain standards, they risk losing the essence of their art. Instead, Nauman advocates for a more **organic and intuitive** approach, where the artist allows their thoughts and feelings to guide the creative process. This philosophy is reflected in his own work, which often features simple, yet powerful and thought-provoking pieces.

Strategies for Creating Art without Trying

So, how can artists apply Bruce Nauman's philosophy to their own work? Here are 10 actionable strategies to help you create art without trying:

  • Let go of expectations: Release the need for your art to be perfect or meet certain standards. Allow yourself to experiment and make mistakes.
  • Follow your instincts: Trust your intuition and let your emotions guide your creative decisions.
  • Keep it simple: Avoid overcomplicating your art with too many elements or concepts. Focus on simplicity and clarity.
  • Experiment with different mediums: Don't be afraid to try new materials or techniques. This can help you discover new ways of expressing yourself.
  • Don't overthink it: Avoid analyzing your art too much. Instead, focus on the process and let your subconscious guide you.
  • Take breaks and step away: Give yourself time to reflect on your work and come back to it with fresh eyes.
  • Collaborate with others: Working with other artists or individuals from different disciplines can help stimulate your creativity and bring new ideas to the table.
  • Embrace failure: View failures as opportunities for growth and learning. Don't be discouraged by setbacks or criticism.
  • Stay true to yourself: Authenticity is key to creating meaningful art. Stay true to your vision and values, even if they don't align with mainstream trends.
  • Practice regularly: Consistency is essential for developing your skills and finding your unique voice as an artist.

Real-World Examples of Bruce Nauman's Philosophy in Action

Bruce Nauman's own work is a testament to the power of his philosophy. For example, his piece "**Untitled (Portrait of Navajo Man)**" (1960) features a simple, yet powerful image of a Navajo man. The piece is not trying to convey a specific message or meet certain standards, but rather allows the viewer to interpret and connect with the image on a deeper level. Another example is his "**Corridor Installation (Nick Wilder)**" (1970), which features a narrow corridor that challenges the viewer's perception of space and navigation. This piece is a prime example of how Nauman's philosophy can lead to innovative and thought-provoking art.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

While Bruce Nauman's philosophy can be liberating, there are common mistakes that artists can make when trying to apply it to their own work. Here are a few pitfalls to avoid:

  • Overthinking and analyzing: While it's essential to reflect on your work, overanalyzing can lead to paralysis and stagnation.
  • Comparing yourself to others: Focus on your own unique voice and vision, rather than comparing yourself to other artists.
  • Trying to force inspiration: Don't try to force creativity or inspiration. Instead, allow yourself to be open to new ideas and experiences.
  • Being too hard on yourself: Remember that mistakes and failures are an essential part of the creative process. Don't be too critical of yourself or your work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Here are some frequently asked questions about Bruce Nauman's philosophy and how to apply it to your own art practice:

Question: What if I'm not sure what kind of art I want to make?
Don't worry if you're not sure what kind of art you want to make. Experiment with different mediums and techniques, and allow yourself to explore different styles and themes.
Question: How do I know if my art is good or not?
Don't worry too much about whether your art is "good" or not. Focus on the process and enjoy the journey of creating. Remember that art is subjective, and what matters most is that you're true to yourself and your vision.
Question: What if I'm not inspired or motivated to create art?
Don't try to force inspiration or motivation. Instead, take breaks, step away from your work, and allow yourself to recharge. Engage in activities that bring you joy and stimulate your creativity.
Question: How do I deal with criticism or negative feedback?
Remember that criticism and negative feedback are a natural part of the creative process. Don't take it personally, and instead, use it as an opportunity to learn and grow. Focus on your own unique voice and vision, and don't let others define your worth as an artist.
Question: Can I still make a living as an artist if I don't try to conform to mainstream trends?
Yes, it's possible to make a living as an artist without conforming to mainstream trends. Focus on building a loyal audience and community, and engage in alternative forms of income, such as teaching, workshops, or commissions. Remember that success as an artist is not solely defined by commercial success, but by the impact and meaning your art brings to others.

Conclusion

In conclusion, Bruce Nauman's philosophy of "**don't try**" offers a refreshing and liberating approach to art-making. By letting go of expectations, following your instincts, and embracing simplicity, you can create art that is authentic, meaningful, and impactful. Remember to avoid common mistakes, such as overthinking and comparing yourself to others, and instead focus on the process and enjoy the journey of creating. Don't be afraid to take risks, experiment, and push the boundaries of what's possible. As Bruce Nauman once said, "**the most important thing is to keep making art**". So, what are you waiting for? Start creating, and let your art speak for itself.
