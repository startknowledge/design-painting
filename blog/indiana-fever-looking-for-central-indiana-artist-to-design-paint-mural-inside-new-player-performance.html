8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

1. Research the Indiana Fever’s Brand & Vision

Before pitching, deeply understand the team’s:

  • Mission & values (e.g., "Empowering Women Through Basketball")
  • Recent initiatives (e.g., youth programs, community outreach)
  • Fanbase demographics (e.g., family-friendly, diverse, sports-enthusiastic)

How to do this:

  • Watch team highlights, press conferences, and social media
  • Read recent news articles (e.g., Indianapolis Star, WNBA.com)
  • Study their current branding (logos, uniforms, marketing campaigns)

Example: If the team emphasizes youth development, your mural could feature young athletes training alongside legends, symbolizing the next generation.

2. Study the Performance Center’s Aesthetic & Space

Since the mural will be inside a functional facility, consider:

  • Location & size (walls, ceilings, or interactive elements?)
  • Lighting conditions (natural vs. artificial—will colors appear differently?)
  • Functional needs (e.g., a mural that doubles as a team motivation board)

How to gather intel:

  • Visit the construction site (if allowed) or check city planning documents
  • Ask the team for renderings or floor plans
  • Research similar facilities (e.g., NBA’s training centers, college gyms)

Real-World Example: The Golden State Warriors’ training facility features dynamic murals that shift with lighting, creating an immersive experience. If the Indiana Fever’s space has smart lighting, your design could incorporate interactive color changes.

3. Develop a Portfolio That Aligns with Sports & Community Themes

Your portfolio should showcase: ✔ Large-scale murals (if possible) ✔ Thematic work (e.g., hoops, resilience, Indiana landmarks) ✔ Collaborative projects (if you’ve worked with brands or nonprofits)

If you lack sports-related work:

  • Create a mockup of a basketball-themed mural
  • Highlight community projects (e.g., murals for schools, parks)
  • Study artists who’ve worked in sports (e.g., Shepard Fairey’s NBA collaborations)

4. Craft a Compelling Proposal with a Strong Narrative

Your proposal should include:

  1. A cover letter explaining why you’re the best fit
  2. 3-5 mural concepts (sketches, digital renderings, or descriptions)
  3. A timeline & budget breakdown (if possible)
  4. Past client testimonials (especially from corporate or public projects)

Pro Tip:

  • Use storytelling—explain how your mural will enhance the player experience and connect with fans.
  • Mention local ties (e.g., "As a Central Indiana artist, I understand the importance of representing our community’s spirit.")

5. Leverage Local & National Networks

  • Connect with the Indiana Fever’s art & design team (LinkedIn, email)
  • Attend local art fairs, sports events, or chamber of commerce meetings
  • Partner with a local gallery or arts organization for referrals

Example: In **2022, the Los Angeles Lakers commissioned a mural by **Los Angeles-based artist Kehinde Wiley after a gallery owner introduced them. Similarly, a Hoosier art collective could help your proposal reach the right decision-makers.

6. Offer a Hybrid Approach (Traditional + Digital)

Since the mural will be inside a high-traffic area, consider:

  • A mix of hand-painted and digital elements (for durability)
  • Interactive features (e.g., QR codes linking to team history)
  • Modular designs (if the mural needs updates over time)

Real-World Example: The Chicago Bulls’ training facility features a digital mural that changes with game stats, keeping the space dynamic. If the Indiana Fever allows tech integration, this could be a unique selling point.

7. Prepare for the Interview & Presentation

If selected for an interview, expect questions like:

  • "How will your mural inspire the players?"
  • "What materials will you use for longevity?"
  • "Can you adapt if the space changes?"

How to impress:

  • Bring physical samples (textures, color swatches)
  • **Show a 3D mockup (if possible)
  • Discuss maintenance plans (e.g., sealing, cleaning)

8. Follow Up Professionally & Stay Persistent

  • Send a thank-you email after the initial pitch
  • Offer to visit the site for final adjustments
  • Stay engaged with the team’s social media

Common Mistake to Avoid:

  • Assuming the team will choose the first applicant—many artists apply, so stand out with creativity and professionalism.

FAQ Section: Everything You Need to Know About Winning the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

1. How do I find out if the Indiana Fever is still accepting mural proposals?

Answer: The Indiana Fever typically announces major projects through press releases, social media, and local art organizations. To stay updated:

  • Follow the team on Instagram (@IndianaFever) and Facebook
  • **Check the Indiana Convention & Visitors Bureau for new developments
  • **Contact the team’s art & design department directly via email (info@indianafever.com)
  • Join local artist groups (e.g., Indiana Art Education Association) for updates

Pro Tip: If the project is already underway, you may need to pitch a smaller mural or future collaboration instead.

2. What materials should I use for an indoor mural in a high-traffic facility?

Answer: Since the mural will be inside a performance center, durability and low VOC (volatile organic compounds) are key. Recommended materials:

  • Acrylic paints (fast-drying, easy to clean, durable)
  • Epoxy or polyurethane sealant (for extra protection against wear)
  • Gesso primer (to prevent paint from bleeding through)
  • High-quality brushes & rollers (for smooth application)

Avoid:

  • Oil paints (slow drying, can yellow over time)
  • Cheap, chalky paints (will fade quickly)

Example: The Los Angeles Lakers’ mural used **acrylic paints with a UV-resistant sealant to ensure longevity.

3. How can I make my mural stand out from other applicants?

Answer: To differentiate yourself, focus on: ✅ A unique concept (e.g., a "time capsule" mural with layers of Indiana history) ✅ Strong storytelling (e.g., "The Journey of the Fever" – from founding to today) ✅ Collaboration with local artists (e.g., inviting a Hoosier painter to co-create)
