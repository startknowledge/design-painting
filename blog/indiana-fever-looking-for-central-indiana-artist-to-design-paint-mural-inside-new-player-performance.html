This state-of-the-art facility isn’t just about basketball—it’s about inspiration, community, and artistic expression. As part of this exciting project, the team is actively seeking a central Indiana-based artist to design and paint a large-scale mural inside the facility, serving as a permanent visual anchor for players, staff, and fans.

This isn’t just another commission—it’s a high-visibility opportunity to leave a lasting mark on a major sports and cultural landmark. With the Fever’s growing fanbase (boasting over 100,000 season ticket holders as of 2023) and a $50 million+ investment in the new facility, this mural could become one of the most iconic pieces of public art in Indiana.

Why This Mural Commission Could Be a Game-Changer for Your Career

Before we explore the how, let’s discuss the why. Why should you—as a Central Indiana artist—care about this opportunity?

1. Exposure to a Massive Audience

The Indiana Fever’s Player Performance Center will be a hub for basketball culture, visited by:

  • Players, coaches, and staff (who will see your work daily)
  • Fans, school groups, and tourists (the facility will likely offer tours)
  • Local media and influencers (who may feature your mural in articles or social posts)

A single mural here could catapult your reputation beyond just Central Indiana—nationally and even internationally.

2. A Chance to Work with a Professional Sports Team

The Fever isn’t just a basketball team—they’re a brand with a mission. Their 2023 community impact report highlighted:

  • Over 100,000 youth reached through their Fever Family Foundation
  • Partnerships with 50+ local organizations (including schools, nonprofits, and hospitals)
  • A strong commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion in their programming

This means your mural won’t just be decorative—it should reflect their values while also inspiring the next generation of athletes and artists.

3. A High-Profile Commission with Long-Term Visibility

Unlike one-off gigs (like a restaurant or private home), this mural will be permanent and high-traffic. Unlike temporary installations, it will age with the facility, gaining more recognition over time.

4. Networking with Industry Leaders

The Fever’s artistic advisory board (if they have one) and facility partners may include:

  • Local art collectives
  • Sports marketing agencies
  • Public art curators
  • Other established muralists who could become future collaborators

This could lead to additional commissions, mentorship opportunities, or even a career shift into public art.

5. A Chance to Shape the Narrative of Indiana’s Sports Culture

Indiana has a rich history of public art, from the Indianapolis Motor Speedway murals to the Monument Circle sculptures. Your mural could become a new chapter in the state’s visual storytelling.

8 Actionable Strategies to Create a Winning Proposal for the Indiana Fever Mural

Now that you understand the opportunity, let’s break down how to position yourself for success. Here are eight key strategies to craft a compelling proposal:

Strategy 1: Research the Indiana Fever’s Brand & Values Deeply

Before picking up a brush, you need to understand the Fever’s identity.

What to Study:

✔ Their Mission & Vision – The Fever’s website and 2023 annual report emphasize:

  • "Empowering women through basketball"
  • "Building a community where everyone belongs"
  • "Inspiring the next generation"

✔ Their Player & Fan Demographics – The team has a strong female fanbase (especially in Central Indiana schools) and a diverse roster (as of 2024, they include players from 10+ countries).

✔ Their Past Public Engagement – Have they commissioned art before? Check their social media (Instagram, Twitter) for any past collaborations with local artists.

✔ The Facility’s Purpose – The Player Performance Center will include:

  • Training rooms
  • Recovery spaces
  • Community engagement areas

Your mural should reflect: ✅ Inspiration (for players) ✅ Community (for fans) ✅ Diversity & inclusion (for representation)

Example: If you’re inspired by Indiana’s agricultural roots, you could blend hoops with farm imagery—but ensure it ties back to the team’s empowerment message.

Strategy 2: Choose a Theme That Resonates with Multiple Audiences

The best murals don’t just look good—they tell a story. For the Fever’s mural, consider themes that:

  • Inspire athletes (strength, resilience, teamwork)
  • Engage fans (nostalgia, local pride, female empowerment)
  • Reflect Indiana’s identity (agriculture, music, history)

Potential Theme Ideas:

  1. "The Journey of a Champion" – A visual timeline of a player’s growth, from little league to the WNBA, incorporating Indiana landmarks (e.g., the Soldiers and Sailors Monument, the Indiana Statehouse).
  2. "Unity in Diversity" – A collage of faces representing the Fever’s global roster, local fans, and Indiana’s multicultural communities.
  3. "Roots & Wings" – A blend of Indiana’s rural heritage (farms, fields) with urban energy (hoops, cityscapes), symbolizing growth and aspiration.
  4. "The Heart of the Hoosier State" – A symbolic representation of Indiana’s music (bluegrass, jazz), sports (baseball, football), and art—all tied to basketball.
  5. "Legacy & Future" – A dual-layer mural where one side shows past Fever legends, and the other depicts future stars (perhaps even local youth who could be inspired to play).

Pro Tip: If you’re struggling for ideas, look at Indiana’s existing murals (like the Indianapolis Public Library’s "Indiana: The Crossroads of America") for inspiration—but put your own twist on it.

Strategy 3: Develop a Strong Concept Statement (Your "Elevator Pitch")

When submitting your proposal, you’ll likely need a 1-2 page concept statement explaining:

  • Your vision for the mural
  • Why this theme matters
  • How it aligns with the Fever’s mission
  • Your artistic approach (technique, materials, symbolism)

Example Concept Statement:

"The mural ‘Roots & Wings’ will serve as a visual metaphor for the Indiana Fever’s journey—rooted in the state’s agricultural and cultural heritage, yet soaring toward the global stage of the WNBA. By integrating Indiana landscapes with dynamic basketball imagery, the piece will inspire players with a sense of place while connecting fans to the team’s legacy. Using layered acrylic and mixed-media techniques, the mural will feature subtle nods to local landmarks, ensuring it feels uniquely Hoosier while remaining timeless and aspirational."

Key Elements to Include: ✔ Emotional hook (why should they care?) ✔ Clear connection to the Fever’s brand ✔ Your artistic process (how will you execute it?) ✔ Potential impact (how will this mural elevate the space?)

Strategy 4: Create a Mood Board (Even If You Don’t Use It in Your Proposal)

A mood board helps you visualize your vision before committing to a final design. You can:

  • Sketch rough ideas (even if they’re messy)
  • Collect color palettes (Indiana’s blue skies, green fields, and red clay could work well)
  • Gather reference images (without copying—just for inspiration)

Tools to Use:

  • Pinterest (for color schemes)
  • Procreate/Photoshop (for digital sketches)
  • Physical collages (magazines, fabric, paint swatches)

Why This Matters: A strong mood board shows depth in your proposal. If the Fever’s team sees you’ve thoughtfully planned your design, they’ll take you more seriously.

Strategy 5: Choose the Right Medium & Technique for the Space

The Player Performance Center is likely a high-traffic, high-impact area, so your mural must be:

  • Durable (spray paint or acrylic on gessoed wall works best)
  • Scalable (if the space is large, consider modular sections)
  • Responsive to lighting (natural light vs. artificial—test your colors!)

Best Mural Techniques for This Project:

Medium Pros Cons Best For
Acrylic Paint Fast-drying, vibrant, durable Can crack if not prepped properly Most indoor murals
Spray Paint Smooth finish, great for texture Requires ventilation, harder to correct Bold, graphic designs
Mixed Media (acrylic + collage) Unique texture, depth More labor-intensive Story-driven murals
Digital Projection + Paint Precise, scalable Requires projection equipment Complex, large-scale work

Pro Tip: If the Fever has a preferred artist or contractor, check if they’ve worked with specific techniques before. Some teams have brand guidelines for public art.

Strategy 6: Plan for Logistics & Budget Realistically

Before submitting, ask yourself:

  • How much time will this take? (A 10’x20’ mural could take 2-4 weeks full-time.)
  • What materials will you need? (Gesso, primer, high-quality paint, stencils if applicable.)
  • Will you need scaffolding or safety gear? (Indoor murals often require fall protection.)
  • What’s your rate? (Public art commissions can vary—$5,000–$50,000+ depending on size and complexity.)

How to Present This in Your Proposal:

*"Based on the mural’s estimated size (20’x30’) and my experience with similar projects, I propose a 4-week timeline with a phased approach to ensure quality. My material costs will be covered by the commission, and my labor rate is $X per hour/day."*

Budget Breakdown Example:

Expense Estimated Cost
Materials (paint, primer, tools) $1,500
Safety gear (harness, scaffolding) $800
Travel & permits $300
Total $2,600 (for a mid-sized mural)

(Note: This is a rough estimate—adjust based on your experience.)

Strategy 7: Showcase Your Portfolio with Relevant Work

Your portfolio is your best sales tool. When submitting, include:

  • 3-5 strong examples of large-scale murals (if possible)
  • A mix of styles (abstract, realistic, symbolic)
  • Before/after shots (if you’ve worked on restorations or renovations)
  • Testimonials from past clients (especially public or corporate ones)

What to Avoid: ❌ Overly complex designs (if the space is high-traffic, simplicity may work better) ❌ Colors that don’t hold up under artificial light ❌ Designs that are too literal (e.g., just a Fever logo—they want art, not advertising)

Example Portfolio Piece:

"My mural at [Local Café, 2023] featured a dynamic abstract take on Indiana’s agricultural history, using geometric shapes to represent crops and machinery. The piece was installed in a high-foot-traffic area and received positive feedback from both patrons and local art critics."

Strategy 8: Submit a Professional, Well-Structured Proposal

Your proposal should be clear, concise, and visually engaging. Here’s a template to follow:

1. Cover Letter (1 page max)

  • Why you? (Your background, experience, passion for the project.)
  • Why the Fever? (How you align with their mission.)
  • Your approach (Briefly summarize your concept.)

2. Concept Statement (1-2 pages)

  • Theme & inspiration
  • Visual description (what will it look like?)
  • Symbolism & meaning
  • Technical approach (medium, technique, timeline)

3. Portfolio Samples (3-5 high-quality images)

  • Labeled with project name, year, and medium.
  • Include a short caption explaining the context.

4. Budget & Timeline

  • Material costs
  • Labor estimate
  • Projected timeline (from sketch to completion)

5. References & Testimonials

  • 2-3 past clients (with contact info if possible).

Pro Tip: If you’re emailing or mailing, use a PDF portfolio (not just a website link—some teams prefer offline materials).
