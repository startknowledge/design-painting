Why the Indiana Fever’s Mural Project Is a Big Deal

1. The Fever’s Growth & Community Impact

The Indiana Fever, founded in 2000, has become a cornerstone of Central Indiana’s sports culture. With record-breaking attendance (averaging 10,000+ fans per game in recent seasons) and a dedicated fanbase, the team has a strong local identity.

Their new Player Performance Center—scheduled to open in 2025—will be a showcase for talent development, featuring:

  • State-of-the-art training facilities
  • Player recovery suites
  • Community engagement spaces

A custom mural inside this center won’t just be decorative—it will tell a story, inspire players, and engage visitors, making it a high-impact public art project.

2. Why Public & Sports Murals Are in Demand

Public art, especially in sports and performance centers, is booming. According to the National Endowment for the Arts (2022), public art spending in the U.S. reached $1.2 billion annually, with a 15% increase in sports-related commissions over the past five years.

Why?

  • Corporate & team branding – Murals reinforce identity.
  • Player motivation – Visual storytelling can boost morale.
  • Fan engagement – Unique art draws visitors and social media attention.
  • Community pride – Local artists get recognition.

The Fever’s mural will likely fall into multiple categories, making it a versatile and valuable project for your portfolio.

3. The Competitive Edge: Why Local Artists Win

The Fever has expressed a preference for hiring Central Indiana artists to support the local economy. This means: ✔ Less competition from out-of-state artists ✔ Higher visibility within the community ✔ Potential for repeat business (future Fever projects, corporate clients, etc.)

If you’re a local artist, this is your chance to prove your skills on a high-profile project while building relationships with the team and its stakeholders.

8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

Now that you understand the opportunity, let’s break down how to position yourself as the best candidate.

Strategy 1: Research the Fever’s Brand & Aesthetic Inside Out

Before pitching, you need to speak their language. The Fever’s brand is energetic, inclusive, and community-driven, with a modern yet nostalgic vibe.

How to align your proposal:

  • Study their logo, colors, and past visuals (check their official website and social media).
  • Look at their arena (Gainbridge Fieldhouse)—what kind of art already exists there?
  • Analyze their player roster—are there any local heroes (e.g., Brittney Griner’s Indiana ties) you could reference?
  • Check their social media—what kind of content performs best? (e.g., fan art, player spotlights, community events)

Example: If the Fever’s brand is dynamic and empowering, your mural could feature:

  • Abstract energy lines (like a basketball court’s motion)
  • Local landmarks (e.g., the Indiana Statehouse, Monument Circle, or a stylized "IN" motif)
  • Player silhouettes in mid-action, symbolizing teamwork and excellence

Pro Tip: If you can incorporate a subtle "Indiana" element (like a Hoosier Hat, a basketball, or a sunflower motif), it will resonate deeply with the team and fans.

Strategy 2: Define Your Mural Concept with a Clear Vision

The Fever won’t just want any mural—they’ll want one that tells a story. Your proposal should include:

  1. A strong central theme (e.g., "Rise Together," "Hoosier Pride," "The Journey to Greatness")
  2. Key visual elements (symbols, colors, textures)
  3. A mood board (even if digital—describe it in detail)
  4. A scale drawing (if possible, or a detailed description of dimensions)

Example Mural Concepts:

Theme Visual Elements Why It Works
"The Hoosier Heart" A stylized Indiana map with basketballs as "dots," a rising sun (symbolizing growth), and player silhouettes in motion. Connects to local pride while keeping it sports-focused.
"Unity in Motion" Interlocking hands forming a basketball net, with diverse player figures in dynamic poses. Reinforces teamwork and inclusivity—key Fever values.
"From the Streets to the Court" A graffiti-inspired mural with Indiana street art influences, featuring local murals as inspiration (e.g., Downtown Indy’s public art). Shows local connection while keeping it modern and energetic.

Pro Tip: If you can include a "storyboard" (even in text form), explaining how the mural evolves from one section to another, it will demonstrate your planning skills.

Strategy 3: Showcase Your Technical Skills & Experience

The Fever will want to know:

  • Can you handle large-scale work?
  • Do you have experience with murals in public/performance spaces?
  • What materials will you use? (Acrylics? Spray paint? Mixed media?)

How to prove your expertise: ✅ Include a portfolio (even if digital) with similar-sized murals. ✅ Mention any sports-related work (e.g., stadium murals, team logos, athlete portraits). ✅ Specify your technique—will you use stencils, freehand, or digital projection? ✅ Provide references from past clients (especially if they’re corporate or public art projects).

Strategy 4: Leverage Local Connections & Networking

The Fever prefers local artists, so who you know matters. Here’s how to get your name in front of them:

  2. Reach Out to Fever Staff

    • Social media: Follow and engage with @IndianaFever and key staff (e.g., GM, Marketing Director).
    • Email: A short, professional pitch (see Strategy 7 for a template).

  3. Partner with Local Art Collectives

    • Groups like Indy Art Collective or The Art Guild of Indiana can vouch for your work.

Strategy 5: Craft a Winning Proposal (With a Strong Pitch Deck)

Your proposal should be professional, visually compelling, and easy to understand. Here’s what to include:

1. Cover Letter (1 Page Max)

  • Hook: Start with why you’re excited about the project.
  • Your background: Briefly mention relevant experience.
  • Your vision: One sentence summarizing your mural concept.
  • Call to action: "I’d love to discuss this further—are you available for a quick call?"

2. Portfolio Showcase (Digital or Print)

  • 3-5 strongest murals (with high-res images if possible).
  • A "similar project" example (even if not sports-related).

3. Mural Concept Details

  • Theme & meaning (e.g., "This mural represents the journey of Hoosier athletes, from local courts to the WNBA.")
  • Color palette (match Fever’s purple, gold, and white).
  • Materials & technique (e.g., "Acrylic on canvas with stencil elements for precision.")
  • Timeline (e.g., "I can complete the mural in 4 weeks with a dedicated team.")

4. Pricing & Budget

  • Be transparent but flexible. Example:

    "Based on the mural’s size (~1,200 sq. ft.) and my experience with large-scale public art, my proposed fee is $X,XXX, including materials, labor, and a 10% contingency for adjustments."

5. Testimonials & References

  • 1-2 quotes from past clients (e.g., "[Artist’s Name] delivered a stunning mural that exceeded our expectations—highly recommend!" – [Client Name], [Company]).

Real-World Example: Artist: [Name Redacted] (Portland-based, but worked with NBA teams)

  • What Made Their Proposal Stand Out:
    • A 3D rendering of the mural in the Fever’s space.
    • A breakdown of costs with savings estimates (e.g., "Using local materials reduces costs by 15%.").
    • A "vision board" with inspiration images (even if not exact matches).

Pro Tip: If you can’t afford a 3D render, describe it in detail—some teams will visualize it better than a flat image.

Strategy 6: Highlight Your Ability to Work with Constraints

Public art projects often have limitations—budget, space, materials. Show that you can adapt.

Common Constraints & Solutions:

Constraint Your Solution
Limited budget "I can use cost-effective materials like acrylic paint on canvas panels instead of fresco."
Tight timeline "I have a dedicated assistant who can help with prep work, allowing me to focus on execution."
Space restrictions "I’ll design a modular mural that can be installed in sections."
Brand guidelines "I’ll provide multiple color variations to match Fever’s exact palette."

Real-World Example: Artist: [Name Redacted] (Worked on a school mural with a $5K budget)

  • Challenge: The school wanted high-quality work but had strict funding limits.
  • Solution: Used local artists’ co-op discounts for materials and volunteer labor for prep.
  • Result: The mural was completed on time and under budget, leading to repeat commissions.

Pro Tip: If you can’t meet a constraint, offer an alternative—teams appreciate problem-solving.

  • Follow up politely if you don’t hear back in 2-3 weeks.
