The Indiana Fever, the WNBA’s only team in the state, is expanding its legacy with the opening of a brand-new Player Performance Center in Central Indiana. This state-of-the-art facility isn’t just about athletics—it’s a visionary space where art, culture, and sports intersect. The team is actively seeking a local Central Indiana artist to design and paint a large-scale mural inside the facility, offering a high-visibility platform for creative expression.

With the Fever’s growing fanbase (boasting over 50,000 season ticket holders as of 2023) and a $100+ million expansion plan, this mural could become a landmark piece in Indiana’s artistic landscape.

By the end, you’ll have a clear roadmap to not only apply for this opportunity but also elevate your career in the process.

8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

Now that you understand the opportunity, let’s break down how to make your proposal stand out.

1. Research the Indiana Fever’s Brand & Aesthetic

Before picking up a brush, you need to speak their language.

What to Study:

  • Team Colors & Logo: The Fever’s purple, gold, and black palette is bold and energetic. Your mural should complement (not compete with) their branding.
  • Player & Fan Culture: The team has a strong fanbase known for passion and community involvement. Highlight local heroes, Indiana landmarks, or sports legends in your concept.
  • Previous Public Art: While the Fever hasn’t done murals before, look at similar WNBA teams (e.g., Las Vegas Aces’ "City of Lights" mural) for inspiration.

Pro Tip: If you can incorporate a local Indiana element (like the Hoosier spirit, Indianapolis skyline, or even a nod to the team’s history), you’ll instantly elevate your proposal.

2. Define Your Unique Artistic Style & Niche

The Fever wants more than just a pretty picture—they want art that tells a story.

Ask Yourself:

  • Are you a realist, abstract, or surrealist painter?
  • Do you specialize in digital murals, frescoes, or traditional acrylic/paint?
  • Can you blend sports motifs with cultural symbols?

Example Artists to Study:

  • Shepard Fairey (for bold, graphic designs)
  • Keith Haring (for energetic, community-driven murals)
  • Jean-Michel Basquiat (for raw, expressive sports-inspired works)

Why It Matters: If you can clearly articulate your style in your proposal, you’ll stand out from generic muralists.

3. Propose a Concept That Aligns with the Facility’s Purpose

The mural isn’t just for aesthetics—it’s part of the player experience.

Key Themes to Explore: ✅ Motivation & Resilience – A mural that inspires athletes (e.g., a rising sun, a phoenix, or a basketball hoop with wings). ✅ Community & Connection – Featuring local faces, Indiana landmarks, or fan symbols. ✅ Innovation & Growth – Since this is a new facility, consider futuristic elements (e.g., digital integration, AI-inspired designs). ✅ Cultural Fusion – Blending Hoosier traditions with modern sports culture.

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles Lakers’ "City of Angels" mural (by Shepard Fairey) wasn’t just a logo—it was a celebration of LA’s diversity, making it iconic. Your mural could do the same for Indiana.

4. Create a Portfolio That Proves You’re the Right Fit

A strong portfolio is your first handshake with the Fever.

What to Include: ✔ Large-scale murals (if possible) ✔ Sports-themed or motivational art ✔ Work that incorporates local culture ✔ A mood board (if you’re submitting digitally) showing your design process

Portfolio Red Flags: ❌ Only small-scale or abstract work (unless it’s highly relevant) ❌ No clear narrative in your pieces ❌ Poor quality images (blurry, poorly lit)

Pro Tip: If you don’t have a sports mural yet, create a mockup—even a digital sketch can show your vision.

5. Submit a Proposal That Goes Beyond "I Can Paint"

The Fever likely receives dozens of applications. Yours needs to stand out.

What to Include in Your Proposal:

  1. A Cover Letter – Explain why you’re excited about this project and how it fits your career.
  2. A Concept Sketch – Even if rough, it shows your creative process.
  3. A Budget Breakdown – Be transparent about costs (materials, labor, permits).
  4. References – Past clients who can vouch for your professionalism and quality.
  5. A Timeline – How long will the mural take? Can you work around the facility’s schedule?

Example of a Strong Proposal: *"I propose a three-panel mural titled ‘Rise of the Hoosier,’ featuring:

  • Panel 1: A stylized Indiana map with player silhouettes emerging from key cities.
  • Panel 2: A dynamic basketball scene with local athletes (past and present).
  • Panel 3: A futuristic, motivational quote in the team’s color palette. This design honors Indiana’s history while inspiring the next generation of players."*

6. Leverage Local Connections & Networking

Sometimes, who you know matters as much as what you know.

How to Network:

  • Attend Fever games and introduce yourself to staff.
  • Partner with local arts organizations (e.g., Indianapolis Arts Council).
  • Reach out to past clients who might refer you.
  • Join Facebook groups like "Indiana Artists & Muralists" to stay updated on opportunities.

Real-World Example: Kehinde Wiley, the artist behind President Obama’s portrait, networked for years before landing major commissions. Your mural could be Indiana’s next big thing.

7. Understand the Technical & Logistical Requirements

Before signing a contract, know the rules.

Key Questions to Ask:

  • What are the mural’s dimensions? (A 10x20 ft. piece is very different from a 50x10 ft. wall.)
  • What surface will it be painted on? (Drywall, concrete, plywood?)
  • Are there structural limitations? (Beams, HVAC units, etc.)
  • What’s the timeline? (Will players be training nearby during painting?)
  • Who handles permits? (Some cities require public art permits.)

Common Mistake: Underestimating prep work. A smooth surface is non-negotiable for a high-quality mural.

8. Prepare for the Interview (If Selected)

If your proposal gets shortlisted, you may be asked to present in person.

How to Impress:

  • Bring physical samples (if possible).
  • Explain your process clearly—why you chose certain colors, themes, or techniques.
  • Show enthusiasm for the team’s mission.
  • Ask insightful questions (e.g., "How do players interact with this space daily?").

Example Interview Answer: "I noticed the facility has open training areas—I’d love to incorporate movement and energy into the mural, almost like a frozen moment of a game. Would that align with the team’s vision?"

Real-World Examples of Successful Sports-Themed Murals

Let’s look at three iconic murals that blended sports, culture, and art—and how you can apply their lessons to your proposal.

1. The Las Vegas Aces’ "City of Lights" Mural (Shepard Fairey)

Description: This massive, neon-lit mural in the Las Vegas Aces’ arena features stylized basketball players emerging from a cityscape, with gold and purple accents mirroring the team’s colors. The design celebrates Las Vegas’ nightlife and sports culture while keeping the team’s branding front and center.

Lessons for Indiana: ✔ Local + Team Branding – The mural feels like Vegas but is unmistakably Aces. ✔ Bold Colors & High Contrast – The neon glow makes it instantly recognizable. ✔ Dynamic Composition – The players aren’t static—they seem to move.

How to Adapt:

  • Instead of a cityscape, use Indiana’s rolling hills or the Indianapolis skyline.
  • Highlight local athletes (e.g., Mark Jackson, Tamika Catchings).

2. The Brooklyn Nets’ "Brooklyn Bridge" Mural (Various Artists)

Description: The Nets’ arena features multiple murals, including one by Faith Ringgold that depicts Brooklyn’s history through sports. Another mural by Jean-Michel Basquiat celebrates the city’s street art culture.

Lessons for Indiana: ✔ Storytelling Through Art – Each mural tells a different part of Brooklyn’s story. ✔ Mixed Media – Some pieces use fabric, paint, and collage for textural depth. ✔ Fan Engagement – The murals encourage visitors to explore the city’s culture.

How to Adapt:

  • Feature Indiana’s history (e.g., Hoosier Hall of Fame athletes, early basketball pioneers).
  • Use mixed media (e.g., embroidery, metalwork, or digital projections).

3. The Golden State Warriors’ "City of Gold" Mural (Various Local Artists)

Description: San Francisco’s Warriors arena has multiple murals by local artists, each reflecting the city’s diversity. One piece features **a basketball player emerging from a San Francisco Bay scene, while another honors the city’s LGBTQ+ community.

Lessons for Indiana: ✔ Community-Centric Design – The murals aren’t just for fans—they represent the city. ✔ Collaboration – The Warriors worked with multiple artists to showcase local talent. ✔ Inclusivity – The designs celebrate different cultures within the city.

How to Adapt:

  • Include diverse Hoosier voices (e.g., Indigenous art, immigrant stories, LGBTQ+ icons).
  • Partner with local artists to co-create the mural (if possible).

Common Mistakes to Avoid (And How to Fix Them)

Even the most talented artists can sabotage their own chances with simple errors. Here’s how to avoid them.

Mistake #1: Ignoring the Client’s Vision

What Happens: You submit a beautiful but unrelated mural (e.g., a surreal landscape when they want sports motivation).

How to Fix It:

  • Ask for a brief (even if informal).
  • Visit the facility (if possible) to understand the space.
  • Align your concept with the team’s values.

Mistake #2: Underestimating Costs

What Happens: You lowball your price, leading to disputes or unrealistic expectations.

How to Fix It:

  • Break down costs (materials, labor, permits).
  • Factor in time (large murals take weeks, not days).
  • Offer tiered pricing (e.g., basic, premium, and custom options).

Mistake #3: Poor Communication

What Happens: You don’t respond to emails, miss deadlines, or don’t clarify details.

How to Fix It:

  • Set clear expectations (e.g., "I’ll send a sketch by [date]").
  • Follow up professionally.
  • Use contracts for large commissions.

Mistake #4: Overcomplicating the Design

What Happens: Your mural is too abstract, too busy, or hard to understand.

How to Fix It:

  • Keep it balanced—one strong focal point with supporting elements.
  • Test your design on a small scale first.
  • Get feedback from trusted peers.

Mistake #5: Not Protecting Your Work

What Happens: You don’t copyright your design, and someone steals your idea.

How to Fix It:

  • Register your work with the U.S. Copyright Office.
  • Include a clause in contracts about ownership rights.
  • Document your process (sketches, emails, contracts).
