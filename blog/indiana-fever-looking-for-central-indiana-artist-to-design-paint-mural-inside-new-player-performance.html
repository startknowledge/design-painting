Indiana Fever Seeks Central Indiana Artist to Design & Paint a Stunning Mural Inside Their New Player Performance Center – How to Win This High-Profile Commission

Introduction: A Rare Opportunity for Local Artists in Central Indiana

The Indiana Fever, the WNBA’s only team in the state, is expanding its operations with the opening of a brand-new Player Performance Center in Indianapolis. This state-of-the-art facility isn’t just about basketball—it’s a visionary space designed to inspire athletes, fans, and the community. And at the heart of this project is a highly visible mural commission—a chance for a Central Indiana artist to leave a lasting mark on the city’s sports culture.

This isn’t just any mural. It’s a public-facing artwork that will be seen by players, coaches, staff, and thousands of fans during games, practices, and community events. The Indiana Fever is actively scouting local talent to bring their creative vision to life, and if you’re a muralist, painter, or visual artist in the area, this could be the career-defining opportunity you’ve been waiting for.

Why This Commission Matters

  • Exposure & Credibility: Being featured in a professional sports facility will elevate your portfolio and attract higher-paying clients in the future.
  • Community Impact: Your artwork will become a landmark in Indianapolis, recognized by locals and visitors alike.
  • Long-Term Revenue: Murals in public spaces often lead to repeat commissions from businesses, schools, and government entities.
  • Networking Opportunities: Collaborating with the Fever means connecting with sports teams, corporate sponsors, and other high-profile clients.

According to the 2023 State of the Arts Report by the National Endowment for the Arts (NEA), public art projects like these boost local economies by increasing tourism, property values, and community engagement. In fact, studies show that art-infused spaces can improve mental well-being, productivity, and even athletic performance—making this mural more than just decoration; it’s an investment in the city’s culture and sports legacy.

If you’re a Central Indiana artist with a passion for bold, meaningful murals, now is the time to position yourself as the top candidate. Below, we’ll break down how to secure this commission, including strategies, real-world examples, common mistakes to avoid, and actionable steps to stand out.

How the Indiana Fever’s Mural Commission Works: What You Need to Know

Before diving into strategies, let’s clarify how the selection process typically unfolds for high-profile mural commissions like this.

1. The Brief: What the Indiana Fever Is Looking For

While the exact details haven’t been publicly released, based on similar projects (like the NBA’s murals in arenas and WNBA team collaborations), we can infer that the Fever is likely seeking:

  • A theme that aligns with the team’s identity – Possible themes:
    • Indiana’s basketball heritage (e.g., Oscar Robertson, Rick Pitino, Indiana University’s success)
    • Female empowerment & sports legacy (honoring WNBA pioneers like Lisa Leslie, Candace Parker, or Tamika Catchings)
    • Central Indiana’s cultural diversity (celebrating the city’s music, art, and community)
    • A futuristic, high-energy aesthetic (since it’s a performance center, the mural could inspire athletes)
  • A mix of realism and abstract elements – Many modern murals blend hyper-realistic figures with dynamic, fluid backgrounds.
  • Durability & maintenance – The mural will be in a high-traffic area, so the materials must withstand UV exposure, humidity, and frequent cleaning.
  • Local flavor – The Fever wants an artist who understands Indiana’s identity and can incorporate unique regional elements.

2. The Selection Process (Likely Steps)

Most professional mural commissions follow a multi-stage process:

  1. Initial Submission (Portfolio Review)

    • Artists submit digital portfolios (high-res images of past work).
    • The Fever’s team (likely including marketing, community relations, and design staff) will shortlist candidates based on style, theme alignment, and technical skill.

  2. Concept Proposals (If Selected)

    • Shortlisted artists are asked to submit detailed concept sketches (digital or hand-drawn).
    • The proposal should include:
      • A storyboard (how the mural will flow visually).
      • Material recommendations (acrylic, spray paint, fresco, etc.).
      • A timeline (how long the project will take).
      • Budget considerations (if applicable).

  3. Interview & Presentation (Final Round)

    • Top candidates may be invited to present their ideas in person (or via video call).
    • The Fever might ask for live demonstrations or mock-ups to assess execution.

  4. Final Selection & Contract Signing

    • The chosen artist signs a contract outlining scope, payment, deadlines, and rights.

3. Payment & Compensation

While exact figures aren’t public, similar professional mural commissions in the $50,000–$200,000+ range are common for large-scale public art. Factors influencing pay include:

  • Size of the mural (the Fever’s performance center is ~10,000 sq. ft., so the mural could be 500–2,000 sq. ft.).
  • Complexity (3D elements, lighting integration, or interactive features may increase cost).
  • Artist reputation (established names command higher fees than emerging artists).

Pro Tip: If you’re new to high-profile commissions, consider partnering with a local art collective or gallery to strengthen your proposal.

8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

Now that you understand the process and expectations, let’s dive into concrete strategies to position yourself as the best candidate.

Strategy 1: Build a Portfolio That Speaks to the Fever’s Vision

Your portfolio is your first impression—it must instantly communicate that you’re the right artist for this project.

What to Include:

✅ Large-Scale Murals – If possible, showcase work that’s 500+ sq. ft. (even if it’s for a business or public space). ✅ Themes Aligned with Sports & Community – Examples:

  • A mural celebrating Indiana’s basketball history (e.g., Oscar Robertson’s dominance, IU’s victories).
  • A female empowerment piece featuring WNBA legends or local female athletes.
  • An abstract, high-energy design that feels like a sports stadium’s energy. ✅ Technical Skill Demonstrations – Showcase:
  • Perspective & depth (critical for large murals).
  • Color theory (how you use color to evoke emotion).
  • Material mastery (acrylic, spray paint, fresco, etc.).

Real-World Example: The "Dreamers" Mural by Shea Hemsley

Shea Hemsley, a renowned muralist, created "The Dreamers" in Los Angeles, a massive, vibrant mural celebrating Black women’s dreams and achievements. While not sports-related, it proves how powerful storytelling can be in public art.

For the Fever, you could combine sports history with Indiana’s cultural narrative—imagine a mural featuring:

  • A silhouette of a player mid-dunk overlaid with historical figures like Tamika Catchings.
  • Local landmarks (Soldiers & Sailors Monument, Canal Walk) woven into the background.
  • A dynamic, fluid color palette that matches the team’s purple and gold.

How to Apply This:

  • If you don’t have a large mural yet, create a mock-up in Photoshop or Procreate showing how you’d approach the Fever’s space.
  • Highlight any collaborations with sports teams, schools, or community centers.

Strategy 2: Research the Indiana Fever’s Brand & Audience

The Fever isn’t just looking for aesthetic talent—they want an artist who understands their brand.

Key Research Steps:

  1. Study the Team’s History & Values

    • The Fever was founded in 2000 and has been a consistent WNBA contender, with Tamika Catchings as a Hall of Famer.
    • Their mission is to inspire the next generation of athletes and leaders.
    • Their social media (@IndianaFever) often features community engagement, player stories, and local partnerships.

  2. Analyze Their Current Visual Identity

    • Their logo is bold, dynamic, and feminine.
    • Their arena (Gainbridge Fieldhouse) has a modern, energetic vibe.
    • Their social media posts often include high-energy graphics and player portraits.

  3. Look at Similar Murals in Sports Facilities

    • NBA’s "The Wall" at Madison Square Garden – A collage of basketball legends.
    • WNBA’s "Power Forward" Mural at the WNBA Hall of Fame – Celebrates female athletes.
    • NBA’s "City of Champions" Mural in Miami – A futuristic, high-tech design.

Actionable Takeaway:

  • Create a mood board (using Pinterest or Canva) with:
    • Color palettes (purple, gold, black, white).
    • Reference images of Indiana landmarks, sports history, and female empowerment.
    • Competitor murals (to see what works in sports spaces).

Strategy 3: Network with the Right People in the Indiana Sports & Art Community

Connections often lead to commissions. The Fever may refer artists through their network rather than just posting an open call.

How to Build Strategic Relationships:

  1. Attend Indiana Fever Events

    • Games at Gainbridge Fieldhouse – Talk to marketing staff, community relations, or team ambassadors.
    • Charity events & fundraisers – Many artists get discovered through non-profit collaborations.

  2. Partner with Local Art Collectives & Galleries

    • Indiana Arts Commission – They often fund and promote local artists.
    • The Arts Council of Indianapolis – Hosts mural festivals and artist showcases.
    • Local galleries (e.g., The Art Kitchen, The Indianapolis Museum of Art) – They can vouch for your work.

  3. Reach Out to Former Players & Coaches

    • Tamika Catchings (former Fever player, now a Hall of Famer) might have connections to the team.
    • Rick Pitino (former IU coach) could endorse an artist who aligns with his high-energy, motivational style.

  4. Join Online Communities

    • Facebook Groups like "Indiana Artists Network" or "Central Indiana Muralists".
    • LinkedIn – Connect with Fever staff, sports facility managers, and art buyers.

Real-World Example: The "Wall of Respect" in Chicago The "Wall of Respect" (a Black art and history mural) was created by a local artist who networked with community leaders before getting the commission. The Fever could follow a similar path—if you build relationships with their team, you increase your chances.

Strategy 4: Submit a Proposal That Stands Out (Even Before the Official Call)

If the Fever hasn’t officially announced the mural, you can proactively pitch your idea.

How to Craft a Winning Proposal:

  1. Start with a Strong Subject Line (If Emailing)

    • "Inspired by the Indiana Fever’s Legacy: A Mural Concept for Your New Performance Center"

  2. Include These Key Elements:

    • A brief introduction (who you are, your experience).
    • A 1-sentence hook (e.g., "I envision a mural that merges Indiana’s basketball history with the fearless spirit of WNBA players—just like the Fever’s own legacy.").
    • 3–5 high-res images of your best work (with dimensions noted).
    • A concept sketch (even if rough, it shows vision).
    • A short artist statement (why you’re the right fit).

  3. Offer a Free Consultation (If Possible)

    • "I’d love to discuss how my style could bring your vision to life—would you be open to a quick call?"

Example Pitch:

"Hi [Fever Marketing Team], As a Central Indiana artist specializing in large-scale murals that celebrate local culture and athletics, I was thrilled to hear about the new Player Performance Center. I’ve attached a few examples of my work, including a recent mural at [Local Business] that blended [specific theme]. For the Fever’s space, I propose a dynamic piece featuring [brief description—e.g., ‘a fusion of Indiana’s basketball heritage with modern WNBA icons, rendered in bold purples and golds to match your team’s identity’]. I’d be happy to refine this idea further—would you be available for a quick discussion? Best regards, [Your Name]"

Strategy 5: Showcase Your Ability to Work with Large Teams & Deadlines

Public art projects rarely happen alone—you’ll likely be working with:

  • Fever staff (marketing, operations).
  • Contractors (for scaffolding, prep work).
  • Other artists (if assisting).

How to Prove You’re a Team Player:

  • Highlight past collaborations (even small ones).
  • Mention your experience with deadlines (e.g., "I completed a 1,200 sq. ft. mural in 6 weeks for [Client]").
  • Offer a project management plan (timeline, milestones).

Common Mistake: Assuming you’ll work completely independently. Many artists underestimate the logistics of large murals (permitting, safety, material delivery).

Solution: If you’ve never worked on a large project, partner with a contractor who has experience in public art installations.

Strategy 6: Leverage Social Media & Local Press to Get Noticed

The Fever follows local artists on social media—being visible increases your chances.

How to Go Viral (In a Good Way):

  1. Post Behind-the-Scenes Content

    • Share sketching sessions, material tests, or mock-ups.
    • Use #IndianaFever #CentralIndianaArt #WNBAArt.

  2. Tag the Fever in Your Posts

    • Example: "Dreaming of creating a mural for the @IndianaFever’s new performance center—here’s how I’d approach it!"

  3. Get Featured in Local Media

    • Pitch a story to:
      • The Indianapolis Star ("Local Artist’s Vision for the Fever’s New Mural").
      • Indy100 or WISH-TV ("How Art Elevates Sports Culture").
    • Offer a free consultation or workshop in exchange for coverage.

  4. Run a Crowdfunding Campaign (If Needed)

    • If the budget is a concern, a Kickstarter or GoFundMe can demonstrate community support.
