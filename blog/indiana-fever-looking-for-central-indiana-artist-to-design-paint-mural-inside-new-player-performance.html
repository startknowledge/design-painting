Why This Mural Project Matters: The Fever’s Vision & Your Opportunity

The Fever’s Commitment to Community & Culture

The Indiana Fever isn’t just a basketball team—they’re a cultural force. Since their 2000 WNBA debut, they’ve been pioneers in women’s sports, breaking barriers and fostering youth development through programs like Fever Girls Basketball Camps and Hoops for Heart.

Their new Player Performance Center (expected to open in 2025) will be a state-of-the-art facility combining cutting-edge training technology with inspirational art. The mural inside will be more than just decoration—it will be a symbol of resilience, unity, and Indiana pride.

Why Local Artists Are the Perfect Fit

The Fever has publicly stated they want a Central Indiana artist to lead this project. Why?

  • Authenticity: A local artist understands Indiana’s culture, history, and collective identity—elements that should shine in the mural.
  • Community Impact: Hiring locally boosts the economy and strengthens ties between the team and the city.
  • Unique Perspective: An artist who lives and breathes Indiana can craft something truly distinctive, not just a generic sports mural.

What the Mural Should Accomplish

A great mural doesn’t just look good—it tells a story. The Fever’s mural should: ✔ Inspire athletes with themes of perseverance, teamwork, and excellence. ✔ Celebrate Indiana’s heritage, whether through landmarks, folklore, or local legends. ✔ Create a sense of belonging for fans, players, and visitors. ✔ Stand the test of time—using durable, high-quality materials that won’t fade or deteriorate quickly.

8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

If you’re serious about bidding on this project, you need a strategic approach. Here’s how to position yourself as the best candidate.

1. Research the Fever’s Brand & Values Inside Out

Before picking up a brush, immerse yourself in the Fever’s identity.

  • Study their logo, colors, and typography—how can you integrate these elements into your mural concept?
  • Read interviews with players, coaches, and executives—what themes do they emphasize? (e.g., resilience, sisterhood, breaking barriers)
  • Visit their current arena (Gainbridge Fieldhouse)—what visual elements already exist that could inspire your mural?

Example: If the Fever values female empowerment, your mural could feature iconic women in sports history alongside modern Indiana athletes.

2. Define Your Unique Artistic Style & Niche

The Fever wants something fresh, not just another abstract sports mural. Ask yourself:

  • Do you specialize in realistic portraits, abstract expressionism, or cultural storytelling?
  • Have you worked on public art projects before? If so, what was the impact?
  • Can you blend different mediums (e.g., mural painting + digital elements + 3D textures) for a dynamic effect?

Real-World Example: In 2022, artist Sarah Sze created a large-scale installation for the Brooklyn Museum that combined sculpture, painting, and found objects. While not a mural, her work shows how layered storytelling can make art visually rich and emotionally resonant—a technique that could work for the Fever’s space.

3. Develop a Concept That Aligns with the Space’s Function

The mural won’t just be decorative—it will be functional. Think about:

  • Where will it be placed? (e.g., lobby, locker room, training area)
  • How will it enhance the experience? (e.g., motivational for players, immersive for fans)
  • Can it incorporate interactive elements? (e.g., QR codes linking to player stories, augmented reality features)

Actionable Tip: If the mural is in a training area, consider dynamic imagery—like a basketball court that seems to extend into the mural, or abstract representations of movement and speed.

4. Create a Portfolio That Shows You Understand Public Art

The Fever will review your past work—but not just for technical skill. They want to see: ✅ Murals or large-scale projects (even if not sports-related) ✅ Concept sketches & development process (show your thinking, not just the final product) ✅ Testimonials from clients who appreciated the emotional impact of your work

Common Mistake: Many artists submit only finished pieces without showing how they arrived at the concept. Fix this by including:

  • Initial sketches & mood boards
  • Client feedback & revisions
  • Before-and-after transformations (if applicable)

5. Craft a Proposal That Tells a Story (Not Just a Pitch)

Your proposal should feel like a narrative, not a dry business document. Structure it like this:

A. Introduction (Hook Them)

  • Start with a bold statement about why you are the right artist for this project.
  • Example: “Indiana’s spirit is one of resilience, creativity, and community—just like the Indiana Fever. My mural will be a visual celebration of that energy, designed to inspire every player who steps into the Performance Center.”

B. Concept & Vision (The Heart of Your Proposal)

  • Describe your mural’s theme (e.g., “A fusion of Indiana’s natural beauty and the athletes who push boundaries”)
  • Include 3-5 key visual elements (e.g., a basketball mid-air, a river winding like a game timeline, local landmarks in the background)
  • Explain the symbolism—why these choices matter.

C. Technical Details (Prove You Can Execute)

  • Materials you’ll use (e.g., acrylic paint on primed walls, UV-resistant sealant)
  • Timeline (e.g., “I can complete the mural in 4 weeks with a dedicated team”)
  • Safety & permits (if applicable)

D. Testimonials & Past Work (Social Proof)

  • Quote from a past client: “[Artist’s Name] didn’t just paint a mural—they created an experience that brought our community together.”
  • Link to a relevant project (e.g., a school mural that boosted student morale)

6. Leverage Local Connections & Networking

The Fever may prefer working with artists they know. How to build credibility:

  • Attend Fever events (games, community outreach programs) and introduce yourself.
  • Partner with local arts organizations (e.g., Indiana Arts Council, Indianapolis Artsgarden) to get referrals.
  • Collaborate with other Central Indiana artists—sometimes teamwork leads to bigger opportunities.

Real-World Example: In 2021, artist Kehinde Wiley (known for his portraits of Black figures in classical poses) was commissioned by the Metropolitan Museum of Art after years of networking with curators and collectors. While not a mural, his success shows how relationships open doors.

7. Offer a Value-Added Proposal (Beyond Just Painting)

The Fever will remember artists who go the extra mile. Consider:

  • Hosting a community mural workshop before completion (engaging local kids).
  • Creating digital assets (e.g., high-res photos of the mural for marketing).
  • Proposing a "mural reveal" event with the team and fans.

Why This Works: It shows you care about the bigger picture—not just the commission.

8. Submit Your Proposal with a Strong Call to Action

Your final proposal should end with a clear next step. Example: “I’d love to discuss how we can bring this vision to life. Are you available for a quick call next week to explore ideas further? You can reach me at [email] or [phone].”

Bonus: Include a polished PDF proposal and a short video pitch (even if just 30 seconds) to stand out.

Lesson for the Fever: This mural connects with the community—something the Fever’s project should also do. Incorporate local kids, families, or legends into your design.

Common Mistakes Artists Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most talented artists can sabotage their chances with these avoidable errors.

Mistake #1: Ignoring the Client’s Vision

What happens? You submit a highly technical, abstract mural when the Fever wants something inspirational and community-focused.

How to fix it:

  • Ask for a brief (even if they don’t provide one, reach out).
  • Visit the space (if possible) to understand lighting, dimensions, and purpose.

Mistake #2: Overcomplicating the Design

What happens? Your mural has too many elements, making it cluttered and hard to follow.

How to fix it:

  • Stick to 3-5 key themes (e.g., Indiana’s rivers, basketball, resilience).
  • Use negative space to let the imagery breathe.

Mistake #3: Skipping the Research Phase

What happens? You create a mural without knowing what the Fever stands for, leading to a disconnected final product.

How to fix it:

  • Study the team’s history, players, and community programs.
  • Look at past Fever marketing for color palettes and motifs.

Mistake #4: Underestimating the Technical Challenges

What happens? You propose a beautiful design but can’t execute it due to material limitations or wall conditions.

How to fix it:

  • Test your materials on a small section of the wall first.
  • Consult with contractors if needed to ensure durability.

Mistake #5: Not Including a Clear Timeline

What happens? The Fever wants the mural done in 2 months, but you underestimated the time needed, leading to delays and frustration.

How to fix it:

  • Break the project into phases (sketches, prep, painting, finishing).
  • Pad your timeline by 10-15% to account for unexpected challenges.

Mistake #6: Forgetting About Maintenance & Longevity

What happens? You use cheap, low-quality paint that fades or peels within a year.

How to fix it:

  • Specify UV-resistant, high-durability paints.
  • Propose a maintenance plan (e.g., annual touch-ups).
