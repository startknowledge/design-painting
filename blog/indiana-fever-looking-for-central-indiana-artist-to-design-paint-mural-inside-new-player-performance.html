Indiana Fever Seeks Central Indiana Artist to Design & Paint a Stunning Mural Inside Their New Player Performance Center: A Guide for Local Creatives

Introduction: The Exciting Opportunity for Indiana Artists

The Indiana Fever, the WNBA’s only team in the state, is expanding its legacy with the construction of a brand-new Player Performance Center in Central Indiana. This state-of-the-art facility is more than just a training hub—it’s a showcase for local talent, and the team is actively seeking a central Indiana artist to design and paint a large-scale mural inside the space.

This isn’t just another mural commission—it’s a high-visibility project that could elevate your portfolio, build your reputation, and connect you with sports, corporate, and community clients for years to come. With the Fever’s growing fanbase (over 100,000+ season ticket holders as of 2023) and their commitment to local engagement, this is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity for artists in Indiana.

If you’re a muralsist, painter, or visual artist looking to make your mark in a high-profile, community-driven project, this post will guide you through: ✅ How to position yourself as the ideal candidate ✅ The creative and technical considerations for a sports-themed mural ✅ Real-world examples of successful sports facility murals ✅ Common pitfalls and how to avoid them ✅ Actionable steps to submit your proposal

By the end, you’ll have a clear roadmap to secure this commission—or at least position yourself for future opportunities with the Fever, local businesses, and sports organizations.

Why This Mural Commission Is a Game-Changer for Indiana Artists

Before diving into the how-to, let’s explore why this opportunity matters beyond just the paycheck.

1. The Fever’s Growing Influence & Community Impact

The Indiana Fever have been a cornerstone of women’s basketball since their founding in 2000, but their impact has only grown. In 2022-2023, they:

  • Set a franchise record for attendance, averaging over 10,000 fans per game.
  • Expanded their youth development programs, reaching thousands of young athletes across Indiana.
  • Partnered with local schools and nonprofits, reinforcing their role as a community leader.

A mural in their new Player Performance Center won’t just be an art piece—it will be a symbol of Indiana’s athletic spirit, creativity, and unity.

2. The Mural’s Role in Employee & Player Morale

Sports facilities are more than just places to train—they’re environments that shape performance, motivation, and identity. Studies show that:

  • Art in workspaces boosts creativity by 30% (University of Florida, 2019).
  • Players and staff in visually stimulating environments report higher job satisfaction (Journal of Environmental Psychology, 2021).
  • Local murals increase community pride and foot traffic (Getty Images & The Creative Independent, 2020).

The Fever’s mural will inspire players, coaches, and staff while reinforcing the team’s connection to Indiana’s culture.

3. The Financial & Portfolio Benefits

While exact compensation details aren’t public, large-scale mural commissions typically range from:

  • $5,000–$20,000 for a single artist (depending on size, complexity, and materials).
  • $20,000–$50,000+ for multi-artist collaborations or large installations.

Even if this specific commission doesn’t fit your budget, securing it will: ✔ Add a high-profile project to your resume—great for future bids on NBA, NFL, or corporate murals. ✔ Attract local clients (restaurants, gyms, breweries) who may have seen your work in a public, well-trafficked space. ✔ Position you as a go-to artist for sports and community projects in Indiana.

8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

Now that you understand the why, let’s break down the how. Here’s a step-by-step guide to positioning yourself as the best candidate for this opportunity.

Strategy 1: Research the Fever’s Brand & Aesthetic

Before pitching your idea, you need to speak their language.

What to Study:

✅ Team Colors & Logo – The Fever’s purple and gold are iconic, but how can you elevate them beyond just a logo? ✅ Player & Fan Culture – Are there local heroes (like Lindsey Harding, a former Fever player turned coach) who could inspire the mural? ✅ Community Themes – Indiana is known for hoosier hospitality, basketball tradition, and natural beauty (think Hoosier Dome, Indiana Dunes, or even the Indiana Pacers’ legacy).

Example Approach:

Instead of a generic basketball mural, consider:

  • A dynamic, abstract take on Indiana’s sports history, blending **Fever players with legends like Larry Bird or Terrell Owens (who played in Indy).
  • A futuristic, performance-focused design that celebrates athleticism while subtly nodding to Indiana’s innovation (e.g., tech hubs in Carmel or Fishers).
  • A storytelling mural that connects players to their roots, perhaps featuring local landmarks in the background.

Pro Tip: If you’re unsure, attend a Fever game and observe how fans interact with the space. Notice what emotions the current environment evokes—then enhance those feelings with your mural.

Strategy 2: Understand the Technical Requirements

Murals aren’t just about art—they’re about execution. The Fever will need an artist who can: ✔ Work with large-scale materials (acrylic, spray paint, or even projection mapping for digital elements). ✔ Adapt to the space’s dimensions (will it be floor-to-ceiling, a hallway feature, or a focal wall?). ✔ Handle logistics (scaffolding, ventilation, and minimal disruption to training).

Key Questions to Ask (If Possible):

  • What is the exact size of the mural? (A 20’x30’ wall is very different from a 50’x10’ installation.)
  • Are there structural limitations? (Reinforced walls? Acoustic needs?)
  • What’s the timeline? (Will you need to work during off-hours to avoid disrupting training?)
  • What’s the budget for materials? (High-end paints vs. durable, low-VOC options?)

Real-World Example: The Golden State Warriors’ Chase Center features massive murals by Shepard Fairey and Kehinde Wiley, but they had to work around the arena’s structural beams and acoustic panels. A local artist in Indy would need to plan for similar constraints.

Strategy 3: Develop a Portfolio That Speaks to Sports & Community

The Fever won’t just hire based on technical skill—they’ll want an artist who understands their mission.

What to Include in Your Portfolio:

✔ Sports-Themed Murals – Even if you haven’t done one before, show similar work (e.g., gym murals, athletic facility designs, or abstract energy pieces). ✔ Community-Centric Art – Murals that celebrate local culture (e.g., Indiana’s music scene, Amish country, or urban revitalization projects). ✔ Large-Scale Work – If you’ve done frescoes, billboards, or commercial installations, highlight those. ✔ 3D Elements or Mixed Media – If the mural includes sculptural components or interactive elements, show examples.

If You Lack Direct Experience:

  • Create a mockup of what you’d do for the Fever.
  • Collaborate with a local sports team (even a youth league or college team) to gain experience.
  • Study successful sports murals (we’ll cover examples later in this post).

Strategy 4: Craft a Proposal That Stands Out

A generic email won’t cut it. You need a compelling, well-researched proposal that shows you get the Fever’s vision.

What Your Proposal Should Include:

  1. A Cover Letter (1 page max) – Explain why you’re excited about this project and how your background aligns with their needs.
  2. Concept Sketches – Even if rough, show your creative direction (digital or hand-drawn).
  3. Material & Execution Plan – How will you prepare the surface, apply paint, and ensure durability?
  4. Timeline & Availability – Are you full-time, part-time, or a team?
  5. References & Past Work – Include client testimonials (especially from public or corporate projects).

Example Proposal Structure:

"Dear Indiana Fever Leadership,

*As an Indiana-based muralist specializing in community-driven, high-energy art, I’m thrilled to submit my proposal for the Player Performance Center mural. My work at [Local Gym/Museum]—where I painted a 15’x20’ mural celebrating Hoosier athletes—shows my ability to blend local culture with dynamic visual storytelling.

For your project, I propose a three-panel mural that:

  • Celebrates the Fever’s legacy while nodding to Indiana’s basketball tradition.
  • Uses gold and purple as a base, with abstract motion lines to represent energy.
  • Includes subtle nods to local landmarks (e.g., the Soldiers and Sailors Monument in a background scene).

I’m available to start in [Month/Year] and can provide a detailed execution plan upon request. I’d love to discuss how we can make this a showcase for Indiana’s artistic talent while inspiring the team.

Best regards, [Your Name]"

Strategy 5: Leverage Local Connections & Networking

Sometimes, who you know matters as much as what you know.

How to Build Relationships:

✔ Attend Fever Games & Events – Strike up conversations with staff, players, or other artists in attendance. ✔ Partner with Local Businesses – If you’ve painted murals for breweries, gyms, or restaurants, ask if they can vouch for your work. ✔ Join Indiana Art & Sports Groups – Organizations like:

  • Indiana Arts Commission
  • Hoosier Artists Guild
  • Indiana Basketball Coaches Association can connect you with decision-makers. ✔ Reach Out to Former Players & Coaches – Someone like Lindsey Harding (former Fever player, now a coach) might advocate for you if they see your style.

Real-World Example: The Denver Nuggets’ new arena (Ball Arena) featured a mural by local artist Jason DeGraff, who was recommended by a former Nuggets player who saw his work at a Denver art festival.

Strategy 6: Consider Collaborations for Larger Impact

If you’re early in your career, a collaboration could help you secure the project while expanding your network.

Ways to Collaborate:

  • Partner with a local graphic designer to develop the concept.
  • **Work with a construction crew who knows the space well.
  • Team up with another muralist to split the workload (if the mural is extremely large).

Pro Tip: If you collaborate, make sure your role is clearly defined—you don’t want to be overshadowed by a more established artist.

Strategy 7: Prepare for the Interview (If Applicable)

If the Fever shortlists candidates, you may get an in-person or virtual interview. Here’s how to ace it:

What They’ll Likely Ask:

✅ "How do you plan to make this mural unique to Indiana?" ✅ "What materials will you use to ensure durability?" ✅ "Can you provide references from similar projects?" ✅ "What’s your timeline and availability?" ✅ "How will you involve the community in the process?"

How to Answer:

  • For uniqueness: "I’d incorporate Indiana’s ‘Hoosier’ spirit with dynamic, hand-painted motion lines that feel like a modern take on old-school basketball posters."
  • For durability: *"I use *high-quality acrylic paints with a sealer finish, ensuring it lasts for years without fading."
  • For community involvement: "I’d host a local artist open house where fans can vote on color schemes or see the process in real time."

Strategy 8: Follow Up & Stay Persistent

If you don’t hear back within 2–3 weeks, follow up politely.

Example Follow-Up Email:

"Hi [Contact Name],

I wanted to check in regarding my proposal for the Player Performance Center mural. I’m very excited about the opportunity to contribute to such an important project and would love to know if there’s any additional information I can provide.

Thank you for your time—I look forward to hearing from you!

Best regards, [Your Name]"

If you don’t get this commission, ask for feedback—it could help you improve for future opportunities.
