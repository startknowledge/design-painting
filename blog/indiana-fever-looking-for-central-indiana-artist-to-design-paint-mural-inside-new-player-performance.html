8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

Now that you understand the potential impact of this project, let’s break down how to craft a proposal that makes you the top choice.

1. Research the Indiana Fever’s Brand & Aesthetic Inside Out

Before picking up a brush, you need to understand the Fever’s identity.

  • Team Colors & Symbolism: The Fever’s primary colors are purple and gold, with white accents. Their logo features a fiery phoenix, symbolizing resilience and rebirth.
  • Player & Fan Culture: The team has a strong female empowerment message, often featuring women’s sports advocacy in their marketing.
  • Local Indiana Connection: The Fever is deeply tied to Indianapolis, so incorporating local landmarks, history, or cultural elements (like the Indiana State Fair, Eiteljorg Museum, or Hoosier heritage) could make your mural more meaningful.

Action Step:

  • Study the Fever’s website, social media, and past campaigns.
  • Look for recurring themes in their branding (e.g., fire, strength, unity).
  • Visit the Indiana Sports Museum or local art galleries to see what resonates with Hoosiers.

2. Define the Mural’s Purpose & Message

A great mural doesn’t just look good—it tells a story. For the Fever’s performance center, your mural should: ✔ Inspire athletes (motivational, high-energy) ✔ Engage fans (nostalgic, celebratory) ✔ Reflect Indiana’s spirit (proud, community-driven)

Possible Themes to Explore:

  • "Rise Like a Phoenix" – A dynamic, fiery composition symbolizing rebirth and perseverance.
  • "Unity in Strength" – A mural featuring diverse athletes working together, representing teamwork.
  • "Hoosier Pride" – Incorporating Indiana landmarks, sports history, or local heroes.
  • "The Journey to Greatness" – A visual timeline of the Fever’s growth, from their 2000 founding to today.

Action Step:

  • Narrow down 2-3 themes and sketch concept thumbnails.
  • Write a 1-paragraph mission statement for your mural (e.g., "This mural celebrates the Indiana Fever’s journey from underdog to champions, using dynamic fire motifs to symbolize their relentless spirit—inspiring players and fans alike to rise together.").

3. Choose the Right Mural Style & Technique

The Indiana Fever’s performance center likely has specific spatial constraints (e.g., high ceilings, curved walls, or large flat surfaces). Your mural should:

  • Scale appropriately (a small, intricate piece won’t work on a 50-foot wall).
  • Use durable materials (acrylic, spray paint, or mural-specific paints that resist wear).
  • Balance realism and abstraction—some murals (like Shepard Fairey’s "Obey Giant") are bold and graphic, while others (like David Hockney’s large-scale works) are detailed and immersive.

Recommended Styles for a Sports Mural:

  • Abstract Expressionism (bold colors, energy, movement)
  • Pop Art (bold outlines, vibrant hues—think Andy Warhol meets sports)
  • Realistic with Dynamic Lighting (to make the mural feel alive)
  • Mixed Media (combining paint, collage, or metallic accents for texture)

Action Step:

  • Test different styles on small canvases before committing.
  • Consider a hybrid approach—e.g., realistic figures with abstract backgrounds.

4. Incorporate Interactive or Multi-Sensory Elements

Since this mural will be in a performance center, it should engage visitors beyond just visual appeal.

Ways to Make Your Mural Interactive:

  • Glow-in-the-dark elements (for night games)
  • Reflective or metallic accents (to catch light from different angles)
  • Hidden details (e.g., Fever players’ names, stats, or fan tributes)
  • Augmented Reality (AR) integration (if the team is open to tech—some modern murals use QR codes that unlock digital content)

Example: The Los Angeles Lakers’ "City of Angels" mural (by Shepard Fairey) features gold accents that shimmer under stadium lights, making it visually striking during games.

Action Step:

  • Propose 1-2 interactive elements in your pitch (even if simple).
  • Mention how they enhance the viewer’s experience.

5. Create a Detailed Proposal with a Clear Timeline & Budget

The Fever will want to see that you’re organized, professional, and realistic about timelines and costs.

What to Include in Your Proposal: ✅ Concept Sketches (3-5 variations) ✅ Material List (paints, primers, tools, safety gear) ✅ Timeline (e.g., "2 weeks for design finalization, 4 weeks for painting, 1 week for touch-ups") ✅ Budget Breakdown (hourly rate, material costs, contingency) ✅ Past Work Examples (with client testimonials if possible)

Example Proposal Structure:

**Project Title:** "Fever Rising: A Mural of Hoosier Resilience"
**Artist:** [Your Name]
**Concept:** A **dynamic, fire-themed mural** featuring **Fever players in motion**, surrounded by **Indiana landmarks and symbolic elements**.
**Materials:** Acrylic paint, gesso primer, high-quality brushes, stencils (if needed).
**Timeline:**
- Week 1: Finalize design with Fever team
- Week 2-5: Surface prep & painting
- Week 6: Final touches & inspection
**Budget:** $15,000 (including labor, materials, and contingency)

Action Step:

  • Use a professional template (Canva, Adobe InDesign, or even a Google Slides deck).
  • Keep it concise but detailed—the Fever’s team is likely busy, so make it easy to review.

6. Leverage Local Connections & Networking

The Fever may already have artists in mind, but proving you’re a "local favorite" can give you an edge.

How to Build Credibility:

  • Partner with a local gallery or arts organization (e.g., Indianapolis Museum of Art, The Children’s Museum of Indianapolis).
  • Get a referral from a Fever employee, coach, or sponsor.
  • Highlight past work with Indiana-based clients (schools, businesses, nonprofits).

Example: If you’ve painted a mural for Heritage High School or Indiana University, mention it—it shows you understand Hoosier culture.

Action Step:

  • Reach out to the Fever’s community relations team (via email or LinkedIn) to express interest.
  • Attend local sports events (even as a spectator) to network casually.

7. Prepare for the Interview (If Selected for a Follow-Up)

If the Fever likes your proposal, they may ask you to pitch in person or via video call. Be ready to discuss:

  • Your artistic philosophy (e.g., "I believe murals should tell a story—this one will celebrate the Fever’s journey through dynamic imagery.")
  • How you’ll handle challenges (e.g., "If the wall has imperfections, I’ll use them as part of the design.")
  • Your process (e.g., "I’ll start with a large-scale sketch, then refine details with the team’s input.")
  • Future collaborations (e.g., "I’d love to work on a follow-up piece if this one is successful.")

Action Step:

  • Practice your pitch (record yourself or do a mock interview).
  • Bring a physical portfolio (if meeting in person) or a high-quality digital file.

8. Follow Up Professionally & Show Enthusiasm

After submitting your proposal, don’t disappear—follow up politely after 2-3 weeks.

Example Email:

Subject: Follow-Up on Indiana Fever Mural Proposal

Dear [Team Contact's Name],

I hope you’re doing well! I wanted to follow up on my proposal for the Indiana Fever mural. I’m very excited about the opportunity to contribute to such an important project and would love to know if there’s any additional information I can provide.

Thank you for your time—I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Your Contact Info]
[Portfolio Link]

Action Step:

  • Send a polite follow-up (but don’t spam).
  • Offer to answer any questions they may have.

Real-World Examples of Successful Sports Murals (And What Makes Them Work)

Studying existing sports murals can help you craft a proposal that stands out. Here are three iconic examples and why they resonate.

1. The Los Angeles Lakers’ "City of Angels" Mural (Shepard Fairey)

Location: Staples Center (Los Angeles) Why It Works:

  • Bold, graphic style that’s instantly recognizable.
  • Incorporates Lakers history (players, logos, iconic moments).
  • Uses gold accents that glow under stadium lights, making it visually striking.
  • Engages fans—it’s a must-see for every visitor.

Lesson for Your Proposal:

  • Think about lighting—will your mural look best in daylight or under stadium lights?
  • Incorporate team history (e.g., Fever’s championship runs, iconic players).

2. The New York Yankees’ "The House That Ruth Built" Mural (Various Artists)

Location: Yankee Stadium Why It Works:

  • Celebrates baseball legends (Babe Ruth, Derek Jeter, etc.).
  • Uses a mix of realism and abstraction—some figures are detailed, others are stylized.
  • Tells a story—from the team’s founding to modern-day heroes.
  • Engages fans of all ages (kids love the cartoonish elements, adults appreciate the nostalgia).

Lesson for Your Proposal:

  • Balance history and modernity—show the Fever’s past and present.
  • Make it accessible—avoid overly complex details that only experts will understand.

3. The Denver Broncos’ "Mountain Strong" Mural (Local Denver Artist)

Location: Broncos Training Facility Why It Works:

  • Incorporates Colorado landmarks (Rockies, ski slopes) to tie into local pride.
  • Uses a rugged, outdoor-inspired style that matches the Broncos’ "mountain strong" branding.
  • Features current players in action poses, making it dynamic and motivational.
  • Durable materials—it’s been standing up to weather and heavy foot traffic for years.

Lesson for Your Proposal:

  • Connect to the location’s geography (Indiana’s lakes, forests, or cities).
  • Use durable, high-quality materials—this mural will be seen daily.
