8 Actionable Strategies to Win the Indiana Fever Mural Commission

Now that you understand the importance and scope of this project, let’s break down how to position yourself as the best candidate.

Strategy 1: Research the Indiana Fever’s Brand & Values

Before pitching your ideas, deeply understand the team’s identity.

What to Study:

  • Team Colors & Logo: The Fever’s purple and gold dominate their branding. How can you incorporate these colors into your mural while keeping it dynamic and modern?
  • Player & Fan Stories: Look for inspiring quotes from players (e.g., Candace Parker’s leadership, A’ja Wilson’s resilience) or fan-driven initiatives (like the Fever’s youth basketball programs).
  • Community Impact: The team has a strong Indiana-focused mission—highlighting local heroes, sports history, or cultural landmarks could make your proposal stand out.

Example: If you were designing a mural for the Indiana Pacers, you might reference Osceola McCarty’s legacy or the 1979 NCAA Final Four victory. For the Fever, consider Indiana’s role in women’s sports history (e.g., Billie Jean King’s connection to Indiana or early women’s basketball pioneers).

Strategy 2: Develop a Strong Portfolio Showcase

The Fever’s selection committee will review your past work to assess your style, technical skill, and creativity.

How to Optimize Your Portfolio: ✅ Highlight Large-Scale Work: If you’ve painted walls, ceilings, or public installations, include high-quality photos and descriptions. ✅ Show Versatility: Mix traditional murals, abstract designs, and digital art to prove you can adapt to different styles. ✅ Include Sports or Motivational Themes: Even if you haven’t worked with sports teams before, showcase art that evokes energy, teamwork, or local pride. ✅ Use a Professional Website or Online Gallery: Platforms like Behance, ArtStation, or a personal website make it easy for the Fever to browse your work.

Real-World Example: Artist: [Fictional Name] – "Hoosier Heroes" Mural This artist created a ceiling mural in a local high school gym featuring Indiana’s most famous athletes (e.g., Larry Bird, Terrell Owens, and Olympic gold medalists) in a dynamic, flowing style. The Fever’s team saw this and thought, "This artist understands our region’s sports culture and can bring that energy to our facility."

Strategy 3: Craft a Proposal That Tells a Story

A great mural isn’t just about aesthetics—it’s about storytelling.

How to Structure Your Proposal:

  1. Introduction (1 paragraph) – Briefly explain why you’re excited about this project and how it aligns with your artistic vision.
  2. Concept & Inspiration (2-3 paragraphs) – Describe your creative direction, including:
    • Themes (e.g., "A fusion of Indiana’s agricultural roots and modern women’s basketball")
    • Influences (e.g., "I drew inspiration from the work of [Artist X], but with a Hoosier twist")
    • Symbolism (e.g., "The mural will feature a rising sun to represent growth, with basketballs woven into the design")
  3. Visual Mockups (3-5 sketches or digital renders) – Even if you’re not a digital artist, hand-drawn concepts can show your creative process.
  4. Materials & Execution Plan – Explain how you’ll approach the mural (e.g., "I’ll use acrylic paint on primed drywall for durability and vibrancy").
  5. Timeline & Budget Considerations – Show that you’re realistic and professional in your approach.

Pro Tip: If you’re not a professional illustrator, use free tools like Canva or Procreate to create rough sketches that convey your ideas clearly.

Strategy 4: Incorporate Local Elements That Resonate

The Fever wants a mural that feels like Indiana.

Ways to Add Local Flavor:

  • Indiana Landmarks: The Eagle Creek Reservoir, Indiana Dunes, or the Soldiers and Sailors Monument could serve as background elements.
  • Sports History: Highlight Indiana’s contributions to women’s basketball (e.g., the first women’s college basketball game in 1895).
  • Cultural Icons: Feature Hoosier sayings ("The Crossroads of America") or famous Indiana figures (e.g., David Letterman, Kurt Vonnegut, or even Indiana Jones).
  • Nature & Agriculture: Since Indiana is known for cornfields and bluegrass, you could weave these elements into the design (e.g., a basketball hoop made of corn stalks).

Real-World Example: Artist: [Fictional Name] – "The Heart of Indiana" Mural This mural combined:

  • A central basketball court with Indiana’s state flag colors
  • Silhouettes of famous Hoosiers (athletes, artists, and inventors)
  • A rising sun symbolizing the team’s future success The design was unique, recognizable, and deeply tied to Indiana’s identity—exactly what the Fever was looking for.

Strategy 5: Showcase Your Technical Skills & Experience

The Fever needs an artist who can execute a large-scale mural with precision.

How to Prove Your Expertise: ✅ List Past Large-Scale Projects: If you’ve painted walls, ceilings, or public art, detail the size, materials, and challenges you overcame. ✅ Mention Specialized Techniques: Do you have experience with spray paint, stenciling, or mixed media? Highlight this. ✅ Provide References: If possible, include testimonials from past clients who can vouch for your work ethic and reliability. ✅ Offer a Consultation: If selected, propose a site visit to discuss logistics, color schemes, and structural considerations.

Common Technical Questions the Fever May Ask:

  • "How will you ensure the mural lasts for years without fading?" → Answer: "I’ll use UV-resistant acrylic paints and apply a protective sealant for durability."

Strategy 6: Propose a Collaborative Process

The Fever likely wants an artist who is easy to work with and open to feedback.

How to Position Yourself as a Team Player:

  • Offer a Brainstorming Session: "I’d love to meet with your team to discuss ideas and refine the concept."
  • Provide Multiple Design Options: Instead of just one idea, offer 2-3 variations so they can choose their favorite.
  • Be Transparent About Timeline & Costs: Avoid surprises by clearly outlining your process from start to finish.
  • Show Willingness to Adapt: If the Fever suggests changes, demonstrate flexibility while maintaining your artistic vision.

Real-World Example: Artist: [Fictional Name] – Collaborative Approach This artist worked with a local high school to design a mural. Instead of imposing their vision, they:

  1. Hosted a community meeting to gather input.
  2. Created three design options based on feedback.
  3. Finalized a mural that everyone felt represented their school.

Getting the word out before the official application opens can give you an edge.

Strategy 8: Prepare for the Interview (If Selected)

If your proposal stands out, the Fever may invite you for an in-person or virtual interview.

What to Expect & How to Prepare: ✅ Bring Physical & Digital Portfolios: Have printed samples and a USB drive with high-res images. ✅ Be Ready to Discuss Your Process: Explain how you’d approach the mural from concept to completion. ✅ Ask Insightful Questions: Show enthusiasm by asking:

  • "What are the key themes you’d like the mural to convey?"
  • "Are there any specific players or moments in Fever history you’d like highlighted?"
