8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of the Morehead State Art & Design Spring Showcase

Attending an art showcase is more than just browsing—it’s about engaging deeply, networking, and discovering opportunities. Here’s how to maximize your experience:

1. Research the Featured Artists Before You Go

Before stepping into the showcase, review the artist bios and past works available on Morehead State’s official website. This helps you:

  • Identify key pieces you want to see
  • Prepare thoughtful questions for artists
  • Recognize trends in the student body’s creative direction

Pro Tip: If an artist’s work resonates with you, mention a specific piece in your conversation. For example: "I loved your use of textural contrast in ‘Fractured Echoes’—how did you develop that technique?"

2. Attend the Opening Night Reception (If Possible)

The opening night is often the most vibrant part of the showcase, with:

  • Live performances (music, poetry, or spoken word)
  • Artist talks where creators discuss their process
  • Networking opportunities with faculty and industry guests

Example: In 2021, the opening night featured a live painting session by Professor Emily Hart, where she demonstrated digital painting techniques in real time. Many attendees left with newfound inspiration for their own work.

3. Take Notes on Techniques, Not Just Aesthetics

Instead of just admiring art, analyze the techniques used:

  • What mediums were combined? (e.g., acrylic + collage)
  • How was lighting used? (e.g., backlighting for depth)
  • What storytelling elements were included? (e.g., symbolic colors)

Example: A sculpture by Daniel Lee used negative space to create an illusion of movement. By noting this, you can apply similar principles to your own projects.

4. Engage with Artists—Ask the Right Questions

Don’t just observe; interact. Ask artists:

  • "What was your biggest challenge in this piece?"
  • "How did you balance creativity with technical skill?"
  • "What’s the next step for your artistic journey?"

Common Mistake: Asking vague questions like "How did you get so good?" Instead, focus on process and inspiration.

5. Explore Beyond the Main Gallery—Check Out Side Events

Many showcases include workshops, lectures, and pop-up exhibitions. This year, look for:

  • A "Design Thinking" workshop where students solve real-world problems
  • A "Future of Art" panel with industry experts
  • A silent auction where you can bid on student works

Example: In 2020, a pop-up "Street Art Workshop" allowed attendees to create their own stencil art with local graffiti artists.

6. Collect Business Cards and Follow Up

If you meet potential collaborators, employers, or mentors, exchange contact info. After the event:

  • Send a personalized email referencing a specific conversation.
  • Share their work on social media (tagging them) to keep the connection alive.

Example: A graphic design student named Priya Mehta connected with a local marketing firm at the showcase. After following up, she landed a freelance gig within a month.

7. Document Your Experience (But Respectfully)

While taking photos is great, be mindful of artists’ rights:

  • Ask before photographing (some artists prefer no photos).
  • Use the experience for inspiration, not just social media clout.
  • Take notes on your phone if you can’t sketch.

Example: A photography student named Leo Chen documented the showcase but only shared edited, high-quality images that highlighted composition and lighting—not just the art itself.

8. Consider Purchasing or Commissioning Work

If a piece speaks to you, don’t hesitate to buy or commission it:

  • Student works are often priced affordably compared to professional artists.
  • Commissioning a piece can lead to long-term collaborations.

Example: A corporate client purchased a digital illustration series from Mia Rodriguez at last year’s showcase, leading to a multi-project contract.

Common Mistakes to Avoid at Art Showcases (And How to Fix Them)

Attending an art showcase is an opportunity to learn, connect, and inspire—but missteps can ruin the experience. Here’s what to avoid:

1. Treating It Like a Shopping Spree (Without Engagement)

Mistake: Walking through the gallery only to buy art, without appreciating the process. Fix:

  • Spend at least 20 minutes per piece before deciding to purchase.
  • Talk to the artist—understanding their vision makes the purchase more meaningful.

2. Overlooking the Smaller Works

Mistake: Focusing only on large, flashy installations and missing small but impactful pieces. Fix:

  • Look for details—sometimes the most thought-provoking work is subtle.
  • Ask artists about their smaller pieces—they often have deep personal significance.

3. Not Networking Beyond the Gallery

Mistake: Only interacting with artists inside the showcase, missing faculty and industry professionals. Fix:

  • Attend the after-party or networking events.
  • Follow up with connections on LinkedIn or Instagram.

4. Taking Photos Without Permission

Mistake: Photographing art without asking, which can violate copyright or artist preferences. Fix:

  • Always ask before taking photos.
  • If unsure, take notes instead of photos.

5. Dismissing "Weird" or Experimental Art

Mistake: Judging pieces too quickly because they don’t fit traditional aesthetics. Fix:

  • Give experimental work a chance—sometimes the most innovative ideas start as "weird."
  • Ask artists about their inspiration—you might discover a new perspective.

6. Not Leaving Your Contact Info

Mistake: Not exchanging contact details with artists or mentors. Fix:

  • Carry business cards or a digital contact form.
  • Follow up within a week with a thank-you message.

7. Ignoring the Technical Details

Mistake: Focusing only on aesthetics, not how the art was made. Fix:

  • Ask about techniques—you might learn new skills.
  • Take notes on materials used—this could inspire your own work.
