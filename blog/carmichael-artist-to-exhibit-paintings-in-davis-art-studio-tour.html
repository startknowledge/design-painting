8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of the Davis Art Studio Tour

Attending an art exhibition is more than just browsing—it’s an opportunity to connect, learn, and invest in creativity. Here’s how to maximize your experience:

1. Research the Artists Before You Go

  • Why it matters: Understanding an artist’s background, influences, and artistic journey helps you appreciate their work on a deeper level.
  • How to do it:
    • Visit the official Davis Art Studio Tour website for artist bios and statements.
    • Follow Carmichael Artist on social media (Instagram, Facebook, or their personal website) to see their process and past works.
    • Look for themes in their work—are they exploring nature, urban life, or abstract emotions?

2. Arrive Early to Avoid Crowds

  • Why it matters: Studios often open at 9 or 10 AM, and the first hour is when artists are most available for personal interactions.
  • How to do it:
    • Plan your route in advance to account for parking and traffic.
    • If possible, arrive 30 minutes early to get a good view of the space before others do.

3. Engage with the Artists Directly

  • Why it matters: Many artists love sharing their stories—this is your chance to ask about technique, inspiration, and the meaning behind their pieces.
  • How to do it:
    • Start with open-ended questions like:
      • "What inspired this particular piece?"
      • "How long did it take you to create?"
      • "Do you have any upcoming projects or exhibitions?"
    • If you’re interested in purchasing, ask about pricing, shipping, and payment options.

4. Take Notes (But Don’t Rush)

  • Why it matters: Art is meant to be experienced slowly. Sketching or jotting down impressions helps you remember details later.
  • How to do it:
    • Carry a small notebook to jot down:
      • Titles of pieces you love
      • Artists you’d like to follow
      • Specific techniques you admire
    • If you’re a collector, note budget-friendly options and high-end investment pieces.

5. Explore Beyond the Main Gallery

  • Why it matters: Many artists have hidden works, sketches, or alternative mediums (like prints or ceramics) that aren’t displayed in the main space.
  • How to do it:
    • Ask the artist if they have a "secret collection" or work-in-progress pieces available.
    • Look for backroom displays or outdoor installations—some artists showcase work in unexpected spaces.

6. Consider Purchasing (If It Resonates with You)

  • Why it matters: Buying local art supports the artist’s livelihood and ensures their work continues.
  • How to do it:
    • Set a budget before you go to avoid impulse buys.
    • Ask about payment plans if a piece is outside your budget.
    • If you’re unsure, offer to commission a custom piece—many artists appreciate this!

7. Share Your Experience Online

8. Plan a Follow-Up Visit or Commission

  • Why it matters: If you love an artist’s work, supporting them long-term ensures they can continue creating.

Common Mistakes to Avoid at Art Exhibitions (And How to Fix Them)

Even the most enthusiastic art lovers can make unintentional mistakes at studio tours. Here’s what to watch out for:

1. Not Asking Questions (Missing Out on Insights)

  • Mistake: Walking through an exhibition without engaging with the artist.
  • Solution:
    • Prepare 2-3 questions before you go (e.g., "What’s your favorite medium to work with?").
    • If you’re shy, start with a compliment ("I love how you use color in this piece—how did you develop this palette?").

2. Overlooking Smaller Works

  • Mistake: Focusing only on large, eye-catching pieces and missing smaller, more intimate works.
  • Solution:
    • Look at the entire room—sometimes the smallest sketch holds the most meaning.
    • Ask the artist if they have limited-edition prints or postcards available.

3. Assuming All Art is Expensive

  • Mistake: Walking away from a piece because you misjudge its price.
  • Solution:
    • Don’t be afraid to ask—many artists offer payment plans or discounts for early buyers.
    • Some studios have affordable "starter" pieces (like small canvases or prints).

4. Not Following Up After the Event

  • Mistake: Leaving without connecting with the artist or planning to return.
  • Solution:
    • Exchange contact info—most artists are happy to send you digital catalogs or updates.
    • If you loved a piece but couldn’t buy it, ask about future opportunities.

5. Ignoring the Surroundings

  • Mistake: Focusing only on the art and missing the artist’s workspace, tools, or process.
  • Solution:
    • Take a few minutes to explore the studio—you might discover sketches, tools, or books that inspire you.
    • Some artists leave out "work in progress"—these can be unique conversation starters.

6. Not Supporting Local (Even If You Can’t Buy)

  • Mistake: Only attending if you plan to purchase something.
  • Solution:
    • Like, share, and follow the artist’s social media.
    • Attend future events—consistent support matters.
    • Spread the word to friends who might appreciate their work.
