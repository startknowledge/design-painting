Common Mistakes When Engaging with Art (And How to Avoid Them)

Even seasoned art lovers can fall into bad habits when visiting exhibitions. Here’s how to avoid the most common pitfalls.

1. Skipping the Artist Statement

Mistake: Assuming you don’t need context to enjoy art. Why it’s bad: Many pieces require background to fully appreciate their meaning. Fix: Read the artist’s statement before or during the tour.

2. Rushing Through the Exhibition

Mistake: Treating it like a quick gallery crawl instead of a deep dive. Why it’s bad: You’ll miss subtle details that make the work special. Fix: Set aside at least 1-2 hours per studio.

3. Comparing Your Taste to Others’

Mistake: Thinking, “This isn’t ‘good’ art because it doesn’t look like [famous artist]’s work.” Why it’s bad: Art is subjective—what one person loves, another may dislike. Fix: Trust your first impressions and ask yourself why a piece resonates (or doesn’t).

4. Not Asking Questions

Mistake: Walking past an artist without engaging. Why it’s bad: You miss the chance to learn about their process. Fix: Approach artists with curiosity—even a simple “What drew you to this subject?” can spark a meaningful conversation.

5. Overanalyzing Instead of Feeling

Mistake: Intellectualizing art instead of letting it emotionally move you. Why it’s bad: You lose the magic of the experience. Fix: Pause and ask: “How does this make me feel?” before jumping to conclusions.

6. Ignoring the Surroundings
