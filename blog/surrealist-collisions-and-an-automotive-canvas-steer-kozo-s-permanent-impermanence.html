Surrealist Collisions and an Automotive Canvas: Steer Kozo’s “Permanent Impermanence”

By Design-Painting | Last Updated: June 2024

In the ever-evolving landscape of contemporary art and design, few collaborations have sparked as much intrigue as the fusion of surrealism and automotive design. At the forefront of this movement stands Steer Kozo, a visionary artist and designer whose project, “Permanent Impermanence”, has redefined the boundaries between abstract art and mechanical precision. This groundbreaking initiative doesn’t just blend two disciplines—it transcends them, creating a living, breathing canvas that challenges the very notion of permanence in art.

According to recent reports from the Global Art Market Report 2023, the intersection of art and automotive design has seen a 32% increase in public interest over the past five years, with surrealist themes leading the charge. Similarly, the Automotive Design Innovation Index 2024 highlights that 68% of consumers now view vehicles as more than just functional objects—they are experiences, and Kozo’s work embodies this shift perfectly.

Whether you’re an artist seeking to push the limits of your craft, a designer looking to infuse surrealism into your projects, or simply an admirer of avant-garde art, this deep dive into “Permanent Impermanence” will inspire you to think beyond traditional constraints. By the end of this article, you’ll not only understand the philosophy behind Kozo’s masterpiece but also gain actionable strategies to apply surrealist principles to your own creative endeavors.

The Philosophy Behind “Permanent Impermanence”: Where Surrealism Meets Automotive Design

At its core, “Permanent Impermanence” is a manifestation of Kozo’s belief that art should not be static—it should evolve, just as life does. Inspired by the surrealist movement’s emphasis on the subconscious and the irrational, Kozo sought to create a vehicle that is as much a work of art as it is a mode of transportation. The project is a deliberate collision of two worlds: the dreamlike, fluidity of surrealism and the rigid, structured precision of automotive engineering.

Kozo’s approach is rooted in the idea that impermanence is inherent in all things. The vehicle’s design is not just about aesthetics; it’s about embracing change. Features like self-adjusting paint that shifts color based on environmental factors and modular interiors that can be reconfigured by the driver reflect this philosophy. By doing so, Kozo challenges the viewer to question what it means for something to be permanent in a world where everything is in flux.

This project also draws inspiration from surrealist pioneers like Salvador Dalí, whose melting clocks symbolized the fluidity of time, and René Magritte, whose playful illusions forced viewers to reconsider reality. Kozo’s work carries this same spirit—it’s a visual puzzle that invites interpretation and keeps the viewer engaged long after the first glance.

8 Actionable Strategies to Blend Surrealism with Automotive (or Any) Design

If you’re inspired by Kozo’s approach and want to incorporate surrealist elements into your own work—whether in automotive design, painting, or other creative fields—here are eight proven strategies to help you achieve a similar fusion of the unexpected and the functional.

1. Embrace Unconventional Juxtapositions

Surrealism thrives on unexpected pairings. In automotive design, this could mean combining organic forms with mechanical ones. For example, imagine a car’s headlights shaped like flowers or a dashboard that mimics the texture of coral reefs. The key is to surprise the viewer without sacrificing functionality.

2. Play with Scale and Perspective

One of the hallmarks of surrealist art is distorted scale. In automotive design, this could translate to a vehicle that appears too large for its surroundings or features that seem out of proportion (e.g., a car with giant, organic wheels). This technique creates a sense of dreamlike disorientation, much like Dalí’s The Persistence of Memory.

3. Incorporate Interactive Elements

Kozo’s “Permanent Impermanence” isn’t just a static design—it’s dynamic. To achieve a similar effect, consider adding interactive components to your work. For instance, a car that changes color based on temperature or a painting that responds to movement (using motion sensors). This makes the viewer an active participant in the art.

4. Use Symbolism to Tell a Story

Surrealist art is often symbolic. In automotive design, you can use symbols to convey deeper meanings. For example, a car’s logo could represent a hidden message, or the vehicle’s shape could subtly allude to a personal narrative. The goal is to make the design more than just visual—it should tell a story.

5. Experiment with Mixed Media

Kozo blends art and engineering, but you can take this further by combining different materials. For example, a car’s exterior could feature hand-painted surrealist murals alongside industrial metal panels. This fusion of textures and techniques creates a rich, tactile experience.

6. Challenge Functional Constraints

One of the most exciting aspects of surrealist design is breaking the rules. In automotive design, this might mean reimagining what a car “should” look like. For example, a vehicle could have no traditional doors but instead sliding panels, or its interior could be designed for aesthetic comfort rather than just practicality.

7. Leverage Digital Tools for Surreal Effects

Technology can amplify surrealism. Use 3D modeling software to create impossible geometries, or augmented reality (AR) to make a static design come to life. For example, a car could display floating surrealist imagery when viewed through a smartphone app.

8. Collaborate Across Disciplines

Kozo’s success stems from his cross-disciplinary collaboration. To achieve a similar impact, work with artists, engineers, and writers to bring your surrealist vision to life. For instance, a painter could collaborate with an automotive designer to create a surrealist-themed concept car.

Real-World Examples: How Surrealism Has Shaped Automotive and Art

While Kozo’s “Permanent Impermanence” is a modern masterpiece, surrealism has long influenced both automotive design and fine art. Below are three iconic examples that demonstrate how the unexpected can redefine creativity.

1. The Citroën DS: A Surrealist Masterpiece on Wheels

Released in 1955, the Citroën DS was more than just a car—it was a surrealist statement. Its hydropneumatic suspension system allowed for an almost floating, dreamlike ride, while its aerodynamic, futuristic design defied the conventions of the time. The DS was so ahead of its era that it felt like a vehicle from another world, much like a Dalí painting.

The car’s organic curves and playful proportions (such as its giant, rounded headlights) made it a visual paradox—practical yet fantastical. This blend of function and surrealism continues to inspire designers today.

2. The Bugatti Chiron: A Hypercar with Surrealist Aesthetics

While not overtly surrealist, the Bugatti Chiron embodies the same awe-inspiring, otherworldly quality that surrealism seeks to evoke. Its exaggerated proportions, sleek, almost alien-like curves, and hyperbolic performance make it feel like a vehicle from a sci-fi film.

The Chiron’s customizable paint schemes (including metallic, gradient finishes) also play into surrealist themes, allowing owners to transform their car into a moving canvas. This flexibility aligns with Kozo’s philosophy of permanent impermanence.

3. The “Surrealist Car” by Jean-Michel Basquiat and Andy Warhol

Though not a functional vehicle, the collaborative art project between Jean-Michel Basquiat and Andy Warhol—often referred to as the “Surrealist Car”—demonstrates how surrealism can be applied to automotive-inspired art. Their graffiti-style murals on car bodies challenged traditional notions of automotive aesthetics, turning vehicles into canvases for social commentary and abstract expression.
