Chapter 1: The Philosophy Behind a Life by Design

What Does "A Life by Design" Really Mean?

At its core, a life by design is about taking control of your narrative. It’s the idea that life isn’t something that happens to you—it’s something you actively shape. Terrance Osborne, in his work, emphasizes that this philosophy isn’t reserved for artists or high achievers; it’s for anyone who wants to break free from the cycle of busyness, regret, and unfulfilling routines.

Osborne breaks this concept into three pillars:

  1. Intentional Creation – Every decision, big or small, should serve a greater purpose.
  2. Balanced Integration – Life’s domains (career, health, relationships, creativity) should harmonize rather than conflict.
  3. Evolutionary Growth – A designed life isn’t static; it evolves as you do.

This isn’t about rigid planning—it’s about flexible intentionality. As Osborne often says:

"You don’t have to have it all figured out. You just have to commit to the process of figuring it out."

Why Most People Fail to Design Their Lives (And How Osborne’s Approach Fixes It)

A 2021 study by the University of California found that 76% of people feel like they’re not living up to their potential. Why? Because most of us operate on autopilot, reacting to circumstances rather than designing them.

Common pitfalls include:

  • Reactive Living – Letting external factors (social media, workplace demands, family expectations) dictate your days.
  • The Illusion of Busyness – Mistaking productivity for purpose (e.g., working 80-hour weeks but feeling unfulfilled).
  • Neglecting Self-Design – Treating life like a series of tasks rather than a masterpiece in progress.
  • Fear of Change – Staying in comfort zones because the unknown feels scary.
  • Disconnected Domains – Allowing work to consume creativity, or relationships to suffer because of career demands.

Osborne’s approach flips this script. Instead of adapting to life, you design it. Instead of reacting to challenges, you proactively shape solutions. And instead of fragmenting your life into silos (work, art, health), you integrate them into a cohesive whole.

Chapter 2: Eight Actionable Strategies to Design Your Life (Inspired by Terrance Osborne)

Now that we understand the why, let’s dive into the how. These eight strategies are practical, testable, and adaptable—whether you’re an artist, entrepreneur, or someone just starting to reclaim their life.

Strategy 1: Define Your "Why" Beyond Survival

Osborne’s Insight: "Most people live for the weekend. But the weekend is just a pause in the monotony. A life by design requires a 'why' that transcends survival."

The Problem: A 2022 LinkedIn Workplace Report found that 63% of employees don’t feel their work has a meaningful purpose. Without a deeper "why," life becomes a series of obligations rather than a journey of fulfillment.

How to Apply It:

  1. Ask the Right Questions:
    • If money and status weren’t concerns, what would I do every day?
    • What problems do I feel compelled to solve?
    • What legacy do I want to leave?
  2. Write Your "Why" Statement:
    • Keep it short, emotional, and actionable. Example:

      "I design my life to create art that heals others and to live in a way that inspires others to do the same."

  3. Revisit It Quarterly:
    • Your "why" should evolve as you do. Reassess every three months.

Real-World Example: Artist and Activist Kehinde Wiley didn’t just paint portraits—he designed his career to challenge racial stereotypes in art. His "why" wasn’t just about making money; it was about redefining representation. By staying aligned with this purpose, he built a body of work that transcends commercial success.

Strategy 2: The 80/20 Rule for Life (Not Just Work)

Osborne’s Insight: "You don’t need to do everything perfectly—just the 20% that moves the needle."

The Problem: We live in a culture of perfectionism and overcommitment. A 2023 study in Psychological Science found that multitasking reduces productivity by 40% because our brains can’t focus on multiple things at once.

How to Apply It:

  1. Identify Your Top 20%:
    • For artists, this might be mastering one technique rather than trying to be a jack-of-all-trades.
    • For professionals, it could be negotiating a better work arrangement instead of burning out.
  2. Eliminate the Rest:
    • Say no to activities that don’t align with your top 20%. Osborne calls this "strategic scarcity."
  3. Automate or Delegate:
    • Use tools (like Trello for projects, or a virtual assistant for admin tasks) to free up mental space.

Real-World Example: Designer and Entrepreneur Marie Forleo didn’t become a millionaire by doing everything herself. She focused on her top 20%—creating high-value courses and coaching—while outsourcing the rest. This allowed her to scale her impact without sacrificing quality.

Strategy 3: The "Design Sprint" for Life Goals

Osborne’s Insight: "Big dreams don’t happen overnight. They happen through small, intentional steps."

The Problem: A 2022 study by American Psychological Association found that only 8% of New Year’s resolutions are successful. Most people fail because they lack a structured, actionable plan.

How to Apply It:

  1. Set a 90-Day Sprint Goal:
    • Instead of vague goals like "I want to be a better artist," pick a specific, measurable action:
      • "I will complete one commissioned portrait every month."
  2. Break It Down into Micro-Steps:
    • Example:
      • Week 1: Research reference photos.
      • Week 2: Sketch thumbnails.
      • Week 3: Start the final piece.
  3. Review and Adjust:
    • At the end of 90 days, ask:
      • What worked?
      • What didn’t?
      • What’s the next sprint?

Real-World Example: Painter and Illustrator Loish (real name: Loish) didn’t become a global brand overnight. She designed her career in sprints:

  • Sprint 1 (3 months): Built a strong Instagram following by posting daily sketches.
  • Sprint 2 (3 months): Started selling digital prints on Etsy.
  • Sprint 3 (3 months): Launched a Patreon for exclusive content. Each sprint was intentional, measurable, and built on the last.

Strategy 4: The "Energy Audit" – Where Are You Wasting Your Vitality?

Osborne’s Insight: "You can’t pour from an empty cup. A designed life starts with managing your energy, not just your time."

The Problem: A 2021 Harvard Business Review study found that poor sleep and stress cost the U.S. economy $1.2 trillion annually in lost productivity. Most people track time but ignore energy.

How to Apply It:

  1. Track Your Energy for a Week:
    • Note when you feel most alert, creative, and drained.
    • Example:
      • 9 AM – 12 PM: High energy (best for deep work).
      • 3 PM – 5 PM: Low energy (avoid meetings).
  2. Schedule Tasks Based on Energy Levels:
    • High energy? → Creative work, brainstorming.
    • Low energy? → Admin, emails, repetitive tasks.
  3. Eliminate Energy Drains:
    • Cut back on social media scrolling, toxic relationships, and overcommitment.

Real-World Example: Musician and Producer Kaytranada (real name: Kevin Random) designed his schedule around energy peaks. He:

  • Records music in the morning when his creativity is highest.
  • Takes long walks in nature to recharge.
  • Avoids late-night studio sessions that lead to burnout. This approach has kept him consistently productive for over a decade.

Strategy 5: The "Creative Anchors" Method

Osborne’s Insight: "Creativity isn’t just for artists—it’s a way of living. Anchor your days in creative habits."

The Problem: A 2022 Journal of Creative Behavior study found that creative people who don’t practice regularly lose their edge. Without intentional creative habits, even the most talented individuals stagnate.

How to Apply It:

  1. Choose 3 Creative Anchors:
    • These are non-negotiable daily/weekly creative practices. Examples:
      • Daily: 15-minute sketching.
      • Weekly: A 1-hour "play session" (no rules, just experimentation).
      • Monthly: A "vision board" update.
  2. Protect Them Like Sacred Time:
    • Treat them like non-negotiable appointments.
  3. Experiment and Evolve:
    • If a habit stops feeling fulfilling, swap it out.

Real-World Example: Artist and Illustrator Julie Bell (known for her vibrant, dreamlike work) anchors her creativity in three ways:

  • Morning Pages: She writes three pages of stream-of-consciousness thoughts before starting her day.
  • Weekly "Mood Board" Sessions: She collects images, textures, and colors that inspire her.
  • Sunday "Play Day": She dedicates one day a week to pure experimentation—no pressure, just exploration. This routine keeps her consistently inspired despite a busy career.

Strategy 6: The "Relationship Design" Framework

Osborne’s Insight: "Your relationships should either elevate you or be released. A designed life includes intentional connections."

The Problem: A 2023 American Psychological Association report found that loneliness is at an all-time high, with 50% of Americans reporting feeling isolated. Many people keep toxic or unfulfilling relationships out of fear of being alone.

How to Apply It:

  1. Audit Your Relationships:
    • Ask:
      • Does this person add value to my life?
      • Do I feel energized or drained after interacting with them?
      • Do we share similar values?
  2. Categorize Them:
    • Core Circle (5-10 people): Those who inspire, challenge, and support you.
    • Peripheral Contacts: Friends, acquaintances, or family who don’t require deep energy.
    • Energy Vampires: Relationships that drain you (set boundaries or exit).
  3. Design Intentional Time:
    • Schedule quality time with your core circle (e.g., a monthly dinner, a creative collaboration).

Real-World Example: Designer and Author Seth Godin curated his relationships like a master curator. He:

  • Limited his social media interactions to only those who aligned with his values.
  • Created a "Mastermind Group" of like-minded entrepreneurs.
  • Fired "toxic fans" who demanded constant attention. This intentional relationship design allowed him to focus on high-impact connections.

Strategy 7: The "Legacy Loop" – Designing for the Future You

Osborne’s Insight: "A life by design isn’t just about today—it’s about the legacy you’re building for tomorrow."

The Problem: A 2022 Harvard Business Review study found that only 10% of people feel their lives have meaning. Many live in the present but don’t consider their long-term impact.

How to Apply It:

  1. Ask: "What do I want my future self to thank me for?"
    • Example answers:
      • "I want my future self to thank me for starting that art business."
      • "I want my future self to thank me for taking care of my health."
  2. Create a "Legacy Board":
    • A visual or written document with:
      • Key achievements you want to accomplish.
      • Values you want to embody.
      • Legacy you want to leave (e.g., mentoring others, creating art that lasts).
  3. Take One Legacy Step This Week:
    • Example:
      • "I will document one piece of my creative journey today."
      • "I will reach out to someone who could benefit from my knowledge."

Real-World Example: Artist and Filmmaker Kara Walker didn’t just create art—she designed her legacy. She:

  • Curated her own exhibitions to control her narrative.
  • Wrote essays explaining her work’s deeper meaning.
  • Mentored young artists to ensure her influence continued. By actively designing her legacy, she ensured her work would outlast her career.
