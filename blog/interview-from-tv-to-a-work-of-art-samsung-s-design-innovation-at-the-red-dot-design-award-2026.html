[Interview] From TV to a Work of Art: Samsung’s Design Innovation at the Red Dot Design Award 2026

In an era where design is no longer just about functionality but experience, Samsung’s recent recognition at the Red Dot Design Award 2026 stands as a testament to how technology and aesthetics can merge seamlessly. This surge reflects a growing consumer demand for products that are not just useful but visually compelling—blurring the line between utility and art.

Samsung’s journey from a traditional TV manufacturer to a design-forward innovator is a case study in how brands can transform perception through intentional design choices. In this exclusive interview with Kim Hyun-Joo, Samsung’s Head of Design Innovation, we dive deep into the strategies behind their Red Dot-winning designs, the challenges of balancing form and function, and actionable insights for designers and brands looking to elevate their products.

Why Samsung’s Red Dot Win Matters: A Shift in Consumer Perception

Before we explore the how, let’s examine the why—why Samsung’s design innovations at the Red Dot Design Award 2026 are more than just awards; they represent a paradigm shift in how we view technology.

1. The Rise of "Design as a Competitive Advantage"

According to a 2025 Nielsen study, 62% of consumers now consider design as a key factor in their purchasing decisions, surpassing even price and brand loyalty. Samsung’s Red Dot wins are not just accolades—they are marketing assets that reinforce their position as a leader in both technology and aesthetics.

2. The TV as a Canvas: From Flat Screens to Interactive Art

The television, once seen as a passive entertainment device, is now evolving into an interactive, multi-sensory experience. Samsung’s QD-OLED TVs and MicroLED displays didn’t just win for their technical specs—they won for their architectural design, ergonomic flexibility, and immersive user interfaces.

The Red Dot Award evaluates designs based on three core principles:

Samsung’s winning designs mastered all three, proving that great design is not an afterthought—it’s the foundation.

Exclusive Interview: Samsung’s Design Philosophy Behind the Red Dot Wins

We sat down with Kim Hyun-Joo, Samsung’s Head of Design Innovation, to discuss the strategies, challenges, and future trends that led to their Red Dot Design Award 2026 victories.

1. "Design Starts with Understanding the User’s Emotional Journey"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "At Samsung, we don’t just design for the user—we design with the user. Our Red Dot-winning products, like the Neo QLED TV series, weren’t just about better picture quality. They were about creating an emotional connection."

Key Takeaway: Great design isn’t about features—it’s about how a product makes the user feel.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Conduct "Emotional Design Audits" – Instead of traditional user testing, ask: "Does this product evoke joy, confidence, or aspiration?" ✅ Use "Micro-Moments" in Design – Small interactions (like a TV’s adaptive sound response) can create lasting impressions.

Real-World Example: Samsung’s The Frame TV (a Red Dot nominee in 2025) wasn’t just a TV—it was a gallery wall. Its minimalist frame design and AI-powered art display turned it into a conversation starter, not just a screen.

2. "Innovation Comes from Breaking Silos Between Teams"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "One of the biggest challenges was getting our engineers, marketers, and designers to speak the same language. The Red Dot winners emerged from cross-functional design sprints where we forced collaboration."

Key Takeaway: Silos kill innovation. The best designs come from unexpected partnerships between departments.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Host "Design Hackathons" – Bring together engineers, UX designers, and marketers to brainstorm outside their usual roles. ✅ Implement "Design Champions" – Assign a single point person to ensure design thinking is embedded in every stage of product development.

Real-World Example: Samsung’s Galaxy Watch 7 (a Red Dot winner) integrated biometric health tracking with a sleek, minimalist watch face. This wasn’t just an engineering feat—it was a design-led collaboration between hardware, software, and UX teams.

3. "Sustainability is No Longer Optional—It’s a Design Imperative"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "The Red Dot jury praised our eco-conscious materials—like the recycled ocean plastic used in the Galaxy Buds Pro. Designers today must ask: ‘How can this product be beautiful and sustainable?’"

Key Takeaway: Consumers are 3x more likely to buy from brands with strong sustainability credentials (Deloitte, 2025).

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Adopt "Circular Design" Principles – Design products that are easy to repair, upgrade, or recycle. ✅ Use "Transparency Reports" – Publish material sourcing data to build trust with eco-conscious buyers.

Real-World Example: Samsung’s Galaxy S24 Ultra used post-consumer recycled aluminum in its frame, reducing carbon emissions by 20%. The Red Dot jury noted that this wasn’t just a greenwashing move—it was a genuine design choice that enhanced durability.

4. "The Future of Design: AI as a Co-Creator, Not a Replacement"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "AI is transforming design, but it’s not replacing human intuition. Our Red Dot-winning AI-powered design tools—like those used in the MicroLED TVs—help us explore more possibilities faster, but the final creative decisions are still human."

Key Takeaway: AI should augment, not replace, human creativity.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Use AI for "Design Exploration" – Tools like Adobe Firefly can generate hundreds of design variations in minutes. ✅ Train Designers in "AI-Augmented Creativity" – Teach teams to refine AI suggestions rather than rely on them blindly.

Real-World Example: Samsung’s QD-OLED TVs used AI-driven color calibration to ensure perfect contrast and brightness across all models. The design team didn’t just accept the AI’s output—they iterated on it to refine the emotional impact of the visuals.

5. "The Power of 'Anti-Design'—When Less is More"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "Some of our most successful designs—like the Galaxy Book Flex—were minimalist. The Red Dot jury loved how we removed unnecessary buttons, simplified controls, and focused on pure functionality."

Key Takeaway: In a world of cluttered interfaces, simplicity is the ultimate luxury.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Apply "The One Less Button Rule" – For every new feature, ask: "Can we remove something to make it cleaner?" ✅ Use "Negative Space Strategically" – Empty space can elevate a design (see: Apple’s iPhone minimalism).

Real-World Example: Samsung’s Galaxy Tab S9 won praise for its edge-to-edge display and hidden controls, making it feel premium despite being a tablet. The Red Dot jury called it "a masterclass in anti-bloat design."

6. "Designing for Accessibility: Because Great Design is Inclusive"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "Our Red Dot-winning Galaxy Watch 6 included adaptive display settings for low vision users. Design isn’t just about aesthetics—it’s about removing barriers."

Key Takeaway: Accessibility isn’t a checklist—it’s a design mindset.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Conduct "Inclusive Design Workshops" – Involve people with disabilities in the design process. ✅ Use "Design for All" Frameworks – Tools like WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) can guide universal design.

Real-World Example: Samsung’s QLED TVs included AI-powered voice commands and high-contrast modes, making them usable for people with hearing or visual impairments. The Red Dot jury highlighted this as a standout feature in their category.

7. "The Role of Storytelling in Design Success"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "A product can be beautifully designed, but if no one understands why it matters, it won’t win. Our Red Dot campaigns—like the ‘Design Beyond Limits’ series—told human stories behind our innovations."

Key Takeaway: Design needs narrative to resonate.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Create "Design Origin Stories" – Share how a product was conceived (e.g., "This feature was born from a user’s frustration"). ✅ **Use Micro-Documentaries – Short films (like Samsung’s "The Frame: A New Way to Live") can humanize your design.

Real-World Example: Samsung’s MicroLED TV campaign featured real families using the TV as a central hub for entertainment and creativity, not just a screen. This story-driven approach helped it stand out at the Red Dot Awards.

8. "The Future: Designing for the 'Metaverse' Era"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "The next Red Dot winners will likely come from immersive tech—like holographic displays or wearable AR glasses. We’re already experimenting with design principles for the metaverse."

Key Takeaway: The next frontier of design is not just physical—it’s digital, interactive, and spatial.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ Study "Spatial Design" – How can 3D interfaces feel intuitive? ✅ Explore "Tactile Digital Experiences" – Even in VR, haptic feedback matters.

Real-World Example: While Samsung hasn’t yet launched a Red Dot-winning metaverse product, their XR (Extended Reality) research includes ergonomic AR glasses designed to feel natural on the face—a critical design challenge for future wearables.

9. "The Psychology of Color in Red Dot-Winning Designs"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "Color isn’t just decoration—it’s emotional engineering. Our Neo QLED TVs used deep blues and warm ambers to create a calming yet aspirational feel."

Key Takeaway: Colors influence mood, perception, and even purchasing decisions.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ **Use Color Psychology Guides – Warm colors (reds, oranges) stimulate energy; cool colors (blues, greens) promote calm. ✅ **Test Colors with Cultural Contexts – A color that’s lucky in Korea might be taboo in Japan.

Real-World Example: Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold 5 used a matte black and gold gradient, which the Red Dot jury described as "a luxury material that feels premium yet approachable."

10. "The Business Case for Design Investment"

Kim Hyun-Joo: "Companies often ask, ‘How much should we spend on design?’ Our data shows that brands investing in design see a 22% higher customer retention rate (Forrester, 2025). The Red Dot is a tangible proof point of that ROI."

Key Takeaway: Design is not an expense—it’s a growth accelerator.

Actionable Strategy: ✅ **Track Design ROI Metrics – Measure customer satisfaction, brand loyalty, and sales lift from design initiatives. ✅ **Allocate 10-15% of R&D Budget to Design – This is the sweet spot for innovation (Harvard Business Review, 2024).

Common Mistakes in Design Innovation (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most innovative brands make design blunders. Here’s how Samsung avoided them—and how you can too.

❌ Mistake 1: Prioritizing Features Over Experience

Problem: Some companies dump features into products without considering user flow. Samsung’s Fix: "We audited every interaction point—from powering on to adjusting settings—before launching the Galaxy Watch 7."

How to Avoid It: ✔ **Use Journey Mapping – Visualize the entire user experience, not just the product. ✔ Ask: "Would a first-time user understand this?"

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring Material Realism

Problem: Some "futuristic" designs feel cheap because they use unrealistic materials. Samsung’s Fix: "Our MicroLED TVs use real glass and metal finishes—not plastic that looks like metal."

How to Avoid It: ✔ **Test with Tactile Mockups – Does it feel premium? ✔ **Work with Material Scientists – Not just designers.

❌ Mistake 3: Overcomplicating the UI

Problem: Too many buttons, menus, and settings frustrate users. Samsung’s Fix: "The Galaxy Tab S9 had zero unnecessary buttons—just one intuitive interface."

How to Avoid It: ✔ **Apply The "One Less Thing" Rule – For every new feature, remove something. ✔ **Use User Testing with Real People – Not just internal teams.

Problem: Many brands add sustainability as an afterthought. Samsung’s Fix: "Our recycled materials were baked into the design process from day one."
