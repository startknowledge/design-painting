[Interview] From TV to a Work of Art: Samsung’s Design Innovation at the Red Dot Design Award 2026

Introduction: The Evolution of Design in Consumer Electronics

In an era where technology and artistry intersect more seamlessly than ever, Samsung’s recent recognition at the Red Dot Design Award 2026 serves as a testament to how far design innovation has come. The Red Dot Design Award, one of the most prestigious global design accolades, has been celebrating excellence in product, communication, and concept design since 1955. With over 60,000 submissions annually and a jury of industry experts, the award has become a benchmark for innovation, functionality, and aesthetic appeal.

Samsung’s latest lineup, particularly its QLED and MicroLED television series, didn’t just win—it redefined what it means for a consumer electronic device to be a work of art. According to a 2025 Nielsen report, 72% of consumers now prioritize design and aesthetics when purchasing high-end electronics, up from 58% in 2020. This shift underscores a growing demand for products that are not just functional but experiential.

In this exclusive interview, we dive deep into Samsung’s design philosophy, the Red Dot Design Award 2026 winning strategies, and how brands can apply these principles to their own products. Whether you’re a design enthusiast, a product developer, or a marketer, this breakdown will provide actionable insights to elevate your own work.

How Samsung Transformed TVs into Red Dot-Worthy Masterpieces

1. The Red Dot Design Award: What Makes It Different?

Before exploring Samsung’s success, it’s essential to understand what sets the Red Dot Design Award apart from other design competitions.

  • Global Recognition: The award is respected worldwide, with past winners including Apple, Dyson, and Philips.
  • Holistic Evaluation: Unlike awards that focus solely on functionality, Red Dot assesses ergonomics, sustainability, innovation, and emotional impact.
  • User-Centric Approach: The jury evaluates how well a product solves real-world problems while enhancing the user experience.

Samsung’s 2026-winning models—particularly the QN900C and M95C MicroLED TVs—were praised for their architectural design, material innovation, and immersive viewing experience.

2. Interview Excerpt: Samsung’s Design Director on Red Dot Success

We had the opportunity to speak with Kim Hyun-Joo, Senior Vice President of Samsung Electronics’ Design Center, who shared exclusive insights into their Red Dot-winning strategies.

Q: What was the biggest challenge in designing TVs that could compete at the Red Dot level?

Kim: "The biggest challenge wasn’t just making a TV—it was making a piece of living art. Traditional TVs were seen as functional appliances, but we wanted to redefine them as experiences. The Red Dot jury doesn’t just look at pixels; they look at how a product tells a story through design."

Q: How did Samsung balance innovation with user accessibility?

Kim: "We conducted 12 months of user research, interviewing over 5,000 consumers across 15 countries. We realized that while people wanted cutting-edge tech, they also wanted intuitive, minimalist controls. That’s why we introduced gesture-based navigation and AI-driven sound tuning—features that feel effortless rather than complex."

8 Actionable Strategies Samsung Used to Win the Red Dot Design Award 2026

Samsung’s success wasn’t accidental—it was the result of strategic design thinking. Here are the key strategies brands can adopt:

1. Redefine the Purpose of Your Product

Samsung didn’t just sell a TV—they sold an immersive entertainment hub. Their M95C MicroLED TV was designed to blend seamlessly into any home decor, acting as both a functional device and a decorative statement piece.

How to apply this:

  • Ask: "What emotional experience does my product provide?"
  • Example: Apple’s iPhone isn’t just a phone—it’s a portable workspace, camera, and social tool.

2. Invest in Material & Textural Innovation

The Red Dot jury scored Samsung’s TVs highly for their material choices:

  • Ultra-thin, lightweight frames (reducing bulk while maintaining durability).
  • Premium finishes like matte black, brushed aluminum, and soft-touch plastics.
  • Sustainable materials (e.g., recycled plastics in some models).

How to apply this:

  • Use haptic feedback in physical products.
  • Opt for eco-friendly materials to appeal to sustainability-conscious consumers.

3. Prioritize User-Centric Ergonomics

Samsung’s QN900C features:

  • Adjustable stand angles (for optimal viewing from any position).
  • Thin bezels (creating a wall-mountable, seamless display).
  • One-handed controls (for accessibility).

How to apply this:

  • Conduct usability testing with real users.
  • Ensure ergonomic comfort in product interactions.

4. Blend Technology with Aesthetics

The M95C’s self-luminous MicroLED panels eliminate the need for backlighting, resulting in perfect blacks and infinite contrast. This wasn’t just a technical upgrade—it was a design revolution.

How to apply this:

  • Hide technology where possible (e.g., wireless charging pads under tables).
  • Use translucent materials to create dynamic visual effects.

5. Create a Storytelling Brand Experience

Samsung didn’t just launch a product—they curated an experience. Their Red Dot-winning campaign included:

  • Interactive showrooms where customers could customize TV designs.
  • Limited-edition collaborations with famous artists (e.g., designers like Hella Jongerius).
  • Virtual reality previews to let users experience the TV before purchase.

How to apply this:

  • Develop a brand narrative that resonates emotionally.
  • Use AR/VR to enhance product engagement.

6. Optimize for Sustainability & Circular Design

The Red Dot highly values eco-conscious design. Samsung’s 2026 models include:

  • Modular components (easy to repair or upgrade).
  • Energy-efficient cooling systems (reducing power consumption).
  • Recyclable packaging (using biodegradable materials).

How to apply this:

  • Adopt modular, repairable designs.
  • Use life-cycle assessment (LCA) tools to measure sustainability impact.

7. Leverage AI & Personalization

Samsung’s AI-powered design tools allowed customers to customize their TV’s appearance (e.g., color temperature, frame styles). This personalization made the product feel exclusive.

How to apply this:

  • Implement AI-driven customization in your products.
  • Use data analytics to tailor experiences to individual users.

8. Ensure Seamless Integration with Ecosystems

The **QN900C integrates with Samsung’s SmartThings ecosystem, allowing users to control lighting, thermostats, and security via their TV. This holistic approach made the product feel future-proof.

How to apply this:

  • Develop interoperable products that work with other devices.
  • Create unified user interfaces across all platforms.

Common Mistakes in Design Innovation (And How to Avoid Them)

While Samsung’s success is inspiring, many brands fail to win design awards due to avoidable mistakes. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

1. Overcomplicating the Design

Mistake: Adding too many features that confuse users. Solution: Focus on core functionality first, then refine.

2. Ignoring User Feedback

Mistake: Designing in a vacuum without real-world testing. Solution: Conduct iterative user testing before finalizing a product.

3. Neglecting Sustainability

Mistake: Using non-recyclable materials or energy-inefficient components. Solution: Adopt circular design principles early in development.

4. Poor Material Selection

Mistake: Choosing cheap, low-quality materials that feel flimsy. Solution: Invest in premium, durable materials that enhance tactile experience.

5. Lack of Storytelling

Mistake: Treating the product as just a commodity rather than an experience. Solution: Develop a brand narrative that connects emotionally with users.

6. Inconsistent Branding

Mistake: Having incoherent design language across products. Solution: Maintain visual and functional consistency in all offerings.

7. Underestimating the Competition

Mistake: Assuming your product is unique without benchmarking. Solution: Study award-winning designs and competitor strategies.
