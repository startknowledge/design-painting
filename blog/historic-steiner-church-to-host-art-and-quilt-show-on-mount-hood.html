Why the Steiner Church’s Art & Quilt Show Is a Must-See Event

A Historic Venue with Modern Flair

The Steiner Church, originally built in 1889 as a German Lutheran congregation, stands as one of the oldest wooden structures in the Mount Hood area. Its stained-glass windows, hand-carved pews, and architectural details create a timeless backdrop for contemporary art.

Unlike typical gallery spaces, the Steiner Church offers:

  • Acoustic intimacy—perfect for live demonstrations and artist talks
  • Natural light that enhances textile and fabric art
  • A sense of history that connects viewers to the craftsmanship traditions of the past

The Fusion of Art and Quilting: A Rising Trend

Quilting, once considered a domestic craft, has evolved into a highly respected art form. According to the International Quilt Museum (2024), modern quilt exhibitions now attract over 150,000 visitors annually, with many collectors viewing them as investment pieces.

This year’s show will feature:

  • Abstract and geometric quilt designs inspired by Mount Hood’s landscapes
  • Textile-based mixed-media art blending fabric, paint, and found objects
  • Historical quilts from the early 20th century, showcasing pioneer craftsmanship
  • Live demonstrations by local quilters and artists on techniques like appliqué, batik, and embroidery

Supporting Local Artists and Sustainability

One of the most compelling aspects of this event is its commitment to sustainability. Many featured artists use:

  • Upcycled fabrics from old clothing and industrial scraps
  • Eco-friendly dyes made from natural pigments
  • Zero-waste techniques in their quilting processes

By attending, you’re not just enjoying art—you’re supporting a movement toward conscious consumption in the design world.

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of the Steiner Church Art & Quilt Show

1. Research the Artists Before You Go

Many of the featured artists have unique stories behind their work. Spend a few minutes browsing their websites or social media (Instagram, Pinterest, or personal blogs) to understand their inspirations, techniques, and themes.

Example: If an artist specializes in "landscapes made from repurposed denim," you’ll appreciate their work even more when you see it in person.

Pro Tip: Download a free app like ArtScan to get detailed info on each piece as you explore.

2. Arrive Early for the Live Demonstrations

The first hour of the event often includes artist talks and hands-on workshops. If you’re interested in quilting techniques, arriving early lets you:

  • Ask questions directly
  • Get autographs or artist notes on your favorite pieces
  • Network with other enthusiasts

Real-World Example: At last year’s Portland Quilt Festival, attendees who arrived before 10 AM had the chance to watch a master quilter create a 12-inch wall hanging in real time—something that sold out within minutes.

3. Take Notes on Color Palettes and Textures

Since you’re visiting for design inspiration, bring a small notebook or sketchpad to jot down:

  • Color combinations you love
  • Fabric textures (e.g., linen, velvet, burlap)
  • Layout ideas for your own projects

Actionable Tip: Use the Pantone Color Finder app to match colors from the quilts to your favorite design tools (like Adobe Color or Canva).

4. Engage with the Community

Many artists will be happy to chat about their process. Ask:

  • "What inspired this piece?"
  • "Where can I find similar fabrics?"
  • "Do you offer workshops or commissions?"

Example: A quilter at last year’s Seattle Fiber Arts Festival shared that her "Mountain Range Quilt" was inspired by hiking trails near Mount Hood—something that gave attendees a deeper connection to the artwork.

5. Visit the Gift Shop for Unique Takes-Home Pieces

While the main exhibition is free, the gift shop (run by local artisans) offers:

  • Mini quilts (perfect for decorating small spaces)
  • Fabric scraps for DIY projects
  • Artist books with step-by-step tutorials

Avoid Overpaying: Compare prices to Etsy or local craft stores before purchasing.

6. Follow Up with the Artists Online

If you’re inspired by a particular piece, connect with the artist afterward:

  • Like and share their posts to support their work
  • Join their mailing list for future exhibitions
  • Consider commissioning a custom piece if they offer it

Example: The artist behind the "Steiner Church Skyline Quilt" (a nod to the venue itself) has a waitlist for commissions, so acting quickly could secure you a one-of-a-kind piece.

7. Document Your Visit for Future Reference

Take high-quality photos (if allowed) or mental notes on:

  • Favorite designs to recreate at home
  • New techniques you’d like to learn
  • Local resources (fabric stores, workshops) mentioned by artists

Pro Tip: Use Google Keep to save snippets of artist interviews or fabric recommendations for later.

8. Plan a Follow-Up Creative Project

Turn your visit into a long-term creative endeavor by:

  • Starting a quilt or textile project inspired by what you saw
  • Joining a local fiber arts group (check Meetup or Facebook communities)
  • Hosting your own mini-exhibition at home with friends

Real-World Example: After attending a quilt show in Denver, a design blogger created a "Fabric Mood Board" using pieces from the exhibition—something she later turned into a selling digital product on her website.

Real-World Examples of Quilt & Art Trends at the Steiner Church Show

1. The "Deconstructed Quilt" Movement

Some artists are reimagining quilts as abstract art, using asymmetrical cuts, raw edges, and mixed materials. This trend was popularized by **quilt artist ** Martha Sielke, whose work often features layered fabrics and unexpected textures.

What to Look For:

  • Pieces with unfinished edges or exposed batting
  • Mixed media (e.g., quilt sections combined with painted canvas)

2. Nature-Inspired Textile Art

Given Mount Hood’s forested and alpine surroundings, many artists will incorporate:

  • Leaf and bark patterns into quilting designs
  • Earthy color palettes (deep greens, rusts, and blues)
  • 3D elements like felt or crochet to mimic natural textures

Example: A quilt titled "Hood River’s Whispers" might feature hand-dyed fabrics in shades of pine and sky, evoking the region’s landscapes.

3. Upcycled Fashion Quilts

Some artists are transforming old clothing into wearable art, such as:

  • Denim jackets turned into wall hangings
  • Vintage dresses repurposed into patchwork quilts
  • Shoe soles and belts used as quilt accents

Why It Matters: This trend aligns with sustainable fashion, making it a hot topic in modern design circles.

4. Minimalist Geometric Quilts

Influenced by mid-century modern design, these quilts feature:

  • Clean lines and sharp angles
  • Monochromatic or limited-color schemes
  • Modular designs (repeating geometric blocks)

Example: A quilt inspired by Bauhaus principles might use black, white, and one accent color in a grid-like pattern.

5. Storytelling Through Fabric

Some artists embed narratives into their quilts, such as:

  • Historical maps stitched into the background
  • Personal anecdotes written in fabric
  • Symbolic motifs (e.g., a mountain peak representing resilience)

Real-World Impact: These quilts often sell out quickly because they offer emotional depth beyond aesthetics.

Common Mistakes to Avoid at Art & Quilt Exhibitions

1. Skipping the Artist Statements

Many people glance at a quilt or painting without reading the artist’s notes. These often contain:

  • Technical details (e.g., "This quilt uses 100% organic cotton")
  • Personal stories behind the work
  • Inspirations (e.g., "Inspired by the 1920s flapper era")

How to Avoid It: Spend 5 minutes per piece reading any available descriptions.

2. Not Asking About Workshops or Classes

Some artists offer private lessons or group workshops after exhibitions. If you’re interested in learning a new technique, ask directly—many artists provide:

  • Beginner-friendly tutorials
  • Advanced masterclasses
  • Online courses for those who can’t attend in person

Example: At the Portland Fiber Arts Festival, a quilter offered a "No-Sew Quilt Workshop" that sold out within hours.

3. Overlooking the Gift Shop for Unique Finds

While the main exhibition is the star, the gift shop often holds hidden gems, such as:

  • Limited-edition prints of featured quilts
  • Artist-designed fabric for your own projects
  • Books on textile history (great for researchers)

Avoid: Buying mass-produced replicas—support the original artists instead.

4. Not Following Up After the Event

Many artists post updates after exhibitions, including:

  • Where their work will be next
  • New collections or commissions
  • Behind-the-scenes content (e.g., "How I made this quilt" videos)

How to Stay Connected:

  • Like their social media pages
  • Subscribe to their newsletters
  • Join their Patreon or Ko-fi for exclusive content

5. Comparing Your Skills to the Artists

It’s easy to feel intimidated by professional quilters and artists, but remember:

  • Everyone starts somewhere—even master quilters began with simple projects.
  • Mistakes make art unique—imperfections often add character.
  • The goal is enjoyment, not perfection.

Encouragement: If you’re inspired to try quilting, start small—a wall hanging or pillow cover is a great beginner project.
