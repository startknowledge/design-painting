Here’s How to Style Textile Art Like an Interior Designer: A Step-by-Step Guide

Introduction: Why Textile Art is the Secret Weapon of Modern Interior Design

Textile art has evolved from simple wall hangings and decorative pillows into a powerful design element that adds warmth, texture, and personality to any space. According to a 2023 report by the Global Textile Market, interior designers increasingly incorporate handcrafted and woven textiles to create visually striking and emotionally resonant environments.

But here’s the catch: Not all textile art blends seamlessly into a room. Poor placement, clashing patterns, or overpowering colors can turn a stunning piece into an eyesore. That’s where strategic styling comes in—just like a pro interior designer would.

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll break down 10 actionable strategies to style textile art like a professional, complete with real-world examples, common mistakes to avoid, and a FAQ section to clarify any doubts.

Let’s dive in.

Why Textile Art Matters in Interior Design

Before we explore styling techniques, it’s essential to understand why textile art is so impactful in interior design:

  1. Adds Depth & Texture – Unlike flat surfaces, textiles introduce tactile contrast, making a room feel more dynamic.
  2. Softens Hard Lines – In modern or minimalist spaces, textiles like sheer curtains or chunky knit throws balance sharp edges and geometric shapes.
  3. Injects Personality – Handwoven tapestries, embroidered wall hangings, or vintage quilts tell a story, making a space feel uniquely yours.
  4. Enhances Color Palettes – A well-chosen textile can tie together a room’s color scheme or serve as a bold statement piece.
  5. Improves Acoustics – Thick rugs and upholstered furniture absorb sound, making spaces like home offices or living rooms more comfortable.

A 2024 study by the American Society of Interior Designers (ASID) found that 78% of homeowners who incorporated textiles into their decor reported feeling more relaxed and satisfied with their spaces.

Now, let’s explore how to style textile art flawlessly.

10 Pro Tips to Style Textile Art Like an Interior Designer

1. Choose the Right Scale for the Space

One of the biggest mistakes is selecting a textile that’s too small for the room or overwhelming in size. Here’s how to get it right:

  • For Large Rooms (Living Rooms, Dining Areas): Opt for floor-length tapestries, oversized rugs, or statement curtains. A 6x9 ft woven wall hanging in a spacious living room creates a focal point without dominating the space. Example: A Moroccan-style kilim rug in deep reds and blues in a modern Scandinavian living room adds warmth while maintaining balance.

  • For Small Rooms (Bedrooms, Home Offices): Smaller textiles like embroidered wall hangings (2x3 ft) or throw pillows work best. A textured throw blanket draped over a chair adds coziness without clutter.

  • For Galleries or Hallways: Vertical textiles (like tall tapestries or fabric panels) draw the eye upward, making narrow spaces feel more expansive.

Pro Tip: If unsure, measure the wall or furniture surface where the textile will hang. A general rule is to allow at least 6 inches of space between the textile and the ceiling or floor for a polished look.

2. Match (or Contrast) with Your Existing Decor

Textile art should complement your room’s aesthetic, not compete with it. Here’s how to align it with your style:

A. Matching the Color Palette

  • Neutral Rooms (Beige, White, Gray): Opt for earthy tones (terracotta, olive green, warm brown) or soft pastels (pale blue, blush pink). Example: A neutral-toned linen tapestry in a minimalist white bedroom softens the space while keeping it cohesive.

  • Bold & Eclectic Spaces: Use high-contrast textiles—like a black-and-white geometric throw pillow in a room with deep reds and golds—to create visual interest.

B. Playing with Patterns

  • If your room has busy patterns (floral, stripes, etc.), choose a solid-colored or subtle-textured textile to avoid visual chaos. Example: A solid cream velvet curtain in a room with floral wallpaper keeps the focus on the furniture without overwhelming the space.

  • For monochromatic or minimalist rooms, a bold patterned textile (like a tribal-inspired wall hanging) adds personality. Example: A deep navy and gold embroidered tapestry in a black-and-white modern apartment becomes the room’s artistic centerpiece.

Pro Tip: Use the 60-30-10 rule—60% of the room’s color scheme, 30% secondary, and 10% an accent. Textile art often falls into the 10% accent category, making it a strategic design choice.

3. Layer Textiles for a Luxurious Feel

Layering textiles creates depth and sophistication, making a space feel more luxurious without spending a fortune. Here’s how to do it right:

  • Rugs: Start with a base rug, then add a smaller throw rug on top for texture contrast. Example: A Persian-style rug under a chunky jute rug in a living room adds warmth and visual interest.

  • Curtains & Drapes: Combine sheer curtains (for light filtering) with heavy velvet drapes (for drama). Example: In a coastal-style bedroom, sheer white linen curtains paired with deep emerald velvet drapes create a romantic yet modern look.

  • Bedding & Pillows: Mix different textures—like linen sheets, wool blankets, and silk pillows—for a luxurious bedding ensemble. Example: A neutral linen duvet with faux fur throw pillows and a handwoven wool blanket in a bedroom adds cozy depth.

Pro Tip: Stick to 2-3 textures per layer to avoid a cluttered look. For example, in a living room, you might combine:

  • A chunky knit throw (texture)
  • A woven jute rug (texture)
  • A solid-colored velvet sofa (color contrast)

4. Use Textile Art to Define Zones in Open-Plan Spaces

Open-concept homes benefit from textile art to create distinct zones without walls. Here’s how:

  • Living & Dining Area Separation: Hang a large floor-length tapestry between the two spaces to visually divide them. Example: A bohemian-style wall hanging in a living room that extends into the dining area softens the transition between the two zones.

  • Home Office & Living Room: Place a textured rug under the desk and add embroidered wall art above the workspace to anchor the area. Example: A deep red velvet rug in a home office with gold-embroidered wall hangings creates a luxurious workspace separate from the living area.

Pro Tip: Use different textures in each zone to reinforce separation. For instance, a smooth leather sofa in the living room vs. a chunky knit armchair in the office.

5. Consider the Room’s Functionality

Not all textile art is created equal—some pieces are better suited for relaxation, while others work better in social spaces. Here’s how to choose:

Room Function Best Textile Art Choices Why It Works
Bedroom Soft throws, embroidered wall hangings, silk bedding Encourages relaxation and comfort
Living Room Oversized rugs, patterned curtains, woven tapestries Creates a welcoming and social atmosphere
Home Office Neutral-toned rugs, minimalist wall art, linen blinds Promotes focus and professionalism
Dining Room Table runners, napkin rings, woven placemats Adds elegance to meals
Bathroom Linen towels, woven baskets, textured shower curtains Enhances luxury and spa-like vibes

Example: In a master bedroom, a handwoven wool throw blanket on the bed paired with silk curtains creates a serene retreat, while in a guest bedroom, a bold patterned rug adds energy for social gatherings.

6. Play with Light & Shadow for Dramatic Effects

Textiles interact with light in ways that can transform a room’s ambiance. Here’s how to use them strategically:

  • Sheer Textiles for Soft Light: Sheer curtains or linen drapes diffuse natural light, creating a gentle, airy feel. Example: In a sunny kitchen, sheer white curtains paired with a woven jute table runner keep the space bright and inviting.

  • Heavy Fabrics for Moody Atmospheres: Velvet, brocade, or blackout curtains absorb light, making spaces feel cozy and intimate. Example: A deep burgundy velvet curtain in a home theater or bedroom adds dramatic depth while blocking harsh light.

  • Metallic or Iridescent Textiles for Glow: Fabrics with shimmering threads (like silk with gold accents) reflect light, adding luxury. Example: A silver-thread embroidered wall hanging in a modern living room catches the light, making the space feel more expansive.

Pro Tip: If using dark textiles, pair them with bright lighting (like a floor lamp or pendant light) to prevent the room from feeling too heavy.

7. Mix Traditional & Modern Textiles for Eclectic Charm

The best interiors often blend old-world craftsmanship with contemporary design. Here’s how to do it seamlessly:

  • Pair a Vintage Rug with Modern Furniture: A Persian rug on a mid-century modern sofa adds warmth without clashing. Example: A deep red Persian rug under a sleek black leather sofa in a Scandinavian-style living room creates a balanced contrast.

  • Use Embroidered Wall Hangings in a Minimalist Space: A single, large embroidered tapestry in a white-walled apartment becomes an artistic focal point. Example: A black-and-white embroidered wall hanging in a monochrome bedroom adds depth without overwhelming the space.

  • Combine Woven Baskets with Sleek Shelving: Handwoven baskets on a minimalist floating shelf add texture while keeping the look organized. Example: A rattan basket filled with linen throws on a black shelf in a modern entryway adds warmth to an otherwise cool space.

Pro Tip: Stick to one traditional piece per room to avoid a busy look. For example, in a modern living room, you might keep the rug traditional but pair it with sleek, linear furniture.

8. Use Textile Art to Tell a Story

The most memorable interiors have a narrative. Textile art can reflect cultural heritage, personal memories, or artistic influences. Here’s how to weave a story:

  • Cultural Heritage: Incorporate family heirlooms or regionally inspired textiles to honor your roots. Example: A Moroccan kilim rug passed down through generations in a living room becomes a family legacy piece.

  • Personal Travel Memories: Display fabrics from your travels—like a Spanish lace tablecloth or an Indian block-printed scarf—to evoke nostalgia. Example: A handwoven Peruvian tapestry in a home office reminds the homeowner of their trip to Machu Picchu.

  • Artistic Influences: If you love boho, Scandinavian, or industrial design, choose textiles that reflect those aesthetics. Example: A boho-style embroidered wall hanging in a Scandinavian-inspired bedroom adds warmth while keeping the minimalist vibe.

Pro Tip: If you’re unsure about a piece’s placement, rotate it seasonally to keep your space feeling fresh.

9. Avoid Common Textile Art Mistakes (And How to Fix Them)

Even the best-intentioned decorators make mistakes. Here are the most frequent errors and how to correct them:

Mistake Why It’s Bad How to Fix It
Hanging textiles too high or low Creates visual imbalance Hang at eye level (about 57-60 inches from the floor for wall hangings)
Using too many patterns in one room Overwhelms the space Stick to 1-2 patterns and balance with solids
Ignoring the textile’s weight Heavy fabrics can sag or look cheap Use proper hangers (like D-rings for tapestries) and reinforce walls if needed
Not cleaning or maintaining textiles Fading, dust, and wear Vacuum regularly, use fabric protectors, and rotate pieces to prevent sun damage
Placing textiles in high-moisture areas Molds, mildew, and fabric rot Avoid hanging textiles in bathrooms or kitchens unless they’re water-resistant
Matching textiles to furniture instead of the room Can look forced Choose textiles that complement the room’s overall color scheme

Example of a Fix: If you’ve hung a heavy velvet curtain too high, lower it slightly and add a woven rod cover to prevent sagging.

10. Experiment with Unconventional Placements

Why stick to walls and floors when textiles can be functional art? Here are creative ways to use them:

  • Textile Backsplashes: A linen or woven fabric behind a kitchen counter adds texture and ease of cleaning. Example: A neutral-toned linen backsplash in a farmhouse kitchen keeps the space cozy while being practical.

  • Fabric Headboards: Instead of wood or metal, a handwoven tapestry or embroidered panel as a headboard adds character. Example: A deep blue embroidered wall hanging used as a headboard in a bedroom creates a dramatic focal point.

  • Textile Room Dividers: In small spaces, a fabric panel on a metal frame divides areas without closing them off. Example: A sheer fabric divider in a studio apartment separates the bedroom from the living area while keeping it open.
