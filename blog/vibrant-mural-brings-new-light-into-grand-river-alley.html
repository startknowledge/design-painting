8 Actionable Strategies to Create a Mural That Transforms a Space

If you’re planning a mural project—whether for a city alley, a corporate building, or a community center—here are eight proven strategies to ensure success.

1. Conduct Thorough Research Before Designing

What to Do:

  • Study the location’s history (e.g., industrial past, cultural roots).
  • Survey the community—ask what emotions or themes they associate with the space.
  • Analyze foot traffic patterns—where do people currently walk, and where do they avoid?

Example: In Detroit’s "Murals of Detroit" project, artists studied each alley’s history before painting. One mural depicted the city’s automotive heritage, while another celebrated Black cultural contributions—making each piece meaningful to locals.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Assuming you know what the community wants—always verify with data. ❌ Ignoring practical constraints (e.g., sunlight exposure, wind direction).

2. Choose a Theme That Resonates Deeply

What to Do:

  • Pick a theme with universal appeal (nature, community, history, futurism).
  • Incorporate local elements (landmarks, famous residents, cultural symbols).
  • Make it interactive (hidden messages, QR codes, augmented reality layers).

Example: The "Grand River Bloom" mural used local flora to symbolize renewal, while hidden QR codes linked to oral histories of the alley’s past.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Overcomplicating the design—keep it visually striking but easy to understand. ❌ Using clichés (e.g., a generic "rainbow" without deeper meaning).

3. Involve the Community in the Process

What to Do:

  • Host public workshops where locals can contribute ideas.
  • Create a mural advisory board with business owners, artists, and residents.
  • Allow for feedback during painting—use social media polls or pop-up discussions.

Example: In Austin’s "Walls of Austin" project, artists worked with local schools to develop mural concepts, ensuring youth engagement.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Treating the community as passive observers—they should feel ownership. ❌ Ignoring dissenting opinions—some may not like the direction, and that’s okay—compromise is key.

4. Work with a Skilled Artist (But Don’t Overpay for Name Recognition)

What to Do:

  • **Look for artists with a proven track record in large-scale public art.
  • Check past projects—have they durability issues? Do their murals hold up to weather?
  • Negotiate a fair price—local artists often charge less than international names but bring deep community ties.

Example: For Grand River Alley, the city partnered with a mid-tier artist (Maria Vasquez) who had local credibility but reasonable rates, ensuring high quality without breaking the bank.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Hiring based on Instagram fame—followers ≠ skill. ❌ Skipping the contract—define payment terms, timelines, and revisions.

5. Prioritize Durability & Weather Resistance

What to Do:

  • Use high-quality, outdoor-rated paints (e.g., Kilz Outdoor, Rust-Oleum).
  • Apply a protective sealant (e.g., gloss varnish, acrylic sealer).
  • Consider the climate—if in a rainy area, use waterproof materials.

Example: The "Grand River Bloom" mural used UV-resistant paint to prevent fading, and a satin finish to reduce glare while maintaining brightness.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Using cheap, indoor paint—it chips and peels within months. ❌ Skipping the sealant—sun and rain will ruin the mural in a year.

6. Enhance the Space with Lighting & Interactive Elements

What to Do:

  • Add LED lighting to highlight the mural at night.
  • Incorporate QR codes or NFC tags for digital storytelling.
  • Include seating or photo spots to encourage lingering.

Example: A similar project in Portland added solar-powered LED strips behind the mural, making it visible after dark and attracting nighttime visitors.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Overcomplicating tech—if QR codes don’t work, people will lose interest. ❌ Ignoring safety—tripping hazards near interactive elements can be dangerous.

7. Market the Mural Before, During, and After Completion

What to Do:

  • Tease the project on social media (e.g., "Coming Soon: Grand River Alley’s New Mural!").
  • Host a grand opening event with live painting, music, and food trucks.
  • Encourage user-generated content (e.g., #MyGrandRiverAlley hashtag contest).

Example: The Grand River Alley mural had a countdown campaign, revealing progress photos weekly. The grand opening drew 1,200 attendees, boosting local business sales by 20% that weekend.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Assuming people will just "discover" it—active promotion is crucial. ❌ Not following up—keep the conversation alive with annual events.

8. Measure Success & Plan for Maintenance

What to Do:

  • Track foot traffic before and after (use Google Analytics for nearby businesses).
  • Survey residents on perceived safety and happiness in the area.
  • Schedule regular touch-ups (e.g., repainting faded spots every 2-3 years).

Example: After Grand River Alley’s mural, the city tracked a 45% increase in alley visits and reduced vandalism by 60% due to increased visibility.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes: ❌ Not budgeting for maintenance—murals require care. ❌ Ignoring long-term impact—check in after 1-2 years to see if the effect is lasting.

Common Mistakes in Mural Projects (And How to Fix Them)

Even well-intentioned mural projects can fail if key mistakes are made. Here’s what to avoid at all costs:

1. Skipping Community Input

Problem: If locals don’t feel represented, they may ignore or even vandalize the mural. Solution:

  • Host public meetings.
  • Use surveys and social media polls.
  • Work with local artists who understand the culture.

2. Poor Quality Materials

Problem: Cheap paint fades, cracks, or peels within months, making the mural look neglected. Solution:

  • Invest in outdoor-rated paints.
  • Use a professional painter (not just a "friend who likes art").
  • Apply a protective sealant.

3. Ignoring Practicality (Lighting, Safety, Accessibility)

Problem: A beautiful but poorly lit mural at night becomes useless. Solution:

  • Plan for lighting (solar, LED, or city-provided).
  • Ensure the space is safe (good lighting, no tripping hazards).
  • **Make it wheelchair-accessible
