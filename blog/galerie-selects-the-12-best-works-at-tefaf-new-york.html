The 12 Best Works at TEFAF New York: A Curator’s Picks

Galerie Selects has handpicked these 12 works based on artistic merit, rarity, market demand, and historical impact. Each piece is a testament to its era, offering both aesthetic and financial value.

1. Marc Chagall – The Green Violinist (1947)

Medium: Oil on canvas Estimated Value: $8–12 million

Why It Stands Out: Chagall’s The Green Violinist is a rare gem from his post-WWII period, where he blended Jewish folklore with European modernism. The vibrant green hues and floating figures create a dreamlike, almost surreal atmosphere—unlike his earlier works, which leaned more toward naïve realism.

Key Features:

  • Symbolism: The violinist, a recurring motif in Chagall’s work, represents music as a bridge between worlds.
  • Technique: His loose, expressive brushstrokes contrast with the precise details in the background, making it a masterclass in composition.
  • Provenance: This piece has never been publicly auctioned, making it extremely rare for collectors.

Investment Potential: Chagall’s works have seen consistent appreciation, with his 1940s–1950s pieces becoming highly sought-after. The Green Violinist is ideal for investors looking for long-term growth in modern art.

2. Henri Matisse – The Snail (1953)

Medium: Gouache on paper Estimated Value: $5–7 million

Why It Stands Out: Matisse’s The Snail is a late-career masterpiece, created during his paper cutouts phase, where he experimented with color and form in a radically new way. Unlike his earlier fauvist paintings, this work is minimalist yet dynamic, with bold, flat colors and precise negative space.

Key Features:

  • Symbolism: The snail represents slowness, introspection, and the passage of time—a theme Matisse explored in his final years.
  • Technique: The gouache medium allows for layered transparency, giving the work a luminous quality.
  • Rarity: Only a handful of Matisse gouaches from this period are available, making this a true collector’s item.

Investment Potential: Matisse’s paper cutouts and gouaches have outperformed his paintings in recent auctions. The Snail is a safe bet for those diversifying their modern art portfolio.

3. Alexander Calder – La Grande Vitesse (1969) – Replica

Medium: Steel, aluminum, and brass Estimated Value: $1.5–2.5 million (replica)

Why It Stands Out: While the original La Grande Vitesse (1969) is one of the most iconic kinetic sculptures of the 20th century, this authentic replica—crafted by Calder’s studio—offers a fraction of the cost while retaining the same artistic integrity.

Key Features:

  • Design: The streamlined, aerodynamic form was inspired by French trains, symbolizing modernity and speed.
  • Movement: The balancing mechanism allows the sculpture to swing gently, creating an optical illusion of motion.
  • Provenance: This replica comes with certified documentation from Calder’s estate, ensuring authenticity.

Investment Potential: Original Calder mobiles appreciate steadily, but high-quality replicas are more accessible for emerging collectors. This piece is ideal for investors who want both aesthetic and financial growth.

4. Eileen Gray – E-1027 Table (1929)

Medium: Lacquered wood and steel Estimated Value: $2–3.5 million

Why It Stands Out: Eileen Gray, a pioneer of modernist design, created E-1027 for her iconic villa in Roquebrune, France. This folding table is a perfect blend of functionality and artistry, with sleek lines and innovative mechanics.

Key Features:

  • Design Philosophy: Gray believed in design that serves life, and this table expands and contracts with ease.
  • Materials: The lacquered finish and steel frame make it durable yet elegant.
  • Rarity: Few original E-1027 tables remain, making this a highly coveted piece.

Investment Potential: Gray’s works have **seen a 300%+ increase in value over the past decade. This table is a smart addition for design-focused investors.

5. Andy Warhol – Campbell’s Soup Cans (Tomato) (1962)

Medium: Silkscreen on canvas Estimated Value: $10–15 million

Why It Stands Out: Warhol’s Campbell’s Soup Cans is one of the most recognizable artworks of the 20th century. This specific tomato can is part of a limited-edition series that has rarely surfaced in private sales.

Key Features:

  • Pop Art Revolution: Warhol democratized art by turning everyday objects into high art.
  • Technique: The silkscreen method allowed for mass production, yet each piece retains unique imperfections.
  • Cultural Impact: This work challenged traditional notions of art, making it a cornerstone of modern pop culture.

Investment Potential: Warhol’s soup cans have consistently outperformed the market, with auction records breaking in recent years. This piece is a blue-chip investment for long-term collectors.

6. Jean-Michel Basquiat – Untitled (Skull) (1982)

Medium: Acrylic and oilstick on canvas Estimated Value: $40–60 million

Why It Stands Out: Basquiat’s Untitled (Skull) is a raw, emotional masterpiece from his early career, blending graffiti-style elements with deep symbolic meaning. The skull motif is central to his work, representing mortality, identity, and rebellion.

Key Features:

  • Style: His bold, jagged lines and layered textures create a visceral, almost primal effect.
  • Themes: The work explores African heritage, urban life, and existential questions.
  • Rarity: Few 1982 Basquiats are available, making this a once-in-a-lifetime acquisition.

Investment Potential: Basquiat’s works have seen exponential growth, with record-breaking sales in the past five years. This piece is a high-risk, high-reward investment for bold collectors.

7. Le Corbusier – LC2 Sofa (1928)

Medium: Leather, steel, and wood Estimated Value: $150,000–$300,000

Why It Stand Out: Le Corbusier’s LC2 Sofa is a legendary piece of modernist furniture, designed for functionality and minimalism. Its clean lines and modular design make it timeless.

Key Features:

  • Design Philosophy: Le Corbusier believed in "a house is a machine for living", and the LC2 embodies this utilitarian yet luxurious approach.
  • Materials: The genuine leather and steel frame ensure durability and elegance.
  • Rarity: Original LC2 sofas are extremely scarce, making this a highly desirable collectible.

Investment Potential: Le Corbusier’s furniture has consistently appreciated, with vintage pieces becoming more valuable each year. This sofa is a smart addition for design investors.

8. Salvador Dalí – The Elephants (1948)

Medium: Oil on canvas Estimated Value: $6–9 million

Why It Stands Out: Dalí’s The Elephants is a surrealist nightmare, featuring elephants with delicate, almost fragile legs—a visual paradox that challenges perception.

Key Features:

  • Symbolism: The elephants represent fragility and strength, a recurring theme in Dalí’s work.
  • Technique: His hyper-detailed realism contrasts with the illogical composition, making it visually striking.
  • Provenance: This piece has never been publicly auctioned, adding to its exclusivity.

Investment Potential: Dalí’s surrealist works have steady demand, with 1940s–1950s pieces becoming more valuable. This painting is a strong investment for art enthusiasts.

9. Isamu Noguchi – Akari Light Sculptures (1951–1955)

Medium: Washi paper and wood Estimated Value: $50,000–$150,000

Why It Stands Out: Noguchi’s Akari series is a revolutionary blend of Japanese paper craftsmanship and modernist sculpture. These light installations were designed to soften light and create ambiance.

Key Features:

  • Design Philosophy: Noguchi believed art should enhance daily life, and the Akari series does just that.
  • Materials: The washi paper and wood frame create a delicate yet sturdy structure.
  • Rarity: Many Akari pieces are one-of-a-kind, making them highly collectible.

Investment Potential: Noguchi’s sculptures have seen steady appreciation, with Akari pieces becoming more desirable. This is a great entry point for design investors.

10. Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec – Aristide Bruant in His Cabaret (1892)

Medium: Lithograph Estimated Value: $200,000–$400,000

Why It Stands Out: Lautrec’s Aristide Bruant is a masterpiece of poster art, capturing the vibrant nightlife of Montmartre. The expressive figures and bold colors make it one of the most iconic prints of the 19th century.

Key Features:

  • Style: Lautrec’s post-impressionist technique blends realism with exaggeration, creating dynamic energy.
  • Cultural Impact: This poster defined the Parisian cabaret scene, influencing modern advertising and art.
  • Rarity: Original lithographs are extremely rare, making this a highly sought-after collectible.

Investment Potential: Vintage Lautrec posters have consistently appreciated, with limited-edition prints becoming more valuable. This is a smart investment for poster and print collectors.

11. Richard Serra – Prop (1969)

Medium: Corten steel Estimated Value: $1–2 million

Why It Stands Out: Serra’s Prop is a landmark in minimalist sculpture, using raw, industrial materials to create a sense of weight and gravity.

Key Features:

  • Design Philosophy: Serra believed in art as a physical experience, and Prop embodies this tactile, immersive approach.
  • Materials: The Corten steel develops a rust-like patina, adding to its organic yet industrial appeal.
  • Rarity: Few Prop sculptures are available, making this a highly desirable piece.

Investment Potential: Serra’s minimalist works have steady demand, with steel sculptures becoming more valuable. This is a strong investment for modern art collectors.

12. Zaha Hadid – Parametric Chair (2002)

Medium: Fiberglass and steel Estimated Value: $100,000–$200,000

Why It Stands Out: Hadid’s Parametric Chair is a revolutionary piece of parametric design, blending fluid forms with structural precision. It redefines modernist furniture with its organic yet geometric shape.

Key Features:

  • Design Philosophy: Hadid’s work challenges traditional architecture, using mathematical algorithms to create dynamic forms.
  • Materials: The fiberglass and steel make it lightweight yet durable.
  • Rarity: Original Parametric Chairs are extremely scarce, making this a highly collectible piece.

Investment Potential: Hadid’s design works have seen significant appreciation, with vintage pieces becoming more valuable. This chair is a smart addition for futuristic design investors.
