Why TEFAF New York 2024 Was a Must-Attend Event

Before we explore the 12 standout works, it’s essential to understand why TEFAF New York has become a must-visit for art professionals.

1. A Global Hub for Rare & High-End Art

Unlike traditional auctions, TEFAF New York offers private viewings, VIP access, and a curated marketplace where blue-chip galleries (such as Christie’s, Sotheby’s, and Phillips) coexist with emerging specialists.

Key Statistic:

  • 68% of 2024’s top-selling works (over $1M each) were pre-sold or sold on the first day (Art Basel Report, 2024).

2. The Rise of "Experience-Driven" Art Fairs

TEFAF has shifted from being just a marketplace to an immersive cultural event. In 2024, the fair included:

  • Live performances by classical musicians
  • Virtual reality exhibitions of historical artworks
  • Workshops on art authentication led by INTERPOL’s Art Crime Unit

3. The Role of Galleries Like Galerie Selects

While big-name galleries dominate headlines, specialist galleries like Galerie Selects play a crucial role:

  • They fill gaps in the market (e.g., African textiles, Soviet-era design)
  • They discover hidden gems before mainstream auctions
  • They educate buyers on niche markets

Pro Tip: If you’re attending an art fair, spend at least 20% of your time with mid-sized galleries—they often have the best value-for-money pieces.

The 12 Best Works from Galerie Selects at TEFAF New York 2024

Galerie Selects curated a diverse yet cohesive collection, balancing historical prestige, contemporary relevance, and investment potential. Below is a detailed breakdown of each work, including artist background, provenance, and why it’s significant.

1. Pierre-Auguste Renoir – Luncheon of the Boating Party (1881) – Study

Why It’s Special: While the full Luncheon of the Boating Party (now in the Metropolitan Museum of Art) is iconic, this rare study (estimated at $8–12M) offers a unique glimpse into Renoir’s process.

Key Details:

  • Medium: Oil on canvas
  • Provenance: Previously owned by Paul Cassirer Gallery (Berlin), a key dealer in the German Expressionist market
  • Market Impact: Studies by Renoir have doubled in value over the past 5 years (Artnet Price Database, 2024)

Why Collectors Love It:

  • Lower entry point than full-scale works
  • Strong provenance (linked to early 20th-century European collectors)
  • Investment potential—Renoir’s works have consistently outperformed the market (Sotheby’s, 2023)

2. El Anatsui – Ghana Freedom (2019)

Why It’s Special: El Anatsui, a Ghanaian-British sculptor, is known for recycled aluminum and copper wire installations. Ghana Freedom (estimated at $1.5–2.5M) is a large-scale textile-like sculpture, blending African textiles with post-colonial themes.

Key Details:

  • Medium: Aluminum and copper wire, mixed media
  • Provenance: Commissioned by MoMA PS1 for their 2020 exhibition
  • Market Impact: African contemporary art has seen a 45% increase in auction prices since 2020 (Phillips, 2024)

Why Collectors Love It:

  • Represents a growing market (African art now makes up 12% of global contemporary sales)
  • Strong institutional backing (MoMA, Tate Modern)
  • Sustainable materials—appeals to eco-conscious collectors

3. Jean-Michel Basquiat – Untitled (Skull) (1982)

Why It’s Special: Basquiat’s skull motifs are among his most iconic and collectible works. This small-scale painting (estimated at $3–5M) is from his early New York City period, when he was collaborating with Andy Warhol.

Key Details:

  • Medium: Acrylic and oil on canvas
  • Provenance: Previously in the collection of a major European banker
  • Market Impact: Basquiat’s works have tripled in value since 2020 (Christie’s, 2024)

Why Collectors Love It:

  • High liquidity—Basquiat is one of the top 3 most traded artists (after Picasso and Warhol)
  • Strong cultural relevance—skulls symbolize rebellion and mortality, themes that resonate today
  • Lower price point than his large-scale canvases (which often exceed $50M)

4. Zaha Hadid – The Curve (1995) – Architectural Model

Why It’s Special: While Zaha Hadid is best known for her buildings, this early architectural model (estimated at $200K–$400K) offers a rare glimpse into her design philosophy.

Key Details:

  • Medium: Wood, metal, and digital renderings
  • Provenance: Directly from Hadid’s London studio
  • Market Impact: Architectural models by starchitects have seen a 50% price surge since 2020 (Sotheby’s, 2024)

Why Collectors Love It:

  • Investment in design history—Hadid’s work is future-proof
  • Lower risk than buying a completed building (which can be illiquid)
  • Strong institutional demand—museums are actively acquiring her models

5. Henri Matisse – The Green Stripe (1905) – Cut-Out Study

Why It’s Special: This rare study for Matisse’s 1905 The Green Stripe (now in the Museum of Modern Art) is a preliminary sketch (estimated at $1.2–1.8M).

Key Details:

  • Medium: Gouache on paper
  • Provenance: Once in the collection of a French art dealer who worked with Picasso
  • Market Impact: Matisse’s early works have outperformed the market by 22% since 2020 (Artnet, 2024)

Why Collectors Love It:

  • Lower price than full paintings but same artistic value
  • Strong provenance—linked to early 20th-century avant-garde circles
  • Investment potential—Matisse’s works appreciate steadily over time

6. Yinka Shonibare – The Swing (After Fragonard) (2001)

Why It’s Special: Shonibare’s reimagining of Fragonard’s The Swing uses Dutch wax print fabric (a nod to colonial trade), making it a powerful commentary on identity and power.

Key Details:

  • Medium: Mixed media (fabric, mannequins, wood)
  • Provenance: Commissioned by Tate Modern for a major exhibition
  • Market Impact: Shonibare’s works have seen a 60% price increase in the last 3 years (Phillips, 2024)

Why Collectors Love It:

  • **Represents a diverse, growing market (African and diaspora art)
  • Strong institutional backing (Tate, MoMA)
  • Conversational piece—great for public-facing collections

7. Salvador Dalí – The Elephants (1948) – Surrealist Study

Key Details:

  • Medium: Oil on canvas

  • **Lower price than his large-scale canvases (which often exceed $20M)
  • Investment potential—Dalí’s works appreciate faster than most

8. Anish Kapoor – Cloud Gate (2006) – Sculpture Fragment
