Actionable Strategies for Success in Collectible Design

To navigate the world of collectible design, it's essential to have a well-thought-out strategy. Here are 8 actionable tips to help you succeed:

  • Stay up-to-date with market trends: Keep an eye on current market trends and demand for different types of design pieces. This will help you make informed decisions when buying or selling.
  • Build relationships with dealers and designers: Establishing relationships with reputable dealers and designers can provide you with access to exclusive pieces and valuable insights into the market.
  • Diversify your portfolio: Spread your risk by investing in a variety of design pieces, including furniture, lighting, and decorative arts.
  • Consider the provenance of a piece: The history and ownership of a piece can significantly impact its value, so be sure to research the provenance of any piece before making a purchase.
  • Get a professional appraisal: If you're unsure about the value or authenticity of a piece, consider hiring a professional appraiser to provide an expert opinion.
  • Keep records of your collection: Maintaining detailed records of your collection, including purchase prices, sales, and conservation work, can help you track the value of your pieces over time.
  • Stay informed about conservation and restoration: Understanding the best practices for conserving and restoring design pieces can help you maintain their value and ensure they remain in good condition.
  • Join a community of collectors: Connecting with other collectors and enthusiasts can provide valuable opportunities for networking, learning, and staying up-to-date with market developments.
  • Set a budget and stick to it: Establishing a budget and sticking to it can help you avoid making impulsive purchases and ensure that your collecting activities remain sustainable.

Real-World Examples of Successful Collectible Design

There are many examples of successful collectible design, from the iconic Eames Lounge Chair to the rare and valuable Art Deco pieces of the 1920s and 1930s. One notable example is the Barcelona Chair, designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich in 1929. This chair has become an iconic symbol of modern design and can sell for tens of thousands of dollars at auction.

Another example is the Works of Ettore Sottsass, an Italian designer who was a key figure in the Memphis Group, a collective of designers who sought to challenge traditional notions of design in the 1980s. Sottsass's work, including his colorful and geometric furniture pieces, has become highly collectible and can sell for significant sums at auction.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Collectible Design

While collecting design can be a rewarding and enjoyable hobby, there are several common mistakes to avoid. These include:

  • Failing to research a piece thoroughly: Not taking the time to research a piece's history, provenance, and market value can lead to overpaying or purchasing a fake or reproduction.
  • Not working with reputable dealers: Buying from unscrupulous dealers can result in purchasing low-quality or fake pieces, so it's essential to work with reputable and established dealers.
  • Not maintaining proper records: Failing to keep detailed records of your collection can make it difficult to track the value of your pieces over time and may lead to difficulties when selling or insuring your collection.
  • Not considering the condition of a piece: The condition of a piece can significantly impact its value, so it's essential to carefully inspect any piece before purchasing and to consider the cost of any necessary restoration or conservation work.
